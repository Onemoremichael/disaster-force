# Quantitative models

The workbooks are executable design arguments. They connect mission demand to capacity, configuration, mass, energy, workforce, readiness, industrial scale, cost, and evidence gates. They are designed for inspection and sensitivity analysis; they are not independent cost estimates, acquisition baselines, forecasts, or proof that a proposed system will work.

All monetary values are real 2026 U.S. dollars unless a sheet says otherwise. Source-backed inputs, derived quantities, and design-study assumptions are visually distinguished inside each workbook. Intentional `OPEN` checks expose a research or program contradiction; they are not spreadsheet defects.

## How to use the models

Begin on each workbook's `Executive`, `Read Me`, or control sheet. Change only designated assumptions first, inspect the linked balances and scenario outputs, then review the checks and sources. A plausible top-line result is not sufficient: a configuration must also close its deployment clock, interfaces, workforce, maintenance, degraded modes, and public-effect chain.

The workbooks answer different questions and their budgets are not automatically additive. The original $1T force-capital constraint, the domain-specific $1T fire and flood thought experiments, the $130B flood RDT&E program, the selected $2T Department-wide technology case, and the $320B proving-network cross-classification are intentionally distinct analytical frames.

## Maturity rule

Arithmetic closure is a prerequisite to engineering evidence, not a substitute for it. Most configuration models are `R2+`: more detailed than a bounded concept, but still awaiting representative hardware, solved site cases, verified rates, reliability evidence, and independent mission-thread trials. Platform renderings remain blocked until `R3` physics closure.
