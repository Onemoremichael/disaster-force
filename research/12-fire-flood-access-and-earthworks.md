# Fire, flood, access, and earthworks systems

## Operating thesis

Fire and flood appear opposite, but their force-design problem is similar: a fast-moving fluid overwhelms ordinary control, access degrades, time value is extreme, and isolated machines are ineffective without sensing, command, logistics and ground work.

The Department should build integrated **hazard-control chains**:

```text
forecast and detect → classify → reach → apply control → hold → monitor → restore
```

Aerial water without a line that crews can hold is transient. A pump without drainage path and discharge authority moves the problem. A bridge without approaches and traffic control is not access.

Pass 11 extends the fire sections below into a complete Ignition Intercept Network, Integrated Fire Control Group and 25-year technology program. See [Fire response force engineering](22-fire-response-force-engineering.md). The earlier platform bands remain exploration boundaries, not specifications.

## Fire aviation missions

Fire aviation has at least six distinct missions:

1. persistent detection and fire-behavior mapping;
2. rapid initial attack before escape;
3. precise support to ground crews;
4. line building with retardant;
5. point protection, evacuation and logistics; and
6. crew, cargo and medical movement.

No aircraft is optimal for all six.

NIFC's current fleet illustrates the trade space. Single-engine tankers deliver up to roughly 800 gallons, large tankers 2,000–4,000 gallons and very large tankers up to 9,400 gallons; water scoopers can repeatedly refill from suitable water, while helicopters trade payload for precision and access.[^nifc-airtankers][^nifc-helicopters] These are current categories, not end-state requirements.

## Purpose-built fire-air family

### FA-1 Persistent Fire Sentinel

**Role:** continuous detection, mapping, weather sampling, communications relay and drop-effect assessment.

**Architecture:** solar/high-endurance and conventional uncrewed variants; multispectral and thermal sensing; onboard edge processing; mesh and satellite relay; automatic handoff to incident systems; collision and airspace integration.

The Sentinel closes the night and smoke information gap. It does not independently authorize suppression drops or evacuation.

### FA-2 Rapid Attack Aircraft

**Role:** arrive early and deliver repeated precise water, gel, foam or retardant before a fire becomes a campaign.

**Concept band:** 800–2,000 gallons; high hot-and-high performance; short or austere runway; rapid ground refill; optional water-scooping variant; high dispatch reliability; two-pilot or optionally piloted growth path.

**Design emphasis:** response time, daily cycles, precision, maintainability and low-speed handling—not cruise efficiency alone.

### FA-3 Regional Amphibious Suppressor

**Role:** sustained high-sortie water application when suitable lakes, rivers, reservoirs or protected coastal water are near the fire.

**Concept band:** 2,000–4,000 gallons; fast scoop cycle; operation from rougher and salt water than current designs where feasible; corrosion-resistant structure; reversible additive injection; high maneuver margin; rapid engine and hull inspection.

Current scoopers demonstrate that refill can take seconds but requires a water body with adequate depth and width.[^nifc-airtankers] The requirement is therefore expressed as **delivered gallons per fireline hour at a stated source distance and sea/lake state**, not tank volume.

### FA-4 Heavy Line Builder

**Role:** place long, controlled retardant lines ahead of high-intensity fire and support major campaigns.

**Concept band:** 8,000–15,000 gallons; purpose-built load path and fatigue spectrum; segmented constant-flow tank; precision doors; terrain-aware flight guidance; robust low-speed control; multiple emergency load-jettison modes; instrumented structural health.

Unlike converted transports, the fuselage, wing, tail, tank and flight controls are designed around repeated low-altitude maneuver, rapidly changing mass and corrosive payload. The aircraft is not assumed to extinguish a fire; its effect is line length and coverage level that ground forces can exploit.

### FA-5 Precision Lift and Suppression Rotorcraft

**Role:** hover refill, point protection, inaccessible terrain, crew/cargo insertion and rescue.

The family should separate a medium rapid platform from a heavy lift/suppression platform. Interchangeable tanks or buckets are retained for flexibility, but integral tanks, snorkels, protected engines, night sensors and mission-specific flight controls improve sortie performance.

### FA-6 Fire Aviation Base System

The base is part of the weapon system in the noncombat sense: retardant mixing, water, fuel, loading, maintenance, crew rest, weather, airspace coordination, spare parts and contamination control. Deployable satellite bases should expand sortie generation without requiring permanent airports at every risk point.

## Fire ground family

### FG-1 Wildland Mobility Engine

A family of narrow, low-center-of-gravity, heat- and smoke-protected vehicles sized for trail, interface and heavy off-road work. Variants carry water, foam, hose, crew, medical rescue or communications. Common driveline and protection reduce support burden.

### FG-2 Autonomous Fireline Tractor

An optionally crewed dozer/tractor with remote operation, thermal cameras, local autonomy, rollover and fire protection, replaceable tools and digital line-quality reporting. Its primary value is sustained work in heat, smoke, falling-tree and steep-slope conditions that would otherwise impose unacceptable exposure.

### FG-3 Long-Reach Water and Hose System

High-flow pumps, relay nodes, hose or pipe, mobile reservoirs and robotic line placement bring water from reliable sources to ground operations when hydrants fail. The family shares hydraulic interfaces with flood and emergency-water systems but uses fire-specific pressure, abrasion and rapid-deployment components.

### FG-4 Structure and Ember Protection System

Rapid exterior wetting, ember-resistant vents and covers, portable pumps, building assessment, clean-agent or water-mist interior systems and neighborhood sensor networks support point protection. The goal is not to promise every-building defense; it is to protect defensible clusters and critical facilities with far less water and crew exposure.

## Fire performance model

Aircraft requirement is calculated from sortie productivity:

\[
G_{delivered/day} = G_{tank} \times N_{cycles} \times A_{dispatch} \times Y_{drop} \times Y_{objective}
\]

where cycle count includes source/base distance, queue, loading, drop and crew limits; (Y_{drop}) accounts for placement and coverage; and (Y_{objective}) accounts for whether the drop supports a tactically useful outcome.

The Forest Service's Aerial Firefighting Use and Effectiveness work emphasizes that performance varies with objective, terrain, fuel, fire condition and ground engagement.[^fs-afue] Bigger payload without higher objective yield may produce less useful effect per dollar or risk hour.

## Flood mission taxonomy

Flood systems must distinguish:

- flash flood and moving-water rescue;
- urban pluvial flooding and failed drainage;
- riverine overtopping and levee breach;
- coastal surge and compound rainfall;
- long-duration basin inundation and dewatering;
- dam or control-structure failure;
- contaminated water and industrial release; and
- post-fire flood and debris flow.

Water depth alone does not specify the mission. Velocity, debris, contamination, wave state, current, ground bearing, elevation and discharge path control equipment selection.

## Flood-control family

### FL-1 Flood Intelligence Mesh

Air, surface and fixed sensors map water depth, velocity, breach flow, ground elevation, drainage capacity, contamination and people. Forecast models update pump and barrier allocation. Disposable or recoverable markers provide navigation through submerged streets.

### FL-2 High-Volume Pump Train

**Role:** drain basins, bypass failed stormwater or wastewater systems, protect critical facilities and transfer water around damaged controls.

**Variants:** rapid trailer/skid pumps; containerized regional sets; very high-volume barge or heavy-trailer sets.

The formation includes suction and discharge hose/pipe, screens, debris management, fuel or electrical power, priming, anchors, manifolds, telemetry, maintenance and discharge assessment. USACE guidance stresses that high river stage can disable normal drainage and that pipes, channels and pump locations must be designed together.[^usace-flood-manual]

Capacity is net m³/s at actual head and debris condition, with energy and downstream effect reported.

### FL-3 Temporary Barrier and Closure System

Rapid inflatable, water-filled, framed membrane, sheet-pile and modular wall systems provide different heights, foundations, seepage control and durations. Autonomous survey and placement improve speed; geotechnical authority determines whether the ground and tie-ins can carry the load.

The system includes pumps and interior drainage because a barrier that traps rainfall and sewer flow can worsen flooding.

### FL-4 Breach and Control-Structure Works

Heavy earthmoving, rock and aggregate placement, geotextile, sheet pile, crane, barge, dredge and concrete modules stabilize levees, dams, channels and coastal defenses. This is a formation with material supply and survey, not a special vehicle.

### FL-5 Amphibious Rescue and Logistics Family

The family separates:

- shallow urban craft with guarded propulsion and high maneuverability;
- high-water wheeled or tracked carriers for low-current zones;
- swift-water rescue craft;
- heavy amphibious cargo ferries;
- accessible evacuation craft; and
- autonomous shuttle and search craft.

Every platform publishes water depth, velocity, wave, debris, contamination, slope, ground-bearing and egress limits. “Amphibious” is not permission to enter every flood.

## Earthworks and debris family

### EW-1 Reconnaissance and Marking Team

Structural, utility, hazardous-material, geotechnical and environmental survey precedes heavy work. Machine-readable markings preserve search status, hazards, ownership and debris class.

### EW-2 Common Autonomous Work Machine

A power and control core accepts dozer, excavator, loader, grapple, cutter, compactor and trenching tools. It can be crewed, teleoperated or supervised autonomously. Common cores reduce spares while tool and chassis sizes preserve task fit.

### EW-3 Debris Processing Line

Mobile lines sort, crush, shred, screen, decontaminate, recover metal and package residuals. They are paired with air monitoring, runoff control, fire protection, traffic management and data capture.

The effect unit is tons or cubic yards reaching a compliant reuse, treatment or disposal state—not merely removed from the road.

### EW-4 Rapid Utility Corridor System

Survey, trench, protective conduit, pipe, cable, access boxes and road-repair modules create shared temporary corridors for water, power and communications. Robotic construction reduces repeated excavation by separate utilities.

## Bridge and route restoration

### BR-1 Immediate Crossing

Light and medium modular spans restore emergency vehicles, pedestrians and utilities over short gaps. They prioritize hours-to-days installation and accept limited life and traffic class.

### BR-2 Heavy Modular Bridge

Reusable components, modular piers, foundations, launch or crane systems and approach kits restore heavy logistics. A catalog of span and load classes is more useful than one bridge.

### BR-3 Accelerated Permanent Bridge

Prefabricated elements, geosynthetic reinforced approaches, slide or lift installation and standardized connections move directly from emergency access to durable civil infrastructure. FHWA reports that accelerated methods can replace some bridges in days and reduce onsite construction through offsite fabrication.[^fhwa-abc]

### Route restoration train

Bridge components deploy with survey, demolition, debris, geotechnical, crane, earthwork, paving, drainage, traffic control and inspection. The requirement is a route reopened to a stated load and daily throughput.

## Port, airfield and logistics-node opening

The Department should create joint opening groups with:

- hydrographic and obstruction survey;
- salvage, towing and channel clearance;
- temporary navigation aids;
- expeditionary piers, ramps and causeways;
- runway and apron assessment and repair;
- cargo handling, cranes, forklifts and autonomous yards;
- fuel, power, water, communications and responder support;
- customs, agricultural, hazardous and medical control; and
- movement scheduling and onward distribution.

Opening is measured by sustained people and cargo throughput under damaged conditions, not by declaring the facility operational.

## Requirement verdicts

| Concept | Verdict | Governing condition |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose-built heavy fire aircraft | Admit | Must demonstrate mission fatigue spectrum, objective yield, basing and ground integration |
| Very large universal firefighting aircraft | Reject | Payload does not solve initial attack, precision, water-source, airspace or ground-hold missions simultaneously |
| Regional amphibious scooper family | Admit | High value where water geometry and cycle time support repeated sorties |
| Autonomous fireline and debris machines | Admit | Strong exposure and tempo case; human authority remains for destructive and high-risk decisions |
| High-volume pump fleet | Admit | Must include drainage, debris, energy, discharge and downstream effects |
| One “flood vehicle” | Reject | Moving water, shallow urban, debris, contamination and heavy logistics require distinct hull and mobility classes |
| Drone-built utility corridor | Admit as combined system | Air systems survey and pull pilot lines; ground and heavy lift place mass and make safe connections |
| Rapid-to-permanent modular bridges | Admit | Standardized foundations, approaches, inspection and load classes are as important as spans |
