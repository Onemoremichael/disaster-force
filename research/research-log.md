# Research log

## Pass 1 — foundations

**Research date:** 2026-09-04

**Question:** What empirical and doctrinal foundation is required before designing the institution, force packages, or platforms?

**Source rule:** Prefer government datasets, doctrine, after-action reviews, technical reports, and original research. Use secondary material only to locate or interpret primary evidence.

### First discovery wave

The first wave identified the main evidence families and answer slots:

- FEMA National Response Framework, National Preparedness Goal, Community Lifelines, THIRA/SPR, National Risk Index, and National Preparedness Report.
- NOAA/NCEI weather and climate loss data and methodology.
- USGCRP Fifth National Climate Assessment chapters on compound events, energy, health, and complex systems.
- GAO and agency after-action evidence on the 2017 hurricane sequence, Katrina, communications, grid restoration, medical response, workforce, debris, wildfire, and housing.
- CBO flood-loss and adaptation analysis; FEMA/USGS earthquake-loss analysis; CDC mortality studies; Census experimental displacement estimates.
- Army and Joint Staff doctrine on force projection and integrated functions, used only to define the projection analogy.

Searches were bounded around exact agencies, document titles, event names, and claim families. Broad news and opinion sources were excluded.

### Gap matrix after discovery

| Claim family | Discovery result | Remaining material gap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hazard and loss scale | Strong but incompatible official series | Harmonized all-hazard ledger and post-2024 NOAA continuity |
| Mortality | Strong evidence that indirect pathways and methods matter | Consistent national direct, indirect, and excess-mortality series |
| Displacement | One recent experimental national estimate | Duration, return, repeated movement, and permanent migration |
| Lifeline doctrine | Strong federal outcome taxonomy | Quantitative national readiness and stabilization thresholds |
| Catastrophic bottlenecks | Saturated across major cases | Current throughput, service curves, and simultaneous-theater capacity |
| Workforce | Persistent saturation and qualification evidence | Mission-by-mission staffing requirement and current deployable inventory |
| Power, water, communications, and medicine | Strong evidence of coupled failure and interface mismatch | Technical requirement classes and current capacity inventories |
| Force projection analogy | Strong process doctrine | Translation into civilian authorities, professional governance, and transfer doctrine |

### Focused follow-up wave

The second wave tested the highest-impact and potentially misleading claims:

- reconciled NOAA costly-event scope against CBO flood and FEMA/USGS earthquake estimates;
- preserved direct, indirect, and excess-mortality distinctions;
- verified that FEMA capability targets combine task, impact, and timeframe;
- checked the transition from impact categories to the eight-lifeline construct;
- verified Puerto Rico temporary-power scale, restoration duration, medical mismatch, and communications-role findings across USACE and GAO;
- tested power-water coupling with Texas public-water after-action data;
- verified compound-event and cross-sector-cascade findings in the Fifth National Climate Assessment; and
- checked the force-projection lifecycle and integrated-function analogy against official Army and Joint Staff material.

### Reconciliation decisions

1. No combined “annual disaster loss” figure is reported. NOAA events, expected annual loss models, CBO scopes, federal obligations, and social costs remain separate.
2. Rising loss is attributed to interacting exposure, vulnerability, measurement, and hazard changes; no single-factor explanation is used.
3. The NOAA 1980–2024 series is labeled historical and archived, not current beyond its endpoint.
4. Platform claims are excluded. Existing systems appear only as operational evidence, not prototypes for the future force.
5. Current political changes at FEMA are not used to justify the concept; enduring capacity and coordination findings across events are sufficient.

### Stop decision

Research stopped when three conditions were met:

1. multiple official cases converged on the same cross-lifeline failure chain;
2. the requirement grammar, service-effect architecture, and denominator guardrails had primary support; and
3. additional retrospective cases were unlikely to change the Pass 1 conclusions.

The remaining gaps require a different research phase—technical throughput, service-restoration curves, readiness inventories, institutional alternatives, and force-sizing—not more general disaster anecdotes.
