# Inventing the flood force: research, prototyping and independent test

## Decision purpose

The flood force defined in Passes 13 and 14 is intentionally beyond the present technological baseline. Its most consequential capabilities—megaflow modular pumping, rapid large-bore drainage, self-instrumented barriers, live-flow breach arrest, mixed autonomous fleets, optionally crewed wet earthworks, environment-specific amphibious systems, contamination-aware dewatering and hydraulic works carriers—cannot be responsibly obtained by writing ambitious specifications and asking industry to promise compliance.

They require an institution designed to **create new technical options, destroy weak hypotheses early, measure complete civilian effects and carry the survivors into production without converting hope into readiness credit**.

This pass designs that institution and its first 25-year flood research campaign. The accompanying [Flood Research, Test & Evaluation Campaign workbook](../../../models/flood-research-test-evaluation.xlsx) allocates the existing $130 billion flood RDT&E envelope, specifies a new national proving network, registers 46 experiments and evaluations, maintains competing prototype cohorts, maps every experiment to the twenty open R3 gates, profiles annual funding and tests the portfolio against cost and schedule failure.

The central judgment is simple:

> A Department of Resilience equal in standing to the defense establishment must be an engine of invention, not merely a premium buyer of existing emergency equipment. But invention becomes national power only when an independent authority can reproduce it, break it, bound its operating envelope and verify the complete service effect.

This is still pre-rendering work. `FIN` and `HCG-100` remain `R2+`. A research plan does not close a physics gate, and a prototype cohort is not a fleet.

## Executive judgment

Seven findings organize the campaign.

1. **Research must target operational coefficients, not technology adjectives.** “Autonomous,” “adaptive,” “AI-enabled,” “nuclear,” “amphibious” and “megaflow” have no independent value. A new capability earns attention only by changing a visible distribution or constraint: action time, delivered flow at actual head, route mass, protected-side water level, breach-flow reduction, human exposure, patient throughput, environmental release risk, formation availability or cost.
2. **The Department needs two separate institutions.** An Advanced Resilience Projects Agency (`ARPA-R`) should create options, fund rival paths and redirect work aggressively. A Resilience Test and Evaluation Authority (`RTEA`) should own evidence standards, configuration control, independent data, destructive ranges and acceptance decisions. The program that needs a result must not certify its own result.
3. **The experimental article is a configuration, not a product name.** Hardware revision, software build, sensors, operators, maintenance state, power source, intake, route, weather, debris, communications and host support are part of the tested system. Evidence cannot migrate silently from one configuration to another.
4. **The nation needs a new proving network at the scale of the proposed force.** Existing USACE, FHWA, NIST, EPA, NOAA and Coast Guard facilities provide authoritative methods and valuable lower bounds. They are not sufficient organizationally or physically for simultaneous full-scale pump, 20-kilometre route, protected-basin, destructive breach, amphibious, contaminated-water and 90-day formation trials.
5. **Failure is a planned research output.** The modeled portfolio begins with 54 rival paths across the ten technology campaigns, carries 43 through TRL 5, 30 through TRL 6 and 20 into TRL 7. It does not presume that every line becomes a program of record. A failed destructive trial can save more public value than a successful demonstration if it prevents premature factories and fleets.
6. **Subsystem evidence is necessary but insufficient.** The campaign escalates from materials and components to full-scale subsystems, integrated formations, 30- and 90-day endurance and a three-theater evaluation. `R3` requires the applicable physics and interfaces **and** an independent complete mission thread.
7. **The $130 billion allocation is large but not automatically robust.** The workbook assigns $24 billion to facilities and $106 billion to science, prototypes, field trials, data, standards and transition. Its baseline peaks at approximately $11.32 billion in year 8. A compound facility, redesign, delay and integration shock raises need to $156.25 billion—$26.25 billion above the envelope. The separate $25 billion program reserve is therefore insufficient by $1.25 billion unless leaders cancel, narrow or resequence work.

## 1. The technological premise

Military power is not produced solely by buying mature commodities. Long-horizon demand, protected research budgets, government laboratories, test ranges, doctrine, exercises, technical intelligence, competing prototypes and assured production allow the state to shift the technological frontier. A peer resilience establishment should use the same seriousness for hostile environmental conditions.

The analogy has limits. A resilience system acts in inhabited civil terrain, under environmental law and civil authority, while trying to reduce rather than impose harm. Its performance measures must therefore include distributional consequences, accessibility, environmental fate, worker exposure, host burden and safe transfer to civilian operators. The relevant innovation system is not a copied defense acquisition bureaucracy. It is a purpose-built civil-protection research state with stronger public-effect accounting.

The Department should pursue three classes of advance simultaneously.

### 1.1 Scientific advances

These change what can be inferred or controlled:

- uncertainty-aware flood-state estimation that assimilates hydrologic forecasts, infrastructure telemetry, radar, local sensors and mobile observations fast enough to support action;
- prediction of intervention effects, including barrier-induced interior flooding, receiving-water constraints, breach growth, contamination transport and infrastructure restart;
- real-time contaminant classification and treatment-envelope inference under mixed, changing floodwater;
- validated human detection and safe navigation in opaque, reflective, debris-filled water; and
- materials, filters, foundations and flow-control mechanisms able to arrest failure without creating worse downstream hazards.

### 1.2 Engineering advances

These turn known physical principles into unprecedented deployable scale:

- common electric drives with exchangeable 1–15 m³/s wet ends, rather than one nominal “disaster pump”;
- recoverable 1–2 metre drainage trunks, crossings, manifolds, anchors and air/transient controls installed as a route system;
- rapidly founded, self-sensing barriers with deliberately coupled protected-side drainage and controlled exceedance;
- five distinct amphibious access families optimized for urban shallows, swift water, high-water land movement, heavy wet-gap logistics and coastal/surf operations;
- remote or optionally crewed excavation, material placement, dredging and debris handling in mud, current, low visibility and contamination; and
- riverine and coastal works carriers designed around launch, lift, repair, pipe, pumping and shore-interface throughput.

### 1.3 Institutional advances

These make capability reproducible:

- open government-owned interfaces and evidence schemas;
- test articles built with production-relevant processes before manufacturing credit;
- independent configuration and data custody;
- common qualification records across active, regional reserve, auxiliary and industrial personnel;
- warm competing suppliers beyond prototype award; and
- lifecycle evidence that joins production rate, spares, depot labor, corrosion, software sustainment and field repair.

This last class is easy to underrate. A laboratory breakthrough without a testable interface, production route, qualified crew and independent failure record is not projected power.

## 2. The evidence architecture

The Department should define evidence as a chain from question to decision:

`mission consequence → uncertain variable → causal hypothesis → competing intervention → controlled configuration → measurement → acceptance threshold → failure disposition → operational credit`

Every experiment begins with a decision, not a machine. For example:

- **Decision:** may an HCG credit 12 m³/s from a pump module in a debris-rich urban intake?
- **Uncertain variables:** head, submergence, debris mix, ragging, wear, power quality, control interaction and clearing time.
- **Hypothesis:** a particular wet end, intake and cleaning architecture sustains no more than 20 percent derating and restores a jam within a specified tail time.
- **Test:** a preregistered full-curve and debris matrix, followed by multi-pump transients and a 30-day endurance block.
- **Threshold:** performance and uncertainty bounds, not a single best run.
- **Credit:** only the tested configuration and envelope enter the force model.

USACE pumping-station guidance already requires field verification of pump performance and interaction, while wet run-in testing observes vibration, bearing temperature, power, noise, startup, shutdown and control behavior. It also calls for testing reverse rotation, backflow prevention and priming, and recommends controlled model testing when field observation is inadequate.[^usace-pump20] The Department should industrialize that logic at modular formation scale, including debris, source transfer, isolation, field maintenance and long endurance.

### 2.1 Six evidence rules

**Pre-register decision errors.** A test plan specifies false-safe and false-fail consequences, measurement quality, repetitions, missing-data treatment and stopping rules before the scored run. EPA quality guidance distinguishes performance criteria for newly generated data from acceptance criteria for external data and connects model quality to intended decisions.[^epa-qag5][^epa-qag5m]

**Lock the configuration.** Every scored result identifies hardware, software, operator qualification, support equipment, consumables, repairs and environmental state. A modified impeller, perception model or barrier termination is a new evidence relationship, not an informal improvement to the old one.

**Measure distributions.** P50 performance is inadequate for life safety and force planning. The campaign records tails, correlated failures and exposure-conditioned performance. Where destructive repetitions are expensive, the design combines physical tests, validated simulation, material variation and sequential Bayesian updating, but it does not manufacture confidence by treating simulation runs as independent physical events.

**Use capability-neutral tests.** NIST's robotics program develops replicable artifacts and measures mobility, manipulation, sensing, power and endurance; its associated safety work emphasizes measurable capability rather than prescribing a design.[^nist-robot][^nist-mobile] RTEA should likewise specify the water state, task, human presence, stop rule and metric without telling competitors which hull, sensor or control architecture must win.

**Publish failure.** Within security, privacy, proprietary and human-subject limits, the default release contains the as-tested configuration, calibration, raw and processed data, excluded observations, interventions, maintenance, uncertainty and causal failure analysis. The model sets a 60-day release target. Negative results remain discoverable when contractors or program managers change.

**Separate maturity coordinates.** NASA defines TRL 6 around a high-fidelity prototype in a relevant environment and TRL 7 around a prototype in an operational environment.[^nasa-trl] GAO's readiness guidance emphasizes evidence-based assessment, while manufacturing maturity progresses through production-relevant components, prototype systems, production-representative processes and pilot-line capability.[^gao-tra][^gao-mrl] This project retains a separate `R` scale: `R3` means the project-specific physics, interfaces, mission effect and evidence gates close. A high TRL for a pump does not make the integrated HCG R3.

## 3. Institutional design

### 3.1 Advanced Resilience Projects Agency

`ARPA-R` should be a Department-level agency with a Flood and Hydraulic Systems Directorate. Its program directors receive time-bounded authority, technical contracting support and budgets large enough to fund competing architectures rather than serial paper studies. Their job is to pose hard operational hypotheses, assemble unconventional teams, set quantitative milestones and cancel or redirect weak paths.

ARPA-E provides a useful method anchor: awards use aggressive quantitative milestones, Go/No-Go decisions and program-director authority to redirect technical work; its technology-to-market practice adds value proposition, economic analysis, market structure, supply chain and cost modeling rather than leaving transition until the end.[^arpae-foa][^arpae-ttm] `ARPA-R` should extend this model to public effects, emergency production, qualification, depot support and exportable civil standards.

Program directors should control opportunity framing, performer competition and research continuation. They should not own R3 acceptance.

### 3.2 Resilience Test and Evaluation Authority

`RTEA` should be a statutory independent authority inside the Department, reporting separately from service acquisition executives and `ARPA-R`. Its functions are:

1. set measurement and configuration standards;
2. approve preregistered test plans;
3. operate or accredit the national proving network;
4. hold raw evidence and software/model versions;
5. employ independent evaluators, statisticians, safety specialists and operational crews;
6. conduct technology and manufacturing readiness assessments;
7. run adversarial and degraded-mode trials;
8. accept, qualify, limit or reject an operating envelope;
9. certify achieved formation and concurrency capacity; and
10. publish evidence summaries and unresolved uncertainty.

`RTEA` is not a compliance office added after design. Its evaluators enter during hypothesis formation so that a program cannot reach a costly prototype with an unmeasurable claim. Independence means the authority can deny credit after a technically impressive demonstration, require a retest after configuration drift or publish that a mature-looking program has not closed the civilian effect.

### 3.3 Safety, environmental and civil authority

Evidence independence does not consolidate all authority in `RTEA`.

- A range safety or specialist board can stop a test immediately.
- Environmental authorities retain discharge and protected-resource decisions.
- Human-subject and medical authorities govern participant risk.
- Civil authorities choose legitimate protection, evacuation and distributional priorities.
- Technical authorities state physical consequences and can refuse unsafe work.
- The Secretary or delegated acquisition executive decides whether accepted evidence justifies production.

Stopping a test protects people and assets. It does not turn incomplete evidence into a pass, nor does a technical failure erase the validity of a safety stop.

## 4. National Hydraulic Proving Network

The proposed National Hydraulic Proving Network (`NHPN`) is a sovereign Department system with multiple cells, distributed field sites and independent scheduling. The workbook allocates $24 billion of facility capital across ten range families.

| Drainage Spine Corridor | $3.0B | year 8 | parallel 20 km routes with road/rail crossings, slopes, anchors, air management and surge basins |
| Barrier and Interior Basin | $3.0B | year 8 | full-height exclusion systems coupled to controlled rain, sewer/backflow, seepage, debris and overtopping |
| Live Breach Range | $2.5B | year 7 | parallel controllable failures across soil, head, opening and intervention mode |
| Amphibious Urban Water Range | $1.2B | year 6 | configurable streets, hidden obstacles, entanglement, accessible embarkation and contaminated washdown |
| Coastal Storm and Surf Field Range | $0.8B | year 9 | natural and controlled wave, tide, current, salt, surf and damaged-quay work |

| Flood State Digital Twin Range | $2.0B | year 5 | instrumented watersheds, synthetic truth, blind forecast scoring and disconnected operation |

| **Total** | **$24.0B** | **all by year 9** | included inside, not added to, the $130B envelope |

The network is ambitious because present facilities show both the feasibility and the limit of the starting point. ERDC's Coastal and Hydraulics Laboratory reports a 1.5-million-square-foot physical and numerical complex with wave, sediment, coastal, navigation and full-scale hydraulic assets.[^erdc-chl] Its full-scale breach facility occupies roughly 11 acres, holds about four million gallons, can accommodate a 40-foot breach and operates around a 2,100 cubic-feet-per-second spillway; it has supported PLUG, rehabilitation and erosion-control research.[^erdc-breach][^erdc-breach-spec] Those are major national assets. The proposed force nevertheless needs concurrent cells, larger configuration families, formation logistics, contaminated-water controls and sustained operational occupation that no one laboratory is designed to provide.

FHWA's J. Sterling Jones laboratory supplies another method anchor through flumes, particle-image velocimetry, force measurement, scour tools, robotics and combined physical/computational work.[^fhwa-jones] ERDC's Field Research Facility supplies natural storm, wave, current and morphology conditions through a 1,840-foot research pier and long-term field instrumentation.[^erdc-frf] The new network should partner with and learn from these institutions during early years while building independent Department capacity. Partnership is a transition strategy, not the end-state architecture.

### 4.1 Capacity as a research variable

The first workbook iteration revealed that a single-cell megaflow range, route corridor, breach basin and joint campus would exceed the 80 percent planning-utilization ceiling. The final configuration uses parallel or distributed cells. Average modeled peak utilization is 75.82 percent at the Barrier and Interior Basin; no facility exceeds the ceiling. Nominal capacity is 1,027 events per year across unlike event types.

This does not prove schedule feasibility. A 90-day formation trial can occupy a campus despite counting as one event. Detailed range scheduling must distinguish setup, reset, environmental windows, waste disposal, instrument calibration, maintenance and article repair. The capacity model is an early bottleneck detector, not a booking system.

The totals are strategic design allocations, not bottom-up cost estimates. The workbook spreads each campaign through a start, peak and end year using an editable ramp. Baseline annual funding rises from $3.64 billion in year 1 to a peak of approximately $11.32 billion in year 8, spends $85.28 billion in years 1–10 and $44.72 billion in years 11–25. That front-loading is intentional: range construction, competing prototypes and early destructive knowledge must precede national production.

### 5.1 Why twenty TRL 7 paths are not redundant

Two surviving operational prototypes per campaign do not imply buying both forever. They preserve competition across three uncertainties:

1. **Technical:** radically different architectures may trade efficiency, damage tolerance, maintainability and mass.
2. **Mission:** an option that loses the national fleet competition may dominate a regional, allied or commercial application.
3. **Industrial:** a technically superior monopoly can still be strategically brittle if its production, software or critical components have one point of failure.

Downselection should therefore occur on complete value: effect, uncertainty, deployment, human exposure, environmental consequence, production maturity, service labor, data rights and lifecycle cost. Unit purchase price alone is not the objective function.

## 6. Experimental program

The workbook registers 46 experiment and evaluation packages. Each package specifies campaign, facility, start/end year, type, allocation weight, repetitions, scale, primary measurement, pass/fail rule, gate and decision authority. The register allocates the full $106 billion non-facility envelope without leaving an orphan program line.

### 6.1 Flood state and causal prediction

The flood-state campaign begins with blind regional hindcasts, sensor-to-map latency under 40 percent sensor and backhaul loss, counterfactual barrier/pump intervention trials and a complete FIN alert-to-action exercise. NOAA's National Water Model demonstrates continental, high-frequency hydrologic guidance across millions of river reaches.[^noaa-nwm] The research target is not merely a denser forecast. It is an operational state estimate that reports uncertainty, identifies threshold-crossing time, predicts the consequence of an intervention and remains useful when local observations or communications fail.

The campaign tests both calibration and decision utility. A model can be statistically respectable but rank interventions badly. The proposed counterfactual test asks whether it identifies the highest-value three actions correctly in at least 80 percent of blind cases, while interval tests ask whether nominal 90 percent uncertainty bands actually cover approximately 85–95 percent of outcomes. Those numbers are hypotheses to refine, not validated standards.

### 6.2 Megaflow pump and intake evidence

Five experimental blocks cover full performance surfaces, debris/ragging, submergence/cavitation, 30-day endurance and black-start/source transfer. The credited object is the pump–intake–drive–control–manifold–crew system.

The workbook proposes:

- full maps across 20–100 percent flow rather than a nameplate point;
- stable operation without hidden exclusion islands;
- no more than 20 percent sustained derating in the qualified debris envelope;
- P90 remote clearing within 20 minutes;
- no damaging cavitation inside the published submergence boundary;
- at least 92 percent mission availability through the endurance block; and
- P90 restoration of stable flow within ten minutes of a source-loss event.

Instrumentation includes traceable flow/head, torque, electrical power, transient pressure, vibration, bearing and winding temperature, acoustics, high-speed intake imaging, debris mass balance, wear metrology, maintenance labor and operator intervention. A successful laboratory pump with an unqualified intake or propagating multi-pump trip does not close the formation gate.

### 6.3 Drainage route, barrier and breach

The Rapid Drainage Spine is tested as public infrastructure temporarily inserted into a functioning or damaged corridor. Experiments include full-diameter joint pressure pulses, road/rail crossing tournaments, a 20-kilometre build-and-recover trial and an alternate-route mission under receiving-stage closure. The target is not pipe deployment. It is delivered `m³/s-km` per tonne and setup-hour with bounded surge, safe crossings, leak detection, recovery and restored public access.

Barrier tests join foundation and termination failure, wave/current/debris load histories, interior drainage and controlled exceedance. This coupling is essential. A barrier that survives exterior water while priority cells flood from rain, seepage, sewer inflow or backflow has failed its mission. The reference trial therefore requires positive protected-side margin with one pump bay unavailable.

Breach research begins by measuring soil/head/opening growth, then competes live-flow arrest systems and tests the filtered transition to durable closure. The proposed performance hypothesis is a median 70 percent flow reduction within 60 minutes in the qualified matrix without creating an unsafe downstream missile, followed by 72 hours without progressive internal erosion. The test authority should deliberately vary soil, compaction, head, access, sensor loss and failed actuators. A plug that succeeds once in a clean rectangular breach is evidence of possibility, not an operating envelope.

### 6.4 Amphibious systems and autonomy

The program rejects “amphibious” as a universal class. Five vehicle families receive distinct hydrodynamic and human-system evidence. Urban craft are scored for low wake, shallow maneuver, submerged obstacles, wires, patient flow and decontamination. Swiftwater craft add current, strainers, night, cold-water crew load and self-righting. Coastal systems add surf, wind, tide, salt, launch/recovery and corrosion. Heavy amphibious logistics are measured in sustained tonnes per day, route damage, availability and recovery.

Coast Guard lifesaving-appliance practice and incorporated IMO procedures provide a lower-bound safety anchor: rescue craft must demonstrate stability, freeboard, crowding and recovery performance rather than rely on nominal capacity.[^uscg-lsa][^imo-lsa] The Department's mission is broader and requires accessible wheelchair, stretcher, bariatric and caregiver flows, but it should not be less rigorous about basic stability.

Autonomy receives separate evidence. Lost-link, GNSS loss, occlusion, reflective water, partial submergence, stale maps, spoofed objects, corrupt commands and network saturation are injected deliberately. Proposed thresholds include no person strikes, at least 99.5 percent safe-state entry in the scored fault set, P90 recovery within 30 minutes, high-sensitivity human detection and at least 60 percent manually sustained mission output during a cyber-corrupted common operating picture.

These are not claims that present autonomy can meet the thresholds. They are the conditions under which the force would be allowed to depend on it.

### 6.5 Wet earthworks and environmental process

Optionally crewed machines are tested first on a task matrix—excavation and placement by soil, water and visibility state—then on mode transfer, contaminated-sediment endurance and breach-to-durable-works integration. The output is a P10/P50/P90 productivity surface plus stop distance, recovery time, repair burden, exposure-hours and quality of placed work. Automation that reduces nominal crew but requires a large exposed recovery team may lose.

Environmental process tests treat water quality as an operational effect. EPA emergency-response research emphasizes that contaminated water may require containment or treatment before discharge and that mobile treatment evaluation includes transport, deployment, operation, cost and contaminant conditions.[^epa-ert] The proposed campaign tests rapid classification, variable-matrix treatment, 30-day residuals mass balance, accountable basin-to-discharge operation and decontamination cycles. Critical false-safe release is a stop condition. Mass balance, chain of custody, downstream monitoring and residual disposition are part of throughput.

## 7. Complete mission threads

FEMA's exercise doctrine links objectives to capability targets and critical tasks, observes performance, and carries findings into improvement planning.[^fema-hseep] The Department should retain that learning cycle while adding engineering configuration control and quantitative acceptance. The workbook defines eight escalating mission threads.

| Trial | Decision window | Duration | Primary question |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Forecast river-rise intercept | years 5–7 | 7 days | can distributed FIN cells act before local thresholds with calibrated uncertainty? |
| Flash/pluvial urban rescue | years 6–9 | 72 hours | can the correct access class isolate, search, rescue, decontaminate and hand off safely? |
| Trapped urban basin control | years 8–12 | 30 days | can an inland HCG maintain positive net removal and protected-side margin after a bay failure? |
| Coastal compound campaign | years 9–14 | 30 days | can a coastal HCG hold service through surge, rain, tide, salt and crew/corrosion burden? |
| Remote live breach | years 9–13 | 14 days | can the formation detect, evacuate, reduce flow, filter/close, dewater and repair with bounded exposure? |
| Contaminated industrial basin | years 10–15 | 21 days | can it characterize, isolate, treat/route, discharge and account for residuals without false-safe release? |
| Carrier-supported port-denied region | years 13–19 | 60 days | can riverine/coastal systems create a shore work interface without an intact port? |
| Three-theater concurrency | years 18–23 | 90 days | can the establishment allocate, deploy, sustain, rotate and reconstitute three formations plus FIN? |

Every scored trial locks the configuration, preregisters measures and missing-data rules, injects credible faults, evaluates civilian effects and retains a failure package. Exercises can train crews with rehearsal. Evidence trials should introduce blind or randomized disturbances where safety and ethics permit.

The 90-day concurrency evaluation is the decisive transition from equipment to institution. It must count ready crews, reliefs, strategic movement, receiving nodes, spares, maintenance, environmental processing, public interfaces, depot regeneration and the uncommitted reserve. If three “ready” formations draw on the same rare technicians, software service, intake component or carrier, the common dependency is revealed rather than averaged away.

## 8. Decision rights and firewalls

The campaign assigns explicit owners:

- `ARPA-R` frames opportunities, holds performer competitions and makes research Go/No-Go decisions.
- `RTEA` approves test plans, locks configurations, holds evidence, determines TRL and accepts or rejects R3 claims.
- safety and environmental authorities control stop and discharge decisions;
- an Industrial Readiness Board determines manufacturing maturity using production-representative evidence;
- the Resilience Acquisition Executive decides production only after technical, manufacturing and lifecycle cases converge; and
- the Joint Effects Commander employs accepted forces under the Department's two-key civil/operational authority.

Five firewalls are non-negotiable.

1. **No self-certification.** Program schedules and sunk costs cannot vote on evidence.
2. **No demonstration substitution.** A rehearsed successful event is not a distribution or an operating envelope.
3. **No TRL shortcut.** A subsystem at TRL 7 does not close formation physics, workforce or complete-thread gates.
4. **No production coercion.** Factory commitments cannot force acceptance of failed evidence.
5. **No silent inheritance.** Data, models, diagnostics and interface rights remain usable when a contractor, software provider or program path exits.

The workbook protects $13 billion—exactly 10 percent of the flood RDT&E envelope—for independent integration and T&E. The share is a floor. It should not be available for program-office overruns.

## 9. Failure, reserve and portfolio control

An innovation program that assumes technical success is not a research program. The workbook applies four transparent shocks beyond the embedded multiple-path strategy:

- facility overrun against the $24 billion range capital;
- redesign against the $106 billion non-facility portfolio;
- schedule burn at $1.2 billion per delay year; and
- integration shock against the $13 billion independent mission-thread line.

| Scenario | Calculated need | Above $130B | $25B reserve sufficient? | Schedule end |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| Baseline | $130.00B | $0 | yes | year 25 |
| 25% facility stress | $136.00B | $6.00B | yes | year 25 |
| 15% prototype redesign | $145.90B | $15.90B | yes | year 25 |
| Two-year slip | $132.40B | $2.40B | yes | year 27 |
| 15% integration shock | $131.95B | $1.95B | yes | year 25 |
| Compound stress | $156.25B | $26.25B | **no** | year 27 |
| Severe compound | $174.80B | $44.80B | **no** | year 29 |

These are sensitivities, not probabilistic forecasts. They reveal governance requirements. The separate $25 billion program reserve cannot be treated as a universal padding account; in the compound case it still leaves a $1.25 billion decision. `ARPA-R`, `RTEA` and Department leadership must choose which failed path merits redesign, which evidence can be sequenced, which facility scope should expand and which attractive concept should end.

Cancellation is not evidence that the institution failed. Refusing to cancel a disproven system is.

## 10. Industrial and economic consequence

The test program is also industrial policy. It creates demand for large electric drives, power electronics, bearings, seals, castings, valves, large-bore structures, rapid foundations, corrosion systems, rugged sensors, autonomous controls, treatment media, marine gear, metrology, calibration, test instrumentation and field repair. Unlike episodic disaster procurement, a 25-year evidence pipeline supports specialized firms whose core business is resilience technology.

Government-owned standards and test artifacts lower entry barriers. A new supplier can demonstrate a compatible pump wet end, barrier termination, sensor package or craft interface without owning a complete prime system. Open failure and lifecycle data also improve exports: partners can evaluate a bounded capability, train locally and substitute maintainable components rather than accept a proprietary black box.

The proposed prime-scale companies should resemble major aerospace and infrastructure integrators in technical depth, but the market need not collapse into a few vertically closed firms. The Department can preserve competition by:

- separating reference interfaces from proprietary implementation;
- buying test data and diagnostic rights;
- funding multiple foundries, drive suppliers, software stacks and repair sources;
- maintaining government test articles and digital reference environments;
- using production-representative competitions before fleet downselect; and
- licensing civil/export variants with local maintenance and manual-safe modes.

The strongest export is not a spectacular carrier. It is a trusted ecosystem of compatible modules, methods, qualification, training, depots and evidence.

## 11. Implications for force composition

This pass does not select exterior geometry, but it changes how the mature force should be composed.

First, the force needs permanent **experimental formations** alongside operational formations. These units supply qualified crews, maintain instrumented prototype configurations, reproduce operational tasks and retain failure knowledge. They are not ceremonial demonstration teams.

Second, every service needs an **operational test cadre** that can challenge—not merely assist—program offices. Hydraulic engineers, hydrologists, statisticians, environmental scientists, human-factors experts, maintainers, logisticians, accessibility specialists and civil authorities must evaluate together because the failure chains cross disciplines.

Third, the depot and industrial base enter testing early. A 30-day pump or 60-day carrier trial measures repair, parts consumption, corrosion, software intervention and technical-data adequacy. The maintainers who restore a failed system are part of the experimental subject.

Fourth, regional FIN nodes are research infrastructure. They can host long-duration sensor evaluation, forecast verification, barrier-socket trials and real exercises under varied watersheds. National ranges create control; distributed nodes create external validity.

Fifth, the Department should expect generational refresh. The first accepted system is not a terminal design. Continuous test should update climate states, infrastructure geometry, contaminants, cyber threats, material aging, workforce ergonomics and public-service expectations. Production and test lines stay warm enough to incorporate learning rather than waiting for a once-in-generation recapitalization.

## 12. R3 gate mapping

The campaign preserves all twenty Pass 14 evidence gates. It gives each one an experiment set and earliest decision year. Early gates cover representative cases, action distributions, basin models, pumps, intakes and drainage routes. Middle gates cover barriers, breaches, amphibious systems, autonomy, earthworks and environmental process. Later gates cover infrastructure interfaces, movement, workforce, concurrency, works carriers, lifecycle/industry and the complete mission thread.

The earliest complete-system decision remains year 23 because `G17` readiness/concurrency and `G20` complete mission thread cannot be inferred from subsystem success. This does not prevent limited earlier production. The Department can field bounded capabilities whose individual evidence closes, provided it labels their configuration and does not claim the full HCG effect.

The rule for future concept development is:

> Geometry may be explored for engineering experiments, but no public reference rendering should imply a selected operational vehicle until the relevant R3 evidence has made its form a consequence of measured requirements rather than imagination.

That distinction honors the user's desire for genuinely novel machinery. It delays spectacle in order to make the eventual designs more radical, coherent and defensible.

## What this pass changes

This pass moves the project beyond a technology roadmap in eight ways.

- It establishes `ARPA-R` and `RTEA` as institutionally separate creation and proof systems.
- It defines a $24 billion sovereign National Hydraulic Proving Network rather than relying on borrowed laboratories.
- It converts the ten flood technology campaigns into a 46-package experimental program.
- It maintains 54 initial competing paths, 43 at TRL 5, 30 at TRL 6 and 20 entering TRL 7.
- It assigns every open R3 gate an experiment family, acceptance rule and earliest decision year.
- It sizes range capacity against planned test demand and preserves a 20 percent scheduling margin.
- It links the $130 billion research envelope to an annual profile and explicit failure sensitivities.
- It keeps all flood systems at `R2+` and all exterior renderings prohibited.

## Gate decision

The research, test and evaluation architecture advances to `R2+`. It is internally reconciled and specific enough to guide facility planning, performer competitions and experiment design. It is not a validated cost estimate, statistical power analysis, safety case, environmental authorization, range design or acquisition baseline.

`FIN`, `HCG-100` and all constituent flood systems remain `R2+`. All twenty R3 gates remain open. No rendering is authorized.

The next first-principles gain should be a cross-hazard innovation doctrine that generalizes this evidence architecture to fire, power, water, medical, heat, communications, logistics and earth systems—or a second empirical campaign that applies the same experimental rigor to the fire force. Vehicle form should remain downstream of those findings.
