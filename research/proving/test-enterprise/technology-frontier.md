# The resilience technology frontier

## From equipment buyer to invention state

The central technological proposition of a Department of Resilience is not that civilian agencies should receive better versions of today's emergency equipment. It is that the United States should create a second strategic innovation system whose organizing purpose is to observe environmental danger, control its physical consequences, sustain populations through infrastructure failure and restore essential systems. Defense investment has repeatedly created capabilities for sensing, mobility, computation, medicine, energy and manufacturing that did not exist at the start of a program. Equal seriousness in resilience requires the same permission to change the technological frontier.

That permission must be disciplined. A speculative capability is not a fielded capability. A component demonstration is not a public service. A prototype that produces water, power or agent flow on a prepared range is not a formation that can deploy, establish itself, operate through uncertain conditions, sustain its workforce, protect bystanders, transfer authority and recover. The Department therefore needs two things at once:

1. an unusually ambitious invention portfolio, willing to pursue capabilities that may require new science, regulation, infrastructure and industrial processes; and
2. an unusually strict evidence regime that prevents ambition from becoming unearned readiness credit.

This pass designs that system across the whole Department. The accompanying [resilience technology frontier workbook](../../../models/resilience-technology-frontier.xlsx) contains three alternative 25-year capitalization cases, sixty technology campaigns, ten common technological primitives, an eight-stage maturity funnel, a ten-family National Resilience Proving Network, annual funding, institutional decision rights, industrial clusters, export pathways and cost/schedule sensitivities. It is a portfolio model, not an appropriation request, acquisition estimate or prediction of technical success.

> A mature Department of Resilience should be able to ask for an effect that present industry cannot deliver, fund several credible ways of creating it, build the facilities required to discover which ways work, terminate most of them without institutional crisis, and convert the survivors into reproducible public service.

## 1. Scale must be stated honestly

The phrase “military-like innovation” is easy to use loosely. The fiscal comparison is clarifying. The Department of Defense's FY2026 request includes **$179.1 billion for research, development, test and evaluation** and 1,135 RDT&E program, project and activity lines.[^dod-fy26] Holding that single annual request constant for twenty-five years yields roughly **$4.48 trillion** before any escalation. This does not prove what resilience research should cost, but it establishes the order of magnitude implied by a genuinely peer innovation effort.

The workbook therefore uses three alternatives:

| Case | 25-year envelope | Average per year | Meaning |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Catalytic | $750B | $30B | Minimum plausible sovereign discovery-to-deployment architecture |
| Strategic | $2.0T | $80B | Selected planning case for a broad national portfolio |
| Peer intensity | $4.5T | $180B | Approximately twenty-five years at the FY2026 defense RDT&E request |

These are **alternative classifications of the same Department-wide technology enterprise**. They are not additive to the project's initial $1 trillion capital constraint or to the separate $1 trillion fire and flood thought experiments. Those earlier models ask different questions. The original workbook asks how much force a constrained capital tranche might buy. The fire and flood allocations explore within-domain strategic balance. This workbook asks what scale and structure an independent national innovation enterprise would require.

The selected $2 trillion case is deliberately below peer intensity. It is large enough to reveal institutional and industrial consequences without claiming that one annual defense number can be mechanically copied into another mission. It averages $80 billion per year and peaks at approximately **$139.8 billion** in years 9 and 10. The first ten years consume about **$953.4 billion**, years 11–19 about **$869.9 billion**, and the final six years about **$176.7 billion** as the system shifts from capacity creation and rival prototypes toward qualification, renewal and retirement.

The funding profile matters because innovation cannot be surge-funded only after disaster. Laboratories, ranges, scientific teams, safety cases, regulatory competence, production tooling and specialist suppliers are stocks. They take years to create and decay when demand becomes episodic.

## 2. The required institution is an innovation state, not a grants program

Existing public research organizations provide valuable methods and evidence. NASA's Earth System Digital Twin work joins continuous observations, dynamic forecasting and impact assessment.[^nasa-esdt] NOAA's Earth Prediction Innovation Center organizes a community modeling system with shared code, data, support and a research-to-operations pathway; Project EAGLE adds rapid comparison of experimental AI forecasts against trusted operational metrics.[^noaa-epic][^noaa-eagle] NSF's Natural Hazards Engineering Research Infrastructure provides distributed experimental facilities, cyberinfrastructure, simulation and rapid post-event reconnaissance.[^nsf-nheri] NIST's digital-twin and robotics programs emphasize interoperability, measurement, verification, validation and uncertainty quantification.[^nist-dt][^nist-robotics]

Those precedents demonstrate important pieces. They do not add up to a sovereign organization that can originate a physical mission requirement, carry several technical paths through full-scale prototypes, destroy them in representative conditions, qualify a production system, maintain a fielded fleet and revise operational doctrine. The Department should absorb their lessons without preserving today's fragmented institutional boundaries as the future architecture.

The proposed innovation establishment has eight non-substitutable elements.

### 2.1 Advanced Resilience Projects Agency (`ARPA-R`)

`ARPA-R` frames measured opportunities rather than issuing broad topic grants. A program begins with a consequential operational variable, its present distribution, the intervention logic and a reason existing institutions or markets will not close the gap. Program directors fund rival mechanisms, set quantitative milestones, stop losing paths and can redirect resources before a traditional program office exists.

In the strategic case, `ARPA-R` receives **24 percent, or $480 billion**. This includes basic and applied science, competing prototypes and high-risk opportunity programs. It does not own final acceptance.

### 2.2 National Resilience Laboratories

A new laboratory system owns long-lived mission science: coupled hazard–infrastructure–population models; extreme-environment materials; hydraulics; combustion; separations; clinical and biological systems; power electronics; geotechnics; autonomy assurance; metrology; and public-effect measurement. The laboratories maintain shared instruments and reference models, reproduce critical mechanisms and publish methods by default.

Their **22 percent, or $440 billion**, is not a subsidy to preserve existing laboratories. Existing federal and university facilities may become partners, staff sources or transition sites. The sovereign requirement belongs to the Department.

### 2.3 National Resilience Proving Network

The proving network receives **16 percent, or $320 billion**, in the strategic case. It is not a collection of demonstration venues. It is the physical evidence infrastructure required to test large flows, combustion, smoke, waves, debris, structural failure, grid faults, contaminated feeds, clinical operations, cyber-physical degradation, manufacturing mobilization and complete community service under controlled but representative conditions.

### 2.4 Resilience Test and Evaluation Authority (`RTEA`)

`RTEA` receives a protected **10 percent, or $200 billion**. It owns test plans, score functions, range configurations, configuration lock, independent data custody, replication, readiness findings and operational evaluation. Its appropriation cannot be consumed by a sponsor's cost overrun, and a program office cannot waive its negative finding.

This separation is the core constitutional mechanism of the innovation system. `ARPA-R` is rewarded for finding new possibilities; `RTEA` is rewarded for discovering their limits.

### 2.5 Service Technology Commands

Service Technology Commands receive **12 percent, or $240 billion**, to integrate mature prototypes with formations, doctrine, training, maintainers, incident command, logistics and civil authorities. They conduct field experiments and create usable increments. They cannot self-certify a system or convert an experiment into force credit.

### 2.6 Resilience Manufacturing and Mobilization Administration

This administration receives **10 percent, or $200 billion**, to mature suppliers, manufacturing processes, tooling, metrology, repair, depot systems and surge plans. It maintains authoritative digital production baselines and conducts cold-start mobilization exercises in which dormant or alternate suppliers must reproduce qualified output without silent configuration drift.

### 2.7 National Resilience University Consortia

Four percent, or **$80 billion**, funds long-horizon scientific centers, doctoral training, shared instruments and disciplinary depth that should not be subordinated to five-year acquisition schedules. Open competition and peer review preserve intellectual diversity.

### 2.8 Standards, Data and Public Assurance Office

The remaining two percent, or **$40 billion**, supports open interfaces, configuration provenance, public-effect accounting, privacy, accessibility, environmental evidence and methods by which outside researchers and affected communities can challenge claims.

The allocation is an architecture test, not a recommendation that every dollar fit one box. It shows that a Department cannot spend nearly everything on promising prototypes and expect independent evidence, production readiness, common standards and field integration to materialize for free.

## 3. The unit of innovation is an operational coefficient

Terms such as artificial intelligence, autonomy, nuclear, quantum, advanced materials, digital twin and biotechnology do not define capability. A technology campaign earns a place only if it changes an operational distribution that matters to public service. The workbook organizes effects into eight families:

| Effect | Example measured variable | Force-design consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Sense | observation-to-trusted-state time and confidence | warning, staging and sensor posture |
| Decide | decision regret across plausible futures | command span, reserve and option value |
| Move | tonne-kilometers per day across a damaged network | mobility mix and theater-opening force |
| Control hazard | net growth, flow or energy after intervention | formation density and campaign duration |
| Sustain life | people-days at a defined service level | utility, medical, food and shelter footprint |
| Restore system | time to stable critical function | repair, bypass and replacement mix |
| Protect workforce | exposure-hours per unit of useful effect | crewing, rotation, robotics and protection |
| Learn | time from event to independently validated update | institutional adaptation rate |

The discipline is consequential. A 15 m³/s pump whose intake clogs, receiving route cannot be built, downstream water cannot be lawfully discharged or repair burden immobilizes the formation has not delivered 15 m³/s of public effect. A predictive model that looks convincing but worsens decisions in rare tail conditions has negative value. A robot that reduces responder exposure by moving risk to trapped residents has failed the mission.

Every claimed advance must therefore expose:

- the baseline distribution it changes;
- the complete causal chain from mechanism to public effect;
- energy, mass, time, workforce, maintenance and data burdens;
- the environmental and distributional consequences of use and non-use;
- failure and degraded modes;
- the range and operational thread that can disprove it; and
- the authority that may accept, limit, redirect or terminate it.

Until representative evidence exists, the program remains a research asset rather than a readiness asset.

## 4. Twelve mission-science directorates

The technology portfolio is organized around twelve directorates, each with five initial campaign areas in the workbook. The boundaries are management devices, not technical walls.

### 4.1 Earth intelligence and causal forecasting — 10 percent

The goal is not a visually impressive universal digital replica. It is a continuously updated, uncertainty-calibrated Earth and infrastructure state from which the Department can evaluate interventions. Research includes a dynamic Earth-state mesh; causal disaster twins; expendable sensor ecologies; impact-based ensemble forecasts; and a post-event learning loop that captures perishable evidence as an operation unfolds.

The far frontier is **closed-loop environmental command**: observe an evolving system, forecast the distribution of consequences under multiple actions, execute a bounded intervention, measure the response and update the model fast enough to alter the next action. NASA and NOAA provide credible lower-bound patterns, but incident-scale causal validation and command accountability remain unsolved at the proposed scope.[^nasa-esdt][^noaa-eagle]

### 4.2 Fire and atmospheric systems — 9 percent

The directorate pursues sub-hour ignition interception, coupled fire–smoke twins, clean-sheet high-cycle aircraft, robotic line and water systems, and precision suppression chemistry with known environmental fate. The frontier is not a larger tanker. It is an integrated air–ground–base–public-protection control system that can operate at night and in smoke, maintain positive closure against the fire, and show where agent use creates downstream harm.

### 4.3 Flood and hydraulic systems — 9 percent

Programs cover common-drive megaflow machinery, rapid drainage spines, adaptive barriers with interior drainage, active flowing-breach arrest and mixed air/surface/subsurface autonomy. Passes 13–15 show why the full hydraulic chain matters: nominal pump flow is easily defeated by head, intake, debris, route, receiving water, power or maintenance. The new directorate treats every one of those as a coupled research problem.

### 4.4 Water and drought systems — 9 percent

The Department should pursue a universal separations stack for seawater, brackish water, wastewater and contaminated sources; brine-to-resource processes; autonomous temporary water grids; managed recharge and watershed control; and mobile water–energy islands.

DOE already frames transformational desalination, resource recovery and small modular energy–water systems for disaster relief as research goals.[^doe-water] The Department's frontier goes further: a self-surveying regional water system able to establish intake, treatment, storage, quality assurance and distribution when the fixed network is partly usable, wholly unusable or dangerous.

Floating nuclear-powered desalination is admitted as a research campaign, not a presumed answer. It must defeat non-nuclear firm power, shore-based systems, distributed treatment and transported water on complete mission energy, time, mass, siting, licensing, security, heat rejection, brine, port dependence, crew, lifecycle and public-acceptance evidence. The same standard applies to inland waterway variants.

### 4.5 Power and thermal systems — 11 percent

The directorate develops self-forming emergency grids; mobile long-duration storage; transportable firm power; rapid district heating, cooling and clean-air utilities; and a universal power-electronic interface. DOE microgrid work already identifies adaptive controls, protection, self-healing and interconnected microgrids, while its long-duration storage programs treat ten hours or more as a distinct demonstration problem.[^doe-microgrid][^doe-ldes] ARPA-E's GRADIENTS program illustrates control co-design across flexible generation, automatic relays and wide-area control.[^arpae-gradients]

The Department-specific breakthrough is a **grid that arrives as a force**: it can inventory an unknown topology, isolate unsafe components, black-start critical loads, combine heterogeneous sources, operate manually if software fails, meter priority service, reject faults and transfer safely back to a local utility.

### 4.6 Health and biosecurity — 10 percent

Programs include a distributed hospital platform, local multi-product biomanufacturing, autonomous clinical logistics, indoor exposure control and privacy-preserving population-health twins. ARPA-H's PARADIGM, GIVE, BREATHE and UPGRADE programs are strong evidence that mobile advanced care, distributed RNA manufacturing with integrated quality control, building-level exposure interventions and high-fidelity cyber-physical hospital twins are active research frontiers.[^arpa-paradigm][^arpa-give][^arpa-breathe][^arpa-upgrade]

The Department must integrate these into a care system rather than a set of devices: diagnostics, imaging, surgery, intensive care, sterilization, oxygen, pharmacy, blood, cold chain, medical records, patient movement, staffing, waste, quality release and referral pathways. A deployable CT scanner without power, trained staff, maintenance, infection control or a receiving pathway is not hospital capability.

### 4.7 Structures, seismic and geotechnical systems — 8 percent

The frontier includes a national structural-state mesh; automated lifeline isolation; robotic stabilization and shoring; rapid ground-state control; and recoverable structures designed for inspection and repair rather than demolition.

USGS ShakeAlert demonstrates the sense-to-automated-action pattern: distributed sensors detect an earthquake already in progress, issue messages and allow systems such as trains and valves to take protective action.[^usgs-shakealert] NSF NHERI demonstrates the value of large shared shake, wind, wave, geotechnical and cyber-physical facilities.[^nsf-nheri] The Department would extend both patterns into a national system that moves from warning through isolation, inspection, entry, stabilization and restoration.

### 4.8 Mobility, access and logistics — 9 percent

This directorate develops autonomous route opening; hydrodynamically distinct wet-gap rescue and cargo systems; temporary heavy ports, bridges and pads; predictive sustainment; and rapidly tasked stratospheric or orbital relay. Its research question is total theater throughput under broken topology—not platform speed on an intact route.

### 4.9 Autonomy and human–machine systems — 7 percent

Programs include an extreme-environment autonomy kernel; human-commandable swarms; embodied rescue intelligence; machine-checkable incident command; and human-endurance systems. NIST's emergency and manufacturing robotics programs provide a method precedent: define repeatable tests for mobility, manipulation, sensing, communications, reliability, autonomy, logistics, safety and operator proficiency rather than accepting a generic autonomy label.[^nist-robotics]

The hardest problem is correlated failure. A hundred machines sharing the same model, map, communications stack or unsafe assumption can fail as one machine. Operationally meaningful autonomy must survive sensor obscuration, link loss, misleading terrain, mixed human traffic, cyber compromise, partial fleet damage and manual reversion.

### 4.10 Materials, manufacturing and repair — 8 percent

The directorate develops disaster-grade modular interfaces; field-qualified additive repair; self-sensing sacrificial materials; a surge-manufacturing digital thread; and circular response materials. The goal is a fleet that can be recomposed, repaired and scaled across decades rather than trapped in bespoke prototypes.

### 4.11 Communications, compute and cyber — 5 percent

Research covers self-healing public-safety communications, rugged sovereign edge compute, zero-trust machine operations, resilient positioning without GNSS and a public evidence ledger. The system must degrade gracefully across power, tower, backhaul, cloud, satellite and identity failure. Offline mission-essential operation is a design requirement, not a contingency patch.

### 4.12 Food and community stabilization — 5 percent

Programs cover rapid nutrition manufacturing, mobile protected agriculture, grid-independent cold chains, accessible shelter utility cores and community-service twins. USDA research on controlled-environment agriculture identifies water, nutrient, energy, mechanization, disease and stress as coupled research opportunities.[^usda-cea] The Department-specific objective is not permanent vertical farming everywhere. It is the ability to restore safe, culturally appropriate nutrition and perishable supply when transport, refrigeration, water and labor fail together.

## 5. Ten common primitives prevent twelve new stovepipes

The most valuable inventions will often be shared. The workbook specifies ten common technological primitives:

1. an observation fabric with time, location, calibration, provenance and uncertainty;
2. a causal digital-twin architecture with explicit model contracts and error;
3. an extreme-environment autonomy kernel with common authority and safe-state rules;
4. a mobile power, fluid and thermal bus;
5. advanced separations shared across water, air, waste and resource recovery;
6. rapid infrastructure fabrication with digital material and load qualification;
7. adaptive, self-sensing and sacrificial structures and materials;
8. distributed biomanufacturing with embedded quality control;
9. an industrial digital thread connecting design, supplier, tooling, test and depot state; and
10. resilient communications and edge compute.

Each primitive must prove portability across at least three hazard mission threads. This is a stronger condition than publishing an interface. A power bus that works for a hospital but fails around high-flow pumps, salt spray or fire bases is not common. An autonomy kernel that works on prepared roads but not in smoke, flood debris or unstable structures is not common. A digital twin that cannot expose uncertainty or accept independent components becomes a centralized source of correlated error.

Commonality is therefore earned empirically. The reward is enormous: shared training, spares, test methods, software assurance, depot competence, supplier depth and the ability to recombine formations without restarting systems engineering.

## 6. The National Resilience Proving Network

Today's research facilities establish that distributed national infrastructure can accelerate hazards engineering. The proposed network is a larger operational successor, not a replacement claim about any specific current institution. The strategic case scales to **$320 billion** across ten families:

- Earth-state and forecast tournaments using blinded archives, prospective events and decision scoring;
- large fire–atmosphere complexes with WUI mockups, smoke, night operations and mixed airspace;
- hydraulic and coastal complexes for full-curve pumps, debris, sediment, barriers, live breach, surf and discharge;
- water chemistry and separations ranges with unknown-feed and closed-mass-balance challenges;
- grid, power and thermal ranges able to black-start, island, fault, rejoin and endure for thirty days;
- health and bioresponse campuses for clinical simulation, containment and distributed quality release;
- built-environment and ground ranges for full-scale seismic, wind, wave, geotechnical and lifeline coupling;
- broken-network mobility corridors with debris, grades, water, unprepared terminals and denied communications;
- manufacturing mobilization ranges that activate alternate suppliers and detect configuration drift; and
- joint public-effect campuses that test accessible service, host burden, civil command and transfer.

The model includes **58 sites** and approximately **3,008 nominal experiment slots per year**. The busiest range family reaches about **75.9 percent** planned utilization, below an 80 percent planning ceiling. These numbers are capacity hypotheses, not facility designs. They expose a systems truth: if expensive prototypes must wait years for one uniquely configured range, test infrastructure becomes the hidden critical path and program offices gain incentives to accept weak surrogate evidence.

Pass 18 replaces this initial aggregate slot screen with the facility-scale [National Resilience Proving Network](national-proving-network.md): ten national flagships, twenty-one regional ranges, twenty-seven satellite/conformance nodes, family-specific campaign capacity, experimental-power screens, measurement systems, safety and environmental cases, mobile test assets, workforce, construction gates and cost sensitivity. Capacity remains comparable only within a facility family.

The network should operate four kinds of trial:

1. **mechanism trials**, isolating whether the claimed phenomenon exists;
2. **integration trials**, exposing interface and common-mode failures;
3. **representative destructive trials**, measuring tails, maintenance and safe degradation; and
4. **complete mission threads**, beginning with a cold alert and ending with public service, civil transfer and recovery.

Real disasters remain essential learning opportunities but are ethically unacceptable as first test venues. The Department needs instrumented artificial extremes so failure can be induced, repeated and understood before the system is placed around vulnerable people.

## 7. A maturity system designed to lose projects

The strategic model begins with **240 paid concepts**—four rival paths across each of sixty campaign areas—and narrows to:

- 180 reproducible TRL-3 mechanisms;
- 132 integrated TRL-4 breadboards;
- 96 TRL-5 relevant-environment prototypes;
- 64 TRL-6 representative system prototypes;
- 40 TRL-7 operational prototypes;
- 28 TRL-8 qualified increments; and
- 20 programs of record or durable open standards.

The exact attrition is illustrative. The doctrine is not. A portfolio with no visible failure is probably selecting safe projects, moving gates after results are known or concealing termination. At the same time, a stopped hardware path may produce an open model, material, test method, supplier or safety standard that remains valuable.

Eight gates separate opportunity from renewal. Each gate changes the question:

1. Is the operational variable consequential?
2. Does a reproducible mechanism exist?
3. Can the critical functions coexist?
4. Does the configuration work in the representative envelope?
5. Does it improve an end-to-end public service?
6. Can industry reproduce and sustain it?
7. Does replicated field use justify force credit?
8. Should it scale, change, open-license or retire?

The scoring function and tested configuration lock before the outcome is known. Sponsors may challenge methods before a trial and submit a response afterward, but they may not remove failed endpoints, substitute a friendlier scenario or redefine a requirement as an “objective” after observing performance.

## 8. Technical ambition requires civil constraints

The Department's purpose is to reduce harm in inhabited civil terrain. That changes the acceptable innovation regime.

### 8.1 Civil command remains external to technology authority

No laboratory, algorithm, service technology command or vendor obtains incident command because it invented a system. Tribal, territorial, state, local, federal and international authorities retain their lawful roles. Machine-checkable incident authority can make constraints executable, but it cannot create political legitimacy.

### 8.2 Public-effect evidence is part of technical evidence

Average output is insufficient. Tests must publish performance by geography, vulnerability, disability, language, infrastructure status and access condition where relevant. A system that raises aggregate throughput while systematically abandoning the hardest-to-reach population is not successful unless that trade is explicit, authorized and compensated by another formation.

### 8.3 Environmental burdens cannot be exported from the metric

Water discharge, brine, fire agents, smoke displacement, debris, waste, noise, wake, heat rejection and site restoration belong inside the mission boundary. “Emergency” does not make them vanish. The relevant question is whether total harm under intervention is lower than total harm under credible alternatives, including delay or non-use.

### 8.4 Nuclear and biological programs require sovereign safeguards

Transportable reactors, floating nuclear water–energy platforms and distributed biomanufacturing may create uniquely valuable capabilities. They also create licensing, security, proliferation, quality-release, waste, workforce and trust burdens that can dominate deployment. The research system must fund those burdens as core engineering work. It may discover that a promising mechanism loses on complete mission value; that is a successful research outcome.

### 8.5 Models advise; accountable humans decide

The Department should invest heavily in causal and AI-enabled models while assuming that rare extremes, distribution shift, adversarial conditions and incomplete data will persist. Forecast tournaments, out-of-distribution tests, counterfactual evaluation, explicit uncertainty and manual-safe operations are mandatory. Visual fidelity is not validation.

## 9. The resilience industrial economy

At strategic scale, this program would create first-tier firms whose core products are resilience systems: high-flow fluid machinery; response shipbuilding; clean-sheet response aviation; mobile power and thermal systems; water separations; heavy robotics; rapid structures; distributed health manufacturing; resilient sensing and compute; and food and shelter utilities.

This is different from waiting for defense, commercial aviation or construction markets to produce surplus platforms. The Department would provide stable family-level demand, shared interfaces, recurring destructive tests, readiness reserves, depot workloads and planned technology refresh. Firms could specialize in the severe duty cycles and public-effect constraints that ordinary markets underprice.

The manufacturing administration should require five mobilization artifacts from every mature family:

1. an authoritative, versioned design and process baseline;
2. qualified alternate materials, components and suppliers;
3. preserved or rapidly reproducible tooling and metrology;
4. workforce and depot certification pathways; and
5. demonstrated cold activation under disrupted supply.

The objective is not autarky. It is the ability to identify dependencies, carry deliberate reserves, substitute without hidden performance loss and expand output during a long domestic or international campaign.

## 10. Export is partner capacity, not equipment dependence

A mature resilience industry could become a major export sector because water, power, heat, flood, fire, health, communications and food continuity are universal needs. The most valuable export unit will often be a complete capability package: equipment, open interfaces, training, exercises, maintenance, spares, data governance, finance and certification.

The strategic test is whether the partner can command, maintain and adapt the capability. Exports should increase partner sovereignty rather than require permanent foreign operation. Public-good layers—hazard data, test methods, safety standards, curricula and environmental evidence—can remain open while firms compete on systems, integration, lifecycle support and locally licensed production.

This produces a form of power projection distinct from military deterrence. A country that can help restore safe water, electricity, health service, access and communications at strategic scale creates trust, interoperability and enduring professional networks. The effect is strongest when assistance arrives without coercive command and leaves behind greater local competence.

## 11. Portfolio failure is financially material

The workbook applies transparent shocks to the selected $2 trillion case. A 25 percent proving-facility overrun adds $80 billion. A 20 percent redesign shock across `ARPA-R`, laboratories and Service Technology Commands adds $224 billion. A 20 percent independent integration shock adds $40 billion. A two-year delay at a 3 percent annual carry rate adds $120 billion. A 15 percent manufacturing supply-chain shock adds $30 billion.

The compound case reaches **$2.494 trillion**, or $494 billion over the base. The 15 percent program reserve is $300 billion and is therefore **$194 billion short**. A severe compound case reaches approximately **$3.002 trillion**, $702 billion beyond base plus reserve.

These are not forecasts. They show why the architecture needs genuine cancellation, resequencing and scope authority. If every directorate can protect every program, the reserve becomes a bookkeeping fiction and independent test becomes the first funding source raided. A mature innovation state expects uncertainty, makes it visible and chooses among failures rather than pretending they will not occur.

## 12. What this changes about force design

This pass changes the larger project in six ways.

First, the Department of Resilience is now explicitly an **inventing institution**, not only an operator and buyer. Its laboratories, ranges, technology commands and manufacturing administration are part of force generation.

Second, the technological horizon is no longer limited to plausible combinations of existing products. Closed-loop environmental command, self-forming utilities, autonomous temporary water grids, flowing-breach arrest, distributed hospital and biomanufacturing systems, extreme-environment swarms, recoverable structures and surge manufacturing are legitimate research objects.

Third, common technological primitives become Department-level programs. This creates a shared architecture beneath hazard services while allowing environment-specific vehicles and tools above it.

Fourth, research is tied to public-service coefficients. The project can be ambitious without inserting fictional pump curves, aircraft yields, autonomous productivity or medical throughput into force models.

Fifth, industrial policy is integrated from the start. Manufacturing readiness, supplier depth, repair, depots, configuration control and export are not post-prototype commercialization questions.

Sixth, the fiscal meaning of “peer” is clearer. A true RDT&E peer to the defense establishment is plausibly a multi-trillion-dollar, multi-decade enterprise. The $2 trillion case is strategic but not fully peer intensity under the FY2026 comparison.

## 13. Research and rendering gates

This portfolio does not authorize any vehicle exterior. It identifies the research state required before geometry becomes defensible. Renderings should wait until a mission family has at least:

- a bounded effect and operating distribution;
- a complete energy, mass, workforce and sustainment chain;
- explicit interfaces with the common technological primitives;
- one or more competing technical configurations;
- a named representative test environment and failure criteria;
- an initial manufacturing and repair concept; and
- a clear statement of what remains speculative.

The next highest-value pass is therefore not image generation. It is either a Department-wide **National Resilience Proving Network design**, resolving facilities, throughput, safety and experimental power across all ten range families, or a **common technology architecture** that specifies the observation, authority, power/fluid/thermal, data, autonomy and manufacturing interfaces shared by future systems. Only then should platform renderings begin to communicate configurations rather than decorate assumptions.
