# The National Resilience Proving Network

## The infrastructure that converts ambition into knowledge

A Department of Resilience with permission to invent beyond current technology immediately encounters a harder question: where can the country discover whether the invention works?

A 250 m³/s hydraulic system cannot be validated in a parking lot. A 300 MW mobile grid bridge cannot earn a safety case through software simulation alone. A new firefighting aircraft is not proven by dropping water on a marked rectangle. A deployable hospital is not tested by counting erected beds. A distributed biomanufacturing system is not ready because one batch passed in its home laboratory. An autonomous earthwork fleet is not safe because individual machines navigated an empty course. A national disaster model is not trustworthy because it reproduces the events on which it was tuned.

The United States therefore needs a second kind of strategic infrastructure alongside the operational force: a sovereign system for creating controlled extremes, measuring them, breaking complete systems, preserving evidence and repeating the result across regions, suppliers and crews. This chapter defines that system as the **National Resilience Proving Network** (`NRPN`).

The accompanying [National Resilience Proving Network workbook](../../../models/proving/test-enterprise/national-resilience-proving-network.xlsx) converts the $320 billion strategic-case allocation established in Pass 16 into ten facility families, fifty-eight requirement sites, twenty initial campaigns, utility and measurement envelopes, experimental-power screens, safety and environmental cases, mobile test assets, workforce, construction gates, annual funding and cost sensitivities. It is not a site-selection decision, construction estimate, environmental review, safety authorization or statistical analysis plan.

The network is intentionally larger than an upgraded collection of current laboratories. Existing facilities supply methods and comparative scale. They do not constrain the new mission. The National Science Foundation's Natural Hazards Engineering Research Infrastructure demonstrates the value of a distributed network joining experimental facilities, cyberinfrastructure, simulation, data and post-event reconnaissance.[^nheri] NIST's National Fire Research Laboratory demonstrates controlled full-scale fire measurement and emissions treatment up to 20 MW.[^nfrl] The U.S. Army Engineer Research and Development Center operates major coastal and hydraulic infrastructure, including a full-scale breach facility designed around roughly 2,000 cubic feet per second.[^erdc-chl][^erdc-breach] NREL's ARIES platform joins hardware and real-time emulation at scales up to approximately 20 MW.[^aries] These are important national assets. The `NRPN` proposal asks what comes after them if resilience research receives strategic-force scale.

> The Department should not ask a program to prove a national-scale claim in an environment the nation has never built.

## 1. Why the proving network is a force-design requirement

Research facilities are often treated as support infrastructure: useful if funding allows, separable from the acquisition program and reusable when convenient. That treatment would cripple the Department's ambition.

The ability to create evidence determines which systems can be built. If a pump manufacturer cannot test full curves with debris, cavitation, transients and receiving-stage reversal, those conditions migrate into assumptions. If only one grid laboratory can accept a representative power interface, its queue becomes the program schedule. If no range can accommodate mixed autonomous aircraft, work machines, responders and public actors, integration becomes a late operational discovery. If destructive structures testing is too scarce, safety factors and models inherit uncertainty that could have been measured. If no closed environmental system can accept contaminated floodwater at relevant flow, treatment performance is extrapolated from chemically tidy feeds.

The proving network affects competition as well. A dominant prime can construct a private test environment around its proprietary stack. A smaller firm cannot. Public reference ranges make performance contestable. They allow two suppliers to confront the same load, fault, environment, measurement and acceptance rule. They allow government to retain evidence after a performer fails. They allow an international partner or local utility to reproduce a boundary rather than trust a sales claim.

The network also creates scientific memory. Disaster events are sparse, nonstationary and ethically impossible to reproduce in the field. Perishable observations from real events must be joined to controlled experiments, causal models and prospective trials. A national evidence system preserves the configuration, measurements, anomalies, failed hypotheses and public effects that allow the next design to improve.

Four objects must remain distinct:

1. **laboratory experiment:** isolates a mechanism and characterizes a distribution;
2. **proving-range test:** places a configuration into representative physical and operational stress;
3. **formation evaluation:** measures the complete mission thread, sustainment, authority and public effect; and
4. **exercise:** trains people and organizations, potentially generating evaluative evidence if independently controlled.

One cannot substitute for another. A successful exercise may conceal weak engineering behind scripted workarounds. A precise laboratory result may have no demonstrated formation value. A full-scale test may reveal a mechanism but have too few repetitions to support a rare-failure claim.

## 2. A federated 58-site architecture

The workbook retains the Pass 16 total of fifty-eight sites but replaces the earlier slot screen with a three-tier physical architecture:

| Tier | Sites | Share of $320B | Function |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| National flagships | 10 | 45%, or $144B | Unique extremes, reference measurement and highest-consequence experiments |
| Regional ranges | 21 | 35%, or $112B | Climate, geography, infrastructure and crew replication |
| Satellite/conformance nodes | 27 | 20%, or $64B | High-repetition boundary tests, event response, supplier access and local training |
| **Total** | **58** | **$320B** | **One governed evidence network** |

The tiers do not imply that all intellectual authority lives at flagships. Unique heavy infrastructure does. Statistical repetition, independent challenge and regional external validity require distribution.

Every one of ten facility families receives one national flagship. Regional and node counts vary with environmental diversity, turn time and repetition demand. Fire and hydraulics each receive seven sites. Water, grid, health, built environment, mobility and manufacturing receive six each. Earth intelligence receives four because much of its physical observation can federate through other ranges. Joint mission and public-effect evaluation receives four large sites because its campaigns are long and expensive.

The architecture does not select cities or parcels. The site register names geographic roles—Atlantic/Gulf, Pacific, Interior/Great Lakes, Arctic/Alaska, Caribbean/Territorial and Mountain/Drylands—to force climatic and infrastructure diversity into later siting. A serious siting analysis must consider hazard covariance, water and energy supply, controlled airspace and waterways, geology, transportation, surrounding population, tribal and territorial authority, environmental justice, workforce, university and industry access, and restoration liability.

The Department should not simply co-locate every flagship at existing federal campuses. Some existing sites will be superior choices; others will carry incompatible missions, physical limits, community burdens or correlated hazards. The future network is a national optimization and consent problem, not a real-estate inheritance exercise.

## 3. Ten national flagships

### 3.1 National Earth State and Decision Range

The Earth intelligence flagship tests the causal chain from observation to public decision. Its working envelope is 100 petabytes per day of ingest and 10,000 concurrent intervention branches across coupled hazards, infrastructure and populations. Those numbers are requirements for architecture studies, not procurement specifications.

The range contains independent observation stores, model execution environments, real-time incident emulators, protected infrastructure data, public-effect records and adversarial teams. It can replay historical events blind, withhold sensors, corrupt timestamps, shift distributions and introduce competing representations of population and infrastructure. It then moves into prospective seasonal and live-event evaluation.

The primary result is not forecast accuracy in isolation. It is **decision regret**: how outcomes differ because a civil authority acted on one model rather than another, including tails and population subgroups. The facility must reveal whether a more accurate average forecast worsens rare evacuation, grid allocation or water-release decisions.

The flagship also owns federation methods. Regional sites may become isolated, create different valid state histories and later reconnect. The test is whether time, authority, uncertainty and dissent survive reconciliation without a silent “last write wins” merge.

### 3.2 National Fire–Atmosphere Range

The fire flagship has two coupled environments.

The controlled environment explores a **250 MW heat-release envelope**, 12.5 times the sustained measurement capacity of NIST's current 20 MW reference. It requires staged calorimetry, independent heat-release methods, high-flow exhaust, emissions treatment, structural loading, ember and agent measurement, contaminated runoff control and sacrificial test articles. The purpose is not to build the world's largest burn for spectacle. It is to close combustion and emissions mass balances at scales relevant to large structures, energy systems and concentrated fuel beds.

The outdoor environment is an approximately 100 km² instrumented mission range with controlled airspace, terrain, vegetation treatments, night operation, water and agent infrastructure, robotic ground systems and public-interface simulation. It tests the full ignition-to-hold thread: detection, decision, dispatch, base cycle, airspace separation, placement, ground control, line hold, structure protection, smoke and responder exposure.

Ecology and air quality bound the design. Some outdoor evidence should come from prescribed fire with genuine ecological benefit. Other conditions require noncombustion plume, ember or heat simulants. The range earns legitimacy by measuring and minimizing total research harm, not by invoking the emergency mission.

### 3.3 National Megaflow and Coastal Range

The hydraulic flagship is a closed, reconfigurable water and terrain system with a proposed 5 million m³ inventory and a 250 m³/s flow envelope at 12 m of head. At 75% drive efficiency, the first-order hydraulic power requirement is approximately **39.24 MW**. The current ERDC breach reference demonstrates around 2,000 cfs, approximately 56.6 m³/s, through a forty-foot breach, along with the value of source, test and catch basins and controlled refill.[^erdc-breach] The proposed instantaneous flow is about 4.4 times that reference and joins multiple routes, receiving conditions and recovery systems.

The facility includes full pump curves, intake basins, debris and sediment injection, large-diameter temporary conveyance, variable terrain, breach cells, barriers, interior drainage, wave/surge generation, scour and receiving-stage control. Redundant gates and independent dump paths must place the water into a known safe state after power, control or structural failure.

The defining measurement is not nominal flow. It is the time-varying mass balance from source through intake, machinery, route, basin and receiving system, including leakage, storage, sediment, energy, maintenance and recovery. A megaflow machine that moves 250 m³/s for minutes but requires days to restore its route may have less campaign value than a smaller, repeatable system.

### 3.4 National Water and Residuals Range

The water flagship explores a closed-loop process envelope of 100,000 m³/day across seawater, brackish water, wastewater, floodwater and mixed contamination. It combines configurable intake, pretreatment, separations, polishing, storage, distribution, wastewater and residual systems.

EPA emergency-response research makes a crucial distinction: reducing the parent contaminant does not demonstrate safe product because transformation products may remain toxic, and mobile treatment must be evaluated with transport, deployment, power, operation and waste.[^epa-water] The proving range institutionalizes that complete boundary.

An independent quality authority owns challenge materials, samples, assays and product release. Operators do not know all seeded contaminants. Orthogonal measurement detects breakthrough. Every treatment train closes a mass balance on concentrate, media, sludge, wash water, air emission and final disposition. The facility can test an autonomous temporary water grid from unknown source through household, shelter or hospital interface rather than treating the plant skid as the whole service.

The Idaho National Laboratory Water Security Test Bed demonstrates a current method for chemical, biological and radiological detection and decontamination research.[^wstb] The new flagship expands the physical flow, source diversity, residual treatment and formation context without requiring high-consequence agents where validated surrogates can answer the mechanism.

### 3.5 National Grid and Thermal Range

The grid flagship has a 300 MW bidirectional exchange envelope, a 115 kV research boundary and approximately 1 GWh of combined electrical and thermal storage or controllable load. This is fifteen times the 20 MW physical scale reported for NREL's larger ARIES environment, while retaining hardware- and controller-in-the-loop emulation for systems too large or dangerous to reproduce physically.[^aries]

The range contains reconfigurable AC and DC buses, generation and load emulators, real rotating and inverter-based sources, storage, mobile substations, protection systems, underground and overhead temporary feeders, district heating/cooling loops, clean-air loads and a physically separate cyber range. It can black-start from a dead campus, connect modules from different suppliers, inject relay and grounding errors, impose motor starts and nonlinear loads, island, rejoin and transfer authority to a utility.

Not all 300 MW must be generated and dissipated as waste. The conceptual design must determine which phenomena require representative physical fault energy, which require power-hardware-in-the-loop, and which can use validated digital emulation. Energy sinks should perform useful research or serve the campus where possible. Safety requires fault-limited zones, independent protection, visible isolation, arc/blast containment and a utility agreement that prevents uncontrolled export.

The thermal range treats cooling, heating and clean air as utilities rather than building accessories. It can test a temporary district system against a 100,000-person-equivalent load under heat, cold, smoke and grid scarcity, recording protected people-hours, fuel, water, refrigerant, emissions and subgroup service.

### 3.6 National Deployable Health Systems Range

The health flagship is a configurable clinical system, not a conventional hospital with a test annex. It emulates 1,000 beds across triage, emergency care, surgery, critical care, isolation, inpatient, chronic treatment, pharmacy, laboratory, imaging, blood, oxygen, sterilization, records, food, sanitation, patient movement, referral and discharge.

Most trials use trained participants, high-fidelity simulators and controlled data. Real clinical studies require separate ethical and regulatory protocols. The facility measures complete appropriate patient journeys, not nominal beds or procedures. It can remove grid power, oxygen, staff, network identity, transport, a referral hospital or a critical supplier and observe which queues and harms move elsewhere.

Distributed biomanufacturing occupies physically and institutionally separate suites. BSL-2 and BSL-3 work follows facility, equipment and practice controls appropriate to the agents and procedures. An isolated BSL-4 reference capability may be required for specific national questions, but it is not a default feature of every node. The NIH/CDC biosafety manual makes clear that containment levels integrate facility design, safety equipment and practices; higher containment also creates specialized ventilation and waste requirements.[^bmbl] The Department must justify which evidence requires live agents rather than surrogates or partner facilities.

Independent clinical, biosafety, ethics and batch-release authorities retain stop powers. A rapid product switch is a failure if decontamination, assay, cold chain, prescribing or pharmacovigilance does not close.

### 3.7 National Structures and Ground Range

The structures flagship joins seismic, wind, water, geotechnical, fire and rapid-repair research at infrastructure scale. The working requirement is a 50 m × 50 m multi-axis reaction platform, up to 10,000 tonnes of representative payload and 100 MN of hybrid actuation distributed through structures and foundations.

The current NHERI network provides an important method base in shake tables, wind, wave, centrifuge, hybrid simulation and mobile field shakers.[^nheri] UC San Diego's outdoor shake-table reference, for example, reports a 12.2 m × 7.6 m platen and 2,000-tonne vertical payload capacity.[^ucsd-shake] The proposed footprint is roughly twenty-seven times the area and five times the payload. That scale jump is a research question in its own right: reaction mass, energy storage, actuator control, soil–structure interaction, specimen construction, debris, cost and test cadence may favor a federation of coupled physical and simulated components over one monolithic table.

The range tests bridges, utility supports, modular buildings, field hospitals, barriers, shoring, ports, towers, pipelines and recoverable structures. It proceeds from damage through sensing, isolation, entry, stabilization, repair, proof and recertification. Seeded hidden defects test whether structural-health systems can distinguish damage from sensor failure. Materials and components are recovered and reused when scientifically valid, because destructive throughput otherwise creates its own large industrial waste stream.

### 3.8 National Broken-Network Mobility Range

The mobility flagship is a fifty-kilometer instrumented damaged corridor joined to a flooded urban district, short and impaired airfield, damaged port, river/coastal zone and austere logistics base. It can vary debris, bridge state, surface bearing, visibility, positioning, communications, fuel, electric charging, navigation aids and public traffic.

The range tests theater opening rather than isolated vehicles. Air, land, water and robotic systems must survey, choose routes, clear obstructions, establish temporary crossings, recover disabled equipment, move patients and cargo, maintain separation and sustain throughput. Range marshals carry an independent stop and truth system.

NIST's emergency-response robot program supplies a powerful existing method: repeatable elemental tests for mobility, manipulation, sensing, communications, endurance, reliability, autonomy, logistics, safety and operator proficiency are combined into mission profiles.[^nist-robots] The Department extends that logic to mixed fleets and complete access formations. Empty-course speed never substitutes for useful tonne-kilometers or people moved under public-interface constraints.

### 3.9 National Mobilization Factory Range

The manufacturing flagship contains ten reconfigurable pilot lines and the utilities, material controls, metrology, secure digital thread, inspection, repair and depot systems needed to reproduce representative resilience products. It is not a state-owned factory optimized for routine output. It is a place where a production claim can fail safely.

Programs run thirty-day cold-start campaigns with the incumbent supplier absent. Alternate firms receive the authorized technical package, interfaces, approved sources and substitution rules. The test measures days to qualified output, yield, process drift, defect escape, repairability and the ability to scale. Some lots are destructively examined; others move to proving ranges under controlled release.

The facility also tests mobilization under scarce materials, compromised software baselines, counterfeit parts, workforce turnover and regional utility failure. Competing process routes are preserved where one national process would create a common mode. A “digital factory” earns value only if another plant can produce an item whose configuration and performance survive independent test.

### 3.10 National Synthetic Municipality

The joint flagship is a controllable municipality-scale infrastructure system representing approximately 100,000 people without involuntarily exposing a real population to research hazards. It includes power, water, wastewater, communications, roads, logistics, shelters, health facilities, public buildings, distribution points, damaged structures and civil command spaces. Loads may be physical, emulated or hybrid, but their fidelity and limits are explicit.

This is where the Department conducts ninety-day campaigns and federates three simultaneous theaters. The range introduces workforce turnover, supplier loss, communications partitions, extreme heat or cold, smoke, flooding, clinical demand, public information failures and authority transitions. Independent evaluators measure population-service-time, mortality and morbidity proxies, distributional service, displacement, workforce exposure, environmental burden, sustainment and transfer.

FEMA's Homeland Security Exercise and Evaluation Program provides a current doctrine for objectives, conduct, evaluation, after-action reporting and corrective actions.[^hseep] The `NRPN` adds engineering configuration control, hidden fault injection, raw evidence custody and acceptance gates. The objective is not a compelling exercise narrative. It is a reproducible causal record of where the formation worked, failed and improvised.

## 4. Capacity is multidimensional

The workbook calculates family-specific equivalent campaigns from test cells, turns per cell and availability. The highest family utilization is approximately **74.3%**, below the 80% planning ceiling. That result is only a first screen.

Equivalent campaigns cannot be summed across unlike facilities. A five-day water challenge, a four-hour fault test and a ninety-day joint campaign are not interchangeable slots. Each detailed design must schedule setup, conditioning, specimen construction, calibration, test, safe teardown, environmental clearance, repair and data release. Long-duration tests may occupy a cell even when active measurement occurs briefly. Hazardous tests may create exclusion conflicts with neighboring cells.

Within each family, the planned demand reserves 20% of used slots for independent `RTEA` replication and adversarial work, 15% for event-response and perishable evidence, and 10% for open competition by smaller firms, universities and partners. These shares are not overhead. Without them, sponsor programs consume every hour, independent replication becomes optional and the public range recreates incumbent control.

Capacity also includes **turn competence**: technicians, safety reviewers, calibrated instruments, spare test articles, waste pathways, data engineers and approval authorities. Adding a concrete pad does not double a range if the same qualified team must prepare both cells.

## 5. Experimental power before experimental scale

Large facilities create a temptation to treat every test as decisive. The workbook includes three simple statistical screens to resist it.

For a binary failure process with zero observed failures, the number of independent trials required for a one-sided 95% upper bound is:

\[
n \geq \frac{\ln(1-0.95)}{\ln(1-p)}.
\]

The screen yields 29 failure-free trials for a 10% upper probability, 59 for 5%, 299 for 1%, 598 for 0.5% and 2,995 for 0.1%. These are not universal certification counts. They show the order of magnitude and assume independent, identically distributed trials. Shared software, suppliers, operators, weather or measurement can destroy that assumption.

For a two-group comparison with standardized effect `d`, two-sided alpha of 0.05 and 80% power, the simple normal approximation is:

\[
n_{arm} \approx \frac{2(z_{\alpha/2}+z_{\beta})^2}{d^2}.
\]

The result is approximately 16 per arm for an effect of 1.0, 25 for 0.8, 63 for 0.5, 175 for 0.3 and 392 for 0.2. A formation experiment with four repetitions cannot reliably distinguish modest improvements simply because each repetition is expensive.

A zero-failure exposure screen similarly requires about three times the target mean time between failures at 95% confidence under a constant exponential hazard. A 100,000-hour reliability target implies roughly 299,573 failure-free article-hours. One heroic prototype cannot supply that evidence within a useful calendar.

The solution is an evidence ladder:

- `L0` analytical and synthetic work explores millions of conditions and designs the experiment;
- `L1` elemental tests achieve hundreds or thousands of repetitions on sensors, materials, connections and algorithms;
- `L2` subsystem tests inject interfaces and faults tens or hundreds of times;
- `L3` full-system tests close mass, energy, flow, crew, repair and safety in representative environments;
- `L4` formation campaigns join systems, sustainment and civil command; and
- `L5` operational replication tests external validity across real regions and events.

Evidence combines only through a preregistered causal model that states which mechanisms and dependencies transfer between levels. Simulation does not manufacture independent trials. Component repetition does not prove formation performance. Full-scale success does not prove a rare failure rate. NIST's engineering-statistics program provides the broader methodological base for experimental design, measurement, process characterization and reliability.[^nist-stats]

## 6. A national measurement system

The proving network is fundamentally a measurement institution. Its common metrology spans time, geospatial state, hydraulics, electricity, fire and atmosphere, chemistry and toxicity, structures and ground, human exposure, autonomy, clinical/public effect and manufacturing.

Every important claim has:

- a defined measurand and unit;
- traceability or a declared reference;
- dynamic range and sampling rate;
- at least one independent measurement channel where consequence warrants;
- calibration before, during and after destructive stress;
- a visible uncertainty budget;
- raw and processed data linked to the tested configuration;
- an independent custodian; and
- a seeded failure that can show whether the measurement system detects invalid evidence.

Some domains require distinct physics. Fire heat release should not depend on one sensor type; NIST's reference uses both oxygen-consumption and fuel-consumption approaches and documents their agreement and uncertainty.[^nist-calorimetry] Hydraulic flow can close against basin inventories and redundant meters. Structural state can pair embedded sensors with independent nondestructive evaluation. Water quality can pair continuous instruments with split laboratory samples and toxicity. Autonomy can pair onboard logs with range truth and human observers.

The data system preserves negative and ambiguous results. A program office may publish its interpretation, but it does not own the raw evidence or delete a failed run from the denominator. Corrections append with reason and signer. Models, calibration, test article, crew, environmental state and authority are versioned together.

## 7. Safety, environment and public legitimacy

The range is permitted to destroy test articles. It is not permitted to lose control of the experiment.

Each flagship begins with a maximum credible event: a 5-million-m³ uncontrolled release; a 250 MW fire that exceeds exhaust or air boundaries; a 300 MW fault or unstable utility export; a structural reaction failure; a containment breach; an autonomous fleet leaving the domain; or an exercise action harming participants or neighbors. The safety case then defines prevention, detection, independent stop, local containment, public exclusion, emergency response, restart and approval.

The independent stop must be physically and institutionally separate from the tested control. A software safety command routed through the system under test is not independent. At high stored energy, the stop may not halt the immediate event; it must place remaining energy and connected cells into the least harmful bounded state.

Environmental accounting begins before operation. Every campaign tracks material, water, energy, emission and waste inputs; air, water and ground pathways; ecological and community exposure; monitoring duration; restoration reserve; stop threshold and release authority. Fire research accounts for smoke, ash, agent and contaminated runoff. Hydraulic research closes the water and sediment balance. Water treatment retains product, transformation products and residuals. Grid research plans for batteries, fuel, refrigerants, transformer materials, firewater and soil. Structural ranges plan concrete, steel, soil and composite recovery. Joint exercises plan wastewater, debris, consumables and participant welfare.

“Emergency” is not an environmental exemption for research. The proving network exists precisely so the operational force can reduce harm. Its own burdens must be lower than the knowledge and public benefit they create, and affected communities require standing access to monitoring and corrective-action records.

## 8. Mobile evidence infrastructure

Not every important event can be brought to a flagship. The workbook allocates **$32 billion** to 448 mobile or reconfigurable assets:

- 120 traceable metrology trailers;
- 24 grid fault and protection sets;
- 30 fluid transient and custody rigs;
- 20 contaminant challenge trains;
- 40 autonomy range-marshal sets;
- 60 independent time and position references;
- 12 fire and atmospheric instrument trains;
- 20 clinical simulation modules;
- 12 structural hybrid-actuation sets;
- 50 secure compute and evidence-custody pods;
- 20 environmental containment trains; and
- 40 rapid event-observation packages.

The assets allow regional ranges to reproduce a flagship reference, suppliers to test before occupying a unique facility, and `RTEA` to arrive after a real event while physical evidence is still available. They also reduce geographic concentration. A national measurement standard that exists only at one campus is not a deployable national capability.

Mobile systems receive the same configuration and calibration controls as fixed facilities. They cannot become untraceable “test gear” circulating among programs. Custodians know where each item is, its reference state, environmental exposure, maintenance, software, adapters and permitted test envelope.

## 9. Workforce as national scientific readiness

The model estimates **36,700 resident full-time positions** across the network at maturity, plus approximately **41,700 visiting or rotating people per year**. These are workforce hypotheses, not a staffing authorization. They exclude most construction labor, vendor field staff and the operational units undergoing evaluation.

The resident mix is approximately 30% scientists and engineers, 30% technicians and operators, 15% safety/environment/quality, 10% data/statistics/evaluation and 15% facilities/logistics/administration. The percentages conceal crucial specialization: causal inference, survey and time metrology, calorimetry, industrial hygiene, hydraulic machinery, divers, protection engineering, biosafety, clinical operations, assay release, geotechnics, nondestructive evaluation, flight and marine test, range safety, manufacturing process control, exercise evaluation and public-effect measurement.

The Department needs a professional **test force**. Personnel should hold career paths that move among laboratories, ranges, field formations, depots, industry and regulatory bodies without collapsing the independence of acceptance. Range technicians and operators are not secondary support jobs. Their tacit knowledge determines whether a once-a-year extreme test produces usable evidence or a damaged article and missing data.

Workforce pipelines begin with facility design. Universities need funded faculty, instruments and graduate cohorts years before commissioning. Apprenticeships and technical colleges need access to actual control, instrumentation, welding, electrical, process, rigging and maintenance systems. Clinical, biosafety, aviation, marine and utility qualifications cannot be generated in a final construction year.

## 10. A $320 billion, twenty-five-year enterprise

The selected allocation is a cross-classification inside the $2 trillion strategic technology case, not an additional program. The workbook divides it by cost object:

| Cost object | 25-year allocation |
|---|---:|
| Land, environmental work and utility backbone | $38.4B |
| Major facility construction | $112.0B |
| Instrumentation, metrology and data | $38.4B |
| Mobile and reconfigurable test assets | $32.0B |
| Commissioning and certification | $25.6B |
| Twenty-five-year operations and trials | $41.6B |
| Renewal, decommissioning and restoration | $16.0B |
| Independent contingency | $16.0B |
| **Total** | **$320.0B** |

The portfolio also allocates by family: $22B Earth intelligence; $38B fire; $42B hydraulics; $28B water/environment; $36B grid/thermal; $34B health/bioresponse; $36B built environment/ground; $32B mobility/autonomy; $24B manufacturing/mobilization; and $28B joint mission/public effect.

Neither classification is a bottom-up estimate. The model asks whether a complete program structure can reconcile to the previously selected envelope. It exposes the next cost-estimating work: land and utility quantities, reference designs, construction schedules, equipment lists, escalation, lifecycle operations, environmental liability and independent risk.

The funding profile places 55%, or $176B, in years 1–10; another 26%, or $83.2B, in years 11–15; 14.5%, or $46.4B, in years 16–20; and 4.5%, or $14.4B, in years 21–25. Annual funding peaks at 7%, or $22.4B, in years 6–10. This front-loads design, early works, heavy construction, instrumentation and commissioning while retaining money for trials, renewal and restoration.

DOE's capital-asset framework is a useful governance analogue because it joins performance baselines, cost and schedule, independent review, safety, security and environmental requirements.[^doe-project] The Department should adapt that discipline but prevent facility programs from marking themselves successful at construction completion. A range exists to create repeatable evidence; facility acceptance includes seeded-fault detection, operator qualification, turn time, measurement uncertainty and environmental closure.

The sensitivity model deliberately excludes the $16B contingency from base need. Base calculated need is $304B. A compound screen—25% land/facility shock, 20% instrumentation, 15% mobile assets, 20% commissioning, 15% operations and 10% renewal—raises need to **$367.04B**, producing a **$47.04B shortfall** against the selected envelope. A severe compound screen reaches **$428.96B**, a **$108.96B shortfall**. These are arithmetic stresses, not probabilistic forecasts. They demonstrate why independent cost, schedule and cancellation authority matter.

## 11. Construction and commissioning law

The network advances through ten facility gates:

1. `F0` validates the evidence gap and alternatives.
2. `F1` compares site, ownership and federation options.
3. `F2` closes concept mass, energy, flow, reaction, waste and data balances.
4. `F3` establishes preliminary design, maximum credible events and independent review.
5. `F4` sets the configuration-controlled construction and authorization baseline.
6. `F5` qualifies packages, suppliers, hold points and operating cadres.
7. `F6` commissions dry, wet, energized, faulted and degraded states.
8. `F7` begins bounded operations with surveillance and cross-site repetition.
9. `F8` admits the facility to full network campaigns.
10. `F9` renews, adapts or restores and retires the site.

Program urgency cannot waive a gate by renaming an experiment a demonstration. Before hazardous operation, all ten flagship safety cases must close. In the current model, **zero are closed**. This is not a workbook defect. It is the correct state for concept-level facilities.

Commissioning uses a rising-consequence sequence. Instrument and control systems operate against benign references. Dry mechanical and software tests precede water, heat, energy, biological or structural hazard. Protective devices are challenged before the primary system controls them. Seeded failures verify detection. Operators perform connected, intermittent, isolated and manual-safe operation. Emergency teams practice containment and recovery. The facility's permitted envelope expands only after independent evidence.

## 12. Governance and access

The `NRPN` belongs to the Department but cannot belong operationally to its program sponsors.

The **Resilience Test and Evaluation Authority** owns range acceptance, test control, independent safety stop, raw evidence, anomaly classification and readiness findings. Facility directors own safe operations and workforce. National laboratories own reference science and measurement development. Service Technology Commands bring formations and doctrine. Civil, clinical, environmental, aviation, marine, utility, biosafety and ethics authorities retain their lawful decisions.

An **Experimental Access Board** allocates scarce unique capacity using public criteria: consequence of the claim, absence of alternatives, readiness of the article, information gained per hazardous or destructive test, cross-program value, outside challenge and event urgency. A sponsor cannot jump the queue by promising a procurement milestone it set without range capacity.

Open access is graduated. Public test methods, interface fixtures, synthetic datasets and non-sensitive results should be available by default. Protected infrastructure, personal health, proprietary process and security data receive bounded access. The existence of protection cannot conceal aggregate performance, adverse events or the tested envelope. Small firms and universities receive technical assistance and reserved capacity so conformance cost does not become a barrier to entry.

International partners may use the network under reciprocal evidence and sovereignty agreements. Shared profiles, test artifacts and measurement methods can become an export advantage. A nation buying a resilience system should be able to reproduce its acceptance test, operate disconnected from a U.S. vendor and retain its own incident data.

## 13. How the proving network changes technology development

The network changes what the Department can ask industry to invent.

It can ask for a flowing-breach arrest system because a controlled megaflow range can destroy and compare prototypes. It can ask for 300 MW self-forming grid bridges because protection and transfer can be tested without risking a city. It can ask for 250 MW fire measurement and precision agent chemistry because emissions and residuals are measured. It can ask for large autonomous work formations because a mixed public-interface range can seed common perception failures. It can ask for rapid bridges with sensed structural state because full-scale damage, repair and recertification are repeatable. It can ask for distributed biomanufacturing because product switching and independent release can fail in a contained environment. It can ask an alternate factory to reproduce a qualified module because the digital thread and range provide an external truth.

The ranges will themselves drive technology: high-power reversible grids, energy recovery, enormous valves and pumps, emissions capture, distributed calorimetry, synthetic terrain, high-rate instrumentation, hybrid physical simulation, safe challenge materials, autonomous range marshaling, privacy-preserving public-effect measurement and automated configuration custody. Like military and space test infrastructure, the proving system becomes an industrial frontier.

It also makes termination easier. A program can fail early against a respected national environment without every negative result becoming a political referendum on the mission. The nation retains the facility, methods, data, workforce and competing approaches. Failure becomes productive capital rather than vanished contractor expense.

## 14. Rendering and acquisition gate

This pass does not authorize vehicle renderings.

The network can eventually support accurate facility architecture drawings: basin sections, exhaust systems, grid one-lines, reaction structures, containment zones, synthetic municipal networks and range-control boundaries. Even those require concept balances and safety envelopes beyond the present workbook.

Platform rendering remains downstream of two closures:

1. the platform's mass, energy, flow, structure, crew, sustainment and lifecycle configuration reaches project `R3`; and
2. the common architecture interfaces and relevant `NRPN` mission profiles identify what external form must actually support.

The proving network changes future images from aesthetic propositions into testable configurations. A flood carrier drawing must show which test cells validate pumps, pipe deployment, craft recovery, maintenance and degraded operation. A firefighting aircraft must show how the range measures base cycle, airspace, precision, thermal exposure, agent mass balance and ground hold. A water–energy platform must show the shore interface, product custody, residual route, protection and manual-safe state that will be tested.

## 15. Conclusions and next research

This pass reaches seven conclusions.

First, a peer resilience innovation system requires peer evidence infrastructure. The proving network is part of force generation, not a discretionary laboratory supplement.

Second, ten unique national flagships are justified by distinct physical extremes, but knowledge production must remain distributed through twenty-one regional ranges and twenty-seven nodes.

Third, the proposed scale is deliberately frontier-seeking: 250 MW controlled fire, 250 m³/s recirculating hydraulics, 100,000 m³/day water process, 300 MW grid exchange, 10,000-tonne structural payload, municipality-scale public service and long-duration multi-theater campaigns. Every value remains an exploration requirement until its own alternatives, physics, safety, environment and cost study closes.

Fourth, statistical repetition is as important as spectacular scale. A 1% zero-failure screen requires 299 independent failure-free trials at 95% confidence under restrictive assumptions. The network must combine high-repetition elemental evidence with subsystem faults, full-scale mechanisms, formation trials and operational replication.

Fifth, safety and environmental control are research outputs. All ten flagship safety cases remain open. The network is not available merely because concrete and machinery exist.

Sixth, the $320B envelope is internally allocated but not cost-validated. Compound stress creates a $47.04B shortfall; severe stress creates $108.96B. Reference designs and independent estimates must decide whether the answer is more money, smaller scale, more federation, different sequencing or terminated facilities.

Seventh, the next highest-value work is to select one flagship for a configuration-level facility study while mapping one platform family through that range. The Megaflow and Coastal Range paired with `HCG-100`, or the Grid and Thermal Range paired with `PRG-100`, would expose the deepest coupled physics. The Fire–Atmosphere Range paired with the integrated fire force would expose the hardest air–ground–environment measurement problem. None requires an exterior rendering yet.

The stop decision is deliberate. The network now has a facility grammar, scale, site tiers, capacity logic, evidence ladder, metrology, safety and environmental doctrine, mobile assets, workforce, construction gates and funding model. It does not have land, reference designs, regulatory findings, accepted safety cases, bottom-up costs or operating evidence. Those are the next research objects—not blanks to be filled with confidence.
