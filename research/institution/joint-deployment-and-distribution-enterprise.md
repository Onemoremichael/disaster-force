# The National Resilience Deployment and Distribution Enterprise

## A first-principles design for entering a damaged theater, closing a civil-service force and sustaining it at strategic scale

### Research state

`R1/R2 institution` · `R2+ quantitative architecture` · all engineering, cost, workforce, access and campaign evidence gates remain open · no platform rendering authorized

## Executive judgment

The Department of Resilience should not own a logistics branch whose task ends when cargo reaches a port. It should operate a **National Resilience Deployment and Distribution Enterprise** (`NRDDE`): a standing national system that turns formations at home into lawful, self-sustaining public service inside a damaged theater.

This distinction is structural. The disaster has often damaged the airport, port, roads, rail, power, water, communications, hospitals, lodging, fuel distribution, public administration and workforce on which conventional logistics assumes it can rely. Delivering a ship or aircraft to the edge of that system does not project resilience. The relevant output is a complete operating formation at the point of need, with authority, utilities, communications, maintenance, responder support, measurement and an exit path.

The Pass 23 order of battle exposed this problem by failing four of five aggregate movement screens and all ten reception screens. Those failures were directionally important but temporally crude: they treated the entire 11.58 million tonnes of deployable force as if it had to arrive inside a seven- or fourteen-day early-effect window. This study replaces that screen with five force-closure bands:

1. `R0`, anticipatory staging from 120 hours before a forecast event;
2. `R1`, a minimum viable service package in the first 72 hours;
3. `R2`, initial operating capability through day 14;
4. `R3`, formation closure through day 45; and
5. `R4`, depth, replacement and endurance through day 90.

The correction does not relax urgency. It defines what must be urgent. Command, access, rescue, communications, medical continuity, route opening and the first bounded utility effects cannot wait for the main body. Heavy process equipment, reserve stocks, redundant routes and replacement pools can arrive later without being credited to the first 72-hour public promise.

Under the declared assumptions, the linked workbook closes all ten reference campaigns across four strategic movement modes, damaged-theater reception, staging and onward movement, responder basing and daily sustainment. It does so with eleven formation families already inside the Pass 23 force, a permanent network of prepared entry and distribution sites, and an illustrative technology program. The attributable crosswalk is approximately:

- **1.208 million affiliated billet-equivalents**, including about 1.079 million direct formation billets and allocated institutional depth;
- **$2.573T** for the eleven Pass 23 formation lines;
- **$1.380T** allocated from existing Pass 23 bases, research, education, digital and industrial lines;
- **$4.744T** including the attributable share of the existing 20 percent program reserve; and
- **$332.5B per year** in mature personnel, operation, maintenance, exercise, stock, research and access costs already nested within the larger Department screen.

These are design-study hypotheses, not acquisition estimates. Their value is that they make the consequences of the national promise explicit and prevent a transportation fleet from being counted without its nodes, crews, bases, industrial system or public-service interfaces.

The severe design stress is more revealing than the reference closure. When the largest entry path and connected host services are lost together, eight campaigns still close. The Cascadia earthquake and allied coastal megacity earthquake do not. In both cases, reception, responder basing and sustainment fail as a correlated system. Buying more long-range lift would not solve the failure. The indicated remedies are geographically independent entry paths, partner-node depth, additional self-sustaining bases, higher sustainment margin and real campaign trials.

The central finding is therefore:

> The Department's decisive mobility asset is not a ship, aircraft, convoy or port. It is a rehearsed chain of independent paths that converts national inventory into measurable public service while the receiving society's own support systems are impaired.

### Pass 25 continuation

The [Cascadia Joint Resilience Campaign](cascadia-joint-resilience-campaign.md) now executes the item-level study proposed below. It replaces the single correlated-loss scalar with nine service islands, eight safe-zone or afloat origins, twelve paths in eleven correlation groups, sixty-four mission-package records, responder bases, ninety-day sustainment, protected reverse and people flow, and reconstitution. ## 1. The object being designed

### 1.1 Not transportation, but service closure

Conventional transportation measures are necessary but incomplete. Tonnes moved, passengers delivered, sorties flown, square feet of deck activated and containers tracked describe activity. They do not establish that the receiving theater can unload, sort, clear, route, operate, sustain or safely transfer the force.

Military doctrine supplies useful lower bounds. Joint doctrine treats global transportation management as an integrated process linked to planning, programming, budgeting and execution, requires time-phased force and deployment information, and identifies theater movement control, in-transit visibility and joint port opening as parts of one system.[^jp401] The Universal Joint Task List defines force closure at the destination with sufficient resources and readiness to conduct the assigned mission rather than at departure from origin.[^ujtl] Air Force contingency-response doctrine similarly treats airfield opening as a cross-functional force able to operate under denied, disconnected, intermittent or limited conditions—not an air-traffic-control detachment sent by itself.[^afi10202]

The Department should adopt the systems discipline but change the purpose. Military distribution exists to generate combat power. Resilience distribution exists to reduce population-service-time loss while protecting civil rights, host capacity, workers and the environment.

For a campaign `c` and time band `t`, closure is therefore:

```text
service closure(c,t)
  = min(
      complete formation available,
      origin loading,
      strategic movement,
      entry-node opening,
      reception throughput,
      staging and onward movement,
      responder-base capacity,
      sustainment and reverse flow,
      lawful authority,
      technical release,
      host acceptance
    )
```

The minimum is deliberate. Extra airlift cannot compensate for a closed bridge. A cleared berth cannot compensate for absent cranes or truck drivers. A field hospital cannot open if its oxygen, sterile water, waste, blood, staff accommodation and referral network remain behind it. A responder population that consumes the damaged community's hotel rooms, potable water, generators and sanitation may increase the very service deficit it was sent to reduce.

### 1.2 The public unit of movement

The Department should not prioritize cargo primarily by commodity code or platform prestige. It should create a **mission-configured service module** whose record binds together:

- the public effect it initiates or protects;
- the population, geography and duration covered;
- every person, item, consumable, container and credential needed for that effect;
- mass, volume, dimensions, handling, temperature, hazardous-material and power conditions;
- admissible transport and transfer modes;
- entry, base, maintenance, data and authority dependencies;
- a latest useful arrival time rather than a generic desired date; and
- a substitution rule stating what can replace the module without changing the service promise.

Examples include a 72-hour dialysis-continuity package, a water-quality laboratory and sampling package, a bridge assessment and controlled-release package, an airfield-opening core, a district-cooling starter package, a port hydrography and clearance package, or a community communications and benefits-access package. The module is not necessarily container-sized. It is an accountable unit of public effect that may occupy several aircraft loads or a heavy-lift ship hold.

## 2. The institutional architecture

### 2.1 One national enterprise, several command relationships

The `NRDDE` is not a seventh operating service. It is a permanent enterprise led by the Department's **Strategic Mobility Command**, drawing forces from the Air Response Service, Maritime Response Service, Land Engineering Service, Infrastructure Restoration Service and joint commands.

Strategic Mobility Command should have four enduring responsibilities:

1. **enterprise design:** own the end-to-end movement and reception architecture, data standards, capacity ledger and exercise program;
2. **global allocation:** recommend modal, node and commercial capacity allocations across simultaneous campaigns;
3. **force provision:** generate strategic-mobility, entry-opening and distribution packages to certified readiness; and
4. **movement control:** operate the national movement and distribution center until responsibility passes to the supported campaign command.

The supported geographic or campaign command owns public objectives, civil integration, priorities and acceptable risk inside the theater. The generating services retain responsibility for formation readiness and technical competence. Independent technical authorities retain safety, environmental, medical, nuclear, airworthiness, seaworthiness and infrastructure-release powers. No movement commander may waive those authorities by declaring urgency.

The enterprise should contain six standing subcommands:

| Subcommand | Enduring task | Crisis output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strategic Air Mobility Command | aircraft, air crews, loading, en-route support, aeromedical interfaces and austere access | time-critical intercontinental and regional service modules |
| Strategic Surface and Sealift Command | ocean, coastal, rail, highway and inland-water fleets; mariner and operator readiness | heavy force closure, replenishment and retrograde |
| Theater Opening and Node Recovery Command | airfield, port, littoral, inland terminal, railhead and route restoration | lawful, measured entry capacity under infrastructure loss |
| Inland Distribution Command | staging, transload, movement control, hubs, last-100-km delivery and reverse flow | cargo and people cleared from entry to operating locations |
| Responder Base Command | independent lodging, food, water, power, thermal safety, sanitation, health and crew recovery | a force that does not displace or burden survivors |
| Deployment Data and Allocation Command | service-module records, schedules, in-transit state, priorities, alternatives and audit | a common operational ledger that survives degraded connectivity |

The existing Theater-Opening Command (`J-TOC`) remains the campaign-level integrator. The Theater Sustainment Command (`J-TSC`) controls distribution, recurring supply and reverse flow after entry. The Sustainment and Reconstitution Command defined in Pass 23 remains the Department-wide owner of depots, replacement and return to readiness. Pass 24 does not create three organizations for the same work; it defines the handoffs among strategic movement, theater execution and national regeneration.

### 2.2 Civil purpose changes the command design

The Department operates within a society, not over conquered terrain. The opening package must therefore include authorities and public-service integrators that military analogies can underweight:

- state, territorial, tribal, local or invited foreign mission authorization;
- port, airport, rail, road, land, utility and spectrum access;
- labor, customs, immigration, credential and mutual-recognition arrangements;
- environmental sampling, waste, discharge and public-health release;
- accessibility, privacy, benefits, family unity and safeguarding;
- local procurement and anti-displacement rules;
- public explanation of priorities, uncertainty and service limits; and
- civil transfer, residual support, claims, records and demobilization.

These are not paperwork trailing the convoy. They are throughput. A ship waiting for a berth agreement, a clinical team waiting for credential recognition or a pipeline waiting for a discharge authorization has zero public output.

## 3. Five deployment waves

### 3.1 `R0`: anticipatory staging

Forecastable hazards create time, but using that time requires reversible decisions. `R0` begins as early as 120 hours before impact and stages integrated service modules at safe-zone nodes. It favors distributed sites whose roads, drainage, power sources and labor pools do not share the projected impact mechanism.

An anticipatory package should be able to disperse, shelter, move again or return without consuming its useful life. The model gives substantial `R0` credit to the hurricane, wildfire, heat, inland-flood and drought cases and none to the no-notice earthquake and tornado cases. This is a policy hypothesis. Real credit requires calibrated forecast distributions, decision thresholds, evacuation conflicts and false-alarm costs.

### 3.2 `R1`: minimum viable service in 72 hours

`R1` is the smallest complete package able to create a bounded, observable effect. It includes command and civil integration; reconnaissance and damage sensing; communications; life safety; air, port or route opening; public-health continuity; and the logistics needed to keep those functions operating.

It does not claim the arrival of the entire formation. Each `R1` module has an explicit endurance period and a handoff condition. The package fails if the next wave cannot replenish or replace it before exhaustion.

### 3.3 `R2`: initial operating capability by day 14

By day 14, the campaign should have a stable multimodal entry network, functioning staging and onward distribution, protected responder bases, recurring sustainment, and the first major hazard-control, health, water, power, communications and community-service effects.

This is the principal reception test in the model. Required daily reception equals the `R0 + R1 + R2` formation mass divided by fourteen days. The calculation is intentionally simple: it does not yet resolve arrival curves, queuing, volume, incompatibilities or intermittent closure.

### 3.4 `R3`: force closure by day 45

The main body arrives in `R3`: heavy engineering, utility corridors, major treatment and generation systems, field hospitals, offshore support, distribution hubs, stocks, maintenance and replacement capability. Ocean, rail and inland-water movement dominate this band. Force closure means the required formations are at their destinations with sufficient resources and authority to conduct the mission, not that every item has crossed a national port.

### 3.5 `R4`: depth and endurance by day 90

`R4` adds redundancy, replacement, reserve stocks, second routes, repair depth and reconstitution capacity. It is essential to a 90–240 day campaign but receives no credit for early life-safety claims. A Department that counts day-90 inventory as if it had saved lives on day two is misstating readiness.

## 4. The national entry lattice

### 4.1 Prepared Resilience Entry Complexes

The enterprise requires permanent geography, not only deployable kits. The study proposes twenty-four **Prepared Resilience Entry Complexes** (`NREC`) as an archetype count. These are not twenty-four giant federal bases and not a siting recommendation. An `NREC` is a governed cluster of interoperable facilities positioned outside a shared regional failure mechanism:

- one or more cargo-capable airfields or auxiliary landing sites;
- deepwater, coastal, inland-water, rail and highway interfaces where geography permits;
- heavy cranes, mobile handling, fuel, shore power and hazardous-material areas;
- configuration-controlled staging, cold chain, medical and utility-module storage;
- responder-base and crew-recovery capacity;
- maintenance, fabrication, inspection and decontamination;
- communications and offline movement-control systems;
- safe-zone water, energy, sanitation and waste capacity; and
- a geographically independent paired complex with exercised transfer plans.

The fixed network also includes forty-eight dispersed auxiliary airfield or terminal sites, seventy-two inland distribution campuses and sixty responder-base campuses in the capital screen. These numbers establish industrial and fiscal scale. Site-specific hazards, land, labor, environment, community benefits and opportunity costs remain open.

Daily public use is essential. Empty emergency campuses decay. The complexes should host service schools, testing, commercial freight, utility exercises, public works, reserve training, depot maintenance and community infrastructure in ordinary time. The operating model should create skilled employment and useful throughput before catastrophe rather than pay for dormant symbolism.

### 4.2 Mobile node-opening forces

Fixed complexes cannot be assumed at the destination, especially abroad. The model allocates six node classes across ten simultaneous campaigns:

| Node class | Modeled inventory | Nominal role |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Prepared Resilience Entry Complex | 24 | large multimodal anchor and intermediate staging base |
| Expeditionary Air Entry Group | 67 | assess, repair and operate damaged or austere air access |
| Expeditionary Port Opening Group | 60 | hydrography, clearance, salvage, cargo handling and movement control |
| Littoral Entry Group | 69 | ship-to-shore transfer across damaged quays, beaches and shallow water |
| Inland Water Entry Group | 54 | river and lake terminals, ferries, barges and access corridors |
| Land/Rail Entry Group | 121 | route assessment, bridge release, railhead and terminal operations |

The inventories come from the Pass 23 readiness calculation. Their assumed throughput is not sourced performance. Each class is a complete operating formation rather than a crane, dredge, boat or repair team.

Official experience supports that distinction. The Army's Rapid Port Opening Element combines planning, in-transit visibility, receiving, transload and initial surface distribution rather than stopping at the airfield edge.[^rpoe] Joint analysis after early Iraq operations found that delaying port-opening and distribution forces produced processing delays and backlogs; it also found that a small port-opening unit could be overwhelmed and had to sit inside a larger theater-opening framework.[^gao07807] The Department should institutionalize that lesson at a much larger civil-service scale.

### 4.3 Independence is a designed property

Node count is not path diversity. Two ports may share a channel, bridge, tug workforce, fuel terminal, grid substation or rail line. Two airfields may share the same floodplain, approach corridor, maintenance contractor or regional communications network. Nominal alternatives can therefore fail together.

Every campaign plan needs an **independence map** covering at least:

- hazard mechanism and geographic footprint;
- approach channel, runway, bridge, lock and critical route;
- energy, fuel, water and communications source;
- cargo-handling and repair equipment;
- workforce residence and transportation;
- environmental and legal authority;
- owner, vendor, cyber and data dependencies; and
- time required to shift the flow.

The model's primary-path-loss factors are placeholders for this covariance. They are not probabilities.

## 5. Purpose-built strategic movement

### 5.1 The fleet is a family of systems

The movement force must span urgent light modules, heavy formation closure, recurring sustainment and reverse flow. The illustrative fleet includes:

- long-range heavy and medium short-field strategic lifters;
- autonomous heavy cargo aircraft as a bounded research path;
- tactical cargo and vertical-lift fleets for hub-to-need movement;
- purpose-built strategic response roll-on/roll-off ships;
- fast response logistics ships;
- offshore support and repair ships;
- shallow landing connectors and modular causeways;
- inland barge trains, workboats and ferries;
- resilience rail mission sets and heavy highway convoy sets; and
- loading, maintenance, fuel, communications and movement-control systems.

The workbook allocates these assets to eleven formation budgets without changing the Pass 23 formation subtotal. The asset counts are **capacity equivalents**, not approved configurations. Payload, range, draft, stability, runway, sea state, cargo density, loading time, crew, maintenance and lifecycle performance remain open.

The maritime analogy is especially instructive. MARAD's Ready Reserve Force provides government-owned surge sealift from outports and uses readiness activation categories, but its current model is largely an aging reserve fleet activated for a military requirement.[^rrf] GAO has documented declining readiness, equipment casualties, maintenance delay and the need for disciplined capital planning.[^gao17503] A resilience fleet should be clean-sheet, used and exercised in peacetime, paired with purpose-built cargo and receiving interfaces, and crewed by a larger national maritime workforce. It should not be a humanitarian mission pasted onto retired defense or commercial hulls.

### 5.2 Four modal balances

The model assigns the 11.58 million tonnes across four strategic modes:

| Mode | Full assigned mass | Modeled effective throughput |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Air | 1.112 million t | 53,627 t/day |
| Ocean | 4.250 million t | 198,800 t/day |
| Land/rail | 4.620 million t | 394,400 t/day |
| Inland water | 1.598 million t | 174,150 t/day |

Each mode has a different due fraction at 72 hours, day 14, day 45 and day 90. The model closes all sixteen mode/deadline checks after bounded prepositioning. This is an arithmetic result, not evidence that the underlying cycle rates or networks can be achieved concurrently.

The distribution is also a strategic industrial policy. A fleet this large creates durable demand for shipyards, aircraft plants, rolling-stock manufacturers, heavy vehicles, barges, cranes, materials, power electronics, cold chain, communications, software, maintenance and skilled operators. Unlike a reserve fleet that waits, the enterprise should purchase readiness through ordinary public-service operations, training, international exercises and commercial-compatible missions that preserve crews and industrial learning.

## 6. Reception, onward movement and responder basing

### 6.1 Reception is a flow, not an opening ceremony

The `R2` reception calculation combines the allocated fixed and mobile nodes, their assumed availability and a campaign-specific damage factor. The reference margins range from 3.73× to 5.88× because node-opening assets are deliberately redundant. That apparent abundance disappears downstream: reference end-to-end margins range from only 1.026× to 1.448×.

This is the central quantitative lesson. The opening node is not the bottleneck once it is funded as a complete system. The limits move to:

- sustaining 20,000–23,000 tonnes per day in the largest campaigns;
- clearing cargo from nodes onto damaged theater routes;
- housing and supporting 40,000–108,000 responders without host draw; and
- maintaining those flows while people, equipment and sites rotate or fail.

### 6.2 Onward movement

The model assigns thirty-three `J-TSC` formations across the ten campaigns. Each receives an illustrative gross onward capacity of 18,000 tonnes per day before availability and route factors. The tightest reference margins are the urban heat emergency at 1.026×, the western wildfire complex at 1.151× and the inland flood at 1.189×.

These small margins are a warning. The next configuration study should model individual road, rail, water and air arcs; cargo volume and compatibility; bridge load classes; truck and locomotive cycles; driver hours; fuel; loading queues; curfews; debris; public traffic; aftershocks; weather; and priority conflicts. It should also distinguish bulk corridor throughput from local distribution to clinics, neighborhoods, shelters and utility work sites.

### 6.3 Responder bases are operational infrastructure

The base force is not morale support. A campaign with 100,000 deployed workers requires a city-scale water, energy, food, thermal, sanitation, medical, transport, communications and waste system. Drawing those services from a damaged community is operationally and ethically unacceptable.

The reference architecture gives every `J-TOC` eight independently placeable base modules at an illustrative 8,250 people per module and 80 percent availability. The modules should be able to split among entry nodes and operating areas. They require accessible accommodation, quiet and shift-compatible spaces, infection control, occupational health, behavioral support, family communication, laundry, decontamination, food safety, fire protection, cooling or heating, water and wastewater, solid and hazardous waste, power, network services and transport.

No large base module should advance beyond `R2` without a 30–180 day occupied demonstration in representative heat, cold, smoke, flood, dust, limited-water and degraded-grid conditions.

### 6.4 Sustainment and reverse flow

The Pass 23 daily supply signal is 148,250 tonnes across the ten campaigns. The reference screen closes with margins between 1.069× and 1.448×. These are narrow for a mature force because the coefficients do not yet include a validated bill of consumption or complete reverse flow.

Sustainment must account for:

- food, potable and process water, fuels, reagents and medical consumables;
- oxygen, blood, pharmaceuticals, cold-chain material and controlled substances;
- filters, membranes, conductors, pumps, tires, batteries, lubricants and spares;
- protective equipment, contamination control and worker health;
- packaging, pallets, containers and materials-handling equipment;
- wastewater, sludge, damaged batteries, fuel, chemicals and contaminated debris;
- patients, fatalities, damaged equipment and returning personnel; and
- maintenance and reconstitution that begin before deployment ends.

The enterprise should measure **service-hours sustained per constrained tonne**, not reward weight moved for its own sake.

## 7. The degraded case

The workbook applies a deterministic correlated-loss case in which the campaign's primary entry path and connected host-service capacity are lost together. Eight campaigns retain an end-to-end margin at or above 1.0. Two fail:

| Campaign | Reference minimum | Degraded minimum | Binding degraded problem |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Cascadia earthquake and tsunami | 1.092× | 0.765× | reception, responder base and sustainment decline together |
| Allied coastal megacity earthquake | 1.093× | 0.765× | partner-node access, base capacity and sustainment lack independent depth |

The identical minima are not a claim that the campaigns are equivalent; they are a consequence of selected screening factors. The value is diagnostic. Both reference cases barely clear on sustainment before the shock, and both rely on coastal systems exposed to correlated damage. The force-design response is not another generic reserve percentage. It is specific:

1. add an independent inland or offshore path whose bridge, labor, energy and authority do not share the coastal failure;
2. pre-negotiate and exercise partner entry complexes rather than count nominal foreign infrastructure;
3. increase responder-base modules and locate them outside the correlated impact area;
4. add sustainment formations or reduce daily burden through local, safely qualified production;
5. carry alternate technical-release and customs teams; and
6. prove the complete shift under a live, multi-week campaign trial.

## 8. Technology that the mission must create

The enterprise should not freeze current transport technology into a trillion-dollar institution. Its scale should drive invention in areas where commercial markets do not price the public value of rare, infrastructure-destroying events.

The workbook allocates an illustrative $220B from the existing Department-wide science and proving line to ten programs:

1. **autonomous multimodal load choreography:** continuously recompute service priority, load plans, reroutes and custody under degraded communications with human override and a public audit trail;
2. **rapid runway and pavement regeneration:** robotic assessment, novel repair materials, debris clearance and high-rate certification under heat, water and aftershock;
3. **robotic port clearance and hydrography:** map, clear, mark and monitor channels and berths while reducing diver and crew exposure;
4. **self-erecting piers and adaptive causeways:** create safe ship-to-shore interfaces across tide, sediment, damaged quays and higher sea states;
5. **zero-host-utility responder bases:** integrate closed-loop water, energy, thermal, sanitation and waste systems for city-scale workforces;
6. **a resilience intermodal container family:** common mechanical, electrical, fluid, data, safety and custody interfaces for water, oxygen, cold chain, power, workshops and laboratories;
7. **distributed cargo aircraft and vertical lift:** move high-value modules when roads and runways fail without making unproven autonomy a readiness assumption;
8. **damage-tolerant in-transit visibility:** preserve identity, priority, condition and custody offline and reconcile cleanly when networks return;
9. **rapid route and bridge digital certification:** fuse remote sensing, structural measurement and engineering authority to release corridors by load class; and
10. **reverse-flow hazard logistics:** package, track, isolate and move waste, contamination, casualties and damaged energy systems without creating secondary harm.

The purpose of the program is not novelty. Each program must change a visible operational coefficient—time, throughput, availability, host draw, safety, labor, mass or recovery—and then survive independent representative testing. Until then, the workbook gives the novel asset no evidentiary status beyond a planning placeholder.

## 9. Workforce and industrial economy

### 9.1 A national operating profession

The eleven formation lines contain about 1.079 million direct billet-equivalents in the Pass 23 component structure. Allocating twelve percent institutional depth produces an enterprise crosswalk of about 1.208 million:

| Component | Affiliated billet-equivalents |
| --- | ---: |
| Permanent federal | 810,327 |
| Federal reserve | 239,845 |
| State/territorial | 71,578 |
| Certified industry | 77,744 |
| Partner/allied | 8,438 |

This is not a federal payroll recommendation. It is a burden map. It includes flight and ship crews, mariners, locomotive and heavy-vehicle operators, port and airfield personnel, engineers, mechanics, communications teams, logisticians, medical staff, base services, environmental specialists, safety authorities, planners, instructors, depot workers and industrial labor.

Existing labor shortages cannot be treated as a procurement detail. GAO has identified potential sustained-activation mariner shortfalls and the need for strategic workforce planning in maritime programs.[^gao19260t] A clean-sheet fleet without schools, sea time, career ladders, medical standards, family support, reserve policies and ordinary operating work is an uncrewed inventory.

### 9.2 Companies at defense-industrial scale

The Department should create a market in which firms can become prime systems integrators for public-service effects—not only component vendors. A resilience prime might own the tested integration of a port-opening system, an independent responder base, a water-service module or an autonomous route-opening formation while remaining subject to open interfaces, government data rights, second sources and independent test.

The industrial architecture should include:

- multiyear procurement tied to demonstrated configurations;
- government-owned or government-accessible critical tooling;
- second-source qualification for bottleneck components;
- hot production lines and exercised surge options;
- modular open interfaces that prevent single-prime captivity;
- public-interest licensing for crisis-critical intellectual property;
- regional supplier development and apprenticeship systems;
- domestic and allied production mapping;
- export configurations separated from sovereign control functions; and
- recurring peacetime orders for maintenance, training and public works.

Export is a plausible strategic consequence, not a guaranteed financial return. Countries and cities may purchase compatible water, power, base, port, airfield, flood and communications modules; access the proving network; license standards; or join regional readiness pools. The United States would project power by becoming the most credible provider of interoperable public-service restoration systems. Recipient sovereignty, debt, maintenance capability, data control and local industry must be designed into that offer.

## 10. Capital and operating architecture

### 10.1 The attributable envelope

Pass 24 is a decomposition of Pass 23. It must not be added to the $12.049T Department total. The eleven relevant formation lines sum to $2.573T. The study then allocates portions of existing common lines:

| Common line | Pass 23 line | Pass 24 allocation |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Non-utility bases, depots and ranges | $650B | $600B |
| Science, technology and proving outside the utility system | $1.500T | $220B |
| Education and workforce infrastructure | $350B | $120B |
| Information, forecast and common digital | $400B | $90B |
| Strategic industry and materials outside the utility system | $500B | $350B |

The resulting $3.953T pre-reserve attribution plus a $790.7B share of the existing program reserve produces a $4.744T envelope. The 25-year profile begins with architecture, prototypes and enabling sites; rises into industrial expansion and serial production; then falls through fleet completion, replacement and closeout. It peaks near $340.7B per year in program years nine and ten.

This phasing is a normalized shape, not an executable appropriation. It excludes escalation, financing, program-specific schedules, learning curves, retirement of prototypes, environmental mitigation and independent cost confidence.

### 10.2 Mature annual operations

The $332.5B mature operating screen includes about $152.7B in component-weighted personnel, $77.2B in formation operation and maintenance, $27.6B in common enabling operation, and $75B in deployment exercises, stock rotation, industrial readiness, continuing research and access. It is nested within the Pass 23 Department-wide $858B annual screen.

The size is not an argument for waste. It is the cost consequence of owning readiness rather than hoping transport, ports, workers and bases can be improvised after simultaneous catastrophe.

## 11. Readiness, exercises and measures

### 11.1 Readiness certification

Every deployable formation should have one configuration-controlled readiness vector:

```text
complete readiness
  = min(material, people, movement, reception,
        sustainment, authority, technical release, host interface)
```

The enterprise should reject equipment-only readiness. A ship without a crew, berth, discharge path and receiving formation is not partially capable. An airfield team without lift is not available. A commercial reservation that conflicts with another federal plan is not capacity.

### 11.2 Campaign exercises

The Department needs an annual exercise program closer to joint campaign rehearsal than a warehouse drill. A credible sequence would include:

- monthly no-notice activation of selected small modules;
- quarterly node-opening and alternate-route transfers;
- annual coast, inland-water, airfield, rail and responder-base closure exercises;
- biennial multi-region `3C + 5M + 2G` command-post and selective field campaign;
- periodic 30–90 day occupied sustainment and reconstitution trials; and
- allied exercises that use real customs, standards, labor, environment, data and civil-transfer authorities.

The test must sometimes delete the expected port, airfield, bridge, communications system, contractor or fuel source after deployment begins. Otherwise the institution will certify its plan rather than its adaptability.

### 11.3 Measures that matter

The national dashboard should show:

- population-service-hours restored or protected;
- time to minimum viable service by lifeline and geography;
- time to initial operating capability and complete formation closure;
- end-to-end capacity margin at the current weakest link;
- backlog age and public-effect priority, not only tonnes in queue;
- percent of responder support supplied without host draw;
- independent path count after correlated hazards;
- daily sustainment and reverse-flow balance;
- workforce fatigue, injury, retention and replacement state;
- environmental excursions and unresolved safety conditions;
- reconstitution debt and date of return to certified readiness; and
- distribution of service by vulnerability, access need and jurisdiction.

## 12. What this pass establishes—and what it does not

This pass establishes:

- the Department-level owner and command relationships for strategic movement and damaged-theater entry;
- a five-wave doctrine connecting logistics deadlines to public effects;
- fixed and mobile entry-node classes;
- a first-order air, ocean, land/rail and inland-water fleet architecture;
- reception, onward-movement, responder-base and sustainment screens for ten simultaneous campaigns;
- an eleven-formation, workforce, capital and operating crosswalk to Pass 23;
- a correlated primary-path-loss stress; and
- a mission-driven technology portfolio.

It does not establish:

- any aircraft, ship, vehicle, base or container configuration;
- payload-range, draft, sea-state, runway, route, structure, queue or reliability performance;
- the geographic siting of a federal facility;
- a validated occupation-level workforce;
- a budget estimate or acquisition schedule;
- legal access to domestic, tribal, territorial or foreign infrastructure;
- commercial capacity that is exclusive and available under concurrency;
- the environmental acceptability of a site or operation; or
- an `R3` evidence basis for renderings.

All twenty Pass 24 evidence gates remain open. Pass 25 has built the first item-level, time-phased Cascadia dataset because the campaign combines no notice, port and route damage, high deployed mass, thin sustainment margin and correlated entry loss. It traces representative formations from safe-zone origins through candidate path classes, queues, node constraints, self-sustaining bases, ninety-day replenishment, reverse flow and reconstitution. The next step is to replace its design coefficients with asset-level damage covariance, item manifests, discrete-event queues, representative prototypes and multi-week independent trials.

## Conclusion

A coequal resilience state cannot be organized around the hope that commercial carriers, intact airports, usable ports, available truckers, empty hotels and functioning utilities will converge after catastrophe. It must own the capability to create those conditions.

The `NRDDE` is consequently larger than a transport fleet and more operational than a procurement portfolio. It is a national lattice of prepared places, deployable node-opening formations, strategic and tactical movement, responder cities, distribution control, sustainment, reverse flow, technical authority, industrial depth and practiced civil access. Its purpose is to make distance and infrastructure failure variables that the nation can deliberately overcome.

That is how rescue and stabilization become force projection: not by painting military logistics in humanitarian colors, but by building a different strategic instrument whose final output is life-support restored under the conditions in which ordinary systems have ceased to be dependable.
