# The PCC-D1 population-continuity district

## Executive judgment

The PCC-50 cannot be closed by assigning a light shelter allowance to fifty nominal districts. A district becomes a public-service unit only when 1,000 people can enter, remain and leave safely through a complete chain: surveyed ground, drainage, structure, floor, accessible routes, environmental control, water and sanitation interfaces, local electrical distribution, fire and life safety, furnishings, communications, repair, technical commissioning and civil release.

Pass 30 configures that unit as PCC-D1, the smallest repeatable occupied element of the Population Continuity Complex:

- twenty isolated 50-person bays, sixteen standard and four accessibility-stress bays;
- two 500-person neighborhood nodes for registration, observation, distribution and accountability;
- 7,525 square metres of occupied and support area on roughly 1.25 hectares at 60 percent coverage; and
- connections to one of ten pod utility and waste islands rather than fifty duplicative source plants.

The result is adverse. The earlier PCC-50 technology target assigned only **26.05 tonnes** to a complete district shelter. After other pod burdens are held constant, the air-entry mass ceiling permits **30.66 tonnes per district**. A twenty-six-line transformational district bill of material reaches **78.71 tonnes** and **446.60 cubic metres**. It misses the air-compatible district budget by 48.05 tonnes and is volume-bound in the declared loading screen.

Propagating that district through all fifty positions raises the technology-target pod from 11,818.20 to **14,451.07 tonnes**. If both upland cells are lost, only eight whole pods complete by hour 72; the tenth cargo completion occurs at **hour 81.52**. Construction does not rescue the schedule. The optimistic transformational installation path is twenty-seven hours after a supplied and released site, with 2,000 builders if all fifty districts are erected concurrently.

The finding changes the force architecture. Fully mobile independence remains a research objective, but it cannot be the only architecture. The Department needs two coequal systems:

1. a **mobile service force** that carries complete effects across damaged geography; and
2. a **prepared receiving lattice** of surveyed, maintained, inspectable civil interfaces that can accept those effects without importing every square metre of occupied structure.

A deliberately dependency-bounded case in which 80 percent of the transformational occupied-area system is already present reduces the pod to **11,302.77 tonnes**. Its tenth cargo completion screens at hour 68.66 by air and hour 70.30 offshore; an eighteen-hour district connection path can be pipelined behind cargo. That is a candidate, not a pass. It depends on pre-event availability, hazard independence, lawful access, maintenance, inspection and post-event usability that have not been demonstrated.

The strategic conclusion is not “use tents” or “prebuild shelters.” It is that **receiving capacity is part of force projection**. Prepared ground, anchors, floors, protected utility interfaces, building-adapter kits, local stocks and continuously maintained records may purchase more time and resilience than another increment of transport. The receiving lattice must therefore be designed, funded, exercised and diversified as a weapons-system-scale national program.

The accompanying [district model](../../../models/population-continuity-district-model.xlsx) contains twenty-six sheets covering geometry, zones, loads, the component bill of material, packed volume, mode positions, site work, task networks, crews, thermal and utility interfaces, degraded modes, alternatives, pod propagation, release timing, technology programs, evidence gates, sources and checks.

## Why the district is the correct design object

The PCC-50 is too large for component-level design and too small to be treated as an abstract regional service. The district resolves that scale mismatch. It is large enough to contain the functions that make occupancy lawful and humane, but small enough to isolate fire, contamination, utility faults, crowding, disease, command error and structural damage.

The district is not self-sufficient in every sense. It receives water, wastewater custody, firm power, food, clinical referral, protection support and higher-level repair through explicit interfaces. Its dependency passport must state:

- which services originate inside the district;
- which arrive from a utility, food, waste, medical or command node;
- how long each interface may be absent;
- what degraded state is safe;
- which component or authority can restore it; and
- what evidence permits occupancy and continuing service credit.

This is the key distinction between **modularity** and **independence**. Modularity makes a unit repeatable and isolatable. Independence describes the bounded chain on which that module can rely. The force needs both, but they are not synonyms.

## The occupied system

### Geometry and zoning

The district inherits the PCC-50 service geometry rather than minimizing area until transport closes.

| Element | Residents | Area basis | District area |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Standard sleeping | 800 | 50 ft²/person | 3,716 m² |
| Accessibility-stress sleeping and support | 200 | 100 ft²/person | 1,858 m² |
| Shared support and activity | 1,000 | 35% of dormitory area | 1,951 m² |
| **Total** | **1,000** | — | **7,525 m²** |

The 50 ft² and 100 ft² values are planning anchors from FEMA's current [Short-Term Shelter definition](https://rtlt.preptoolkit.fema.gov/Public/Resource/ViewFile/9-508-1197?a=False&p=2&q=mass+care&s=FemaId&type=Pdf), which also notes that support and sanitation space are additional. They are not proof that the future deployable configuration is sufficient. The [OSHA temporary-labor-camp standard](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.142) provides a second lower-bound analogue for drainage, floors, sleeping area, water, toilets, washing, refuse and maintenance; it is not adopted as the district's disaster-occupancy code.

The area is allocated to sleeping, accessibility support, registration, health observation, dining, thermal and clean-air refuge, sanitation, command/storage/repair and circulation. A small circulation percentage in the workbook reflects net internal allocation after circulation embedded in bay and support layouts; detailed plan geometry must replace it before R3.

### Complete bill of material

The district mass account includes components often omitted when shelter is reduced to a shell.

FEMA's [Commonly Used Sheltering Items catalog](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_commonly-used-sheltering-items-catalog.pdf) lists one enhanced disaster-survivor cot per person and a maximum weight of 40 pounds per cot. At that maximum, 1,000 current cots alone would weigh 18.14 tonnes—about 70 percent of the entire Pass 29 district shelter target. The comparison does not prescribe the future bed. It demonstrates why furnishings cannot remain a massless afterthought.

They are interface screens, not certified load plans. Dimensions, center of gravity, restraint, hazardous separation, cold power, handling sequence and receiving equipment can only increase the burden.

### Load and environmental boundary

The district must survive as occupied infrastructure, not as stored cargo. The model carries test cases for wind, rain, snow, ponding, fire, smoke, heat, cold, seismic motion and aftershock reinspection. The [UFC for non-permanent DoD facilities](https://www.wbdg.org/FFC/DOD/UFC/ufc_1_201_01_2022_c4.pdf) supplies technical analogues—including 100 psf first-floor live load and an unrestricted 110 mph, three-second-gust wind case—but the district does not adopt those values as a universal code. Site-specific law, climate, occupancy, duration and authority control.

The correct acquisition object is not “a shelter rated to 110 mph.” It is a configuration family with declared environmental envelopes, rapid site classification, compatible anchors and foundations, embedded condition evidence, repair procedures and a civil authority able to accept or reject occupancy.

## Installation is part of capability

Airbeam inflation time is not district setup time. An [Army account of Force Provider modernization](https://www.army.mil/article-amp/66467/providing_shelter_for_soldiers) reports a 150-person airbeam camp becoming operational in under four hours with eight people and individual shelters inflating in minutes. That is a useful lower-bound demonstration of one subsystem under a military camp concept. It does not include the district's full public occupancy, site, accessibility, civil-release and 1,000-person service boundary.

The PCC-D1 task network contains fifteen activities:

> Survey and exclude → grade, drain and protect access → install anchors, service spine and primary frame → enclose, insulate and floor → install partitions, furnishings, sanitation, thermal, clean-air and electrical systems → accept fire and life safety → commission and release.

The declared critical paths are 66 hours for the current case, 42 hours for the advanced case and 27 hours for the transformational case. Those clocks begin only after the site, cargo, crews, authority and external interfaces needed by the activity are available.

Fifty concurrent transformational teams require 2,000 builders. The pod's separate transformational operating roster is 2,694 people. Even if half the construction workforce can transition into operations without violating qualifications, fatigue, handover or shift coverage, the peak unique requirement is approximately 3,694 people—well above the 2,500-person pod target. Labor is a first-class transported system, not a percentage reduction applied after equipment design.

## Cargo completion is not public-service release

The movement model now distinguishes four moments:

1. shelter cargo is present;
2. complete pod cargo is present;
3. district systems are installed and technically commissioned; and
4. civil authority releases occupancy and service credit.

The optimistic release floor is the later of complete pod cargo and shelter-first cargo plus the installation path. The serial upper screen adds the installation path after complete cargo. Neither is a schedule prediction; actual release requires item-level arrival, spatial work fronts, crew movement, rework, inspection and authority simulation.

| Case | District import | Recalculated pod | Tenth cargo completion | Optimistic release finding |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Independent | 100% | 14,451.07 t | 81.52 h | Fails before release burden |
| Prepared receiver | 20% | 11,302.77 t | 68.66 h air / 70.30 h offshore | Candidate if the receiver survives and eighteen-hour connection is pipelined |

This distinction should propagate throughout the project. A route opening, generator start, treatment skid output, berth discharge or medical module arrival receives no final effect credit until user-facing service is safe, reachable, staffed and authorized.

## Architectural alternatives

No single technical bet should own the national promise.

### Independent mobile district

Every occupied-area component arrives after the event. This is the strongest dependency posture and the weakest movement posture. It remains the right target for isolated, unprepared, contaminated or politically sensitive destinations, but it does not close the current hour-72 architecture.

### Prepared receiving lattice

The site preserves inspected foundations, anchors, floors, utility stubs, drainage, access geometry, records and possibly protected local structure. The force imports connection, repair, environmental control, furnishings, safety and control packages. This is the only modeled case below both movement ceilings, but it exchanges transport mass for permanent geography, maintenance, inspection and covariance risk.

### Building-adapter district

The force occupies surviving schools, arenas, warehouses, hotels or civic buildings through standardized electrical, water, wastewater, clean-air, accessibility, life-safety and monitoring adapters. Each building needs a pre-event passport, post-event inspection path, known service topology and fallback when the structure or neighborhood fails.

### Thermal-refuge contract

The force supplies a smaller protected environment for the highest-risk cohort while most residents remain in repaired or locally supplied structures. This may save more lives per tonne in heat, cold or smoke campaigns, but it is a narrower public promise and cannot be reported as 1,000 fully protected residents.

### Local structure compiler

The force imports high-value interfaces, automation, membranes, connectors, controls and quality assurance while sourcing low-specific-value structure, ballast, aggregate or panels locally. This requires characterized feedstock, machine recipes, structural acceptance, material custody, environmental limits and an alternative when the local industrial base is part of the disaster.

## Technology program pulled by the failure

The district creates seven mission-driven technology programs:

1. **Integrated occupied-area material:** a repairable structural, thermal, fire-safe envelope and floor system below 30.66 tonnes per district.
2. **Ultralight accessible sleep system:** bed, privacy, storage, transfer, charging, cleanability and pressure-injury protection below five kilograms per resident.
3. **Autonomous site constructor:** survey, grading, drainage, anchoring, placement and recovery below 400 labor-hours and eighteen hours per district.
4. **Volume-compacting logistics:** a complete district below 250 packed cubic metres without unacceptable damage, unpack time or one-use waste.
5. **Self-commissioning service spine:** keyed fluid and electrical couplers, topology discovery, protection settings, fault isolation and verified black start within two hours.
6. **Inspectable adaptive envelope:** continuous evidence of load, leakage, damage, repair and safe degraded occupancy.
7. **Prepared receiving lattice:** a maintained national system of sites, structures, adapters, records, exercises and independent inspection.

Each program owns a measured contradiction in mass, volume, labor, installation or release. Rival teams should receive the same functional contract and be compared by protected person-hours per delivered tonne, cubic metre, builder-hour and hour of response—not by component performance alone.

## Institutional consequences

The Department of Resilience requires an organizational layer that does not exist in a conventional logistics or shelter agency.

- **Population Continuity Service:** owns the protected-person service contract, district doctrine, resident rights and operational formations.
- **National Receiving Lattice Command:** maintains sites, host agreements, building passports, utility interfaces, local stocks and regional exercises.
- **Occupied Systems Acquisition Directorate:** buys complete configurations rather than tents, cots, HVAC and utility kits through unrelated programs.
- **Resident Systems Laboratory:** studies accessibility, thermal exposure, sleep, infection, dignity, food acceptance, family integrity, privacy and human performance.
- **Civil Release Authority:** remains institutionally distinct from program delivery and can withhold occupancy credit.
- **Independent Resilience Test and Evaluation Authority:** owns destructive environmental, installation, degraded-mode and representative-resident trials.

The receiving lattice should be treated as capital infrastructure and readiness inventory simultaneously. A site that has not been inspected, exercised, supplied and verified against correlated hazards is not a force asset.

## Evidence program and rendering gate

The district remains R2+: its equations reconcile, but the system is not proved. Fifteen gates remain open across service sufficiency; site availability; structure; weather; fire; smoke; thermal safety; accessibility; sanitation; utility isolation; packed transport; construction labor; civil release; repair and reuse; reliability; industry and full-pod propagation.

No exterior rendering is authorized. The first useful visualization will follow a closed configuration and must show site section, drainage, anchors, utility boundaries, protected zones, accessible routes, cargo decomposition, work fronts, crews, waste, degraded states and the authority path to occupancy. A beauty view would conceal the system this pass reveals.

## Decision

Pass 30 establishes four governing conclusions:

1. The 26.05-tonne district shelter target in Pass 29 is superseded by a complete-system mass account.
2. The transformational independent district does not close the current air or offshore movement architecture.
3. Prepared receiving capacity is not a convenience; it is a coequal layer of national force projection with its own readiness and covariance burden.
4. Future design must optimize public-service release across mobile mass, fixed receiving infrastructure, labor, volume, time, authority and failure independence as one system.

The next pass should configure the **National Receiving Lattice** as a force: site classes, catchments, hazard covariance, building passports, fixed/mobile division, inspection cycles, regional inventories, occupation and maintenance workforce, capital cost and the ability to lose multiple regions without breaking the hour-72 promise. In parallel, district technology programs should begin rival mass-volume-labor target allocations rather than converge prematurely on one shelter form.
