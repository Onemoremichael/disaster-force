# The `PCC-50` Population Continuity Complex

> **Configuration lineage:** Pass 30 now configures the complete 1,000-person [`PCC-D1` district](district-configuration.md). Its 78.71-tonne transformational bill of material supersedes the 26.05-tonne district shelter target below. The resulting 14,451.07-tonne pod completes its tenth worst-case cargo load at hour 81.52. The PCC-50 cohort, topology, food, water, sanitation, energy and workforce findings remain authoritative.

## Executive judgment

The `PSP-10` public-service pod cannot be treated as a list of proportional water, power, shelter, food and health capacities. People experience a service system, not a portfolio. A shelter resident without safe water, sanitation, thermal protection, food, access, health observation or accountable protection is not a fractionally served person. A generator, kitchen or tent produces no public effect when its interfaces, crews, consumables, residuals or release authorities are missing.

Pass 29 therefore configures the human-sustainment core of one pod as a **Population Continuity Complex**, designated `PCC-50`. It is a distributed, fifty-district public-service campus for 50,000 protected residents, with a separate community food channel reaching another 210,000 people. The complex is not a refugee camp, military base or enlarged present-day shelter. It is a clean-sheet piece of national infrastructure designed to appear where normal systems have ceased to function, preserve life and civil agency for seventy-two hours, and then connect to recovery rather than become a permanent parallel city.

The result overturns the favorable “independent-essential” screen in Pass 28.

At the declared planning density, 50,000 residents require about **4.05 million square feet**, or **376,257 square metres**, of dormitory and support area. The Pass 28 shelter allowance of 1,250 tonnes—25 kilograms per resident—therefore permits only **3.32 kilograms per square metre** for structure, floor, thermal envelope, furnishings, utilities and district systems combined. That was a useful technology objective. It was not a closed configuration.

A first-order independent membrane campus screens at **3,545.67 tonnes**. When combined with a hybrid food network, configured sanitation, seventy-two-hour liquid-equivalent service energy and an advanced-assisted shift roster, the pod grows from 11,738.09 tonnes to **15,152.86 tonnes**. Under the existing Service Entry Force, the worst two-cell-loss case delivers only eight whole pods by hour 72. The tenth arrives at approximately **hour 84.02**. The earlier hour-71 claim is superseded.

The failure exposes a clean design discriminator. If both upland cells are lost, each surviving air cell must deliver a third pod by hour 72. At the declared 669.375-tonne/hour net rate and hour-18 activation, the pod can weigh no more than **12,048.75 tonnes**. The equivalent offshore ceiling is **11,781 tonnes**. A configured independent pod exceeds those ceilings by 3,104.11 and 3,371.86 tonnes respectively.

This does not mean the mission is impossible. It means independence must be designed as a coupled technology and force-architecture program. The workbook identifies a numerical target configuration at 11,818.20 tonnes using an ultra-light complete shelter, dense public food, lower-mass sanitation and transformational labor. It clears the air ceiling by only 230.55 tonnes and misses the offshore ceiling by 37.20 tonnes. Every constituent claim is unproved. A more aggressive fuel-flex pathway screens at 11,477.67 tonnes and clears both ceilings, but adds an equally demanding energy program.

The institutional conclusion is more important than the exact mass: **population continuity is a primary force effect and deserves its own service architecture, acquisition programs, professions, proving facilities and industrial base.** It cannot remain the residue left after transport, medicine and utilities receive their platforms.

The accompanying [Population Continuity Complex model](../../../models/population-continuity-complex-model.xlsx) contains twenty-one sheets covering cohorts, district topology, occupied area, shelter pathways, thermal loads, food production and distribution, water and residuals, energy alternatives, shift-complete labor, integrated pod cases, mobility ceilings, all fifteen two-cell-loss combinations, technology programs and fourteen evidence gates.

## The correction: from capacity list to service bundle

### Capacities do not identify who is served

The `PSP-10` contract includes shelter and sanitation for 50,000 people, food for 260,000 people per day, 2,000 cubic metres of water per day, 65 megawatts of critical power, health capacity, communications and other effects. Those numbers cannot be added or divided by a common population. They have different service objects:

- shelter, sanitation and thermal safety attach to protected residents;
- food can reach both residents and a much larger community population;
- water may be consumed by people, kitchens, clinics, hygiene systems and technical processes;
- power attaches first to a load registry of facilities and utility islands;
- health capacity is a staffed clinical and referral system rather than a population fraction; and
- responder sustainment is an internal force obligation, not public-effect output.

Pass 29 therefore creates a **service-cohort ledger**. The core protected cohort contains 50,000 residents who must receive the complete minimum bundle. The community food cohort contains 210,000 additional people served through distributed commodity or meal-equivalent issue. Responders form a third internal cohort. Critical facilities form a fourth class of service object.

This distinction changes the water interpretation. Two thousand cubic metres per day divided across all 260,000 food beneficiaries is only 7.69 litres per person before any kitchen, hygiene, clinical, staff or process use. It is near the lower emergency drinking-and-basic-cooking band, not a complete service bundle. By contrast, the modeled protected campus uses 1,504.46 cubic metres per day, including 15 litres per resident, food preparation, workforce use, a clinical/process allowance, hygiene and ten percent distribution loss. That leaves 495.54 cubic metres per day for other priority uses. The water effect can close for the campus; it cannot be silently credited to every food recipient at the same standard.

### The service floor is conjunctive

The complex receives credit only when all protected-campus functions operate together:

```text
safe occupied area
    + accessible circulation and care
    + thermal and clean-air control
    + potable water and pressure
    + food production and distribution
    + sanitation and residual custody
    + power, data and communications
    + health observation and referral
    + protection, registration and civil authority
    = protected person-hours
```

This is a stricter measure than bed spaces or meals produced. It is also the only measure consistent with the project's first principle: the output is safely delivered public service over time.

## The operating object

### A distributed complex, not a megatent

The `PCC-50` uses four nested operating layers:

| Layer | Count | Nominal scale | Function |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Complex command | 1 | 50,000 residents | Holds the service contract, civil priorities, allocation and inter-district picture. |
| District | 50 | 1,000 residents | Provides registration, shelter, care, sanitation, thermal refuge and local service control. |
| Neighborhood node | 100 | 500 residents | Creates the accountable sleeping, accessible-support, health-observation and distribution unit. |
| Utility/waste island | 10 each | 5,000 residents | Forms and protects power, maintains water pressure, treats or stores residuals and isolates failure. |

Eight food-production nodes support resident prepared meals. Seventy community-distribution nodes reach the additional 210,000 beneficiaries. These counts are configuration hypotheses, but the distributed logic is foundational.

One undivided campus creates unacceptable common modes: a single feeder, water trunk, controller, kitchen, sanitation failure, fire, security incident or management collapse can remove the whole effect. Fifty districts make supervision human-scaled and permit sectional isolation. Ten utility islands prevent every district from carrying a full source plant while keeping a single failure below catastrophic scale. Neighborhood nodes align health observation, identity, accessible service and local accountability.

The complex is held together by standard boundaries rather than a proprietary nervous system. Medium- and low-voltage electrical interfaces, potable and non-potable fluid connections, sealed wastewater custody, data identity, load records, safety releases and manual degraded modes are common. Source hardware, controllers, treatment processes and suppliers remain deliberately diverse.

### Site creation is part of the force

The operating footprint is too large to be a logistics afterthought. A complete campus needs survey, drainage, soil-bearing and settlement assessment, accessible routes, fire separation, anchors or ballast, stormwater handling, material staging, vehicle circulation, staff areas, protected clinical and food zones, fuel separation, waste custody and eventual site restoration.

The Department must therefore own a high-rate site-preparation formation. Its output is not hectares graded. It is released, drainable, accessible occupied area by district and time. Site selection and preparation begin before shelter cargo arrives; otherwise airlift merely creates a queue of unusable modules.

## The occupied-area constraint

FEMA's current Short-Term Shelter resource definition uses an average 40–60 square feet of dormitory area per resident, notes that disability and access accommodations may require up to 100 square feet per person, and states that support areas and supplemental sanitation may be additional. These are current planning anchors, not the desired end-state architecture. See [FEMA Short-Term Shelter](https://rtlt.preptoolkit.fema.gov/Public/Resource/ViewFile/9-508-1197?a=False&p=2&q=mass+care&s=FemaId&type=Pdf).

The model selects 50 square feet for eighty percent of residents and 100 square feet for a twenty-percent accessibility stress share. It then adds thirty-five percent for registration, dining, care, circulation, storage and other support functions:

| Area element | Result |
| --- | ---: |
| Standard dormitory area | 2,000,000 ft² |
| Accessible dormitory area | 1,000,000 ft² |
| Support and activity area | 1,050,000 ft² |
| **Total** | **4,050,000 ft² / 376,257 m²** |

The twenty-percent share and thirty-five-percent factor are assumptions, not population estimates or codes. They are exposed controls because their product governs mass, thermal load, setup labor and land demand.

The arithmetic reveals why “lightweight shelter” is not a sufficient requirement. At 1,250 tonnes, the Pass 28 screen allows 3.32 kg/m². At the 300-tonne bridge allocation it allows 0.80 kg/m². Those envelopes cannot be judged until the claim states which occupied area is imported, which is reused, and whether floors, insulation, heating/cooling, filtration, furnishings, accessibility, anchors, repair and district service systems are inside the mass.

## Rival shelter architectures

The workbook maintains five pathways rather than selecting a favored visual form.

| Pathway | Imported occupied area | Screened mass | Mass/resident | Dependency claim |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Adaptive-reuse network | 20% | 1,025.75 t | 20.52 kg | Requires inspected safe buildings and interfaces. |
| Hybrid district campus | 50% | 2,172.84 t | 43.46 kg | Combines prepared receivers and imported membrane districts. |
| Independent membrane campus | 100% | 3,545.67 t | 70.91 kg | Imports the complete occupied-area system. |
| Independent panel campus | 100% | 11,885.20 t | 237.70 kg | Durable but incompatible with current first-entry mass. |
| Technology-target campus | 100% | 1,302.51 t | 26.05 kg | Requires radical materials and service integration. |

These are deliberately transparent screens. The membrane case applies 6.5 kg/m² across the full footprint for shell, frame, floor and thermal layers, then adds furnishings, district cores and other systems. The technology case demands a 2 kg/m² integrated area system, 6 kg/person furnishings, three-tonne district cores and a 100-tonne central allowance. None of those figures is observed performance.

The Army's Force Provider Expeditionary system is a useful lower-bound analogue because it treats billeting, kitchen, latrine, shower, laundry, power and distribution as one camp. Its 150-person configuration still declares food, potable water, fuel, trash and blackwater disposal as user-provided dependencies. That is exactly the distinction this project must retain: “stand-alone” equipment can remain dependent on an external service system. See the [2024 Army Force Sustainment Systems fact book](https://www.peocscss.army.mil/assets/PM-FSS-Factbook-30JUL2024.pdf).

## Thermal safety and clean air

The campus envelope must create tolerable exposure rather than simply cover people. The workbook applies four areal thermal cases to the full 376,257 m² footprint and translates them through assumed coefficients of performance, fans, filtration, lighting and district service loads:

| Case | Thermal load | Campus electricity | Share of 65 MW |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Mild | 6.77 MWth | 3.99 MWe | 6.1% |
| Hot-humid | 16.93 MWth | 8.50 MWe | 13.1% |
| Heat plus smoke | 14.30 MWth | 8.12 MWe | 12.5% |
| Cold | 20.69 MWth | 10.43 MWe | 16.0% |

The result is encouraging but bounded. Thermal safety does not consume the entire 65 MW pod contract. It does, however, create large duct, filtration, zoning, condensate, heat-rejection, noise, maintenance and backup obligations that were absent from the mass allowance. Extreme heat and smoke are not independent cases: high outdoor temperature, filter loading, door opening and generator exhaust can compound.

The correct program metric is safe person-hours inside declared temperature, humidity and air-quality bands after one district source, filter train or control segment is lost. Equipment coefficient of performance is only an intermediate variable.

## Food is two service systems

The protected campus requires 150,000 resident meals per day. The broader pod contract adds 630,000 community meal-equivalents per day. Treating all 780,000 as one central feeding line creates unnecessary mass and labor; treating them all as compact rations creates nutrition, dignity, acceptance, waste and public-health risks.

The selected configured pathway is therefore hybrid:

- eight fixed production nodes prepare the resident meals;
- seventy distribution nodes issue compact food to the community cohort;
- cold-chain and custody backbones serve both; and
- every district retains a cold-meal fallback.

FEMA's Field Kitchen Unit is a present-practice anchor: the Type 2 scale reaches 20,000 meals per day and still depends on a fixed operating site and coordinated fuel, water and wastewater. The model rounds 150,000 resident meals to eight nodes and uses current staffing only as a labor screen. See [FEMA Field Kitchen Unit](https://rtlt.preptoolkit.fema.gov/Public/Resource/View/9-508-1000?q=mass+care).

The pathway comparison is stark:

| Food pathway | System mass | Key consequence |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Compact ration for all | 521.20 t | Near the baseline allowance; weakest meal quality and acceptance. |
| Hybrid public feeding | 885.20 t | Balances resident meals with broad low-mass distribution. |
| All prepared meals | 2,083.00 t | Requires thirty-nine kitchens plus high labor, energy and wastewater. |
| Three ready-to-eat meals | 2,087.72 t | Avoids cooking but creates very high package mass and waste. |
| Novel dense food target | 427.60 t | Mass-attractive; nutrition, acceptance and process claims unproved. |

The future technology target is not a calorie brick. It is a culturally adaptable public-food platform at or below 0.42 kg/person-day including its finishing system, with measured water, energy, allergen, waste, refusal and health outcomes. The Department would fund competing dry, locally finished, bioprocess and adaptive-menu pathways through representative population trials.

## Water, sanitation and return flow

The water balance closes only after the protected cohort is explicit. The selected daily campus allocation is:

| Demand | m³/day |
| --- | ---: |
| Resident drinking and basic cooking | 750.00 |
| Food preparation | 150.00 |
| 4,513-person workforce | 67.70 |
| Clinical and service processes | 100.00 |
| Hygiene increment | 300.00 |
| Distribution/process loss | 136.77 |
| **Total** | **1,504.46** |

The model simultaneously records about 1,148 m³/day of wastewater return before uncertain stormwater, washdown and decontamination flows. It configures 2,500 toilet positions, 1,000 shower positions, 1,000 handwashing positions, ten paired treatment/storage nodes and seventy-two-hour consumables at 780 tonnes—300 tonnes above the Pass 28 sanitation allowance.

These unit masses and fixture ratios are assumptions. Their purpose is to make missing functions visible. The sanitation effect is not the number of toilets delivered. It is safe, accessible use; hand hygiene; cleanout; odor and vector control; sealed storage; treatment or transfer; sampling; residual custody; and no uncontrolled release when one node is lost.

## Service energy is a utility, not a generator park

The configured case retains the Pass 28 liquid-prime-power screen: 700 tonnes of source hardware plus 1,140.53 tonnes of fuel-equivalent reserve. It is the most physically legible near-term pathway, not the desired end state.

DOE defines a microgrid as interconnected loads and local energy resources within a controllable electrical boundary that can operate grid-connected or islanded. DOE's islanding examples make black start, critical-feeder selection, source coordination and noncritical load shedding operational functions. Those principles support a source-neutral utility-island architecture; they do not validate a mobile 65 MW design. See [DOE Microgrid Systems](https://www.energy.gov/oe/microgrid-systems) and [DOE Islanding a Microgrid](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/femp/articles/islanding-microgrid).

Five pathways remain in competition:

| Pathway | Screened system mass | Main issue |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Liquid prime power | 1,840.53 t | Fuel covariance, emissions, transfer and resupply. |
| Eight-hour battery plus liquid | 5,553.80 t | Current storage mass overwhelms first-entry benefit. |
| Local-resource hybrid | 1,901.86 t | Lighter recurring fuel only if 25 MW of local resource is verified. |
| Compact reactor target | 2,900.00 t | Licensing, shielding, security, heat rejection and activation. |
| Transformational fuel-flex | 1,500.00 t | Requires a 35-percent recurring-fuel reduction and new assurance evidence. |

The compact-reactor pathway is important even though it is not the lightest seventy-two-hour solution. Its value may emerge at longer duration, in repeated service, or when liquid-fuel routes fail. The Department should test energy pathways across duration and campaign recurrence rather than crown one source in advance.

Every pathway must provide grid formation, protection, grounding, black start, source transfer, sectional isolation, load shedding, metering, cyber-resilient control, heat/exhaust management and accountable technical release. A common electrical interface is valuable; one common controller is not.

## Labor is part of the machine

The Pass 28 current-analogue workforce screen reached 6,643 people per pod but did not derive every function from an operating topology. Pass 29 builds an eleven-function, shift-complete roster from posts, nodes and relief factors:

| Workforce case | People | Reduction from current roster | Additional basing mass |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Current topology analogue | 6,742 | — | 1,122.60 t |
| Advanced assisted | 4,513 | 33.1% | 453.90 t |
| Transformational | 2,694 | 60.0% | 0 t above existing 3,000-person capacity |
| Declared target | 2,500 | 62.9% | 0 t |

The roster includes district command, resident service and protection, shelter health, food production, community distribution, utilities and thermal control, sanitation and waste, logistics and handling, command/data/administration, repair/safety, and supply custody. The advanced and transformational factors are not staffing forecasts. They are task-level research targets.

The adverse result persists: even a sixty-percent labor reduction produces 2,694 people, 194 above the pod target. The model refuses to make them disappear. The remaining gap must be closed by one or more explicit choices:

1. reduce or change the service contract;
2. credit trained local labor and preserve it as a visible dependency;
3. move some functions to resilient remote cells and count the remote labor;
4. improve automation beyond the current target while preserving safety floors; or
5. increase pod staffing and the national force-generation system.

Automation is successful only when total labor falls after remote operators, technicians, software assurance, cyber defense, maintenance, spares, recovery and training are added. Resident co-production can be legitimate only when voluntary, compensated, accessible and not used to transfer hazard or hide headcount.

## The integrated mass contradiction

The model replaces five Pass 28 allowances rather than adding a second support layer.

| Configuration | Pod mass | Change from bridge baseline | Hour-72 status |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Bridge/reuse allowance | 9,870.56 t | — | Pass 28 screen closes. |
| Pass 28 independent-essential screen | 11,738.09 t | +1,867.53 t | Closed at hour 71, but allowances were not configured. |
| Configured prepared receiver | 12,632.94 t | +2,762.38 t | Exceeds both third-pod ceilings. |
| Configured independent membrane campus | 15,152.86 t | +5,282.30 t | Eight pods by hour 72 in worst N-2; tenth at hour 84.02. |
| Durable independent panel campus | 23,492.39 t | +13,621.83 t | Incompatible with current first-entry architecture. |
| Transformational independent target | 11,818.20 t | +1,947.64 t | Clears air ceiling; misses offshore ceiling by 37.20 t. |

The prepared-receiver case matters. Even after reusing eighty percent of safe occupied area, the configured pod weighs 12,632.94 tonnes. It fails the current mass ceiling because food, sanitation, energy and labor were also closed. “Use existing buildings” does not restore the hour-71 claim by itself.

### Inverting the mobility model

The most useful calculation starts with the arrival promise and solves backward for shelter mass.

Under hybrid food, configured sanitation, liquid energy and advanced-assisted labor, the 12,048.75-tonne air ceiling leaves only **441.56 tonnes** for the complete shelter system: 8.83 kg/person or 1.17 kg/m². That is below even the adaptive-reuse screen.

Under novel dense food, a 600-tonne sanitation target, liquid energy and transformational labor, the air ceiling permits **1,533.06 tonnes** of shelter: 30.66 kg/person or 4.07 kg/m². The offshore ceiling permits **1,265.31 tonnes**: 25.31 kg/person or 3.36 kg/m².

That yields four legitimate architectural responses:

- invent a complete occupied-area system inside the mass budget;
- enlarge or accelerate air and offshore cells;
- preposition prepared receiving capacity so it is not part of first-entry mass; or
- narrow the independent public-service contract and state the lost effect honestly.

The Department should fund all four as competing campaign solutions. It should not assume that one spectacular transport will compensate for a heavy, labor-intensive or incomplete service system.

## Technology programs pulled by the failure

Pass 29 creates eight linked programs. They are not independent gadgets; each owns a failed term in the service equation.

### `PCC-01` Ultra-light occupied-area system

The objective is a complete, accessible, climate-safe 50,000-person campus at or below roughly 24–30 kg/person depending on the movement path. Rival paths include tension membranes, airbeams, cellular panels, integrated inflated floors and receiving-site reuse. Bench evidence must include areal mass, fire behavior, toxicity, leakage, insulation, acoustics, privacy, repair and installation labor. Integrated evidence is a seventy-two-hour, environmentally stressed occupied trial.

### `PCC-02` Service-spine robotics

Autonomous reels, pipe/cable robots and self-identifying manifolds route and connect power, potable water, non-potable water, data and sealed residual lines. The metric is released district service per hour with leakage, fault isolation, exposure and recovery measured. Every system requires a manual degraded mode.

### `PCC-03` Dense public food system

The objective is not maximum caloric density in isolation. It is an acceptable, adaptable seventy-two-hour food service at or below 0.42 kg/person-day with bounded water, energy, cold-chain, allergen, waste and labor burdens. Representative publics—not only employees or soldiers—must participate in trials.

### `PCC-04` Closed sanitation lattice

Vacuum, dry-separation, membrane, oxidation and local-treatment pathways compete against a 600-tonne system target. The measured outputs are safe uses, water per use, pathogen control, odor, overflow probability, residual mass, service labor and environmental release under node loss.

### `PCC-05` Low-labor care and operations

Telehealth, resilient diagnostics, robotic handling, machine-readable custody and resident co-production compete at the task level. The success criterion is no more than 2,500 total people while preserving safety floors, clinical outcomes, accessibility, fatigue and manual continuity.

### `PCC-06` Portable service-energy mesh

Fuel-flex sources, storage, verified local harvest and compact reactors compete behind a common interface. Tests measure firm tonnes per megawatt-hour across duration, black-start time, protection, fault containment, emissions, heat rejection, fuel custody and controller common modes.

### `PCC-07` Cohort and service digital twin

The complex needs a privacy-preserving, disconnected ledger that answers who and which facility received which service, when, at what quality, through which dependency chain. It must expose missing and duplicate credit, reconcile to paper-compatible operations and preserve civil consent.

### `PCC-08` High-rate site preparation

Robotic grading, deployable floors, distributed anchors and drainage systems must create and later restore 376,000 m² at campaign speed. Measures include released area per hour, settlement, water management, accessibility, soil damage, strike time and restoration liability.

## The force and industrial consequences

### Population continuity becomes a formation family

The Resilience Force needs standing Population Continuity Groups rather than an incident-specific assembly of shelter, feeding and commodity contracts. A group generates certified `PCC-50` packages, trains their district teams, controls stocks and service data, maintains rival technical configurations, and deploys within Service Entry Cells.

Its permanent professions include public-service systems engineering, shelter structures, thermal and indoor-air engineering, mass feeding, nutrition, sanitation process operations, utility protection, accessibility, public health, safeguarding, logistics, privacy, civil liaison, repair and evidence science. These are not auxiliaries to transport. They are the operators of a primary national effect.

### A new prime-contractor ecology

A Lockheed-scale population-continuity industry would not be a tent manufacturer. It would integrate occupied-area systems, district service spines, utility islands, food and sanitation processes, controls, load/custody records, robotic deployment, repair and lifecycle support. Competition should be maintained at three levels:

- complete-campus integrators compete on protected person-hours per delivered tonne;
- subsystem firms compete behind stable power, fluid, data, cargo and evidence interfaces; and
- the Department retains government reference designs, data rights and independent proving authority so no prime becomes the sole source of truth.

Factories should produce both standing-force inventory and exportable civil variants. The export unit is a service contract with training, spares, test evidence and local interfaces—not a pile of shelters. Domestic normal-time use at heat refuges, temporary utility projects, migrant reception, public-health operations and planned infrastructure outages can create operating hours without redefining the force as a routine service substitute.

### Prepositioning becomes a technology choice

Prepared receiving sites can buy mass more efficiently than transport. Surveyed and drained land, embedded anchors, protected utility connection points, distributed storage, building inspection records and local workforce agreements can convert an independent campus into an adaptive-reuse or hybrid mission. But preparedness is a real asset class with maintenance, geographic exposure, political rights and common-mode risk. It must be budgeted and exercised like mobile inventory.

## Assurance and maturity

The model contains fourteen open gates: cohort consistency, complete-bundle delivery, shelter geometry, environmental envelope, thermal safety, utility deployment, food performance, sanitation closure, workforce, service energy, common modes, site creation, mode-compatible manifest and civil transfer.

The `PCC-50` is therefore `R2+`: its mission boundary, topology, major balances and contradiction are explicit; its physics and human performance are not closed. The workbook's twenty-five independent checks pass and no formula errors remain. Those facts validate the stated arithmetic, not the system.

Rendering remains blocked. The configuration has not earned stable exterior geometry. A credible future image must show the full district footprint, accessible routes, anchoring, drainage, kitchens, power islands, water and waste trunks, fuel or energy sources, repair spaces, staff flows, community distribution and civil control points. Drawing a beautiful module now would falsely freeze the least certain part of the design.

## What this pass proves—and what it does not

Pass 29 proves, under declared assumptions, that:

- the protected-campus geometry reconciles to 376,257 m²;
- the Pass 28 shelter mass screen is only 3.32 kg/m²;
- the configured independent membrane pathway is 3,545.67 tonnes;
- the selected campus water allocation remains below 2,000 m³/day;
- the selected sanitation system is 780 tonnes;
- the current, advanced and transformational rosters reconcile to 6,742, 4,513 and 2,694 people;
- the configured independent pod is 15,152.86 tonnes;
- the air and offshore third-pod ceilings are 12,048.75 and 11,781 tonnes;
- the configured independent case delivers eight pods by hour 72 in the worst two-cell-loss case and the tenth at hour 84.02; and
- a deliberately transformational 11,818.20-tonne target would numerically restore the air path but not independently validate any technology claim.

It does not prove that:

- the area factors are sufficient for every population and hazard;
- any shelter mass intensity is achievable;
- the thermal cases represent the tails of future compound heat, cold and smoke;
- the food pathways achieve health, cultural and acceptance outcomes;
- the water and sanitation assumptions close at full occupied scale;
- 2,500 people can safely operate the complex;
- the package fits aircraft, ships, roads, staging nodes or installation sequences;
- the six entry cells are probabilistically independent; or
- a jurisdiction will authorize and accept the complex.

## Next research decision

The next pass should not add another capability family. It should attack the largest recoverable term: **occupied-area mass and site creation as one system**. The work should configure one 1,000-person district through shell, floor, anchoring, thermal zones, accessible circulation, power, water, sanitation, furnishings, repair, packed volume, crew tasks and installation sequence. That district can then be multiplied only after its interfaces and failure boundaries close.

In parallel, the force-level alternative should be preserved: what air, offshore or prepositioning architecture would be required if the complete district refuses to become light enough? That keeps technology honest. The mission may drive a new material system, a new transport system, a new receiving-site system—or all three.
