# Programmatics

Programmatics asks what a warranted force design demands of capital, annual operations, workforce, readiness, industrial capacity, incident expenditure, recovery transfers and public value. These are different accounts and cannot be collapsed into a single impressive total.

[Force and resource modeling](force-and-resource-model.md) preserves the original Department-wide top-down screen and first $1 trillion constraint test. Later bottom-up force allocation supersedes its headcount and capital totals, but not its central discipline: no design may reconcile by moving personnel, maintenance, training, bases, stocks, replacement, test infrastructure or risk outside the boundary.

The direction of reasoning is binding: the public promise sizes the force; the force reveals the industrial and fiscal consequence. A budget may select among architectures or phase their creation, but it cannot silently redefine service closure.

Current figures are design-study scale hypotheses, not acquisition estimates or budget recommendations. The [model catalog](../../models/README.md) identifies the workbook that owns each quantitative claim and distinguishes nested from additive costs.

The [constitutional order model](../../models/programmatics/department-of-resilience-constitutional-order-and-national-power-state-machine.xlsx) remains the predecessor whole-department reconciliation: USD 1.00T technology, USD 17.4T mature capital, USD 40.2T ramped operations and USD 1.31T of separate useful-output market activity produced a USD 58.6T twenty-five-year public-outlay screen.

The current reconciliation is the [workforce, service-citizenship and reserve model](../../models/programmatics/department-of-resilience-workforce-service-citizenship-and-reserve-compact.xlsx). Inherited component headcounts and loaded billet-year factors yield a USD 1.36T/year people-and-household floor, USD 515B above the predecessor forty-percent people allocation. Preserving the predecessor USD 1.27T/year nonpeople readiness floor raises mature operations to USD 2.63T/year, ramped operations to USD 50.0T and twenty-five-year public outlay to USD 68.4T. A ±20-percent personnel-rate sensitivity yields USD 63.2–73.6T. These are constant-2026-dollar constitutional scale tests, not spending authority or wage forecasts.

The [domestic sovereignty and activation model](../../models/programmatics/department-of-resilience-domestic-public-service-sovereignty-and-activation-compact.xlsx) cross-classifies the receiver covenant inside that total. A 1–3 percent common-capital and 0.5–1.5 percent operating sensitivity produces USD 80–240B capital and USD 10.6–31.7B/year operations. Its base twenty-five-year exposure is about USD 592B. None is additive: the model asks how much of the inherited estate must carry civil sensing, receiver seams, a six-day local bridge, rights, compensation, exercises and handback.

The [canonical force architecture](../force/force-architecture.md) keeps the next accounting requirement explicit. Capital must be owned once by a complete formation product line or generating institution; standing operations, research and test, industrial readiness, and incident employment remain separate accounts. Further work must map those accounts to the formation registry rather than add top-down, technology, proving and system-specific envelopes.

The [carrier–node requirements and industrial program](../force/projection/carrier-node-requirements-and-industrial-program.md) is the first 25-year subsystem comparator derived from the canonical cargo burden. Its three cases span $5.222T–$9.693T in real 2026-dollar carrier-and-node resources. That range is not additive to the $15.179T canonical force screen and is not a Department total: carrier, base, common-system, research and operating boundaries overlap, while receiver, full formation, workforce and incident accounts remain incomplete. Its proper use is to value activation, distribution and node release and to expose the scale that the next unified work breakdown must reconcile.
