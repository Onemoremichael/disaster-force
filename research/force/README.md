# Force-design spine

The Resilience Force is easier to understand as four coupled machines than as a catalog of formations.

> generate capacity → move it through damage → receive and release it → sustain public service

The papers in this directory own successive decisions in that chain. Older results remain useful as model lineage, but later configuration work supersedes their headline arithmetic where noted.

## The canonical path

### 1. Generate a national force

[Mature force design](mature-force-design.md) defines the organizational grammar: operating forces, enabling forces, commands, authorities, bases and generation cycles. [Joint force allocation and order of battle](joint-force-allocation-and-order-of-battle.md) sizes the first Department-wide reference against three catastrophic, five major and two invited global campaigns plus immediate reserve.

These papers answer **what must exist before an event**. They do not yet prove that the formations fit through movement and destination constraints.

### 2. Project it through damaged networks

[Joint deployment and distribution](joint-deployment-and-distribution-enterprise.md) joins origin, strategic movement, reception, onward movement, responder basing, sustainment, reverse flow and regeneration. The [72-hour Service Entry Force](72-hour-service-entry-force.md) then creates six independently originated paths so the loss of any two cells does not automatically defeat first service entry.

These papers answer **how capacity reaches a region despite covariance**. Their continuous-mass timing is superseded by the indivisible configuration studies below.

### 3. Make the delivered increment complete

[Public-service pod configuration](service-entry-pod-configuration.md) defines `PSP-10` as the smallest indivisible package of command, access, base, water, food, shelter, sanitation, power, communications, health and hazard control. Ten complete pods—not fractional tonnes—close the reference effect contract.

The pod result is then compiled by [population continuity](../capabilities/population-continuity/README.md). `PCC-50` assigns services to explicit populations; `PCC-D1` configures one complete 1,000-person occupied district. That physical configuration supersedes the pod's earlier shelter allowance and breaks the mobile-only hour-72 screen.

### 4. Treat destination readiness as force capacity

[The National Receiving Lattice](national-receiving-lattice.md) makes fixed sites, surviving buildings, utility interfaces, stocks, records, inspection, labor and civil release a coequal operational force. Its balanced reference restores the aggregate hour-72 movement screen, but a clustered damaged-region campaign still fails.

This is the current frontier: **national capacity is not yet a time-feasible geographic allocation proof**.

## Supporting papers

[Force composition](force-composition.md) and [readiness and force generation](readiness-and-force-generation.md) retain the component, profession, availability and regeneration logic used by later models. They support the spine but no longer carry the most current force totals.

The campaign papers test the force against geography. The capability papers close the physical service systems. The architecture papers govern shared interfaces and rendering admission. The model catalog identifies the executable argument behind each decision.

## Current research decision

Advance the National Receiving Lattice only as an `R1/R2` reference with `R2+` arithmetic. Replace its synthetic regions with population-weighted multimodal catchments, preserve correlated receiver and route damage, and test dense local capacity against interregional transfer. In parallel, produce complete rival receiver reference designs and continue the mobile occupied-system technology program.

All eighteen NRL evidence gates remain open. No platform rendering is authorized.
