# The National Receiving Lattice

## A fixed–mobile force for service entry after catastrophe

> **Geography note — Pass 32:** this paper remains authoritative for receiver classes, fixed/mobile division and lattice assurance. [National Receiving Geography](national-receiving-geography.md) supersedes its equal-region allocation screen with thirty-two population-weighted catchments and corrects “receiver transfer” to lawful population relocation into surviving receivers.

The Department of Resilience cannot solve national disaster response by making every useful thing mobile. Nor can it count whatever buildings, warehouses, parking lots, hospitals, utilities and local staff happen to survive as free capacity. Both approaches hide the same failure: the force is sized at its origin while the public effect depends on a receiving system at the destination.

The **National Receiving Lattice (NRL)** is the permanent force that closes that gap. It is a continental and partner network of land, structures, utility interfaces, protected stocks, records, trained people, civil authority and alternate access. Its purpose is to convert arriving service formations into safe, lawful public service before their deadlines. It is not a registry of possible sites. It is generated, inspected, exercised and replaced as operational capacity.

This changes the idea of force projection. The operative weapon-system analogue is no longer a ship, aircraft or convoy. It is an origin-to-effect chain:

> certified mobile formation + surviving movement path + released receiving capacity + occupied service system = protected person-hours

If any term is absent, the Department has activity but not effect.

The first national configuration is deliberately ambitious. It posits 7,200 domestic certified-district equivalents of nominal receiving capacity—enough physical provision for 7.2 million residents—plus 720 equivalents in a territorial and invited-partner layer. After provisional readiness factors, loss of two independent domestic portfolios and a five-percent common-mode deduction, 3,707 equivalents remain against a 3,000-equivalent domestic requirement. The same test leaves only 372.8 partner equivalents against a requirement of 360. These are planning screens, not validated readiness.

The architecture solves one problem and exposes another. It closes the aggregate capacity and movement arithmetic. It does not close a geographically clustered campaign when eight receiving regions are damaged together. That adverse result is retained as a design control. A national total is not a time-feasible allocation proof.

## 1. Why reception is a force function

### 1.1 The destination owns the last conversion

A mobile pod can bring command, water, food, sanitation, power, communications, health, access and hazard-control equipment to a region. It still cannot safely occupy a thousand people without suitable ground or structure, drainage, anchoring, utility isolation, fire and environmental controls, inspection, local records, labor and civil release.

Pass 30 made the hidden burden visible. One complete transformational `PCC-D1` occupied district weighs 78.71 tonnes and occupies 446.60 cubic metres. Fifty districts inside one population-continuity pod raise that pod to 14,451.07 tonnes and push the tenth cargo completion to hour 81.52 after both upland entry cells are lost. The attractive hour-72 claim fails.

The NRL does not erase those requirements. It changes when and where they are carried. The selected first-order mix supplies an estimated 72.1 percent of district occupied-area mass through maintained fixed systems. The remaining mobile district averages 21.94 tonnes, bringing the pod to 11,612.76 tonnes. Under inherited movement assumptions, the tenth cargo completes at hour 70.05 by the faster surviving pair, leaving only 1.95 hours of margin. Cargo arrival remains distinct from connection, inspection and civil occupancy release.

This is therefore a coupled design optimization:

\[
\text{public effect} = f(\text{mobile completeness},\ \text{receiver survival},\ \text{connection time},\ \text{civil release})
\]

Reducing mobile mass increases dependence on the receiver. Hardening every receiver increases capital concentration, land burden and correlated exposure. Relying on ordinary buildings increases inspection uncertainty. The Department must choose and continuously test the balance rather than treat one side as an assumption.

### 1.2 A receiver is a readiness vector, not an address

An NRL site contributes zero event capacity unless it passes every required dimension:

| Readiness dimension | Operational question |
| --- | --- |
| Land and access | Does the Department possess lawful, physically usable access with at least one credible alternate route? |
| Structure and drainage | Can the site or building accept the declared occupancy, anchoring, wind, water, smoke, fire and aftershock cases? |
| Utilities | Can power, water, wastewater, thermal and data systems connect, island and disconnect without harming either network? |
| Stocks | Are complete, compatible and serviceable sets physically present rather than merely recorded? |
| People | Are activation, inspection, utility, logistics, command and civil-release teams available at the same time? |
| Inspection | Can post-event evaluation bound false-safe and false-denial errors quickly enough for the service deadline? |
| Records and data | Are current drawings, hazards, capacities, rights, configurations and maintenance histories available offline? |
| Authority | Can an accountable civil authority admit, refuse, limit and release occupancy with due process? |
| Fallback | What mobile or alternate-site path replaces the receiver when any preceding dimension fails? |

The workbook multiplies provisional fleet-planning factors to make uncertainty visible. Operations do not. An actual receiver either passes its complete event-specific vector or contributes no certified capacity. This distinction prevents portfolio averages from licensing an unsafe site.

Post-disaster building evaluation is already understood as a system of pre-event planning, trained evaluators, management, agreements, information, placards and quality assurance rather than an improvised walk-through. FEMA's P-2055 guidance supplies that institutional lower bound. It does not validate the NRL's site counts, automated inspection concepts or activation times.[^fema-p2055] NIST's community-resilience method likewise begins with social functions, maps them to buildings and infrastructure, and makes dependencies and recovery time explicit.[^nist-crpg]

## 2. Force architecture

### 2.1 Command relationships

The NRL should be a permanent operational command inside the Resilience Force, not a property office. Its commander generates certified receiving capacity for domestic and invited international campaigns. The command does not own public-health judgment, building-code authority or individual rights. Those remain with independent technical and civil authorities whose release decisions bind operations.

The mature architecture has four interacting levels:

1. **National Receiving Lattice Command** sets the service contract, allocates capacity across campaigns, maintains the common technical boundary and controls national reserves.
2. **Eight domestic assurance cells** own independent portfolios of sites, suppliers, software, depots, control paths and trained personnel. Each cell spans every domestic operating region. Loss of a region must not erase a cell; loss of a cell must not erase a region.
3. **Sixteen operating regions** manage geography, land, building and utility knowledge, exercises, route alternatives and local civil relationships. They are synthetic planning regions in this pass, not proposed administrative boundaries.
4. **Site detachments and mobile interface teams** maintain specific receivers, activate them, conduct repairs and connect incoming pods.

The partner and territorial layer uses six smaller assurance cells across twelve planning regions. Host invitation, sovereignty, standards, custody, environmental approval and alternate access are part of readiness, not diplomatic footnotes.

The two-dimensional organization is intentional. Geographic commands understand place; assurance cells preserve independent capacity through portfolio ownership. A purely regional system can lose all its local capacity to one hazard. A purely national pool can possess enough capacity in the wrong place.

### 2.2 Four receiving classes

The reference portfolio uses four physical classes. These are service roles, not architectural drawings.

| Class | Reference quantity | Nominal capacity | Activation screen | Fixed occupied-area share | Strategic role |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Hardened Regional Receiver Complex (`RRC`) | 48 | 3,840 CDE | 12 h | 80% | Large, deliberately engineered hubs with protected utilities, stocks, command, maintenance and alternate access. |
| Distributed Prepared Site (`DPS`) | 192 | 1,920 CDE | 18 h | 65% | Smaller repeatable sites that trade some hardening for geographic density and dispersed access. |
| Building Adapter Node (`BAN`) | 1,440 | 1,440 CDE | 24 h | 50% | Surveyed, instrumented and contractually prepared buildings that accept standardized service, environmental and occupancy kits after inspection. |
| Partner or Territorial Receiver (`PTR`) | 144 | 720 CDE | 24 h | 70% | Jointly governed nodes with host-specific standards, stocks, movement paths and custody rules. |

The reference domestic inventory is therefore 1,680 sites, not 1,680 identical facilities. Each operating region holds three `RRC`, twelve `DPS` and ninety `BAN` units in the synthetic equal-allocation screen. Real posture must be population-weighted, network-constrained and hazard-covariant.

The classes form a portfolio:

- `RRC` units carry dependable high-capacity service entry but create expensive concentrations.
- `DPS` units create practical depth along multiple movement corridors.
- `BAN` units turn the enormous national building stock into conditional capacity, but their credit is dominated by record quality, post-event inspection, owner agreements and utility isolation.
- `PTR` units extend the instrument beyond the continental network without pretending U.S. domestic authority transfers abroad.

The existence of a large public building portfolio demonstrates that national-scale asset management is administratively possible; it does not demonstrate disaster suitability. GSA reports more than 1,600 owned and more than 6,500 leased assets across over 355 million rentable square feet. Every NRL building still requires its own hazard, use, utility, access and authority proof.[^gsa-afr]

### 2.3 Building and utility passports

Every receiver has an authoritative, offline-capable passport. It is a controlled operational record rather than a real-estate database. The minimum record contains:

- parcel boundary, tenure, access rights, alternates and staging limits;
- as-built structure, modifications, fire protection, drainage and known vulnerabilities;
- hazard exposures and the joint-loss assumptions that place the site in an assurance cell;
- power topology, fault duty, grounding, isolation, black-start and accepted mobile interfaces;
- water source, pressure, storage, treatment, backflow, sampling and wastewater paths;
- thermal zones, ventilation, filtration, smoke isolation and verified occupancy envelopes;
- accessible routes, sanitary facilities, family, medical-observation and protected spaces;
- stock identity, maintenance, calibration, shelf life, custody and issue condition;
- trained teams, alternates, contact-independent activation procedures and civil authority; and
- last inspection, last exercise, unresolved defects and the explicit event states that deny credit.

The passport does not declare a site safe after an event. It allows an evaluator to compare a known configuration with observed damage, accelerates isolation and connection, and preserves evidence for a civil release decision. A digital twin that cannot operate without national cloud services is not a readiness system.

## 3. Assurance, geography and the clustered-demand failure

### 3.1 From nominal capacity to assured capacity

Nominal capacity is reduced in three steps.

First, each site class receives provisional planning factors for availability, hazard survival, inspection passage and interface compatibility. These produce 5,203.22 domestic readiness-adjusted CDE from 7,200 nominal CDE. The factors are not probabilities validated by data; they are controls awaiting parcel, building and exercise evidence.

Second, the force loses two of eight domestic assurance cells. Because every domestic cell carries the same 900 nominal CDE distributed across all sixteen regions, six surviving cells leave 3,902.42 readiness-adjusted CDE.

Third, a five-percent common-mode deduction reduces the result to 3,707.30 CDE. The remaining margin over the 3,000-CDE domestic contract is 707.30 CDE. The arithmetic fails when common-mode loss exceeds about 23.1 percent.

This is a more demanding definition of diversity. Eight logos do not create eight cells. Each cell needs materially independent depot, software, identity, inspection, supplier and command paths while conforming at common service boundaries. The design rule is:

> standardize the boundary; diversify the critical implementation

The military analogy is prepositioning as one leg of a strategic-mobility triad with airlift and sealift. GAO's review of defense prepositioned stocks also shows that joint policy and usable readiness information matter; inventory without integrated readiness is not strategic mobility.[^gao-preposition]

### 3.2 Catchments are time-dependent

Each of the sixteen synthetic domestic regions contributes 231.71 assured CDE after the N-2 and common-mode screen. In a single-catastrophe test, a home region at 50-percent survival, a near region at 80 percent and two outer regions at 90 percent produce 718.29 CDE against a 500-CDE campaign requirement. A three-region catchment produces only 509.75 CDE in the corrected model—nominally above the screen, but too dependent on a 9.75-CDE margin to serve as the design case. The four-region catchment is retained.

The national concurrency test is more revealing:

| Spatial case | Available capacity | Demand | Result |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Eight campaigns distributed across all sixteen regions | 3,707.30 CDE | 2,500 CDE | Closes aggregate screen |
| Eight campaigns clustered into eleven intact regions | 2,548.77 CDE | 2,500 CDE | Closes by only 48.77 CDE |
| Same cluster with eight affected regions at 50% capacity | 1,621.94 CDE | 2,500 CDE | Fails by 878.06 CDE |
| National corridor access to eight degraded and eight intact regions | 2,780.47 CDE | 2,500 CDE | Closes arithmetic; travel time open |

The failure defines the next decision. There are five legitimate responses:

1. build roughly 1.54 times the balanced capacity, about 11,100 nominal domestic CDE, so the damaged cluster closes locally;
2. create high-rate interregional receiver-transfer corridors and prove that intact distant capacity can be reached in time;
3. increase the mobile fraction so service can bypass failed receivers;
4. move people to surviving receivers while preserving accessibility, family unity, health and rights; or
5. narrow the early national service contract and state the unserved consequence explicitly.

No single answer should be selected by spreadsheet elegance. The reference architecture retains the balanced lattice and funds rival dense, corridor and mobile paths for test.

The United States Geological Survey's national seismic model illustrates why a receiving portfolio must be built from spatially varying hazard rather than state quotas.[^usgs-nshm] Earthquake is only one layer. Flood, surge, fire, smoke, wind, heat, drought, contamination, infrastructure dependence and access must be represented jointly. Correlation—not average exposure—governs assurance.

## 4. Operations and force generation

### 4.1 Activation sequence

The NRL begins before warning. Parcels, structures, interfaces, stocks, labor, access and authority remain under continuous configuration control. Activation is a release sequence:

1. campaign assignment and cell selection;
2. remote record check and local contact-independent alert;
3. physical access and perimeter control;
4. rapid structural, fire, environmental and utility inspection;
5. isolation, black start, water and wastewater protection, thermal-zone activation and data restoration;
6. stock issue, adapter staging and mobile-team arrival;
7. pod reception, connection, commissioning and joint technical acceptance;
8. independent civil occupancy release; and
9. continuous condition monitoring, repair, resupply and eventual demobilization.

The reference activation screens are twelve hours for `RRC`, eighteen for `DPS`, and twenty-four for `BAN` and `PTR`. Hour 54 is a provisional latest-ready screen when the connection and release path consumes eighteen hours. A site that opens at hour 24 but cannot lawfully accept residents until hour 80 has missed the mission.

### 4.2 Inspection and independent authority

The model separates delivery authority from safety and occupancy authority. Operators can repair and present a receiver. They cannot mark their own work safe, convert a probabilistic portfolio factor into site-level permission or waive civil rights to protect a deadline metric.

The permanent inspection system includes structural, fire, utility, environmental, accessibility, public-health and configuration disciplines. It trains with seeded damage, blind comparisons and independent quality assurance. It must measure both false-safe release and unnecessary denial. Autonomous sensing may prioritize work and compare observations to the passport; final authority remains bounded by tested action classes and accountable law.

### 4.3 Stocks and maintenance

The reference sites preposition about 322,320 tonnes of local stock: interface sets, distribution hardware, environmental controls, sanitation components, accessible furnishings, life-safety equipment, consumables, spares, tools and restraint systems. Stock credit depends on completeness and compatibility, not tonnage.

Each class follows a scheduled inspection and renewal cycle, with annual program review, rotating component samples, unannounced issue tests and full activation exercises. The force also maintains mobile substitutes for high-consequence fixed failures. This is more expensive than warehousing because it buys configuration integrity.

### 4.4 Workforce

The first-order establishment contains 59,832 permanent positions and 163,020 affiliated billets after force-generation depth and institutional overhead. The workforce is distinct from the personnel that operate the arriving population-continuity pods.

| Function | Permanent positions | Affiliated billets before overhead |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Site operations and maintenance | 25,632 | 56,390 |
| Regional and assurance-cell command | 4,800 | 14,400 |
| Inspection and engineering | 8,000 | 20,000 |
| Depots and inventory | 6,500 | 15,600 |
| Training and exercises | 4,500 | 9,000 |
| Data, passport and cyber | 2,800 | 6,160 |
| Civil occupancy authority | 2,600 | 6,500 |
| Science, test and acquisition | 3,000 | 4,800 |
| Land, environment and agreements | 2,000 | 3,000 |

The modeled domestic maximum campaign requires about 13,570 concurrent activation, inspection, utility, logistics, civil-release and command personnel. That total is not credible until occupations, qualifications, travel, fatigue, local conflicts and simultaneous private-sector demand are modeled. A nominal reserve engineer who is also restoring the commercial grid cannot be counted twice.

## 5. Industrial and fiscal scale

### 5.1 A resilience construction base

The NRL creates an industrial sector comparable in institutional significance—not necessarily in product form—to the defense industrial base. Its enduring products include hardened receiver complexes, repeatable prepared sites, building conversion kits, utility interfaces, sensing and inspection systems, strategic stocks, offline operational software and training ranges.

The design should avoid one national prime. The eight assurance cells create room for rival systems at common interfaces. The initial industrial screen requires at least four qualified prime families for the major site classes, six for building adapters and utility interfaces, and additional suppliers for stock sets. Provider-share limits, second sources, surge tooling, repair capacity and substitution trials are readiness requirements.

The product is not construction alone. It is a maintained service entitlement: a site with a current configuration, trained team, complete stocks, exercised interfaces and a lawful release path.

### 5.2 Planning envelope

The selected capital hypothesis is $563.808 billion in real 2026 dollars, including a twenty-percent reserve:

| Capital element | Planning value |
| --- | ---: |
| 48 hardened regional receiver complexes | $201.60B |
| 192 distributed prepared sites | $80.64B |
| 1,440 building adapter nodes | $25.92B |
| 144 partner and territorial receivers | $13.68B |
| Land, regional commands and depots, common standards, passports, test, initial stocks and industrial activation | $148.00B |
| Program reserve | $93.968B |
| **Total** | **$563.808B** |

Mature annual operations are $36.32 billion: $12.12 billion for site-class operations and $24.20 billion for command, inspection, stock rotation, exercises, data, access and industrial readiness. With a twenty-five-year build and operations ramp, the cumulative program screen is $1.138 trillion. Peak annual expenditure is $71.45 billion in 2036 under the declared profile.

These values are dimensional planning assumptions. They are not independent estimates, appropriations proposals or additions to the Department-wide total. Site acquisition, civil works, utility reinforcement, regional labor, environmental mitigation, stock consumption, recapitalization and program interaction can move them materially. The value of the model is that those uncertainties now have named accounts.

## 6. Rival architectures and the technology program

The balanced lattice is a reference, not a foregone conclusion.

| Rival | Strategic advantage | Governing failure or unknown |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mobile-only breakthrough | Minimizes fixed exposure and host dependence | The complete district produces a 14,451-t pod and misses hour 72 under current movement assumptions. |
| Hardened-hub network | High fixed share and fast activation | Concentrates capital, people and correlated hazard risk; damaged-cluster capacity still fails. |
| Building-first network | Uses existing structures and can scale numerically | Inspection, records, owner access, utilities and N-2 assurance dominate; aggregate assured capacity fails in the reference screen. |
| Interregional corridor lattice | Uses intact national capacity instead of duplicating it locally | Must prove damaged-route travel, scheduling, fuel, custody and relocation under clustered demand. |
| Dense balanced lattice | Closes the modeled damaged cluster locally | Requires about 11,100 nominal domestic CDE with much greater cost, land and maintenance burden. |

The NRL technology portfolio is not a list of gadgets. Each program owns a measured system failure:

- **Hazard-covariant siting:** optimize portfolios against joint hazard and infrastructure loss, adversarially test the assumed independence, and value capacity by reachable surviving CDE per dollar and travel-hour.
- **Rapid structural and utility state estimation:** combine permanent instrumentation, robotics and inspection methods to reduce false-safe risk and release time.
- **Universal service interfaces:** create protected power, water, wastewater, thermal, data and mechanical boundaries that accept diverse implementations without creating a common-mode national defect.
- **Adaptive occupied systems:** move more floor, envelope, air quality and accessibility into light, reusable mobile kits when fixed structures fail.
- **Autonomous activation and repair:** reduce scarce labor in survey, stock handling, connection, cleaning, inspection support and repetitive construction while counting remote and maintenance labor.
- **Offline operational passports:** preserve authoritative configuration, identity, rights and local control through loss of cloud services and backhaul.
- **Receiver-transfer corridors:** create modular rail, road, waterway and austere movement systems able to connect distant intact capacity to clustered demand.
- **Dynamic civil service release:** test machine-assisted evidence packages, bounded automated actions and rapid accountable appeals without weakening occupancy safety.

The programs compete at complete mission threads. A faster sensor does not earn capacity if inspectors, utility isolation or civil release remain binding. A universal connector that makes every site vulnerable to the same fault is a regression.

## 7. Program sequence

The twenty-five-year build should proceed through evidence, not through early architectural lock-in.

**Years 1–5: reference designs and destructive knowledge.** Establish Lattice Command, independent release authority, assurance-cell boundaries and the passport schema. Survey representative parcels and buildings. Build rival `RRC`, `DPS` and `BAN` prototypes. Conduct compound-hazard, utility-fault, disconnected-operation and occupied-service trials. No national readiness credit is awarded.

**Years 6–10: regional force experiments.** Field complete competing cells in several different hazard and infrastructure contexts. Exercise simultaneous activation with missing staff, denied routes, stale records, damaged buildings and recalled components. Measure reachable service by deadline. Select multiple implementations at common boundaries.

**Years 11–15: national initial capability.** Expand all eight assurance cells across the sixteen-region posture. Build strategic stocks, depots, schools and transfer corridors. Demonstrate N-2 cell loss and at least one geographically clustered multi-campaign allocation. Partner nodes proceed only through joint evidence and consent.

**Years 16–25: full posture, regeneration and recapitalization.** Complete the reference inventory or the better-performing rival architecture. Exercise national concurrency, rotate stocks, replace obsolete systems, preserve second sources and revise posture as population, hazard and infrastructure change.

The acquisition unit is a certified slice of service capacity with a generation system, not a construction contract. Payment and readiness reporting should follow complete passed capacity, time-to-release, duration and regeneration—not buildings opened or equipment purchased.

## 8. Decision and research frontier

Pass 31 supports four decisions.

First, establish the National Receiving Lattice as a coequal force element. Reception is too consequential to remain a local assumption and too place-dependent to be carried entirely from origin.

Second, adopt the balanced four-class lattice as the reference architecture for testing, not procurement. Its 7,200 domestic nominal CDE and 72.1-percent fixed district share close the current national arithmetic and preserve a small hour-72 movement margin.

Third, deny national closure credit. The clustered damaged-region case fails by 878 CDE. The force must prove a dense lattice, interregional transfer, greater mobility, lawful relocation, a changed contract or a composite of them.

Fourth, create the profession and industrial base early. Readiness comes from maintained records, inspection, interfaces, stock rotation, exercises, labor and independent authority. Those systems cannot be added after concrete is poured.

Pass 32 performs the first population-weighted, multimodal allocation screen in [National Receiving Geography](national-receiving-geography.md). It finds that only 2,355.80 CDE survive against 2,500 demanded and that baseline corridors serve 2,066.42 CDE. The next pass must replace screening paths with actual networks, add population subgroup and accessibility demand, and fit joint receiver/route damage. In parallel, representative `RRC`, `DPS` and `BAN` reference designs should close structure, utilities, occupancy, maintenance and lifecycle accounts.

The NRL remains `R1/R2` with `R2+` arithmetic. All eighteen evidence gates remain open. No receiver, building conversion, vehicle or formation is authorized for rendering.
