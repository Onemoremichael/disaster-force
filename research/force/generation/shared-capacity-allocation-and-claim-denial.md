# Shared Capacity Allocation and Claim Denial

## Making a coequal resilience force physically honest

### Research state

Pass 60 converts the shared-capacity deductions in the [canonical force allocation](canonical-force-allocation-and-fiscal-reconciliation.md) into an explicit national claims problem. It is a planning and governance study, not an operational plan, acquisition baseline, optimization policy or validated logistics estimate. Its purpose is to determine what must be true before one formation may count people, capital, movement, basing or support supplied by another object.

The accompanying [`shared-capacity-allocation-ledger.xlsx`](../../../models/force/generation/shared-capacity-allocation-ledger.xlsx) contains the arithmetic authority: 480 formation–campaign demand records, 100 serialized operational claims, workforce and capital credit-control ledgers, sensitivity controls and twenty-one integrity tests. The serialization proves conservation and denial. Its order is deliberately not a recommendation about which communities or missions should receive scarce service.

Pass 58's national totals remain held. Pass 60 does not yet replace them because the source inventories are not item-identified and the canonical transport requirement has not been rebuilt. It establishes the accounting and command architecture that a lawful replacement will require.

## Executive judgment

A mature Department of Resilience cannot use “shared” as a synonym for “not carried on this formation's books.” Capacity is shareable only if a named source can satisfy a named claim, in the required configuration, at the required place and time, under the required authority, without violating an earlier obligation or the reserve needed for the next catastrophe.

That proposition changes the force design.

The [canonical force allocation](canonical-force-allocation-and-fiscal-reconciliation.md) deducted $2.268 trillion of pre-reserve capital and 915,209 active non-Household-Collection affiliated billets on the theory that another line supplied them. Pass 59 then identified 519,644 additional supporting affiliations associated with the Household Collection Group screen, including about 207,858 simultaneously on duty. Those deductions prevented obvious double purchase, but they did not identify the people, equipment, site, transport, release authority or competing obligation that would make the claimed service real.

Pass 60 places those claims against the largest available source ledgers. Its findings are decisive:

| Question | Pass 60 result | Decision consequence |
|---|---:|---|
| Simultaneous responders requiring physical basing | 2,011,407 people | Basing follows gross deployed people, not a net workforce accounting line |
| Effective base capacity | 1,482,624 people | 73.7% coverage; 528,783 responder places remain absent |
| Early mass after 25% prepositioning | 6,677,140 t | Canonical entry burden remains an order-of-magnitude force problem |
| Effective 72-hour lift | 2,462,931 t | 36.6% coverage; 4,214,209 t remain outside the inherited entry system |
| Daily sustainment demand | 173,895 t/day | Passes the inherited aggregate screen, but only under incomplete commodity and distribution assumptions |
| Active non-HCG shared-affiliate credits | 915,209 billets | Only 198,198 have a quantified common source; 717,011 remain unallocated |
| HCG supporting workforce screen | 519,644 affiliations | Remains separate and potentially overlapping; it cannot be added or deducted yet |
| Capital credits | $2.268T pre-reserve | Most have coarse accounting ceilings, not item or operational allocation; $240B lacks even a named source ceiling |

The correction is conceptually important. An accounting credit may remove duplicate ownership, but it does not remove a responder from a damaged theater, a tonne from an origin, a bed from a base, a licensed shift from a plant or a reservation from a carrier. **Net fiscal ownership and gross operational burden are different ledgers.** The Department must govern both.

The proper institutional response is a **Joint Capacity Authority** operating a configuration-controlled capacity graph. Generating services attest what exists and its readiness. Joint commands state public-service demand. Technical authorities govern safe release. Civil authorities set public priority and rights constraints. The Capacity Authority binds source to claim, protects reserve, denies conflicts and propagates the consequence of denial. Independent assurance tests whether the record describes physical reality. No single actor should collapse these powers.

This is not clerical machinery. It is one of the defining command systems of a coequal resilience force. Defense institutions invest in command, control, logistics, mobilization and readiness because platforms without governed allocation do not make military power. A Department of Resilience would require an equally serious civil capacity system because nominal desalination, hospital, lift, linework or shelter capacity that is promised twice is not power.

## 1. The object being governed

### 1.1 Capacity is a bounded promise

A source capacity object is not merely an asset or workforce total. It is a bounded promise with at least these coordinates:

\[
K = (i, e, q, c, l, t, a, r, d),
\]

where:

- `i` is a persistent identity;
- `e` is the public effect or service produced;
- `q` is the quantity and unit;
- `c` is the configuration, including required complements and interfaces;
- `l` is present location and reachable geography;
- `t` is the availability interval, activation latency and endurance;
- `a` is the release and employment authority;
- `r` is readiness, including degraded modes and correlated hazards; and
- `d` is the regeneration debt created by use.

A claim has the same grammar. It must identify the effect, quantity, configuration, destination, deadline, duration, authority and acceptable substitution. A match exists only when the source can meet every binding coordinate. Capacity that is “available nationally” but cannot reach the claimant before the service deadline has zero operational value for that claim. Capacity that arrives without a compatible interface, licensed crew, receiving site or lawful release also has zero value.

This definition is stricter than ordinary resource accounting because disaster service is serial. Water production does not substitute for safe household water if conveyance, pressure, testing or distribution is absent. A railcar does not substitute for civil mobility if the transfer node, accessible operations, route, destination admission or return obligation is absent. A medical module does not substitute for care if staffing, patient regulation, oxygen, power, waste or referral is missing.

### 1.2 Six capacity classes behave differently

The allocation kernel cannot treat every source as a divisible commodity.

| Capacity class | Allocation rule | Typical failure if treated as generic quantity |
|---|---|---|
| Divisible flow | Allocate rate over a stated interval, with network and storage limits | Megawatts or water volume counted without a delivered path |
| Indivisible module | Reserve the complete package; partial inventory earns no service credit | Half a treatment train or incomplete clinical unit counted proportionally |
| Prepared site | Reserve inspected, released, connected capacity at a named place | Nominal beds or district packages survive on paper after the site fails |
| Licensed labor | Bind a qualified person or crew, shift, location and relief path | One worker appears in multiple formations or home-station duties |
| Authority-bound service | Bind custody, legal authority and information access as well as technical capacity | Data, clinical receiving or utility operation exists but cannot lawfully be used |
| Serial work queue | Allocate production or restoration slots through time, including setup and regeneration | A depot, airfield or route-repair unit is promised to simultaneous jobs |

The classes can be nested. A power-restoration claim may require an indivisible generation-and-protection module, licensed shifts, a prepared connection site, a fuel or renewable flow and a serial commissioning queue. The service receives no complete credit when one indispensable object is absent.

### 1.3 Four ledgers are required

One inventory column cannot represent the force.

1. The **availability ledger** records what physically exists, its configuration, present state, location, endurance and readiness evidence.
2. The **obligation ledger** records standing public guarantees, campaign demands, home-station duties, treaty or partner commitments, maintenance windows and regeneration needs.
3. The **reservation ledger** binds capacity to obligations across time and geography and records protected residual reserve.
4. The **ownership ledger** records capital, workforce, lifecycle and budget responsibility so the Department neither buys the same object twice nor leaves it institutionally orphaned.

The ownership ledger can legitimately show one object supporting many formation designs over its life. The reservation ledger cannot show that object satisfying incompatible simultaneous claims. Conversely, removing an object from a formation's ownership ledger does not remove the formation's operational dependence on it. Pass 58 used the ownership distinction; Pass 60 adds the reservation distinction.

## 2. Conservation rules for a credible force

### 2.1 No double claim

For source `s` over any binding interval and configuration state, admitted allocations cannot exceed effective available capacity:

\[
\sum_f x_{s,f,l,t,c} \leq A_{s,l,t,c} - R_{s,l,t,c},
\]

where `x` is the admitted allocation and `R` is the protected reserve. The workbook includes a deliberately small unit test: one unit of capacity faces two overlapping claims of one unit. The first serialized claim receives one; the second receives zero. The result passes.

This is elementary arithmetic and profound institutional doctrine. A force that cannot deny the second claim has no reliable readiness measure.

### 2.2 No time-overlap reuse

The same asset may support several campaigns sequentially only after travel, employment, decontamination, repair, crew rest, replenishment and return to readiness. A nominal end time is insufficient. The reservation interval must include activation and regeneration tails:

\[
T_{reserved}=T_{activate}+T_{move}+T_{employ}+T_{recover}+T_{regenerate}.
\]

This is especially important for disasters because a second event can occur while the first force is still dispersed, contaminated, depleted or politically committed. Protected reserve is therefore an explicit claimant, not unused inventory waiting to be optimized away.

### 2.3 No substitution without proof

Two capacities are substitutes only if they produce the same required public effect under the claimant's conditions. Trucks cannot automatically replace rail, water or air lift: their payload, route survival, loading systems, crew cycle, fuel, bridge limits, discharge nodes and deadline differ. A megawatt of generation cannot automatically replace a megawatt of delivered protected load. A general volunteer cannot replace a licensed plant operator or clinical team.

The ledger should record a substitution set and its evidence rather than allow an optimizer to infer equivalence from a common unit.

### 2.4 No partial readiness for an indivisible service

If a service package requires complements `j`, credited service is bounded by the weakest complement:

\[
S_f \leq \min_j \left(\frac{x_{f,j}}{k_{f,j}}\right).
\]

Ten of twelve required items do not create five-sixths of an operational package when the missing two are safety or interface gates. Partial items remain valuable inventory, but they do not receive formation-readiness credit until the service chain closes.

### 2.5 No workforce credit without an identity and release path

A workforce pool requires persistent roster identity, qualification, currency, home-station duty, employer release, travel time, shift assignment, relief, family-support conditions and post-deployment recovery. “Industrial partner” or “state support” is not a source. A person cannot be simultaneously assigned to a utility, a contractor production line, a reserve formation and a local response role merely because each institution carries a separate roster.

The same principle protects the civil economy. Mobilization that closes a response formation while silently disabling the utility, hospital, railroad or supplier the force depends on is a transfer of failure, not added capacity.

### 2.6 No capital credit without a source object

Capital credits require item or work-breakdown identity, quantity, useful configuration, location, readiness ownership and replacement responsibility. A coarse ceiling can prevent an obvious accounting duplication, but it cannot show which formation receives the capacity.

Pass 60 preserves the $2.268 trillion Pass 58 pre-reserve credit ledger as a claim register, not an admitted allocation. One claim is more severe: the $240 billion pre-reserve Civil Rail Mobility rolling-stock credit—$288 billion after the program reserve—has no named source ceiling in the canonical model. It must be quarantined until a source inventory is created.

### 2.7 Denial must propagate to public consequence

When a claim fails, the system must not merely report an equipment shortage. It should identify the formation, campaign, service, population, time window and downstream commitments affected. The commander and civil authority then see the actual choice: delay water to one district, accept a smaller protected population, move a reserve, invoke a substitution with known risk, or refuse a new commitment.

The denied-service record is also an accountability instrument. It makes structural scarcity visible instead of converting it into unexplained lateness at the household.

## 3. The first national claims ledger

### 3.1 Method and boundary

The workbook expands the forty-eight canonical families across ten simultaneous campaign packages, producing 480 formation–campaign records. It then aggregates claims against ten operational pools:

- responder basing;
- total early strategic movement;
- air, ocean, land/rail and inland-water early movement;
- daily sustainment throughput;
- prepared district packages;
- route-assurance detachments;
- temporary power blocks; and
- temporary water islands.

The last four pools are tested against the Pass 59 HCG external-service screen. Four additional pools—disconnected civil information, maintenance and regeneration, clinical receiving and licensed labor—are represented in the schema but remain explicitly **unquantified**. Unquantified is not zero. It means the model cannot yet compute a truthful coverage ratio.

The 100 campaign-pool claims are serialized in a fixed computational order to test conservation and denial. This ordering does not encode public value, legal priority, vulnerability or campaign command judgment. Systemwide aggregate coverage is invariant to claim order; which claimant is denied is not. A lawful policy layer must precede discretionary optimization.

### 3.2 Gross people govern physical support

The most important correction concerns personnel. Shared-workforce credits reduced Pass 58's accounting total, but the credited people still deploy if they are to provide service. Basing, food, water, sanitation, clinical support, transport and force protection must therefore be sized from gross simultaneous on-duty claims.

Across the ten campaign packages, the workbook derives 2,011,407 responder places. The inherited effective base ledger supplies 1,482,624. Coverage is 73.7 percent, leaving 528,783 responder places absent.

The result is not an estimate of a final Department population. It is a consistency test using the canonical package and inherited base assumptions. It invalidates any method that calculates theater support from net budgeted billets after shared credits. The governing rule is:

> Use net ownership to prevent duplicate institutional cost; use gross on-duty demand to size the physical campaign.

This distinction will materially influence every future base, camp, sanitation, feeding, health, transport and regeneration model.

### 3.3 Canonical entry remains deeply deficient

After the inherited 25-percent prepositioning assumption, the ten campaigns present 6,677,140 tonnes of early movement demand. The inherited 72-hour deployment enterprise supplies 2,462,931 effective tonnes, for 36.6-percent aggregate coverage and a 4,214,209-tonne shortfall.

The first mode screen is:

| Mode | Declared demand | Effective 72-hour capacity | Coverage |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Air | 585,931 t | 160,881 t | 27.5% |
| Ocean | 1,971,681 t | 596,400 t | 30.2% |
| Land and rail | 3,618,692 t | 1,183,200 t | 32.7% |
| Inland water | 500,836 t | 522,450 t | 104.3% |
| **Total** | **6,677,140 t** | **2,462,931 t** | **36.6%** |

These mode shares are routing hypotheses introduced to expose incompatibility, not an approved movement plan. They do not yet model origin geography, asset type, hazardous compatibility, loading and discharge systems, route damage, queueing, crew cycle or last-mile delivery. Sensitivity testing confirms that changing a campaign's air/ocean shares conserves its total mass and recalculates the pool claims correctly.

The apparent inland-water surplus does not cancel deficits in other modes. Capacity can cross modes only through a feasible route and compatible cargo chain. The next deployment model must start from item-identified cargo and origin–destination paths rather than divide aggregate tonnes by nominal mode shares.

### 3.4 Sustainment passes only as an aggregate

The canonical force claims 173,895 tonnes per day of formation sustainment against 501,228 tonnes per day of inherited effective throughput. The 288.2-percent ratio passes the aggregate test.

That result is weaker than it looks. The demand side omits or coarsely represents public commodities, fuel pathways, treatment residuals, medical replenishment, construction consumables, spare parts, reverse logistics and displaced-population support. The capacity side does not close damaged-theater distribution, compatible handling, storage, security, delivery to formations or follow-on event reserve. The pass therefore says only that the inherited top-down allowance is not contradicted by the current narrow formation supply coefficients. It does not certify sustainment.

### 3.5 The HCG support pools pass arithmetic, not service

The Pass 59 HCG screen requires, across simultaneous demand:

| External service | Direct claim | Effective planning pool | Aggregate result |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Prepared district packages | 2,346 | 2,815.5 | Pass |
| Route-assurance detachments | 483 | 579.6 | Pass |
| Temporary power blocks | 690 | 828.1 | Pass |
| Temporary water islands | 621 | 745.5 | Pass |

These are planning allocations derived from national inventory hypotheses. They have no item identity, home location, travel time, damage covariance, inspection status, release authority or competing claims outside the HCG ledger. A district package in the wrong city or a water island without a compatible source and distribution path cannot satisfy the service.

Accordingly, the result does not admit the Pass 59 credits. It identifies a tractable proving experiment: place two HCG demands against the same item-identified district, route, power and water pools; apply travel, release, failure and reserve; and require the second claim to be denied or met by a distinct source.

## 4. What the credit audit reveals

### 4.1 Workforce credits are mostly institutionally unowned

Pass 58 carried 915,209 active non-HCG shared-affiliate credits. The only quantified common workforce source is the National Utility Projection System at 198,197.55 affiliations. Even if every NUPS affiliation were compatible and available—which has not been shown—717,011.45 active credits remain without a quantified source.

The workbook sensitivity control expands the NUPS source to 491,304. Under that hypothetical all four NUPS-linked claims pass and the unallocated active credits fall to 423,905. Restoring the input restores the baseline result. This is a model-integrity test, not a proposed workforce increase.

The HCG support screen remains separate. Its 519,644 supporting affiliations and 207,858 simultaneous on-duty positions may overlap with NUPS, route, local, contractor or other pools already counted. Adding them would risk duplication; deducting them would risk erasure. They must remain quarantined until the role-level roster and obligation resolver can test identities and time conflicts.

The legacy Pass 58 HCG credit of 870,400 is superseded by the Pass 59 role-based configuration and must not be used in a new total.

### 4.2 Capital credits are ceilings, not allocations

The capital ledger performs a useful but limited task. It prevents the same broad common program from being purchased wholesale inside several formation lines. Most source values are therefore accounting ceilings: NUPS, bases and depots, common information, strategic movement or a broad mobility enterprise.

They do not yet answer the operational question. If Water Restoration, Power Restoration and Grid Bridge each deduct capital associated with NUPS, which treatment trains, generation blocks, switching modules, vehicles, crews and spares are assigned to each formation and campaign? What remains for independent utility missions? What happens when a common component is unavailable? Who funds recapitalization after one claimant consumes life or damages the asset?

Until those questions are answered, credits should be classified as:

- **supported**: an item-identified source and nonconflicting reservation exist;
- **bounded**: a coarse source ceiling exists but no item allocation;
- **unsupported**: no source inventory or authority exists;
- **conflicted**: the same source is reserved to incompatible obligations; or
- **superseded**: later configuration has changed the claim basis.

None of the present capital deductions reaches supported status. The Civil Rail Mobility credit is unsupported; most others are bounded. Quarantine preserves the information without allowing it to certify force affordability or readiness.

## 5. The Joint Capacity Authority

### 5.1 Why this must be a national command function

The problem is not procurement bookkeeping. Shared capacity crosses generating services, operational commands, jurisdictions, contractors, utilities, licensed professions and international commitments. It can determine which population receives power, water, evacuation, clinical care or hazard control. That makes allocation both a command act and a public-governance act.

The Department should establish a Joint Capacity Authority with four duties:

1. maintain the authoritative capacity, obligation and reservation graph;
2. enforce conservation, compatibility, protected reserve and regeneration rules;
3. expose conflicts and denied-service consequences to lawful decision-makers; and
4. preserve an auditable record from public guarantee through source allocation and civil transfer.

The Authority should not independently decide public priority, certify technical safety, declare a formation ready and audit its own decision. Those powers belong to different actors.

### 5.2 A deliberately divided authority structure

| Actor | Binding responsibility | Power it should not hold alone |
|---|---|---|
| Civil leadership | Set public-service floors, rights rules, geographic priority and invited international commitments | Declare technical readiness or conceal denied service |
| Generating service | Attest configuration, ownership, personnel, maintenance and readiness evidence | Reserve its capacity to favored missions outside joint priority |
| Joint Capacity Authority | Match source to claim, preserve reserve, deny conflicts and maintain the ledger | Redefine the public guarantee or waive safety |
| Joint theater command | State campaign demand, sequence operations and employ admitted capacity | Manufacture readiness by changing the record |
| Technical release authority | Approve safe operating envelope, interfaces, licenses and degraded modes | Allocate public priority |
| Independent assurance | Reproduce balances, test controls, investigate divergence and report maturity | Operate or self-certify the force |

This separation is essential to democratic legitimacy. Scarcity decisions should be visible, reviewable and tied to stated public-service rules. Technical refusal should remain possible where unsafe use would create a second catastrophe. Operational urgency should not silently erase protected reserve or future regeneration.

### 5.3 Reservation phases

Allocation should occur in four ordered phases:

1. **Quarantine.** Exclude unverified, damaged, incompatible, legally unavailable, maintenance-bound or unreachable capacity.
2. **Guarantee floors.** Reserve capacity for explicit minimum public-service levels, home-station continuity, safety complements and the next-event floor.
3. **Complete service claims.** Assemble indivisible chains; do not spend scarce complements on fragments that cannot produce the promised effect.
4. **Discretionary optimization.** Allocate remaining capacity against declared objectives, uncertainty and appeal rules.

Optimization belongs last. A mathematically efficient allocation that violates rights, consumes the follow-on reserve or distributes incomplete packages is institutional failure.

### 5.4 The operating cycle

The capacity system should run continuously, not activate after impact:

```text
REGISTER → ATTEST → QUARANTINE → RESERVE → RELEASE → EMPLOY
    ↑                                                ↓
REGENERATE ← RECONCILE ← OBSERVE EFFECT ← DENY / REALLOCATE
```

Registration gives each asset, crew, site and service package identity. Attestation binds evidence and authority. Quarantine removes capacity that cannot lawfully or physically serve. Reservation creates time-bound obligations. Release transfers operational control within technical limits. Employment observations update consumption and service. Denial or reallocation propagates scarcity. Reconciliation compares the record with reality. Regeneration restores the source before it can be promised again.

The system must operate disconnected. Catastrophe may destroy networks precisely when obligations change fastest. Every reservation therefore needs a versioned authority token, bounded local decision rights, conflict rules and later reconciliation without assuming continuous cloud access.

## 6. A technology portfolio worthy of the mission

The capacity problem should drive novel technology as aggressively as military logistics and command problems have done. The objective is not a prettier dashboard. It is to create forms of civil power that do not currently exist.

### 6.1 Configuration-controlled capacity graph

The Department needs a persistent graph connecting public effects to formation packages, people, assets, software, sites, stocks, interfaces, suppliers, authorities, routes, maintenance and regeneration. Every node requires identity and evidence state; every edge requires compatibility and time semantics. The graph must answer both directions: which sources support this household service, and which public guarantees fail if this source disappears?

This requires common identifiers and data rights from acquisition onward. A vendor should not be able to deliver a billion-dollar system whose configuration, maintenance state, interface dependencies or software authority cannot enter the national ledger.

### 6.2 Offline reservation and denial protocol

The force needs a civil equivalent of contested command and control: authenticated low-bandwidth reservations, bounded delegation, conflict detection, store-and-forward updates and deterministic reconciliation after partitions. A local command may need authority to reserve a power block or medical lift while disconnected, but the protocol must expose the risk that another partition made the same claim.

Research questions include conflict-free versus authority-prioritized data structures, cryptographic custody without public surveillance, graceful expiry, human-readable denial, and operation under compromised devices or clocks.

### 6.3 Mission-aware common identifier

Asset identifiers should persist through ownership transfer, containerization, maintenance, software update and component replacement. Personnel identities must protect privacy while resolving conflicting qualifications and assignments. Site identities must connect inspection, access, utility interfaces and hazard state. Claim identities must persist from demand through denial, substitution, employment and civil effect.

The hard problem is not labeling. It is preserving enough relational meaning to prevent double use without building an unrestricted population or workforce surveillance system.

### 6.4 Route, latency and configuration engine

Allocation must know whether capacity can become service before the deadline. That requires damage-aware multimodal paths, loading and discharge compatibility, queueing, crew cycles, site release, construction time, weather, fuel, security and last-mile distribution. The engine should reason over intervals and conditional paths rather than use a single distance or payload factor.

The next canonical movement model should begin here. Its fundamental record is not “one tonne moved.” It is an item or complete package, at an origin, entering a compatible mode chain, reaching a released receiver by a service deadline.

### 6.5 Covariance and follow-on hazard engine

Nominal inventory is not available inventory when common bases, suppliers, networks, crews or geographies fail together. The system needs a living dependency model that can withhold readiness credit for correlated assets and preserve reserve across plausible follow-on events. This is not reducible to one universal haircut. Dependency must be explicit enough to show which capacities fail together and why.

### 6.6 Explainable allocation and appeals

Once guarantee floors and complete-service rules are enforced, an optimizer can explore remaining allocations across lives protected, time to service, vulnerability, restoration leverage, geographic equity, future risk and regeneration burden. The result must remain explainable to operators, civil leadership and affected communities. Every denial should expose the binding shortage and the rule applied. Appeals and overrides should create an immutable rationale, not silently rewrite history.

### 6.7 Digital evidence twin

The Department should maintain a linked evidence twin for every claimed capacity: model assumption, test result, field observation, configuration version, reliability state, known limitation and authority. When an asset or algorithm changes, the system should know which readiness conclusions no longer follow. This would turn acquisition data rights, test instrumentation and maintenance records into operational power rather than archival compliance.

## 7. The proving program

The allocation system should be tested adversarially before it is trusted. At minimum, the next program should include:

| Trial | What must be demonstrated |
|---|---|
| Duplicate claim | A second overlapping claim is denied and its public consequence appears immediately |
| Two-HCG competition | Item-identified district, route, power and water pools support two simultaneous demands without hidden reuse |
| Cross-mode incompatibility | An apparent surplus in one mode is refused when cargo, route, terminal or deadline makes substitution impossible |
| Base loss | Gross responder demand is reallocated after a common base failure without using net workforce credits as physical capacity |
| Disconnected command | Partitioned reservations reconcile deterministically; conflicts remain visible and accountable |
| Workforce conflict | One licensed person on several rosters is counted once, with home-station and employer-release consequences |
| Contractor nonperformance | A certified industrial source becomes unavailable and dependent formations lose readiness automatically |
| Follow-on catastrophe | Protected reserve survives political pressure from the first event and can open the second campaign |
| Regeneration debt | Used assets and crews remain unavailable until inspected, replenished, repaired and rested |
| Independent reproduction | A separate assurance team reconstructs every admitted and denied claim from canonical records |

The pass criteria must include more than arithmetic. The system should survive network loss, hostile data, stale readiness, clock disagreement, reconfiguration, partial authority, privacy review and human overload. Operators must be able to understand why the machine denied service and what action could close the gap.

## 8. Strategic and economic consequence

### 8.1 Capacity truth changes acquisition

An item-identified shared ledger reveals where modularity genuinely creates leverage and where apparent commonality creates a single point of failure. It can support families of interoperable power, water, medical, communication, mobility and construction modules while still requiring distinct complements and safety cases. It can also create demand for standardized civil interfaces in ports, rail terminals, utility substations, buildings and receiving sites.

That demand is industrial policy. Persistent identifiers, configuration data, open interfaces, depot rights, repair tooling, workforce credentials and surge obligations become parts of the product. Companies at Lockheed Martin scale would compete not only to build spectacular platforms but to deliver verified service packages that remain allocable, maintainable and interoperable across decades.

### 8.2 Export power depends on allocation discipline

A resilience force used abroad by invitation would operate amid sovereign authority, damaged infrastructure, local firms and multiple donors. Shared-capacity discipline becomes even more important. The United States should be able to state what service it can guarantee, what host interfaces are required, what reserve remains, when the commitment ends and how capacity transfers to local institutions.

This reliability is itself power projection. A country that promises water, grid bridging, clinical depth or disaster mobility and can show the complete reservation behind the promise possesses credibility. One that promises the same scarce asset to several partners exports disappointment.

### 8.3 A truthful force may be larger—or differently composed

The ledger is not a mechanism for mechanically shrinking the Department. It may show that pooled capacity is efficient when demands are temporally and geographically diverse. It may also show that the current architecture depends on scarce common crews, bases, carriers or interfaces whose failure creates national collapse. The solution could be more inventory, different geography, greater preparation, new technology, stronger substitution or a smaller public guarantee. That choice belongs to strategy and democratic authority, not hidden spreadsheet credits.

The immediate evidence points toward greater ambition. The force lacks more than four million tonnes of early movement throughput under the current screen and more than half a million responder-base places. The answer is unlikely to be a minor adjustment to current agencies. It implies purpose-built lift and theater-opening systems, prepared national receiving geography, new construction and utility technologies, large professional communities and a command architecture capable of allocating them honestly.

## 9. Decision boundary and next research move

Pass 60 supports five decisions.

First, Pass 58 remains the held national planning baseline; unsupported credits are quarantined rather than silently removed or admitted. Second, future physical support models use gross on-duty people and complete package mass, not net ownership totals. Third, the Department requires a Joint Capacity Authority and four-ledger architecture. Fourth, allocation begins with guarantee floors, complete services and protected reserve; optimization follows. Fifth, canonical deployment must be rebuilt from item-identified cargo and compatible origin-to-effect paths.

It does not support a new national cost total, a final force size, a public-priority ordering or an acquisition program. The 48-by-10 campaign matrix remains a declared stress architecture. Source inventories remain coarse. Four critical pool classes remain unquantified. Mode shares are provisional. No present capital credit has earned item-level operational admission.

The next force-model pass should therefore create a canonical cargo and transport register. It should identify complete deployable packages, origin families, dimensions, mass, hazardous classes, handling interfaces, activation, mode compatibility, discharge, onward movement, receiver prerequisites and service deadlines. It should then allocate actual carrier and node objects while preserving follow-on reserve and regeneration.

Only after that work, and after the representative service configurations close their physics, should the project render major vehicles. The form of a civil airlifter, response carrier, inland flood vessel, autonomous pipeline train or mobile base should emerge from the contested allocation and service chain—not substitute for it.

## Evidence limits

- The 480 demand records inherit the Pass 58 ten-campaign force package and its provisional formation assumptions.
- The 100 operational claims are aggregated by campaign and pool; they do not establish origin, route, item, site or authority feasibility.
- Mode allocations are declared hypotheses used to reveal constrained pools; they are not transport plans.
- The base and movement capacities are inherited planning allowances, not verified national inventories.
- Sustainment excludes material public commodities, detailed distribution and reverse-flow burdens.
- HCG support pools are national planning quantities without item identity, location, time, release, failure correlation or non-HCG claims.
- NUPS is the only quantified common workforce source in the current credit ledger; compatibility of its occupations with the claimed roles is unproved.
- Capital source ceilings prevent coarse double purchase but do not prove asset assignment or operational availability.
- Disconnected information, maintenance and regeneration, clinical receiving and licensed-labor pools remain unquantified rather than zero.
- The serialization tests conservation but encodes no public-priority judgment.
- All twelve evidence gates remain open. No formation, shared pool, vehicle or platform is authorized for rendering.
