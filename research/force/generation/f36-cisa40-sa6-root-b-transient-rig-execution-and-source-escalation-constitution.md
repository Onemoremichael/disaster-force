# F36 `CISA-40` `SA6` Root B transient-rig execution and source-escalation constitution

## Executive judgment

The preceding [Root B technology program](f36-cisa40-sa6-root-b-two-phase-relief-and-deployable-retention-technology-program.md) defined three unlike candidate architectures and a 306-event campaign. The [full-scale transient-rig specification](f36-cisa40-sa6-root-b-full-scale-transient-rig-physical-specification.md) then defined six neutral public rigs capable of imposing the common source state without imposing a common downstream answer. Neither document, however, was yet an executable institution. A campaign count is not an event register. A source ladder is not an authority system. A sensor list is not an admissible evidence record. A safe-state concept is not a clocked abort sequence.

This pass creates `RHE-1`, the execution and source-escalation constitution for that institution. Its primary judgment is:

> Preserve the inherited 306-event denominator exactly, register every event before execution, distinguish event validity from candidate outcome, allocate a dormant 55-attempt invalidation reserve, and make recovery, evidence custody and independent adjudication coequal with source release.

The campaign is now composed rather than merely counted. Seventy-two development events qualify six candidate–builder roots through empty mechanical, hydrostatic, dry-nitrogen, inert two-phase surrogate and cold-soak work. Ninety-six quarter-source and ninety-six full-source events form ninety-six matched control/challenge pairs across three candidates, two unlike builders, two operational sites and eight challenges. Twenty-four destructive events expose controlled refusal, breach, receiver loss and compound denial. Twelve serialized reset events prove recovery at both operational sites. Six cross-site reconstructions require each root’s evidence to be adjudicated outside the site that produced it.

The event population is deliberately conserved. A valid candidate failure remains a result and remains in the denominator. An invalid attempt receives no campaign credit, is never deleted, and may consume one separately identified reserve token. The reserve contains fifty-five attempts derived from declared prospective invalid rates by stage. It adds 413 rig-days and 53 tonnes of source to the 2,202 rig-days and 288 tonnes in the base population. Total planned provision is therefore 2,615 rig-days and 341 tonnes, while the scientific denominator remains 306 events.

Every event follows one public clock. Configuration freezes at `T-72 h`; recovery capacity is proved at `T-48 h`; source identity is admitted at `T-24 h`; candidate readiness is accepted at `T-12 h`; initial conditions stabilize at `T-6 h`; rescue and exclusion are independently polled at `T-2 h`; the measurement estate is armed at `T-30 min`; the final safety poll occurs at `T-10 min`; full-rate recording begins at `T-60 s`; the registered challenge enters at `T-10 s`; and the single accountable release command occurs at `T0`. The terminal-source interval ends at `T+120 s`, full-rate observation at `T+240 s`, the six-hour tail follows, mass closes by `T+24 h`, the immutable packet freezes by `T+72 h`, and validity and outcome are dispositioned by day seven.

Readiness, abort, recovery and adjudication are distinct systems. Twenty-four readiness holds prevent an event from starting when configuration, source, candidate, condition, safety, measurement, challenge or site state is unresolved. Sixteen abort rules protect public pressure, load, fire, gas, thermal, timing, route, recovery and evidence limits during execution. Eight safe-state actions define immediate source isolation, candidate isolation, protected depressurization, atmosphere control, ignition-energy removal, continued remote measurement, rescue posture and configuration freeze. Eight recovery states then return the serialized root to an accountable baseline. A stop does not determine validity, and validity does not determine candidate outcome.

Eighteen measurement functions allocate 504 native channels across mass, composition, pressure, temperature, phase, loads, dynamics, geometry, gas, fire, weather, energy, controls, time, imagery, acoustic state, recovery inventory and custody. The source-to-loss mass budget assigns six one-sigma terms of 0.70, 0.80, 0.80, 1.00, 1.18 and 0.80 kg. Their root-sum-square is approximately 2.19 kg; the `k=2` expansion is approximately 4.38 kg against the inherited 5 kg limit. That is an allocation to be physically proved, not a claim that an event has closed.

The campaign requires 518 operating positions over forty-two months. Six rigs at a 62 percent ready-rig fraction provide approximately 4,756 ready rig-days, putting the base-plus-reserve demand near 55 percent of that envelope. The unused fraction is not waste: hazardous preparation, repair, calibration, source conditioning, weather holds, forensic preservation and protected re-entry all occupy elapsed time that the simple rig-day ledger does not count.

## 1. Decision, scope and epistemic boundary

### 1.1 Decision question

`RHE-1` answers:

> What exact event population, initial-condition system, public clock, hold authority, abort logic, evidence requirement, validity rule, recovery state, reserve policy, site allocation, operating establishment, schedule and fiscal commitment are required to convert `RHP-1` from a designed facility into an independent public learning institution?

The answer is not a test plan in the ordinary sense. It is a constitution because it binds events before their results are known, separates powers that would otherwise be combined in one program office, and preserves adverse evidence against managerial incentives to repeat, relabel or average it away.

### 1.2 What this pass fixes

This pass fixes:

- the identity and stage of all 306 base events;
- the candidate, builder, site, rig, protocol, condition and pair membership of every event;
- the source mass, initial temperature, initial pressure, heat boundary, planned rig-days and within-pair order;
- the 96 matched pairs and their control/challenge sequence;
- the 55 reserve tokens and their stage-specific source and time provisions;
- twenty-four initial-condition cards;
- the public event clock from configuration freeze through adjudication;
- twenty-four readiness holds, sixteen abort rules and eight safe-state actions;
- eighteen measurement functions and their 504-channel allocation;
- validity and candidate-outcome taxonomies;
- eight recovery states, twelve serialized reset clocks and six cross-site reconstructions;
- the 42-month work sequence and 518-position establishment;
- the capital, annual and corrected portfolio boundaries; and
- 618 planning, 742 physical and nine downstream release gates.

### 1.3 What this pass does not claim

`RHE-1` does not certify a rig, accept a calibration, admit a hazardous source, authorize hydrostatic work, authorize dry or surrogate work, authorize quarter-source or full-source operation, authorize destructive work, select an architecture, freeze Root B, authorize production or authorize exterior form.

The distinction matters because a complete event card can still depend on an unaccepted rig. A complete safety rule can still depend on an unproved final element. A complete uncertainty budget can still fail calibration. A complete economic model can still lack an appropriation. The workbook therefore assigns every planning gate a passing design state while leaving every physical observation at zero or blank.

## 2. The unit of learning is a serialized root

### 2.1 Six roots, not three concepts

The campaign unit is the candidate–builder root:

| Candidate | Builder A | Builder B | Candidate reset ceiling |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| `RC-1` direct segmented low-pressure capture | `RC-1-BA` | `RC-1-BB` | 72 h |
| `RC-2` staged pressure recovery and condensation | `RC-2-BA` | `RC-2-BB` | 96 h |
| `RC-3` hybrid recovery with measured last-resort destruction | `RC-3-BA` | `RC-3-BB` | 84 h |

Treating the candidate alone as the unit would hide builder-specific process assumptions, software, joining, sensor installation, cleaning, valve selection and quality control. Treating every article as unrelated would prevent architecture-level learning. The root is the middle object: materially independent enough to reveal production lineage, yet stable enough to carry a common architecture claim through sites and stages.

### 2.2 Site roles

`PTC-A` is the destructive campus. Every root executes four controlled-failure cases there. `PTC-B` and `PTC-C` are operational siblings. Every root executes every paired challenge at both sites, and every root completes a recovery/reset demonstration at both sites. A root’s cross-site reconstruction is performed at the operational site opposite its primary origin assignment.

This allocation creates three kinds of disagreement:

- builder disagreement within an architecture;
- within-root disagreement between operational sites; and
- live-event disagreement between the origin record and outside-site reconstruction.

The design does not average those disagreements away. Each is a causal lead requiring disposition.

The two conditions are a matched clean control and the registered challenge. The 192 events form 96 pairs. `PTC-B` and `PTC-C` each execute one pair for every root and challenge. The pair—not a pooled campaign mean—is the first comparison object.

### 3.4 Quarter-source challenges

The first four challenge families use 500 kg:

1. `Q01` cold start: 258 K and 0.35 MPa.
2. `Q02` half available area: a calibrated one-half-area restriction cassette.
3. `Q03` receiver wrong-side state: initial receiver pressure at 0.8 times the candidate-specific limit.
4. `Q04` receiver fifty-percent fill: the receiver begins at fifty-percent liquid-equivalent fill.

Quarter source is not a small version of the full problem. It is the first hazardous source tier at which the candidate must show that its sensing, routing, separation, containment and recovery logic have the right signs under controlled initial-state errors. Passing it does not infer full-source capacity.

### 3.5 Full-source challenges

The final four use 2,000 kg:

1. `F05` hot full source: 323 K and 1.20 MPa.
2. `F06` fire heat plus energy denial: 353 K, 2.00 MPa, an 8 MW measured boundary and denial of candidate active energy.
3. `F07` partial holder plus wind: fifty-percent holder deployment under a 1.5 kPa wind screen.
4. `F08` network and primary-time denial: campus network and primary time disappear at `T-10 s`.

`F05` is the full-source anchor. It establishes the source tier before the program adds external fire, incomplete deployment or information-system denial. `F08` is not a cybersecurity exercise attached to a process test. It asks whether the physical system and its public evidence remain safe and reconstructable when services normally treated as ambient disappear at the worst useful instant.

### 3.6 Order balance

Source-tier anchors `Q01` and `F05` run control first. For the remaining challenge numbers, odd challenges run control first and even challenges run challenge first. The rule gives every root–site sequence a known first exposure at each source tier while counterbalancing conditioning, wear, operator learning and residue effects across the rest of the challenge set.

Order is a registered field. A pair executed in the opposite order without prior method amendment is not silently reclassified; it is a validity question.

### 3.7 Destructive cases

Every root receives four `PTC-A` destructive cases:

- `D01`: powered isolation refusal;
- `D02`: route breach at a controlled sacrificial segment;
- `D03`: receiver or terminal capacity loss; and
- `D04`: compound heat, active-energy denial and primary-time loss.

The purpose is not spectacular failure. It is to discover which public barriers intervene, which candidate boundaries fail first, where residual inventory remains, whether protected measurement survives and whether the article can be recovered without improvising the rescue plan.

## 4. Source escalation as a chain of earned authority

### 4.1 Ten stages

The sequence is intentionally longer than a conventional qualification ladder because the object under study couples cryogenic or flashing behavior, long routes, receivers, deployable containment, active and passive safety, public evidence and recovery.

`SQ-00` qualifies configuration, restraint and dry interlocks. `SQ-01` qualifies shell, flange, route and pad integrity under hydrostatic loading. `SQ-02` exercises logic, timing, isolation and sensing with dry nitrogen. `SQ-03` introduces an inert two-phase surrogate to expose phase diagnostics and receiver response without the full source consequence. `SQ-04` qualifies contraction, icing, insulation and protected access. Only then do `SQ-05`, `SQ-06` and `SQ-07` introduce quarter, full and destructive source conditions. `SQ-08` and `SQ-09` qualify restoration and institutional memory.

### 4.2 Stage release is conjunctive

A stage disposition is a physical gate in its own right. Completing the last event is necessary and insufficient. The stage board must determine that:

- the registered event set is complete or validly dispositioned;
- required pair decisions are complete;
- invalid attempts have retained originals and named replacements;
- measurement sufficiency remained inside the method;
- recovery states closed for every source event;
- unresolved anomalies do not change the next stage’s safety basis; and
- the next stage’s source, site, candidate and rescue capacities are actually available.

No schedule authority can waive a stage disposition.

## 5. Dormant invalidation reserve

### 5.1 Why a reserve is necessary

A hazardous learning campaign without reserve has two predictable failure modes. Either managers pretend every completed release is scientifically usable, or they repeat failed methods informally and let the denominator drift. `RHE-1` instead declares stage-specific prospective invalid rates before execution and converts them into discrete reserve tokens:

\[
N_{reserve}=\left\lceil\frac{N_{base}}{1-r}\right\rceil-N_{base}.
\]

With declared rates of 10, 15, 10, 15, 15, 12, 15, 20, 10 and 10 percent across `SQ-00` through `SQ-09`, the reserve is 55 attempts.

Reserve use follows four rules:

1. The original event retains its identity, data, outcome and invalidity reason.

## 6. The public event clock

### 6.1 Before source release

At `T-72 h`, the configuration authority freezes the candidate serial, rig serial, route, receiver, method card, software and calibration references. At `T-48 h`, the recovery authority proves receiver capacity, vapor treatment, residual storage and casualty margin. At `T-24 h`, the source authority accepts lot identity, mass, chemistry and custody. At `T-12 h`, the candidate authority accepts the as-tested configuration and reset history. At `T-6 h`, the test conductor accepts the temperature, pressure, fill, wind and heat dwell.

At `T-2 h`, the safety authority independently confirms accountability, exclusion, rescue readiness and protected access. At `T-30 min`, the evidence authority requires all measurement functions, both native originals, calibration links and time domains. At `T-10 min`, every console authority must return an explicit `GO` or named hold. Silence is not consent. At `T-60 s`, all high-rate channels record and time alignment is measured. At `T-10 s`, the challenge lead inserts the denial or perturbation and proves its state.

### 6.2 Source and terminal interval

At `T0`, one accountable test conductor issues the source-release command. The source clock is not inferred from a downstream sensor; command, valve position, source response and flow observations all retain their own timestamps. The candidate has 120 seconds to reach its registered terminal state. That inherited requirement remains a decision bound, not a promise that the public system will wait 120 seconds before protecting itself.

From `T+120 s` to `T+240 s`, the high-rate estate observes terminal behavior, residual flow, receiver response and any public intervention. Full-rate recording then transitions without a custody discontinuity to the six-hour one-percent tail. A remote survey may support limited approach no earlier than `T+30 min`; default entry review occurs at `T+60 min`, but neither time creates an entitlement to enter.

### 6.3 Closure

The tail closes at `T+6 h` only if registered thermal and inventory criteria are met. Mass closure is due by `T+24 h`. The evidence packet freezes by `T+72 h`. The independent board signs validity first and candidate outcome second by day seven. Sequence matters: an attractive outcome cannot cure invalid evidence, and an adverse outcome cannot make a valid event disappear.

## 7. Holds, aborts and safe state

### 7.1 Twenty-four readiness holds

The hold system covers eight domains with three principal checks each:

- configuration: identity mismatch, open engineering change, unresolved prior damage;
- recovery: insufficient receiver capacity, unavailable vapor treatment, unaccepted residual path;
- source: chemistry, mass uncertainty or custody failure;
- candidate: expired reset, unavailable passive boundary, foreign energy in a denial case;
- initial condition: unstable dwell, wrong wind/heat state, wrong receiver state;
- safety: nonsterile exclusion, unavailable rescue access, unavailable independent stop;
- measurement: missing function, time skew above 5 microseconds, missing native original; and
- final poll/challenge/site: missing explicit `GO`, indeterminate challenge or site state outside the card.

A hold belongs to a named authority. The test conductor coordinates holds but cannot clear another authority’s hold.

### 7.2 Sixteen abort rules

Hard rules protect source pressure above 3.00 MPa, candidate receiver pressure above its registered limit, public receiver pressure above 0.30 MPa, route axial load above 1.20 MN, pad vertical load above 1.20 MN and pad overturning moment above 1.50 MN-m. Gas detection, flame beyond the registered zone, thermal exceedance, loss of an independent stop path, loss of both native originals, time skew above 5 microseconds, route breach, holder or terminal loss and failure to reach terminal safe state by 120 seconds each invoke their registered action. Any console authority retains an unconditional manual stop.

An abort is intentionally not called a candidate failure. Some aborts are candidate outcomes; some are rig faults; some are site faults; some are evidence faults. The later validity and outcome decisions preserve those distinctions.

### 7.3 Eight-action safe-state sequence

The safe-state sequence is:

1. close the source-isolation train with two independent barriers;
2. isolate candidate inlet and outlet boundaries without external energy;
3. depressurize the protected segment to the registered recovery path;
4. inert exposed volume when oxygen and flammability criteria require it;
5. remove nonessential ignition and thermal energy;
6. maintain remote measurement and protected originals;
7. maintain accountability, exclusion, rescue and medical posture; and
8. freeze configuration and custody before repair, cleanup or media handling.

The sequence rejects a common institutional error: treating evidence preservation as something done after safety. Protected measurement is part of safe state because responders need trustworthy remote state, and because an unexplained event creates risk for every later crew.

## 8. Validity and candidate outcome

### 8.1 Validity taxonomy

Validity asks whether the event tested the registered proposition with sufficient evidence. Ten domains govern: configuration, source identity, initial condition, challenge realization, measurement sufficiency, time integrity, public safety-envelope integrity, mass closure, custody continuity and independent adjudication.

The public-rig safety rule is asymmetric. If the candidate crosses its own limit but the public rig safely contains the consequence and preserves evidence, the event may be valid and the candidate may fail. If the public rig corrupts the challenge or loses the causal record, an apparent candidate failure may be invalid.

### 8.2 Outcome taxonomy

Candidate outcomes include clean success, degraded success, safe refusal, recoverable shortfall, unsafe excursion, public-rig intervention, terminal-time failure and reset-time failure. Evidence-limited, method-limited, site-limited and causally indeterminate classes are invalid-attempt dispositions rather than candidate performance claims.

No weighted score can convert an unsafe excursion into success because another metric is strong. The later architecture-selection board inherits the conjunctive discipline established in `RHT-1`.

### 8.3 Pair disposition

A pair closes only when both control and challenge events are valid and the pair disposition is signed. If one member is invalid, a reserve replacement addresses that member without deleting the other. If the control itself exposes an unexpected baseline fault, the pair may still be scientifically important, but it cannot be credited as the registered challenge contrast until adjudicated.

### 9.2 Mass closure

The event ledger resolves:

\[
m_{source}=m_{receiver}+m_{candidate\ residual}+m_{recovery\ residual}+m_{samples}+m_{bounded\ loss}.
\]

The six declared one-sigma allocations combine by root-sum-square:

\[
u_c=\sqrt{0.70^2+0.80^2+0.80^2+1.00^2+1.18^2+0.80^2}\approx2.1914\ \text{kg}.
\]

The expanded allocation is:

\[
U=2u_c\approx4.3829\ \text{kg}<5\ \text{kg}.
\]

The remaining margin is approximately 0.617 kg. The narrow margin argues for redundant mass chains and disciplined recovery; it does not justify rounding the result into apparent certainty.

### 9.3 Evidence volume

At the inherited 288.736 MB/s native rate, 300 seconds of full-rate recording, two originals and a 1.35 custody factor produce approximately 0.234 TB per event. The six-hour one-percent tail contributes approximately 0.168 TB. A bounded engineering-view allowance adds approximately 0.017 TB. The modeled total is approximately 0.419 TB per event and 128.3 TB across 306 events before reserve attempts.

Engineering views never replace originals. Every transformation retains source identity, software identity, parameters and cryptographic lineage. The six cross-site exercises must reach an event decision using evidence outside the origin site and the transferred forensic package.

## 10. Recovery is a test result

### 10.1 Eight recovery states

Recovery closes in order:

1. source isolated;
2. candidate isolated;
3. accountable inventory captured;
4. atmosphere safe for the authorized approach class;
5. thermal state stable;
6. mass closed;
7. evidence frozen; and
8. serialized root restored.

The order prevents restoration work from destroying causal evidence or transferring residual inventory into an unregistered store.

### 10.2 Reset ceilings

`RC-1` has a 72-hour ceiling, `RC-2` 96 hours and `RC-3` 84 hours. The differences are hypotheses about their distinct receiver, condensation, holder and terminal systems, not allowances to excuse poor maintainability. Each root demonstrates its ceiling independently at `PTC-B` and `PTC-C`, giving twelve reset records.

Reset time begins when source isolation is proved, not when the maintenance team declares the work convenient to start. It ends only when `RS-08` restores the serialized baseline, including damage disposition, calibration status and configuration identity.

`RHE-1` is designed against institutional as well as physical failure:

3. **Facility-selected winner.** Prevented by the public boundary ending before candidate-specific downstream geometry.

10. **Budget understatement.** Prevented by gross, overlap, net-new, corrected and upper views remaining separate.
