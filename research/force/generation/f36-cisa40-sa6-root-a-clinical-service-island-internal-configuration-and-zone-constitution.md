# RCA-1 — CSI-25 Clinical-Service-Island Internal Configuration, Patient-Flow, Utility One-Line and Clean–Controlled–Contaminated Zone Constitution

## Pass 163 research decision

> A clinical island is not a deployable building filled with beds. It is a separable care, custody and utility fault domain that can admit a person, deliver the correct episode, contain contamination, survive declared utility losses and complete transfer or supported release without borrowing an unproved local system.

`RBZ-1` replaced a hospital-shaped power proxy with `HCSG-100`, a bounded thirty-day clinical service for 100,000 people. It established ten unlike care lines, 951 required care positions, four operating clinical islands, one complete replacement island, 4,400 deployed people and a candidate set of utilities and support systems.

That work still represented each `CSI-25` clinical service island with one row: 240 positions, 2,400 dry tonnes and USD 2.2B. It did not say how people enter, how clean supply and contaminated return avoid one another, how isolation air is separated, how a cell loses power without darkening the island, how a ready island remains clinically ready, or what internal articles create the stated mass and cost.

This pass supplies that missing constitution. Its central result is corrective rather than decorative:

- one `CSI-25` contains **28 care cells** and exactly **240 care positions**;
- arrival, accepted transfer and supported release require **11 additional process modules** and **57 transient process positions**;
- care, process and support systems form **119 nested serialized articles**;
- enclosed or covered program area is **20,060 m²**;
- the declared working and controlled-boundary geometry screens at **72,770 m²**, about **18.0 acres**;
- dry mass is **2,803 tonnes**, 403 tonnes above the inherited proxy;
- first-unit capital is **USD 3.284B**, USD 1.084B above the inherited proxy;
- a nine-train, domain-specific air system retains clean, controlled and contaminated airflow separately after one same-domain train loss;
- an active island requires **3.9702 MW continuous** before the HCSG-common share and **2.2735 MW** of no-break service;
- four active islands, common support and a real ready-island standby state raise `HCSG-100` to **18.2808 MW continuous**, **21.02292 MW peak** and **10.244 MW no-break**;
- five `SPC-5` source clusters no longer close the reference after one loss; six do;
- common electrical articles rise from twenty-one to twenty-three because protection nodes rise from seven to eight;
- `HCSG-100` therefore becomes a **146-top-level-article, 33,377-tonne, USD 30.697B** first-unit planning screen; and
- every clinical, production, substitution, procurement and force admission remains zero.

The correction matters methodologically. The project is not required to protect its own earlier numbers. The internal article is allowed to falsify the parent formation. That is how an ambitious program avoids becoming speculative industrial illustration.

## 1. Decision object

Authorize `CSI-25` as a candidate internal clinical-service architecture with three physical custody domains:

1. **clean** — unopened supply, sterile processing, pharmacy, utility product, staff entry, data roots, maintenance and repair;
2. **controlled** — non-isolation patient care, diagnostics, internal patient transfer and supported-release staging; and
3. **contaminated** — isolation care, decontamination, dirty return, segregated effluent and medical-waste custody.

Arrange those domains as a **double-loop campus**:

- an inner clean logistics and utility loop delivers outward through controlled pass-throughs;
- a controlled care band receives people from public and acute portals;
- an outer contaminated and dirty-return loop removes people, instruments, linens, residuals and waste without crossing clean delivery; and
- each interface has a locally visible state, an automatic stop condition and a named manual authority.

Authorize the next research object, `RCB-1`, to create unlike Root A and Root B implementations of the same service contract and to define their build-release evidence.

Do not authorize:

- a building, tent, ship, barge, aircraft or vehicle exterior;
- a national quantity;
- clinical efficacy;
- fielding or procurement;
- release of any predecessor article or dollar;
- substitution credit against another portfolio; or
- a claim that audit closure is representative evidence.

## 2. Why the island must be constituted before its carrier

A hospital image silently answers questions that have not been asked. It suggests corridors, floors, elevators, bed grids, centralized utilities and a fixed clean/dirty arrangement inherited from permanent construction. A ship image does the same with hull subdivision, vertical access, ventilation zones, pier assumptions and aviation interfaces. A trailer image privileges highway geometry. A tent image assumes available land, benign drainage and external utilities.

None of those are neutral containers.

The first-principles sequence is:

> clinical obligation → patient and custody states → care cells → arrival and release processes → air and utility fault domains → staff work → spatial program → article bill → deployment tranches → carrier and site variants

Reversing that sequence makes clinical service conform to a vehicle. The Department of Resilience should make the vehicle conform to clinical service.

This is also why “modularity” cannot be treated as a universal good. A module can shorten assembly and isolate a defect. It can also add joints, transition errors, accessible-route discontinuities, data handoffs, air leakage, cleaning burden and mass. Modularity is accepted only where the service benefit exceeds the interface burden.

## 3. Service boundary

### 3.1 Unit of clinical credit

The parent `CCSC-1` service unit remains the **completed appropriate clinical episode**. An episode closes only when these fields remain bound:

- identity and authority;
- acuity;
- qualified staff;
- specialty-appropriate care position;
- utility and supply state;
- medications, devices, specimens and blood custody where applicable;
- care record;
- adverse state; and
- accepted transfer or supported release.

The `CSI-25` is credited only for the episodes it can complete. Empty spaces, unused equipment and nominal positions do not count.

### 3.2 Island obligation

One active island carries approximately one quarter of the `HCSG-100` regional clinical obligation. Because the care lines are unlike and indivisible, the island is not defined as “one quarter of 951.” It receives a care-line-specific allocation:

| Care line | Cells | Positions per cell | Installed positions |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Community continuity | 1 | 19 | 19 |
| Emergency and resuscitation | 2 | 6 | 12 |
| Observation | 3 | 7 | 21 |
| Ward inpatient, 27-position cell | 2 | 27 | 54 |
| Ward inpatient, 26-position cell | 2 | 26 | 52 |
| Critical care | 5 | 7 | 35 |
| Procedural suite | 3 | 1 | 3 |
| Maternal and neonatal | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| Dialysis | 3 | 1 | 3 |
| Isolation | 4 | 9 | 36 |
| Behavioral health | 2 | 2 | 4 |
| **Total** | **28** | — | **240** |

The split preserves the parent care-line total exactly. It does not imply that a one-position maternal cell is an acceptable long-term design. A cell-level loss is handled through the fifth island in this reference; `RCB-1` must test whether the cell partition itself needs revision.

### 3.3 Cell as fault domain

Each care cell must be removable from service without exporting its failure. The governing rule is:

> cell unavailable → interfaces isolate → affected patients enter safe transfer state → matching capacity on the ready island activates → evidence preserves the interrupted episode

A cell may share HCSG source power, water production, oxygen production, data standards or repair systems. It may not share an unisolated path whose failure disables an unlike care line.

## 4. The hidden service layer: arrival, transfer and release

The 240 care positions do not control the boundary between a damaged region and clinical service. That boundary requires its own queue, identity, infection, accessibility and destination capacity.

### 4.1 Public continuity portals

The island receives 998.875 public continuity presentations per day in the reference. With an eighteen-hour window, a 2.0 peak factor, a 10.5-minute transaction and 80-percent target utilization:

\[
N_{public} = \left\lceil
\frac{998.875}{18}\times2.0\times\frac{0.175}{0.8}
\right\rceil = 25
\]

Five seven-position modules install thirty-five positions. After one module loss, twenty-eight remain, leaving a three-position reference margin.

This is a narrow closure. At a 2.5 peak factor, the requirement rises to thirty-one positions. The reference after-loss capacity fails and a sixth seven-position module is required, installing forty-two and retaining thirty-five after loss.

### 4.2 Acute portals

The acute portal combines emergency, maternal and isolation presentations in the bounded screen: 111.875 per day. With twenty-four-hour operation, a 2.0 peak factor, a fifteen-minute transaction and 75-percent utilization, four positions are required.

Two four-position modules install eight. One module may be removed while four remain.

The portal performs more than triage. It binds identity, consent or lawful authority, infection state, acuity, accessibility needs, medication and device custody, caregiver status, destination and time.

### 4.3 Accepted-transfer staging

The island allocation is 12.981875 accepted clinical transfers per day. A one-hour dwell, twenty-four-hour window and 75-percent utilization produce one required staging position. Two one-position cells retain one after loss.

This low numerical count does not make transfer simple. The position is valid only when a destination has accepted the patient and the transport path preserves the required clinical state.

### 4.4 Supported-release staging

The island allocation is 132.2925 supported releases per day. A half-hour dwell, sixteen-hour window and 80-percent utilization require six positions. Two six-position modules install twelve and retain six after loss.

Release fails if the destination lacks necessary power, water, food, medicines, accessibility, caregiver support, follow-up or legal custody.

### 4.5 Process-position result

| Process family | Modules | Installed positions | After one module loss | Required |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Public continuity portal | 5 | 35 | 28 | 25 |
| Acute portal | 2 | 8 | 4 | 4 |
| Accepted-transfer stage | 2 | 2 | 1 | 1 |
| Supported-release stage | 2 | 12 | 6 | 6 |
| **Total** | **11** | **57** | — | **36** |

The 57 positions are transient process capacity, not care capacity. Adding them to the island does not raise the 240-position clinical claim. It makes the claim operationally reachable.

## 5. Spatial topology

### 5.1 Double-loop campus

The proposed topology is functional rather than stylistic.

**Inner loop — clean service.** Clean logistics docks, staff entry, sterile processing, pharmacy, data roots, biomedical repair, medical-gas headers, water-grade nodes, electrical feeders and reserve stores occupy a protected inner system. Deliveries pass outward through one-way or controlled two-way interfaces. Patients do not use this loop.

**Middle band — controlled care.** Public and acute portals enter the controlled care band. Community, emergency, observation, ward, critical, procedural, maternal, renal and behavioral cells connect through accessible patient routes. Internal clinical transfers do not cross the clean logistics path.

**Outer loop — contaminated return.** Isolation cells, decontamination, dirty instrument return, soiled linen, segregated wastewater, clinical waste and contaminated patient exit connect to an outer route. This loop does not cross the clean dock or staff entry.

The topology can be implemented on land, a prepared deck, a shallow-draft platform, a fixed receiver or another carrier only after that host proves the same route and fault-domain relations.

### 5.2 Ten controlled streams

| Stream | Forward direction | Return or exit | Stop condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| patients | portal to correct care cell | separate supported-release path | unknown identity or zone state |
| isolation patients | acute portal to contaminated care | dedicated contaminated transfer exit | air boundary unavailable |
| staff | clean entry through hygiene/PPE transition | dirty staff transition | credential or PPE state fails |
| sterile supplies | clean dock through pass-through | no reverse path | seal or custody fails |
| instruments | care cell to sealed dirty container | clean pass-through after release | container breach |
| linen | clean store to care cell | sealed dirty route | bag or route breach |
| waste | care cell to tracked dirty route | no reverse path | custody or capacity fails |
| air | clean-side plant to domain terminal | no contaminated recirculation | differential or filter state fails |
| water and gas | clean utility root to clinical terminal | segregated drain or vent | quality or delivery state fails |
| data and medication | authorized root to care point | signed reconciliation | identity, consent or custody mismatch |

The important design object is the interface, not merely the zone label. Every interface needs a state sensor, manual inspection route, local indication, authoritative stop rule and evidence record.

### 5.3 Spatial program

The internal bill produces:

- 12,820 m² of care-cell area;
- 1,230 m² of arrival, transfer and release area;
- 6,010 m² of utilities, transitions, logistics, repair, staff support and circulation;
- 20,060 m² total enclosed or covered program area;
- 52,789 m² gross working area at a 38-percent coverage assumption; and
- 72,770 m² inside a twenty-metre controlled-boundary allowance around an equivalent square, about 18.0 acres.

These values are a site-screening geometry, not a site design. Fire access, drainage, slope, soil, noise, thermal rejection, helipad or landing-zone separation, public transport, parking, staff housing, fuel, bulk stocks and long-duration waste areas remain outside it.

## 6. Air-domain constitution

### 6.1 Why aggregate HVAC is rejected

An island could possess enough total airflow and still fail because the surviving capacity is in the wrong contamination domain. Air must therefore close separately for clean, controlled and contaminated volume.

The reference assigns the 20,060 m² program as follows:

| Domain | Area share | Volume at 4 m | Air-change assumption | Required flow |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Clean | 24% | 19,257.6 m³ | 6 ACH | 115,545.6 m³/h |
| Controlled | 56% | 44,934.4 m³ | 8 ACH | 359,475.2 m³/h |
| Contaminated | 20% | 16,048.0 m³ | 12 ACH | 192,576.0 m³/h |
| **Total** | **100%** | **80,240.0 m³** | — | **667,596.8 m³/h** |

### 6.2 Train allocation

| Domain | Train class | Installed | Capacity after one loss | Reference margin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Clean | 120,000 m³/h | 2 | 120,000 m³/h | 4,454.4 m³/h |
| Controlled | 120,000 m³/h | 4 | 360,000 m³/h | 524.8 m³/h |
| Contaminated | 100,000 m³/h | 3 | 200,000 m³/h | 7,424.0 m³/h |

The controlled-domain margin is only 0.15 percent. It is a deliberate warning, not comfort. Door opening, filter loading, wind, stack effect, heat, smoke and commissioning error could consume it immediately.

### 6.3 Air laws

- clean space is positive relative to controlled space;
- contaminated space is negative relative to controlled space;
- contaminated return air is not recirculated into another domain;
- clean-side cross-connect is permitted only behind isolation and contamination sensing;
- differential, airflow, filter, door and contaminant state remain locally visible without an external network;
- loss of the boundary stops new admissions to the affected cell; and
- the provisional objective is to restore the declared boundary within three minutes or begin safe transfer.

The report does not prescribe clinical air setpoints. Those require qualified design, code review and representative trials.

### 6.4 High-infection falsification

If contaminated area rises from 20 to 30 percent and the assumed rate rises from 12 to 15 ACH, required contaminated flow becomes 361,080 m³/h. Three 100,000 m³/h trains cannot even meet demand before a loss, much less after one.

The next root design must either:

- install at least four higher-capacity contaminated trains;
- reduce contaminated volume by creating smaller individually controlled cells;
- shift some isolation duty to the ready island; or
- prove a lower safe air demand through a different technical approach.

## 7. Electrical one-line

### 7.1 Island-local topology

Each island receives two physically separated medium-voltage entries from the HCSG ring. Inside the island:

- three 2.5 MW feeder trains supply the local buses;
- any two retain 5 MW, above the 4.56573 MW island peak;
- eight sector branch panels separate clinical and facility blocks;
- three 0.2 MWh local no-break pods bridge switching;
- clinical devices and environmental systems enter declared safe states rather than merely remaining energized;
- selective protection isolates a cell without taking an unlike care line or the clean utility loop; and
- local manual operation remains possible when external networking and the HCSG controller are unavailable.

The two HCSG entries do not constitute two independent sources if they share upstream generation, protection, route, grounding or control. `RCB-1` must draw the exact one-line and fault tree.

### 7.2 Active-island demand

The inherited care allocation generates 1.6702 MW of direct clinical load per active island. RCA-1 allocates 2.3 MW of local support:

- air and environmental control;
- local diagnostics;
- medical-gas distribution;
- sterile and biomedical systems;
- water and residual pumps;
- records and local control;
- staff services; and
- accessible internal transfer systems.

The active-island total is therefore 3.9702 MW continuous and 4.56573 MW at the 1.15 peak factor.

### 7.3 Formation demand correction

The parent calculation carried direct clinical positions and shared support but did not explicitly hold the ready island in a real standby state. RCA-1 uses:

| Load block | Continuous MW | Peak MW |
|---|---:|---:|
| Four active-island direct-care loads | 6.6808 | 7.68292 |
| Four active-island local-support loads | 9.2000 | 10.58000 |
| HCSG common support | 1.7500 | 2.01250 |
| Ready-island standby | 0.6500 | 0.74750 |
| **Total** | **18.2808** | **21.02292** |

At 90-percent source availability, one-source loss and the inherited reserve rule, six `SPC-5` clusters are required. Five retain only 18 MW after loss and fail the 18.2808 MW continuous requirement. Six retain 22.5 MW, a 4.2192 MW margin.

The installed 30 MW source root raises protection from seven to eight `PTN-5` nodes. With three `BSS-10/5` articles and six control cells, the common electrical subset becomes twenty-three articles.

### 7.4 Why this correction is important

The change from five sources back to six is not an argument for always keeping the larger number. It shows that readiness terms must remain inside the demand model. A “ready” clinical island whose air, cold systems, water-quality state, gas system, data root and controls are unpowered is stored equipment, not a replacement island.

The HCSG reference is:

| Load block | No-break MW |
|---|---:|
| Four active-island direct care | 5.094 |
| Four active-island local support | 4.000 |
| HCSG common no-break | 0.900 |
| Ready-island standby no-break | 0.250 |
| **Total** | **10.244** |

With a half-hour duration, twenty-percent energy reserve, eighty-percent usable fraction and 92-percent efficiency, required nameplate energy is 8.351087 MWh. Three `BSS-10/5` articles retain 20 MW and 10 MWh after one article loss.

The island-local five-minute ride-through requirement is 0.308899 MWh. Three 0.2 MWh local pods retain 0.4 MWh after one loss.

Stored energy is only one condition. The safe-state program must define, by care line:

- which loads remain uninterrupted;
- which devices may restart;
- which procedures must stop;
- which patients enter transfer state;
- how alarms remain perceptible;
- how staff verify the local state; and
- how the event is recorded under network loss.

## 9. Oxygen, water and residual custody

### 9.1 Water

One island receives 47.7725 m³/day of direct clinical water allocation. Its 829-person active roster at eighty litres/person/day adds 66.32 m³/day. Allocated local process use adds 75 m³/day.

The base is 189.0925 m³/day. With twenty-five-percent reserve, protected demand is 236.365625 m³/day. An eight-hour buffer requires 78.788542 m³.

Three 40 m³ protected storage nodes retain 80 m³ after one loss. Two additional water-grade nodes provide dialysis and sterile polishing. Quantity earns no credit when feed chemistry, product quality, temperature, custody or release is unknown.

### 9.2 Wastewater

At a 90-percent return assumption, base wastewater is 170.18325 m³/day and protected demand is 212.729063 m³/day. The eight-hour buffer is 70.909688 m³.

Three 40 m³ segregated tanks retain 80 m³ after one loss. A fourth interface owns sampling, routing and lawful release. Contaminated isolation effluent may not be silently combined with ordinary clinical wastewater.

### 9.3 Oxygen

The island allocation is 1,539.25 m³/day. With twenty-five-percent reserve, protected demand is 1,924.0625 m³/day. Four hours require 320.677083 m³.

Two isolated headers and three 200 m³ reserve pods form the local system. One reserve-pod loss leaves 400 m³.

The local reserve does not replace HCSG oxygen production. Purity, delivery state, alarm, fire safety, connection error, contamination and clinical consequence remain separate evidence objects.

### 9.4 Medical waste

Base clinical waste is 0.888256 tonnes/day. The protected one-day quantity is 1.110320 tonnes. Three 0.6-tonne custody pods retain 1.2 tonnes after one loss.

The system must preserve classification, container identity, cold or treatment need, staff protection, temporary storage, transfer documentation and final disposition.

- supply retrieval and inventory;
- medication and specimen custody checks;
- environmental-state monitoring;
- linen and waste handling;
- records synchronization;
- equipment diagnostics;
- queue prediction;
- bed and cell state; and
- setup, inspection and teardown documentation.

It is rejected where the safety case depends on unavailable networks, opaque authority, unreviewable triage, absent human supervision or a fictitious reduction in specialty skill.

## 13. Activation and relocation

The island deploys as service tranches rather than undifferentiated cargo.

| Tranche | Window | Dry mass | Primary service | Release condition |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| T0 authority and portal | 0–6 h | 340 t | command, identity, public and acute portals | receiver compact and site safety |
| T1 acute utility kernel | 6–18 h | 620 t | power, air, oxygen, emergency and critical care | complete acute path |
| T2 controlled care | 18–36 h | 880 t | observation, procedure, isolation and transfer | zone separation |
| T3 full care lattice | 36–60 h | 700 t | ward, renal, maternal, behavioral and sterile systems | 240 care positions |
| T4 repair and relocation reserve | 60–72 h | 263 t | spares, handling and reset | whole-island readiness |
| **Total** | **0–72 h** | **2,803 t** | — | — |

At a 1.12 packaging factor, transport mass is 3,139.36 tonnes. An eighteen-tonne heavy-cargo-position screen produces 175 positions.

This number does not select road, rail, air or water carriage. The 119 articles have different volume, hazardous-material, cold-chain, vibration, tilt, restraint, handling and deadline requirements. `RCB-1` must turn mass positions into a serial manifest before any carrier design is justified.

Relocation is a clinical operation. A cell cannot be declared mobile merely because its structure can be lifted. Safe relocation requires:

- patient disposition;
- medicines and specimens reconciled;
- sterile and dirty custody closed;
- wastewater and waste transferred lawfully;
- utilities safe;
- records complete;
- equipment cleaned, secured and inspected;
- destination site commissioned; and
- the clinical episode uninterrupted or explicitly transferred.

## 14. Failure-world constitution

Twenty failure worlds organize the next evidence program.

### 14.1 Scalar closures

The reference calculation closes:

- one whole active island unavailable, if the fifth island activates within six hours;
- one local feeder unavailable;
- one same-domain air train unavailable in the reference air allocation;
- one water-buffer pod unavailable;
- one oxygen-reserve pod unavailable;
- one wastewater-buffer pod unavailable;
- one public portal module unavailable; and
- one acute portal module unavailable.

These are arithmetic states. Each needs a representative operation.

### 14.2 Open physical and clinical worlds

The following remain open:

1. one care cell fails during an active episode;
2. contaminated air crosses an interface;
3. one local data root fails;
4. network and external time sources disappear for seventy-two hours;
5. a specialty staff cohort becomes unavailable;
6. extreme heat and smoke coincide;
7. a receiver refuses transfer;
8. a supported-release destination loses utilities;
9. isolation census doubles;
10. a second region activates before reset; and
11. a root-wide latent defect appears.

### 14.3 Safety hierarchy

The operational hierarchy is:

> preserve life → contain contamination → maintain identity and custody → hold safe clinical state → transfer to accepted capacity → restore the affected cell → reconstruct evidence

No throughput target may override that order.

## 15. Technology agenda

RCA-1 allocates USD 32B across nine research lines. The allocation is cross-classified inside the inherited USD 75B `TCP-06` medical technology and proving line; it is not additional spending.

| Program | USD B | Advance sought | Disqualifying result |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Reversible care cells | 7 | fast assembly and repeated relocation without clinical compromise | care quality cannot survive repeated moves |
| Air and infection separation | 4 | adaptive domain-specific air and sensing | cross-domain contamination under one-train loss |
| Local power and no-break | 4 | fault-contained three-feeder clinical lattice | selective isolation or manual state fails |
| Clinical fluids and residuals | 3 | compact oxygen, high-grade water and closed residual custody | purity or release state cannot be proven |
| Patient portals and custody | 3 | high-rate, accessible identity and acuity intake | peak queue breaks identity or rights |
| Workload reduction | 3 | supervised automation for logistics, monitoring and documentation | safety depends on absent network or staff |
| Offline clinical state | 2 | signed local records with later reconciliation | split identity or medication state |
| Assembly and manufacturing | 3 | unlike roots, rapid interfaces and repair | latent root defect or unrepairable interface |
| Independent proving | 3 | tracer trials, fault insertion, human factors and outside reconstruction | operator controls evidence or adverse record |

### 15.1 Novel technology is mission-pulled

The Department should not limit the island to present commercial catalogues. It can create demand for:

- compact, high-efficiency clinical air systems that prove domain state continuously;
- reversible sealed joints that tolerate repeated assembly without hidden contamination paths;
- oxygen systems with inline purity evidence and automatic clinical downscope;
- clinical-water systems that switch source chemistry without losing dialysis or sterile service;
- autonomous clean delivery and dirty-return systems that preserve chain of custody;
- local clinical data roots that remain trustworthy during long disconnection;
- modular electrical protection that recognizes clinical consequence, not just fault magnitude;
- instrumented surfaces and seals that reveal cleaning and breach state;
- robotic assembly and teardown that reduce staff exposure; and
- manufacturing methods that make hundreds of unlike clinical modules repeatable and repairable.

### 15.2 Industry structure

A mature program would sustain at least:

- two unlike prime integration roots;
- multiple clinical-cell manufacturers;
- dedicated air, oxygen, water, electrical and waste suppliers;
- public interface and data standards;
- regional assembly and regeneration plants;
- component quarantine and forensic capacity;
- training hospitals and controlled clinical simulation campuses;
- independent proving facilities; and
- exportable configurations whose governance and service interfaces can be adapted without surrendering local authority.

The export product is not a box hospital. It is a configurable clinical-service system with a transparent service contract, controlled interfaces, training, maintenance, public evidence and host-country authority.

## 16. Representative proving campaign

The parent campaign carries eight `CSI-25` top-level experiment articles. At 119 nested serials each, they contain 952 installed nested articles.

RCA-1 assigns:

- four islands to Root A and four to Root B;
- 240 controlled defect insertions;
- seventy-two complete service runs;
- sixty-six months inside the parent medical campaign; and
- 8,400 peak research, clinical, engineering, manufacturing and assurance positions.

### 16.1 Six campaign phases

1. **Service and interface freeze — six months.** Freeze patient states, care-line obligations, zone interfaces, utility states, rights, event record and stop rules.
2. **Care-cell roots — twelve months.** Build and exercise unlike cell architectures, repeated assembly, cleaning, repair and clinical simulation.
3. **Air and utility roots — twelve months.** Exercise domain air, feeder faults, no-break transitions, oxygen, water, residuals and disconnected controls.
4. **Complete Root A island — twelve months.** Run the full double-loop campus under declared queues and failures.
5. **Complete Root B island — twelve months.** Repeat the service with a technically unlike architecture.
6. **Cross-root loss and relocation — twelve months.** Deny a root, exchange an island, repeat assembly and reconstruct the evidence independently.

### 16.2 Required experiment contexts

The same service contract should be exercised in at least:

- a prepared inland site;
- a bare inland site;
- a damaged fixed facility;
- an inland-water receiving site;
- a littoral receiving site; and
- a dispersed multi-receiver operation.

These are context configurations, not exterior platform approvals.

### 16.3 Evidence ownership

The Independent Clinical Service Assurance Authority should own:

- reference patient and utility states;
- sealed failure insertion;
- adverse-event record;
- air and contamination tracers;
- independent data copies;
- outside-team reconstruction;
- production-equivalent acceptance; and
- public release of safety-critical findings.

The operating service cannot certify its own clinical system. A manufacturer cannot define the acceptance test for its own root.

## 17. Sensitivity and design signals

### 17.1 Public-arrival peak

The reference public portal retains three positions after one module loss. A 2.5 peak factor requires thirty-one; the remaining twenty-eight fail. This is a low-cost correction relative to the island and should be resolved in `RCB-1` unless queue evidence justifies a different service time or peak.

### 17.2 Infection burden

The high-infection case fails the contaminated-air configuration decisively. Isolation volume, air rate and train capacity are therefore major architecture discriminants.

### 17.3 Extreme thermal duty

Scaling local and common support by 1.25 raises HCSG continuous demand to 21.1808 MW. Six `SPC-5` clusters remain sufficient in the scalar screen after one loss, but environmental heat rejection, conductor temperature, storage derating and air-filter load are not represented.

### 17.4 Fully hot fifth island

Holding the fifth island at full active power instead of 0.65 MW standby raises continuous demand to 21.601 MW. Six sources still close the isolated power formula. The workforce and consumable consequences are larger and remain unclosed.

### 17.5 Controlled-air margin

The reference controlled-air system retains only 524.8 m³/h after one train loss. That is too narrow to treat as a mature configuration. Either capacity, area allocation, cell isolation or the accepted demand model must change.

## 18. Authority, rights and civil character

The Department of Resilience is an instrument of civilian continuity. Its scale increases the need for constrained authority.

### 18.1 Consent and refusal

Routine care and transfer require informed consent or a named lawful basis. Refusal, incapacity, emergency exception and review must be recorded locally during communications loss.

### 18.2 Accessibility

Accessibility applies to the entire episode:

- arrival and wayfinding;
- communication;
- examination and diagnostic equipment;
- toilets and hygiene;
- caregiver presence;
- service animals and mobility devices;
- internal transfer;
- shelter and staff support; and
- destination release.

A nominal position unusable by the person assigned to it is zero capacity for that person.

### 18.3 Family and caregiver unity

The island must preserve identity links among patients, caregivers, dependent children, mobility devices, essential property and destination. Separation can create clinical harm and reduce willingness to seek care.

### 18.4 Privacy and offline data

Disconnected operation does not suspend privacy. The local data root must enforce minimum access, record every override, preserve patient visibility and support later reconciliation without overwriting conflicting clinical histories.

### 18.5 Death, remains and adverse events

Death custody, identification, cultural and religious practice, family communication and independent review remain part of the clinical system. Neither bodies nor adverse records may disappear into a logistics stream.

### 18.6 Local clinical authority

Every deployment needs a receiver compact defining:

- clinical authority and credential recognition;
- land and utility access;
- referral priority;
- patient and data custody;
- waste and remains disposition;
- local staff integration;
- public communication;
- transfer acceptance; and
- withdrawal or transition.

The island does not supersede local health governance merely because it owns more equipment.

## 19. Economic interpretation

The USD 3.284B island and USD 30.697B formation are first-unit engineering screens. Their value is not predictive precision. They reveal where a defense-scale civilian industry would exist:

- clinical modular construction;
- advanced ventilation and air assurance;
- medical-gas generation and distribution;
- high-grade water and residual processing;
- grid-forming and no-break electrical systems;
- biomedical repair;
- clinical automation;
- offline health information systems;
- accessible transport interfaces;
- large-scale sterile supply; and
- rapid site assembly and regeneration.

Five islands account for USD 16.420B of the revised HCSG screen. That is large enough to justify continuous production, competing roots, public standards, dedicated test campuses, supplier reserves and career manufacturing labor. It is not a converted-equipment niche.

The program should pursue learning curves, but not by stripping service content. Cost decline is accepted only when the same mission, failure and rights gates still close.

No national fleet cost is produced. Multiplying the unit by an inherited medical proxy would invent geography, concurrency, availability, staffing and receiver assumptions.
