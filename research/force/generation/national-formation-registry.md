# The National Formation Registry

## Reconciling the mature Department of Resilience architecture with the current quantitative order of battle

> **Quantitative successor:** Pass 58's [Canonical Force Allocation](canonical-force-allocation-and-fiscal-reconciliation.md) now adds all fifteen absent families, retires `I-UPG` operationally and preserves shared systems through explicit credits. This registry remains the authority for family identity and evidence state; the successor workbook owns current national sizing.

### Research state

`R2 institutional architecture` · `R1/R2 formation registry` · `R2+ configuration proxies for eight families` · no formation has reached `R3` · no rendering authorized

## Executive judgment

The Department of Resilience now has a coherent institutional grammar, but it does not yet have a coherent order of battle. The [canonical force architecture](../force-architecture.md) distinguishes generating services, civilian effects, formations, employment commands and components. The [mature force design](mature-force-design.md) names the permanent formations needed to make that architecture operational. The current [joint force allocation model](joint-force-allocation-and-order-of-battle.md), however, predates that settlement. It quantifies thirty-four formation lines, omits the entire Civil Mobility and Continuity Service, leaves several joint, maritime, health and community families without distinct lines, and represents water, power and grid restoration through one aggregate utility-projection group.

This pass creates the first governed **National Formation Registry**. It records forty-eight canonical formation-family hypotheses and one legacy aggregate retained for quantitative lineage. It does not claim that forty-eight is the final number. It establishes something more important: every proposed family now has one stable identity, one lifecycle owner, one primary civilian effect, one operational responsibility, one maturity state, one evidence authority, one dependency hypothesis and one next admissible evidence action.

The reconciliation produces five findings.

1. **The mature architecture is larger than the quantified force.** Only thirty-three of forty-eight canonical families have distinct lines in the Pass 23 order of battle. Fifteen are absent. The old workbook still contains thirty-four rows because one of them—`I-UPG`, the utility projection group—is a legacy aggregate rather than a canonical mature family.
2. **The utility aggregate must be disassembled without changing totals by assertion.** Water restoration, power restoration and grid bridge groups have different physics, workforces, industrial bases, movement burdens and acceptance authorities. They cannot remain one operational unit. Yet the existing `I-UPG` capital, workforce and campaign quantities cannot simply be copied to three children. They must be reallocated through campaign demand and a reconciled work breakdown.
3. **Configuration evidence is concentrated rather than distributed.** Eight families have `R2+` configuration proxies: flood and hydraulic control, ground fire and landscape, maritime production, water restoration, power restoration, population support, household collection and continuity transfer nodes. Most of the force remains a bounded organizational hypothesis.
4. **The hardest missing fields are relational.** Thirty-one of forty-nine registry records lack even a bounded damage-envelope record. More fundamentally, zero records have configuration-grade evidence for supporting dependencies, sustainment and waste, civil and technical authority through transfer, or regeneration. The corpus has become comparatively strong at describing machines and immediate operating packages while remaining weak at proving how formations coexist, endure, hand over service and return to readiness.
5. **No formation is ready to govern a rendering.** No record has all ten service-contract fields at configuration grade, and none has survived a representative full-formation trial. Renderings remain blocked because external geometry would imply decisions about capacity, interfaces and operational concept that the evidence has not earned.

The immediate consequence is not a new procurement list. It is a change in the object of research and acquisition. The Department should commission **formation baselines**: configuration-controlled records that join people, equipment, movement, receivers, operating bases, sustainment, authority, transfer and regeneration to one measurable public effect. Technology programs, industrial policy and future vehicle designs should be downstream of those baselines.

The accompanying [National Formation Registry workbook](../../../models/force/generation/national-formation-registry.xlsx) contains the complete registry, ten-field contract screen, dependency hypotheses, technology pull, portfolio gaps, internal source lineage and formula-backed integrity checks.

## 1. Why a registry is now necessary

The project began with mission systems: water production, power restoration, fire control, flood response, medical care, population continuity, mobility and damaged-theater entry. That was appropriate. It prevented a premature organization chart from preserving existing agencies or importing military forms by analogy. As configuration studies accumulated, however, the unit of argument changed. A pump, ship, aircraft, shelter or field hospital could no longer be evaluated as a self-contained capability. Each depended on a larger organizational package and a complete path to public use.

Pass 56 therefore made the formation—not the platform—the smallest object eligible for national readiness credit. The formation service contract contains ten fields:

1. supported population, civil function or geography;
2. required effect at the point of safe use;
3. alert, entry, initial, full and transfer time bands;
4. damage and degraded-mode envelope;
5. organic people, equipment, stocks, command and data;
6. supporting formations and prepared local interfaces;
7. movement, reception and operating-base requirements;
8. sustainment, waste, replacement and maintenance burden;
9. civil, technical and independent release authorities; and
10. regeneration standard after employment.

That definition creates a governance problem. The corpus contains family names in force papers, formation quantities in workbooks, reference configurations in system studies, campaign packages in proving files and technology concepts in the platform register. Without a single controlled crosswalk, one name can silently refer to an effect, a program, a platform, a command or an aggregate budget line. Conversely, several names can describe parts of the same formation and acquire duplicate workforce or capital credit.

The registry is the control surface between architecture and models. It performs four functions.

**Identity control.** Each canonical family receives one identifier and one generating owner. A family may contribute to several effects, but it has one primary effect for portfolio accounting and one lifecycle service for force generation.

**Lineage control.** A registry record distinguishes current canonical families from legacy quantitative aggregates. Historical arithmetic remains traceable without being mistaken for mature structure.

**Evidence control.** Each record states what the corpus actually contains: a functional concept, bounded system architecture, configuration-level proxy or representative formation trial. Maturity attaches to the evidence, not to the confidence of the prose.

**Work-program control.** Every record names the highest binding gap and the next admissible evidence. A formation cannot advance because its platform is visually compelling or because another family passed a loosely related test.

The registry is consequently not an administrative inventory. It is a constitutional instrument for preventing category error and evidence laundering.

### 2.1 Forty-eight canonical families

The joint families are included in the registry because they are permanent force-generating objects even though they are not an eighth service. They must own cadres, doctrine, training, equipment and readiness. Their joint character means no single technical service can produce their complete effect.

### 2.2 One legacy aggregate

The forty-ninth registry record is `I-UPG`, the Pass 23 utility projection group. It is not counted among the forty-eight canonical families. It remains in the registry because it owns real lineage: campaign-package quantities, inventory, component shares, workforce logic and its share of the existing Department-scale screen.

The mature design replaces its operational meaning with three distinct families:

- `I-WRG`, Water Restoration Group;
- `I-PRG`, Power Restoration Group; and
- `I-GBG`, Grid Bridge Group.

This is not cosmetic nomenclature. Water service is governed by source, treatment, quality release, storage, pressure, distribution, wastewater and residual control. Power service is governed by load, generation, storage, protection, synchronization, conductor, fuel and thermal limits. A grid bridge is governed by damaged-substation bypass, transformation, protection coordination and utility acceptance. Their common logistics and technical interfaces justify joint employment; they do not justify one readiness certificate.

The legacy line therefore receives a retirement condition rather than immediate deletion: disaggregate demand, components, workforce and the work breakdown across the three children, reconcile the resulting campaign packages and preserve the existing Department total until the new arithmetic establishes a change. This prevents both undercounting and a threefold mechanical duplication.

### 2.3 What the count does not include

The registry does not count every technical team, detachment, mission module, national command, prepared receiver or platform concept as a family. `WRG-50`, `PRG-100`, `HCG-100`, `PCC-50`, `CTN-10` and `NCAC-35` are configuration references or formation-scale proxies inside canonical families. A National Receiving Lattice site is prepared force capacity, not necessarily a deployable formation. A service-entry cell is a campaign package composed of several formation increments. A nuclear water-energy system is a candidate mechanism inside maritime production and infrastructure-restoration chains, not a separate service.

That boundary keeps the registry from becoming another list. A new family is justified only when one indivisible operating package owns a distinct public-effect obligation, professional lifecycle and readiness decision that cannot be governed inside an existing family.

## 3. Method and evidence scale

### 3.1 Evidence states

The registry uses the project’s maturity language conservatively.

| State | Registry interpretation |
|---|---|
| `R1` | Functional concept: a mission and organizational responsibility are stated, but essential service-contract fields remain absent. |
| `R1/R2` | Bounded architecture or provisional quantitative line: the formation has a complete-thread hypothesis and some force-design arithmetic, but no configuration-controlled manifestation. |
| `R2` | Deterministic system architecture or closure beyond a label, still dependent on unvalidated design coefficients and incomplete formation evidence. |
| `R2+` | Configuration-level proxy: mass, people, interfaces, timing or other first-order engineering is unusually developed, while consequential gates remain open. |
| `R3` | Representative full-system and complete-formation evidence has been independently admitted. No current record qualifies. |

The distribution is one `R1` record, thirty-eight `R1/R2` records including the legacy aggregate, two `R2` records, eight `R2+` records and zero `R3` records. Among canonical families alone, thirty-seven are `R1/R2`.

This distribution should not be interpreted as forty-eight attempts to build equally mature units. It describes a research portfolio in which several physical systems have advanced through configuration arithmetic while institutional, clinical, distributional and regeneration systems remain comparatively abstract.

### 3.2 Ten-field contract score

Each service-contract field receives an evidence-presence score:

- `0`: the field is absent or only implied by a label;
- `1`: the field is bounded by an architectural or analytical claim; and
- `2`: the cited source contains a configuration-level record for that field.

The maximum is twenty. This is a triage device, not a readiness index. It answers whether a field has been made concrete enough to challenge, not whether the claim is correct. Twenty points would be necessary but insufficient for `R3`; independent testing, source quality, statistical uncertainty, safety and public acceptance would still govern admission.

The mean completeness across canonical families is 52 percent. Most Pass 23 quantitative families score 45 percent: they have an effect, people and mass assumptions, generic time and movement logic, sustainment coefficients and a transfer idea, but they lack a formation-specific damage envelope and itemized configuration. The eight `R2+` proxies score 80 percent because they include substantially more detailed population, output, time, damage, organic package and movement records. They remain below full configuration because supporting dependencies, sustainment and waste, authority and transfer, and regeneration have not closed at the same level.

### 3.3 Internal authority only

This pass introduces no new empirical source. It reconciles existing project authorities: the force architecture, mature design, joint allocation model, deployment enterprise, civil mobility force, receiving lattice, platform register and configuration studies for water, power, fire, flood, population continuity, neighborhood access and service-entry nodes.

That method has an intentional limitation. It can identify contradiction, omission, double-count risk and evidence concentration. It cannot improve the empirical validity of an inherited coefficient. A complete-looking registry built from provisional sources remains provisional.

## 4. The structure revealed by the registry

### 4.1 The force is not evenly developed

Mean contract completeness varies from 45 percent for Air Response to 59 percent for Land Engineering and Civil Mobility and Continuity. Those averages conceal opposite conditions.

Land Engineering’s score is lifted by two detailed hazard-control programs: `HCG-100` for flood control and the Integrated Fire Control Group for ground fire and landscape operations. Civil Mobility’s score is lifted by `NCAC-35` and `CTN-10`, while all seven of its canonical families remain absent from the Pass 23 quantitative order of battle. Air Response, by contrast, has all six families quantified but none configured at the complete-formation level.

This distinction matters. Quantitative inclusion and configuration maturity are different achievements. A family can have national inventory and workforce numbers without a credible item-level operating package. Another can have unusually detailed configuration arithmetic without any place in the national concurrency model. The mature force requires both.

### 4.2 Civil Mobility is the largest reconciliation gap

The seven Civil Mobility and Continuity families are:

- household collection;
- continuity transfer nodes;
- civil road mobility;
- civil rail mobility;
- civil air mobility;
- civil water mobility; and
- return and durable continuity.

None has a distinct Pass 23 line. This is not because movement was ignored. Strategic air, tactical distribution, aeromedical movement, littoral access and other transport capacities were counted. The omission arises because earlier work treated person movement as a subset of common-user logistics. Later Harris County and New York research showed that seats and tonnes do not close the human chain. Household contact, building access, caregivers, mobility devices, clinical separation, identity, custody, destination admission, benefits, property and return are separate operational obligations.

Adding Civil Mobility will not simply add seven fleets to the budget. Some carriers, nodes and movement infrastructure will be supporting capacity shared with Air and Maritime Response. The new model must assign supported and supporting responsibility and count shared capacity once. The workforce increase may nevertheless be large because contact, collection, transfer, care continuity and return are labor-dense. `NCAC-35` already demonstrates that a neighborhood-scale service promise can imply thousands of deployed personnel when the true origin is an occupied building rather than a curb.

### 4.3 The missing families are system integrators

The fifteen absent quantitative lines are not peripheral embellishments. They cluster at seams the earlier model compressed:

- a resilience information group to maintain shared civil and infrastructure state;
- maritime production to connect offshore utility generation to shore service;
- separate water, power and grid-bridge formations;
- theater hospital and patient-movement integrators;
- economic restart; and
- the seven Civil Mobility families.

These are precisely the organizations needed to make specialized technical capacity usable. Their absence explains why a platform-rich portfolio can still fail at public effect. The missing layer is often not another machine but the formation that owns the interface, receiver, human pathway or transfer condition.

### 4.4 Effect portfolios reveal a different imbalance

The nine civilian effects cut across service ownership. Sixteen canonical families have mobility, access and distribution as their primary effect, while only two have water, sanitation and hygiene as their primary effect. That does not mean mobility deserves eight times the budget. It means movement appears in several operationally distinct chains: common-user force deployment, damaged-node opening, tactical distribution, patient movement, household collection and passenger continuity.

Water presents the opposite pattern. Its two primary families—water restoration and maritime production—average 80 percent contract completeness and both have `R2+` proxies, yet neither has a distinct Pass 23 quantitative line. The earlier utility aggregate counted water-related resources without preserving water formation identity.

Housing, public works and economic continuity is the least mature effect portfolio at 43 percent mean completeness and no `R2+` family. Health and medical continuity contains seven families but averages 46 percent with no complete-formation `R2+` proxy. These are warnings against equating engineering depth with societal closure. The Department cannot claim stabilization if it can create megawatts and liters but not maintain care pathways, lawful occupancy, local institutions, livelihoods and transfer.

## 5. The four relational deficits

The field-by-field screen produces the pass’s strongest organizational conclusion. Configuration work has advanced the “inside” of selected formations, but not the relationships that turn them into a force.

### 5.1 Supporting dependencies

Every formation depends on other formations and prepared local capacity. Water requires power, source access, corridors, laboratories, wastewater and public distribution. Acute care requires patient movement, power quality, oxygen, sterile supply, diagnostics, waste and receiving hospitals. Fire aviation requires sensing, bases, water or agent, airspace control, maintenance, ground containment and public protection.

All registry records identify a dependency hypothesis, but none has configuration-grade evidence that the dependencies can be supplied concurrently under the same damage case. The dependency map therefore records common modes—movement, energy, communications, skilled labor and receivers—as test targets rather than assuming independence. The next generation of campaign models should treat a formation as a node with a dependency pedigree, named safe-zone origins, specific receiving interfaces and a correlation budget.

### 5.2 Sustainment and waste

Every productive formation creates a counterflow. Water treatment creates concentrate, sludge, chemicals and wastewater obligations. Medical care creates regulated waste, oxygen and sterile-supply demand. Debris removal creates sorting, transport and disposal burdens. Population support creates food, sanitation, laundry, solid waste and safeguarding requirements. Aviation and maritime systems create fuel, maintenance, crew-rest and environmental burdens.

No formation has configuration-grade sustainment and waste closure. Existing models use daily-tonnage coefficients or bounded subsystem calculations, but they do not yet prove complete multiweek flows through crew rotations and major maintenance. The force should refuse to count output that externalizes an unbounded residual to the damaged community.

### 5.3 Authority and transfer

Civil priority, operational command, technical release and independent assurance are distinct authorities. A campaign commander can allocate effort but cannot certify a grid interconnection, potable-water release, surgical service, airworthiness, seaworthiness or building occupancy. A technical authority can stop unsafe operation but cannot choose which population receives service. A host government owns legitimate priorities and transfer acceptance but cannot make missing capacity exist.

The corpus defines these roles architecturally, yet no family has demonstrated them as a configuration-grade operating process. A credible formation baseline must include named release points, records, stop powers, dispute escalation and transfer tests. The test program must intentionally create a case in which schedule pressure conflicts with technical or rights authority and verify that the system fails safely and visibly.

### 5.4 Regeneration

The force promise is concurrent and repeated. A formation is not available merely because it has returned home. Personnel exposure, fatigue and family obligations; equipment damage and decontamination; stock replacement; software and data reconciliation; depot backlog; retraining; and recertification determine when it can be committed again.

No current formation has configuration-grade regeneration evidence. This makes national concurrency arithmetic fragile. Rotation factors and readiness fractions can estimate the size of the problem, but only a formation-specific regeneration standard can determine whether the same national force survives sequential catastrophes. Every future configuration should therefore end with a return-to-readiness demonstration, not with the last day of public operation.

## 6. Technology consequence: invent against formation failure

The registry maps all forty-nine records to the six mission threads established by the force architecture:

| Mission thread | Registry records | Governing research question |
|---|---:|---|
| Enter a broken theater | 9 | Can complete formations cross damaged strategic and theater-entry networks by the required time? |
| Reach occupied civil terrain | 6 | Can the force lawfully find and reach people inside buildings and neighborhoods under disconnected conditions? |
| Control an active hazard | 4 | Can intervention reduce the damaging process before escape, breach or cascade? |
| Produce and distribute lifelines | 6 | Can safe utility service reach the point of use per constrained tonne, worker-hour and unit of energy? |
| Preserve care and human continuity | 15 | Can care, custody, accessibility and household relationships survive movement and prolonged disruption? |
| Sustain, transfer and regenerate | 9 | Can public service endure, transfer locally and return the formation to readiness? |

This mapping prevents the technology portfolio from hardening around predetermined platforms. A purpose-built firefighting aircraft competes because it may change time-to-effective-action, base productivity, delivered suppressant, crew exposure or hold probability. A nuclear water-energy flotilla competes because it may change long-duration water and power availability at prepared coastal nodes. Autonomous earthmoving competes because it may change route-release or barrier-construction rates under dangerous exposure. Rapid pipe and conductor systems compete because they may change the mass, labor and time required to move bulk service from production to use.

The acquisition object remains a formation increment. A successful technology program must deliver its production article with crew concept, training, software and data rights, handling and support equipment, spares, depot plan, receiver interfaces, environmental and safety cases, supply-chain map and mobilization tooling. A program that improves one coefficient while transferring an unbounded burden to another formation has not improved the national force.

This standard is ambitious by design. It creates room for technological advances that present institutions would not attempt, while making novelty accountable to complete public service.

## 7. Industrial and fiscal consequence

The registry changes the future industrial map even before it changes a dollar total. Each canonical family implies an enduring systems-integration market: design authority, configuration control, production, depot maintenance, software, training, test equipment, spares, supplier surveillance and continuous improvement. Several could sustain prime contractors at defense-industrial scale.

But the industrial objects are not simply forty-eight bespoke fleets. They fall into three layers.

**Common technical primitives** include power conversion, energy storage, pumps, pipe, conductor, autonomy stacks, rugged communications, edge compute, modular structures, cold chain, clinical utilities, environmental controls, materials handling and condition monitoring. Open interfaces and government data rights should allow these primitives to move across formation product lines.

**Formation integrators** own the complete package for a public effect. They are accountable for interfaces, mass, labor, movement, sustainment and test—not merely for the most visible platform.

**National readiness networks** include bases, prepared receivers, depots, shipyards, airfields, ranges, schools, stock rotation and supplier mobilization. These are part of delivered capacity, not overhead to be cut after program award.

The registry deliberately leaves the Pass 23 `$12.049T` capital and `3.95M` affiliated-billet screen unchanged. The architecture reconciliation is real; a financial adjustment is not yet earned. The next Department-wide model must:

1. replace `I-UPG` with non-duplicative water, power and grid-bridge demand lines;
2. add the twelve other missing canonical families;
3. crosswalk shared carriers, receivers, bases, information systems and sustainment once;
4. translate each family into occupations and component-specific readiness terms;
5. assign capital through a common work-breakdown structure; and
6. rerun the ten-campaign concurrency, movement, reception, sustainment and regeneration screens.

The result could be larger or smaller than the present total. Disaggregation may expose omitted mass and labor; shared systems may remove duplicated assumptions; technology may change force ratios; and empirical evidence may narrow or expand the public promise. Holding the total until that reconciliation is an accounting discipline, not a belief that the old number is correct.

## 8. What should be configured next

The registry supports a balanced next cohort rather than selecting only the most mature or spectacular systems. Five formation baselines should advance in parallel as the minimum diverse proof of the architecture.

### 8.1 Heavy technical formation: Water Restoration Group

`WRG-50` already supplies a strong configuration proxy. The next baseline should add surveyed hydraulics, vendor-guaranteed envelopes, itemized maintenance, water-quality authority, wastewater and residual closure, receiver variants, crew rotation and regeneration. Its purpose is to prove that a heavy utility formation can move from production arithmetic to publicly accepted service.

### 8.2 Mobility formation: Continuity Transfer-Node Group

`CTN-10` should split into prepared, mobile and expeditionary variants and prove queues, utilities, custody, accessibility, clinical separation, cleaning, records, onward release and seven-day labor. It tests whether movement can be governed as human continuity rather than passenger throughput.

### 8.3 Care formation: Mobile Acute-Care Group

The mobile acute-care group should be configured around acuity-specific staffed pathways rather than beds. It must close diagnostics, surgery, critical care, oxygen, sterile supply, pharmacy, laboratory, power quality, water, waste, patient regulation, discharge, referral, replacement staff and clinical governance.

### 8.4 Community formation: Population Support Group

`PCC-50` and `PCC-D1` provide an unusually rich starting point. The formation baseline must prove occupied environmental performance, accessible service, safeguarding, nutrition, sanitation, laundry, waste, workforce endurance, local procurement, transition and civil transfer. This is the test of whether the Department can sustain people rather than operate equipment near them.

### 8.5 Theater-support formation: Theater-Opening Command

The theater-opening command should integrate named port, airfield, rail, road and inland-water nodes; cargo handling; responder bases; movement control; alternate paths; utility independence; public traffic; queues; onward distribution and civil access. It is the formation most likely to reveal that otherwise mature technical groups cannot enter a damaged system concurrently.

These five baselines deliberately span heavy engineering, human movement, clinical service, labor-dense community support and joint theater integration. A force architecture that cannot configure all five is not mature merely because it can configure one exceptional utility or vehicle.

## 9. Governance of the registry

The National Formation Registry should become a controlled Department record rather than a periodic paper exercise.

The Resilience Staff should own the canonical schema and approve creation, merger, retirement and primary-effect assignment. Generating services should own formation configuration and readiness data. Employment commands should validate campaign demand and supported-supporting relationships. Independent assurance authorities should control evidence maturity and prevent a program office from grading its own formation. Programmatics should attach workforce and work-breakdown consequences only after the registry establishes the operational object.

Every change should preserve four histories:

- identity history: names, mergers, splits and retired aggregates;
- configuration history: people, equipment, software, interfaces and approved variants;
- evidence history: tests, failures, waivers and maturity decisions; and
- allocation history: demand, readiness cost, commitment and regeneration.

A formation identifier should never be recycled. A material change to output, dependency, crew, movement, safety case or transfer condition should create a new configuration baseline. Campaign models should reference exact baselines rather than generic family names. Industrial contracts should state which baseline and readiness quantity they deliver.

The registry must also resist bureaucratic inflation. Creating a family generates lifecycle obligations across training, acquisition, depots, doctrine, component structure and readiness reporting. The presumption should favor modules inside an existing family until a distinct indivisible public-effect obligation requires separate ownership.

## 10. Decision boundary

This pass establishes:

- forty-eight canonical formation-family hypotheses across seven services and permanently joint capacity;
- one legacy `I-UPG` record with an explicit retirement condition;
- a reconciliation showing thirty-three canonical families quantified and fifteen absent from the current order of battle;
- a common ten-field service-contract screen;
- eight `R2+` configuration proxies and zero `R3` formations;
- first-order dependency and common-mode hypotheses;
- a mission-failure-to-technology mapping; and
- the next five balanced formation baselines.

It does not establish:

- validated tables of organization or equipment;
- final formation names, echelons or quantities;
- independent dependency or covariance evidence;
- revised campaign packages, component shares, workforce or cost;
- a complete industrial mobilization plan;
- field performance, safety acceptance or local legitimacy; or
- an evidence basis for platform rendering.

Pass 58 has now completed the first registry-based rebuild. The [canonical allocation](canonical-force-allocation-and-fiscal-reconciliation.md) preserves the ten-campaign stress promise, disaggregates the utility line, adds the fifteen missing families, exposes shared-capacity credits and identifies household-continuity labor, strategic movement and responder basing as the dominant new contradictions. The next force-design pass should configure the Household Collection Group unit of account and replace provisional shared credits with item-identified allocations. In parallel, the five balanced formation baselines above should advance toward item-level configuration.

## Conclusion

A mature Department of Resilience cannot be inferred from a list of remarkable machines. It must be able to state which permanent organizations produce which public effects, what each formation contains, what it requires from others, where it can operate, how long it can endure, who may release and transfer its service, and when it has genuinely returned to readiness.

The National Formation Registry turns that requirement into a governed research object. Its first result is sobering but productive. The project has identified a plausible mature force of forty-eight canonical families, yet only thirty-three are represented distinctly in the national quantitative screen, only eight have configuration-level proxies, and none has closed the relational fields that make a collection of units into a regenerable force.

That is precisely the kind of contradiction a defense-scale innovation institution should expose early. It directs capital away from decorative platform acquisition and toward the missing operational system: complete formations, prepared interfaces, shared dependencies, industrial depth, independent proof and repeated public service under damage.
