# RCB-1 — `CSI-25` Rival Care-Cell, Zone-Interface and Utility-Trunk Build-Release Constitution

## Executive judgment

Pass 163 converted a 240-position medical proxy into a real clinical-service-island hypothesis. It exposed a 119-article, 20,060 m², 2,803-tonne and USD 3.284B object with arrival, transfer, release, clean supply, contaminated return, air, power, fluids, staff and repair obligations. It also left three immediate failures: one public-portal loss could not retain the 2.5 arrival case, the controlled-air margin was only 524.8 m³/h, and the high-infection air case failed.

`RCB-1` does not patch that reference article. It creates two technically unlike implementations of the same `CSI-25` service contract and takes both to development-build release:

- **Root A — Segmented Gallery Campus (`SGC-A`)** uses rigid service galleries, centralized environmental plants, larger assembly blocks and relatively few public interfaces. It concentrates complexity in inspectable clean and contaminated utility galleries.
- **Root B — Federated Cell Matrix (`FCM-B`)** uses smaller self-registering cells, distributed environmental cassettes, local isolation manifolds and a dense data-and-utility mesh. It spends more money and accepts more interfaces to reduce the largest local common-cause loss.

Both roots retain 240 care positions, add a sixth seven-position public portal, provide 64 transient process positions, close the reference clean and controlled air cases after one same-domain train loss, and close a 30-percent-contaminated-area / 15-air-change high-infection case after one contaminated train loss. Neither receives clinical, fielding, procurement, substitution or force credit.

The roots are not equivalent. `SGC-A` contains 121 nested serial articles, covers 21,175 m², weighs 3,204 dry tonnes and screens at USD 3.653B. `FCM-B` contains 218 articles, covers 21,464 m², weighs 3,179 tonnes and screens at USD 3.995B. Root B is 25 tonnes lighter and can release a prepared site in a 60-hour planning sequence, but it adds ninety-seven serial articles, 927 registered physical interfaces and USD 342M relative to Root A. Root A is cheaper and simpler to configure, but a gallery-segment loss can place as many as fifty-six care positions at risk, compared with twenty-seven under Root B's cell-local isolation hypothesis.

This is the central result:

> The first clean-sheet clinical-island competition should not seek a preferred exterior. It should compare two different answers to where common cause is allowed to live.

Both roots advance. Selection is withheld until repeated assembly, contamination challenge, utility-fault, service, cleaning, repair, teardown and relocation evidence exists. Exterior rendering remains blocked.

## 1. Decision object

`RCB-1` is a development-build constitution. It authorizes complete design packages and development construction for two rival island roots under a common public service and interface boundary. It does not accept either root as a clinical facility or fielded force article.

The decision sequence is:

```text
COMMON CLINICAL SERVICE CONTRACT
240 care positions + 64 process positions + ten custody streams
                         │
              ┌──────────┴──────────┐
              ▼                     ▼
     SGC-A CENTRAL GALLERIES   FCM-B FEDERATED CELLS
     fewer, larger articles    more, smaller articles
     centralized plants       distributed cassettes
     longer fault reach        shorter fault reach
              │                     │
              └──────────┬──────────┘
                         ▼
      SAME SERVICE, FAILURE, RIGHTS AND EVIDENCE GATES
                         │
                         ▼
        BUILD BOTH; SELECT NEITHER BEFORE EVIDENCE
```

### 1.1 Authorizations

This pass authorizes:

1. one Root A development configuration baseline;
2. one Root B development configuration baseline;
3. exact planning sections through clean, controlled and contaminated domains;
4. public interface-control records for every named service crossing;
5. root-specific article bills and deployment tranches;
6. root-specific assembly, commissioning, cleaning, teardown, repair and relocation protocols;
7. an eight-island comparative development campaign; and
8. a revised USD 45B cross-classified `RCB-1` allocation inside the inherited USD 75B `TCP-06` medical technology and proving line.

### 1.2 Withheld authorities

This pass does not authorize:

- a hospital license or a standard of clinical care;
- patients, clinical efficacy or an accepted outcome claim;
- fielding, procurement or production;
- a national quantity;
- an aircraft, vessel, vehicle, building or carrier;
- mutual substitution between unlike care lines;
- a preferred root;
- a mixed-root island;
- a root-common supplier, controller, credential or decisive evidence chain; or
- exterior rendering.

## 3. Root A — Segmented Gallery Campus (`SGC-A`)

### 3.1 Architecture

Root A makes the utility path physically obvious. Two clean-side service galleries carry medium- and low-voltage power, no-break power, water grades, oxygen, data, sterile supply and maintenance access. Four contaminated-return galleries carry wastewater, contained waste, used instruments, soiled linen and dedicated exhaust interfaces. Care blocks bridge between them through controlled transition walls.

The root uses ten centralized air trains:

- two 130,000 m³/h clean-domain trains;
- four 130,000 m³/h controlled-domain trains; and
- four 130,000 m³/h contaminated-domain trains.

One clean train, three controlled trains and three contaminated trains survive the loss of one same-domain train. The gallery sections carry no air between domains. They carry utilities and access; every air train retains a dedicated supply and exhaust identity.

### 3.3 Root A article constitution

The complete root contains:

- 28 care cells;
- 12 process modules;
- 10 centralized air trains;
- 3 three-megawatt local feeder trains;
- 8 sector branch panels;

### 3.4 Root A design wager

Root A argues that fewer, larger, surveyed joints can make configuration, cleaning and repair more reliable. Its public development burden is to prove that the galleries do not become hidden common causes. A gallery segment may not silently carry a fault across unlike care lines, and a utility isolation that protects equipment but strands patients is not a safe state.

The planning maximum under one gallery-segment loss is fifty-six care positions. The number is a rejection boundary, not an accepted consequence. Root A must either demonstrate local isolation inside the gallery, reduce the segment reach, or prove the fifth island can accept the affected care lines within their transfer clocks.

## 4. Root B — Federated Cell Matrix (`FCM-B`)

### 4.1 Architecture

Root B makes the cell the dominant physical and utility boundary. Each cell presents a clean service face, a controlled patient face and a dirty service face. Air cassettes sit above or beside the cell they serve. Local connection-and-isolation manifolds terminate electrical, no-break, water, oxygen, wastewater, data and custody interfaces. Short accessible connectors form patient corridors without becoming utility trunks.

The root uses eighteen distributed air cassettes:

- four 60,000 m³/h clean-domain cassettes;
- eight 60,000 m³/h controlled-domain cassettes; and
- six 80,000 m³/h contaminated-domain cassettes.

After one same-domain cassette loss, three clean, seven controlled and five contaminated cassettes remain. Root B therefore carries materially more clean and controlled margin than Root A, but it must prove that the distributed control and sensor estate does not create a shared latent fault.

- 5 0.10-MWh local no-break pods;

### 4.4 Root B design wager

Root B argues that more physical boundaries can confine faults and accelerate parallel assembly. It caps the planning local common-cause exposure at the largest single care cell, twenty-seven positions. That advantage is purchased with 1,640 registered physical interfaces, more local controllers, more leak paths, more cleaning edges, more configuration records and more possible partial states.

Root B fails if a local interface cannot prove its own identity, seal, polarity, quality, time, software and custody state before connection. A cell that is physically present but not configuration-complete is unavailable.

### 6.3 Interface-specific laws

**Air.** Supply and exhaust carry separate identity. Contaminated exhaust has no recirculation path into clean or controlled domains. A door or pressure state that cannot be observed locally closes admission to the affected cell.

**Electrical.** Medium voltage, low voltage, no-break power, protective earth, equipotential bonding and evidence power are separate controlled services. Converter-limited fault behavior and rotating-source fault behavior cannot borrow one another's protection case.

**Medical gas.** Product identity, sample, pressure, reserve source and terminal identity remain bound. A mechanically compatible connector is not proof of the right gas.

**Water.** Potable, clinical-process and high-grade water are different services. Each connection requires flush, sample and release. The drain cannot open before a receiving path is known.

**Wastewater and waste.** A full receiver, failed sample or lost destination stops the producing service before overflow. Dilution is not release.

**Data and custody.** Local service continues through external-network loss. Every later reconciliation preserves the original local transaction, time uncertainty, identity and override authority.

**Patient and staff routes.** The route includes gradients, turning spaces, door forces, lift states, handoffs, family or caregiver state and the accepted destination. A passage is not complete because its nominal width fits a stretcher.

## 12. Cleaning, teardown, repair and relocation

### 12.1 State machine

Every article cycles through:

```text
IN SERVICE
  → clinically empty and reconciled
  → dirty inventory closed
  → decontamination complete
  → environmental verification
  → utility isolation and retained samples
  → teardown survey
  → transport configuration
  → arrival inspection
  → repair or disposition
  → reassembly
  → recommissioning
  → service release
```

Skipping a state creates an unknown article, not a faster deployment.

### 12.2 Root-specific repair law

Root A permits gallery-segment replacement, local pipe or bus section replacement, removable air-unit exchange and surveyed reclosure. Any repair affecting a gallery datum, pressure boundary, protective-device setting, contamination surface or egress geometry reopens the associated commissioning gate.

Root B permits cell, cassette, connector, manifold, controller or partition exchange. A repaired self-registering interface must prove more than geometric fit: it replays electrical, air, fluid, data, custody and cleaning acceptance appropriate to that interface.

### 12.3 Relocation campaign

Each of eight development islands completes at least:

- eight complete build–service–teardown cycles;
- two full relocations between unlike prepared sites;
- twelve complete service runs;
- one whole-island exchange with the fifth-island role; and
- root-specific cleaning and repair challenges after controlled contamination.

The campaign therefore contains sixty-four complete cycles, sixteen relocations, ninety-six service runs and at least eight whole-island exchanges. A cycle earns credit only when all serials, interfaces, defects, cleaning findings, repairs and recommissioning states reconcile.

## 14. Industrial and technology program

### 14.1 Why the roots justify a real industry

The unit screens are each larger than USD 3.6B before carrier, production plant, bases, staff, stocks or national quantity. This is not a temporary-building market. A mature program would require prime-scale organizations for:

- clinical cell and portal systems;
- air and contamination control;
- clinical microgrids and no-break service;
- oxygen, water, wastewater and residual custody;
- structural galleries and self-registering interfaces;
- accessible rapid assembly;
- sterile service and biomedical repair;
- disconnected identity, time and clinical records;
- cleaning, teardown and regeneration; and
- public test, metrology and causal reconstruction.

The Department buys executable service options, public interfaces, technical data, repair rights, second-team reproduction and retained industrial readiness. It does not pay merely for stored modules.

### 14.2 Mission-pulled technology

The roots deliberately require advances beyond current practice:

- compact air plants with continuous, local and independently challengeable proof of domain state;
- self-registering fluid and electrical interfaces that reveal wrong identity, degraded seal, polarity, contamination and configuration before service;
- washable or replaceable interior systems whose cleaning state is instrumented rather than inferred from process completion;
- disconnected clinical data systems that preserve local authority and later causal reconciliation;
- robotic survey, placement, connection, inspection and teardown without removing human admission authority;
- reversible structural and utility systems that survive repeated relocation without hidden fatigue or contamination debt;
- cell-local power and environmental controls that fail into declared care-line safe states;
- rapid sterile-service and biomedical-repair cells equal in maturity to treatment cells; and
- public digital configuration models bound to physical serials, tools, samples and calibration ancestry.

Technology earns credit only when complete service survives representative failure and relocation. A fast connection demonstration is not enough.

Four Root A islands screen at USD 14.612B and four Root B islands at USD 15.980B, consuming USD 30.592B before independent instrumentation, destructive articles, tooling, clinical simulation or reserve.

## 15. Comparative proving campaign

### 15.1 Campaign population

The campaign includes:

- four complete Root A islands;
- four complete Root B islands;
- 1,356 installed nested serial articles;
- 320 predeclared controlled-defect insertions, with root-specific allocation by causal burden;
- 96 complete service runs;
- 64 build–service–teardown cycles;
- 16 cross-site relocations;
- at least 8 whole-island exchanges;
- 84 sequential months; and
- 10,800 peak program positions.

The 1,356 serials equal four times 121 plus four times 218. The campaign retains unlike builders, sites, crews, clocks, configuration services, evidence recorders and teardown authorities. Root A and Root B may share the public service contract and measurement grammar. They may not share the decisive technical ancestry that the competition is intended to challenge.

### 15.2 Phases

1. **Common interface and method qualification — 12 months.** Qualify datums, measurement, contamination tracers, utility simulators, patient surrogates, accessibility fixtures and evidence reconstruction.
2. **Root-native subarticles — 12 months.** Build and destructively examine galleries, cells, cassettes, connectors, manifolds, partitions and repair processes.
3. **First complete islands — 14 months.** Assemble one island per root, close all gates, conduct no-patient service simulations and tear down.
4. **Repeated builds and faults — 16 months.** Exercise all eight islands through build, utility fault, contamination, cleaning and repair.
5. **Relocation and island exchange — 16 months.** Cross unlike sites, impose carrier and receiver variability, and exchange the fifth-island role.
6. **Independent reproduction and selection dossier — 14 months.** Rebuild from public packages, reconcile all adverse evidence and issue a root decision or retain both.

Sequential months are not equivalent to elapsed calendar if phases overlap. The workbook uses eighty-four months as the governed campaign horizon and does not infer a schedule acceleration.

### 15.3 Selection law

The selection board asks in order:

1. Does the root close every common service and civil-rights gate?
2. Does it retain every required service after the declared single fault?
3. Does it prevent cross-domain contamination and prove that state independently?
4. Does it enter the declared care-line safe states under compound loss?
5. Can a second team reproduce the build and evidence from the public package?
6. Can it complete repeated cleaning, repair, teardown and relocation without accumulating an unknown state?
7. Only then: which root has the better verified time, labor, mass, capital and support burden?

The board may select one root, retain both for different site classes, demand a new root, or reject both. It may not average their evidence into one fictional design.
