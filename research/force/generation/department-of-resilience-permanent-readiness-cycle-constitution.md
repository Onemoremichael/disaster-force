## Branch and resolution

The deepest live branch is `DFG-3`, the third of five permitted Department force-generation passes.

**Resolution test:** reversing the readiness cycle would change the number actually available for ten campaigns, the integrity of the protected reserve, geographic-loss survival, the use of USD 2.11T in annual operations and whether the force can regenerate without going hollow; the object passes at Department scale.

Pass 192 created the build campaign. It did not determine where each formation lives after production: committed, waiting, proving, repairing or rebuilding.[^1]

## Decision: a family-specific rolling lattice

Five architectures face nine conjunctive gates. A fixed annual rotation cannot accommodate 56–281-day occupations. A one-third deploy/train/maintain rule fails the inherited ready fractions. Service-owned cycles break joint mission availability. A readiness market allows the same scarce people and receivers to be sold twice. Only a **family-specific rolling lattice** preserves each family floor, long occupation, crew relief, district loss, protected reserve, joint proof, overhaul overlap, international custody and public truth.

The 5,251 formations occupy six non-fungible accounts:

| Account | Formations | Readiness treatment |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Committed campaign force | 2,420 | Ready but occupied by the ten-campaign promise |
| Protected reserve | 502 | Ready; activation requires explicit reserve authority |
| Geographic and rounding buffer | 745 | Ready; preserves family-specific loss margins |
| Requalification and exercise | 514 | Not ready |
| Maintenance | 527 | Not ready |
| Deep overhaul and regeneration | 543 | Not ready |

The first three sum to the inherited **3,667 instantaneous ready floor**. The latter three conserve the remaining **1,584** formations. These are accounts, not permanent unit classes; serials rotate while the family totals remain invariant.

This corrects an aggregate shortcut. Twenty percent of 2,420 is 484, but a heterogeneous force cannot hold 0.2 of a formation. Applying the reserve separately to forty-eight families produces **502** formations. The eighteen-formation difference is the cost of preserving every scarce family rather than rounding at Department level.

## Geography and loss

Ready serials distribute across seven districts as evenly as integers permit. No family places more than `ceiling(ready family / 7)` in one district. The model tests all 336 family–district cells. Loss of the largest allocation in every family removes 546 ready formations, leaving 3,121—**199 above** the integerized protected demand of 2,922. All forty-eight families pass independently.[^2]

The 745-formation geographic account is therefore not a spare fleet available for routine use. It absorbs district concentration, family rounding and transition friction. An aggregate national surplus cannot cure a failed water, clinical, access or mobility family.

## Time, people and custody

A deployed article remains committed through preparation, movement, 56–281 days of mission occupation, withdrawal, quarantine, repair and recertification. Transfer of crews does not release the article. The inherited family factors create **15,123.6 crew-set equivalents**, generally 2.5–3.4 per formation. They are already represented inside the 11.5 million affiliated-billet screen; they do not multiply the number of formations.[^3]

The three non-ready accounts have different exit evidence. Requalification requires a no-notice complete-formation trial. Maintenance requires restored stocks and technical release. Deep overhaul requires a cold-rebuilt or replacement serial to earn all ten warrants before its predecessor leaves service. Reference 30-, 90- and 365-day clocks are operational hypotheses, not universal maintenance intervals.

International custody remains hard. Once invited capacity is accepted, its exact serials enter the committed account. They remain unavailable domestically through employment, transfer, withdrawal and return. Only bilateral release followed by domestic recertification restores them to the family pool.

## Continuous proof and operating economy

Joint readiness cannot be an annual ceremonial exercise. Each of six mission systems runs one adversarial campaign rotation each quarter—twenty-four trials annually—so all ten campaign worlds recur, including the weakest prior cell. Every trial requires interface evidence and civil admission; planned events earn no authority.

The inherited USD 2.11T mature annual operating screen is cross-classified to expose the readiness economy: 40% people, crews and household continuity; 22% maintenance and overhaul; 15% stocks and residual custody; 10% bases and receivers; 8% proving and recertification; and 5% strategic mobility and reserve availability. These are transparent planning shares, not a new appropriation or spend-to-readiness formula.

Technology at this scale includes a reconstructable formation-state graph; a governor that rejects assignments breaking any family floor; causal overhaul scheduling; crew-endurance twins; civil receiver reservation; bilateral custody ledgers; adversarial quarterly mission worlds; autonomous cold-rebuild cells; distributed timing roots; and a public rollback observatory. This is the nervous system of a permanent force, not a dashboard layered over self-reported units.
