# F36 `CISA-40` `SA6` Root A Clinical Utility, Acuity and Medical-Continuity Service Contract

**Research object:** `RBZ-1`

**Program object:** `CCSC-1` — Clinical Continuity Service Contract

**Reference formation:** `HCSG-100` — Health Continuity Service Group for a declared 100,000-person region

**Parent objects:** `RBY-1 / OSPL-1`, `RBX-1 / MPSC-1`, `H-THC`, `H-PMG`, `PRG-100`, Health Protection Service

**Status:** platform-neutral service, demand, formation and representative-campaign constitution; no clinical efficacy, national quantity, common-force credit, procurement, fielding, site, hull, vehicle or exterior-form authority

---

## Executive finding

The Department of Resilience should not define medical force by beds, tents, hulls or electrical load. It should define a **completed appropriate clinical episode**: a person in a known state reaches the right staffed and supplied care, receives it within the applicable system time, retains identity and rights, and leaves through an accepted transfer or supported discharge without an avoidable break in therapy.

That rule changes the force.

`RBZ-1` replaces the inherited 22 MW hospital challenge with a provisional but complete service kernel. The reference mission supports a declared 100,000-person region for thirty days after local clinical capacity has been reduced unevenly and demand has changed by care line. Ten care lines produce unlike obligations. Their daily counts may not be added into one false patient total because a community medication contact, an emergency episode, an occupied intensive-care day, an operation and a dialysis session are different transactions.

The planning model derives:

- 3,850 community-continuity contacts per day;
- 412.5 emergency and resuscitation episodes per day;
- 951 required staffed treatment positions across all ten care lines;
- 11,215.5 direct clinical work-hours per day;
- a 1,613-person direct clinical roster and 4,400-person deployed formation;
- 51.9275 accepted definitive transfers per day;
- 529.17 supported discharges or continuing-care handoffs per day;
- 17.4098 MW net continuous electrical demand and 20.02127 MW peak;
- 9.7887 MW of no-break load and 7.979918 MWh of required storage nameplate;
- 7,696.25 m³/day of oxygen production capacity after reserve;
- 1,053.8625 m³/day of protected water production;
- 948.47625 m³/day of wastewater treatment;
- 4.441281 t/day of segregated medical-waste treatment;
- 3,707.52 laboratory tests per day of protected capacity; and
- 174.33 blood units per day with a seven-day protected stock objective.

These are design hypotheses generated from disclosed rates, multipliers, retained local capacity and resource coefficients. They are not population epidemiology, clinical guidance or observed disaster demand.

The selected planning architecture is a **federated clinical lattice**. Four clinical service islands carry the reference obligation. A fifth is a ready replacement. Each care line is divided independently, so loss of one island leaves the other four with enough positions in that same care line. Community continuity, specialty support, patient regulation, discharge support, medical utilities, workforce care, repair and evidence remain visible formations around those islands.

The complete planning bill contains 144 controlled articles, 31,217 dry tonnes and USD 25.077B of unit capital. It includes five clinical service islands, ten community units, three specialty-support nodes, five patient-regulation and movement cells, six discharge-continuity cells, clinical utilities, the Pass 161 power lattice and complete support. It excludes consumable inventory, primary energy, strategic transport, bases, receiver construction, national reserve, replacement production and mature operations.

The service-derived electrical requirement is below the inherited 22 MW diagnostic allocation by 4.5902 MW. Five 5/7 MW source clusters satisfy reserve, derating, continuous-loss and peak-loss rules; Pass 161 provisionally carried six. The difference is not a saving. It is evidence that medical power must follow a clinical service contract. At 125,000 people under the same coefficients, the model reaches 21.7327 MW and requires six clusters. A different acuity mix, retained local capacity, infection state or receiver topology can also reverse the result.

Three 10 MW / 5 MWh storage articles remain required because the medical load is governed by a 9.7887 MW no-break subset and a thirty-minute ride-through requirement. One storage article may be lost while 20 MW and 10 MWh remain. The source count changes; the storage count does not.

The formation also requires protected non-electrical utilities. Three 4,000 m³/day oxygen generators leave 8,000 m³/day after one loss. Five 4,000 m³ oxygen-reserve banks leave 16,000 m³, enough for forty-eight hours of reserved demand. Four 400 m³/day water units, four 720 m³ storage units, four 350 m³/day wastewater units and three 2.5 t/day medical-waste units each close their own single-loss screen. None may be replaced by a generic logistics tonnage.

`TCP-06`, the inherited USD 75B clinical and shelter continuity technology line, is allocated once across patient-state compilation, modular care, infection control, clinical utilities, diagnostics, workforce systems, patient flow, receiving continuity and representative proving. The broader Pass 160 failure reserve remains outside this allocation.

No exterior rendering is authorized. A platform image would still choose a land campus, ship, barge, aircraft-carried system or fixed-facility reinforcement package before the care block, patient-flow, utility, transport, mass and failure geometry has been constituted.

The next object is `RCA-1`: the `CSI-25` clinical-service-island internal configuration, patient-flow, utility one-line, clean–controlled–contaminated zoning and article constitution.

## 1. Why a medical force needs a service constitution

### 1.1 The inherited gap

The [five-portfolio compiler](f36-cisa40-sa6-root-a-five-portfolio-concurrent-service-compiler-common-force-quantity-and-technology-convergence-constitution.md) carried two bounded medical proxies:

- fifty-two `H-THC` theater hospital commands; and
- ninety-one `H-PMG` patient movement groups.

Their personnel, mass and capital lines were useful as allocation placeholders. They did not specify population, demand by acuity, care duration, clinical positions, specialty staffing, oxygen, water, waste, infection separation, receiving capacity or discharge.

The [open-service power lattice](f36-cisa40-sa6-root-a-open-service-power-lattice-cross-portfolio-translation-and-representative-substitution-constitution.md) therefore refused to treat 22 MW as medical force capacity. It retained the number only as a diagnostic load from `PRG-100`.

`RBZ-1` addresses that refusal. It asks what health service the force is promising, what resources make the promise executable and what must remain specific to medical use even when power, water, mobility or communications are nationally common.

### 1.2 Research question

What complete, platform-neutral medical-service formation can preserve a declared regional care pathway for thirty days after local capacity and infrastructure fail, while surviving one clinical-island loss and one loss within each governing utility family?

### 1.3 Unit of account

The unit of account is not a bed-day alone. It is:

> one completed appropriate clinical episode, bound to patient state, time, staff, place, utilities, supplies, evidence, transfer and continuing care.

A bed can exist without a nurse, oxygen, laboratory, sterile supply, receiving clinician or discharge destination. It is then furniture, not capacity.

### 1.4 Scope

This pass:

- creates one 100,000-person, thirty-day reference mission;
- separates ten care lines and six acuity states;
- derives force obligation from changed demand and retained local capacity;
- sizes care positions, direct clinical work and workforce depth;
- creates accepted-transfer and supported-discharge requirements;
- derives power, no-break storage, oxygen, water, wastewater, medical waste, laboratory and blood requirements;
- competes five whole-system relationships;
- defines a five-island clinical lattice and a twenty-family formation bill;
- allocates unit capital and inherited clinical technology funding;
- defines eighteen failure worlds and a 160-article representative campaign; and
- preserves every prior force and authority boundary.

It does not:

- assert observed incidence, prevalence, mortality or clinical effectiveness;
- prescribe patient treatment;
- select a clinical technology, hospital design, hull, vehicle or site;
- derive national formation quantity;
- establish staffing credentials or licensure in any jurisdiction;
- approve a production line, medical device, drug, biologic or facility;
- reduce `H-THC`, `H-PMG`, power or common-force quantities; or
- authorize exterior rendering.

## 2. Clinical service grammar

### 2.1 Six patient states

`CCSC-1` uses a service state rather than a platform label.

| State | System meaning | Typical service boundary | Completion condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| `A0` | continuity and public-health support | medication, device, oxygen, surveillance, outreach | continuing therapy or safe self-care is restored and recorded |
| `A1` | ambulatory or urgent | assessment and bounded treatment | episode closes locally or advances under accepted referral |
| `A2` | monitored stabilization | emergency, observation, diagnostics | patient leaves monitoring safely, enters inpatient care or departs by accepted transfer |
| `A3` | inpatient and controlled isolation | ward, infectious, renal and continuing treatment | care course reaches supported discharge or higher-acuity transfer |
| `A4` | critical or procedural | intensive care, surgery, maternal and neonatal intervention | critical episode reaches stable next care under complete custody |
| `A5` | definitive specialty or long recovery | care outside the deployable formation | named receiver accepts the patient and continuing pathway |

Acuity is a controlled state that may change. It is not a permanent label attached at intake. Every transition preserves the prior state, decision, clinician, time and destination.

### 2.2 Episode closure law

A local episode is complete only when all required terms are true:

```text
episode complete = identity and authority
                 AND state and priority assigned
                 AND qualified team available
                 AND correct clinical position available
                 AND utility and supply state valid
                 AND care action and result recorded
                 AND adverse state controlled
                 AND accepted transfer OR supported discharge
```

The conjunction prevents one abundant resource from hiding another absence.

### 2.3 Time is clinical capacity

The formation must distinguish:

- time to population contact;
- time from arrival to assessment;
- time to resuscitation or procedure;
- occupied care duration;
- time awaiting diagnostics;
- time awaiting transfer acceptance;
- transport time under the correct escort state;
- time to discharge support; and
- time until staff, room, instrument and utility state can be reused.

A patient waiting for an unavailable receiver still occupies staff, position, oxygen and risk capacity. Queue time therefore remains inside the service account.

### 2.4 Rights and authority

Emergency conditions do not erase:

- patient identity and correction;
- consent or recorded emergency authority;
- privacy and minimum necessary disclosure;
- disability and language access;
- caregiver and dependent continuity;
- medication and device custody;
- clinical refusal and escalation;
- adverse-event review;
- death identification and family notification; or
- local, Tribal, territorial and partner clinical authority.

The Department may carry systems that keep these functions alive. It may not convert their absence into a throughput advantage.

## 3. Reference mission

### 3.1 Declared boundary

`HCSG-100` supports a declared 100,000-person region for thirty days. It assumes:

- local care remains unevenly available rather than uniformly absent;
- demand changes differently by care line;
- communications may be disconnected;
- power, water, oxygen, waste and environmental control may be unavailable at receiving sites;
- roads, airfields and ports may support only selected routes;
- definitive `A5` care remains a regulated external network function; and
- the force must regenerate staff and equipment for a later mission.

The population is a modeling denominator, not a promise that one formation always serves the same number of people.

### 3.2 Activation sequence

| Time band | Required service state |
|---|---|
| 0–6 h | clinical command, patient-state registry, public warnings and time-critical therapy contacts begin |
| 6–12 h | community continuity and patient regulation cells operate |
| 12–24 h | first emergency, observation, diagnostic, oxygen and transfer pathway operates |
| 24–72 h | four complete clinical islands and support nodes reach reference duty |
| day 3–30 | full service, replacement shifts, replenishment, accepted transfer, discharge and regeneration operate |
| after day 30 | service transfers, reinforces, relocates or continues under a separately authorized campaign |

These are system-availability objectives. They do not replace a clinician’s time-critical treatment judgment.

### 3.3 Demand law

For care line `i`:

```text
gross_i = population / 1,000 × baseline-rate_i × hazard-multiplier_i

local_i = population / 1,000 × baseline-rate_i × retained-local-capacity_i

force-obligation_i = max(0, gross_i - local_i)
```

The subtraction represents retained local service, not merely an intact building count. A hospital with no staff, oxygen, sterile supply or referral path contributes no capacity to the applicable line.

For a census service:

```text
required positions_i = ceil(force admissions_i/day × dwell days_i
                            / target occupancy_i)
```

For a flow service:

```text
required positions_i = ceil(force episodes_i/day × interaction hours_i
                            / (operating hours_i/day × target utilization_i))
```

The model keeps the two formulas separate. Dividing an occupied inpatient day by twenty-four a second time would erase beds. Treating an ambulatory visit as a full-day census would manufacture them.

### 4.3 Five-island loss rule

Four islands must carry the reference duty. A fifth is a ready replacement. Each care line is divided independently:

```text
positions per island_i = ceil(required positions_i / 4)

installed positions_i = positions per island_i × 5

post-loss positions_i = positions per island_i × 4
```

The resulting five-island system installs 1,200 positions. Loss of any one island leaves 960. More importantly, every individual care line retains at least its required count. The nine-position aggregate margin may not be shifted between incompatible care lines.

The fifth island is not a shell reserve. It requires staff, utilities, supplies, control, clinical records, transport interfaces and return capacity.

## 5. Workforce as a clinical utility

### 5.1 Direct work

The ten care lines require 11,215.5 direct clinical work-hours per day under the registered coefficients. These hours represent mixed licensed and qualified work. They are not yet a specialty roster.

At eight effective clinical hours per deployed person-day and a 15 percent resilience factor:

```text
direct clinical roster = ceil(11,215.5 / 8 × 1.15) = 1,613 people
```

The factor protects handoff, supervision, illness, training, documentation and imperfect matching. It does not permit unsafe hours or remove specialty constraints.

### 5.2 Support formation

The reference assigns 2,787 additional deployed positions:

| Support function | Deployed positions |
|---|---:|
| clinical command, ethics, quality and safety | 200 |
| diagnostics, laboratory, pharmacy, blood and imaging | 430 |
| clinical engineering, sterile processing, gases and waste | 460 |
| patient regulation, movement and reception | 420 |
| medical logistics, cold chain and warehouse | 280 |
| records, identity, communications and cyber-independent control | 160 |
| security, accessibility and civil liaison | 180 |
| staff food, lodging, sanitation, health and rest | 407 |
| environmental health, mortuary and forensic custody | 130 |
| reserve technical and replacement teams | 120 |
| **support formation** | **2,787** |

Together with the direct roster, `HCSG-100` deploys 4,400 people.

### 5.3 Establishment depth

The planning establishment multiplier is 3.5:

- one deployed or immediately ready cohort;
- two rotation, recovery or home-station cohorts; and
- one-half cohort for school, depot, standards, evaluation and attrition depth.

One `HCSG-100` therefore implies 15,400 public positions before national command or shared institutions. National multiplication remains prohibited until concurrent demand, home-region risk and shared-workforce identity are compiled.

### 5.4 Technology cannot erase authority

Automation should reduce exposure and repetitive load in:

- pharmacy and warehouse picking;
- sterile-instrument handling;
- laboratory preparation;
- oxygen, water and waste operation;
- bed and equipment movement;
- documentation and translation assistance;
- supply prediction;
- telepresence; and
- facility cleaning and environmental surveillance.

It may not independently assign triage priority, consent, involuntary treatment, discharge, transfer destination or end-of-life action. Clinical authority remains accountable and locally valid.

## 6. Patient regulation, transfer and discharge

### 6.1 Definitive transfer

The model applies care-line-specific transfer shares and derives 51.9275 `A5` patient journeys per day. A valid journey includes:

- current patient state;
- named origin and destination;
- receiving acceptance;
- route and mode;
- equipment, medication and oxygen custody;
- escort qualification;
- clinical power and environmental limits;
- family, caregiver, disability and language continuity;
- event record; and
- confirmation of arrival into the receiving care state.

Five patient-regulation and movement cells each provide a target fifteen accepted journeys per day. Four are required. One is reserve. After loss of one cell, sixty journeys per day remain, leaving 8.0725 per day of planning margin.

Transport departure without destination acceptance is not a completed journey except when immediate lifesaving departure is unavoidable. That exception creates a visible unresolved receiver obligation; it does not create throughput credit.

### 6.2 Supported discharge

The model derives 529.17 daily discharge, follow-up or continuing-care handoffs. Six discharge-continuity cells each support 120 per day. After one loss, five cells provide 600 per day and leave 70.83 of margin.

A supported discharge binds:

- medication and consumables;
- wound, device or oxygen plan;
- accessible destination;
- caregiver state;
- water, power and thermal conditions;
- follow-up time and provider;
- return warning and contact path; and
- transportation if required.

Moving a patient out of the clinical island into an unsafe shelter does not restore capacity. It exports the failure.

### 6.3 Patient-state ledger

The ledger must operate disconnected. It records state transitions locally, prevents duplicate identities and conflicting orders, preserves corrections and later reconciles with civil and partner systems. A missing national network may delay synchronization. It may not stop lifesaving care or erase chain of custody.

## 9. Oxygen, water, waste, laboratory and blood

### 9.1 Oxygen

Care-state coefficients produce 6,157 m³/day of oxygen demand. A twenty-five percent reserve produces 7,696.25 m³/day.

Three 4,000 m³/day oxygen-generation nodes provide 12,000 m³/day installed. Loss of one leaves 8,000 m³/day and 303.75 m³/day of margin.

Forty-eight hours of reserved demand equals 15,392.5 m³. Five 4,000 m³ reserve banks provide 20,000 m³ installed and 16,000 m³ after one loss, leaving 607.5 m³.

Generation and reserve are unlike functions. A generator depends on energy, intake air, maintenance and product quality. Stored oxygen depends on vessel, filling, pressure, transfer and replenishment. One cannot silently replace the other.

### 9.2 Water

The ten care lines require 191.09 m³/day of direct clinical water. Staff service requires 352 m³/day. Sterile processing, laundry, food and housekeeping add 300 m³/day. With 25 percent reserve, production demand is 1,053.8625 m³/day.

Four 400 m³/day water nodes leave 1,200 m³/day after one loss. Four 720 m³ storage nodes leave 2,160 m³ after one loss against a forty-eight-hour requirement of 2,107.725 m³.

Water quality differs by use. Potable, hand hygiene, dialysis feed, sterile process, equipment cooling and waste wash may not share one release standard merely because they share a unit.

### 9.3 Wastewater and medical waste

At a 90 percent wastewater-return fraction, the protected treatment requirement is 948.47625 m³/day. Four 350 m³/day treatment nodes leave 1,050 m³/day after one loss.

Care-line coefficients produce 3.553025 tonnes/day of medical waste before reserve. With 25 percent reserve, the treatment requirement is 4.441281 tonnes/day. Three 2.5 t/day nodes leave 5 t/day after one loss.

The mass total does not authorize mixing. Sharps, pathological material, pharmaceuticals, chemicals, infectious material, ordinary refuse and wastewater retain separate custody and terminal destinations.

### 9.4 Laboratory and blood

Care demand produces 3,089.6 laboratory tests per day before reserve and 3,707.52 after a twenty percent reserve. Three 2,000-test/day nodes leave 4,000 tests/day after one loss.

Blood demand is 145.275 units/day before reserve and 174.33 after twenty percent reserve. Seven days require 1,220.31 units. Three 800-unit protected banks leave 1,600 after one loss.

Test count and blood units are only capacity denominators. Assay mix, turnaround, specimen custody, compatibility, expiration, cold chain, infectious risk and replenishment remain configuration-specific.

## 10. Architecture competition

### 10.1 `A1`: central medical megacampus

One large campus can concentrate complex equipment and scarce specialties. It also concentrates access, power, oxygen, infection, staff-flow and site loss. Nominal efficiency is purchased with a single operational boundary.

`A1` remains useful where a protected prepared site exists. It is rejected as the universal field reference.

### 10.2 `A2`: federated clinical lattice

Four operating islands and one ready replacement reproduce every care line. Three specialty-support nodes, five patient-movement cells, six discharge cells and separate utility families provide service around them. The architecture can be implemented on land, across barges or ships, or through prepared facilities without changing the patient-state contract.

`A2` is the selected planning reference because it is the only candidate that closes all fourteen declared architecture screens without assuming an intact fixed hospital or external receiving network for local `A0`–`A4` care.

Planning closure is not physical evidence. Duplication may prove too heavy, specialty distribution may create staff inefficiency and common clinical devices may defeat apparent island independence.

### 10.3 `A3`: fixed-facility reinforcement network

This architecture sends staff, utilities, supplies and repair into several surviving hospitals. It can be fastest and least massive when receiving facilities are structurally, clinically and legally usable.

It fails the absent-receiver world. It remains a required mission variant, not the sovereign reference.

### 10.4 `A4`: littoral clinical platform

A purpose-built maritime system can carry deep utilities, specialty support and staff endurance near a damaged coast. It still requires patient connectors, weather access, shore regulation, discharge and inland reach.

The ship is an implementation of the service contract. It is not the contract. `A4` remains a future platform competition after internal clinical and utility geometry is known.

### 10.5 `A5`: patient-export network

This architecture emphasizes stabilization and rapid transfer to intact regional or national care. It reduces local inpatient mass when destinations and routes exist.

It fails when several regions are affected, receiving care is saturated or transport is interrupted. Export remains a governed branch of `A2`, not an independent universal answer.

### 10.6 Selection rule

No weighted score may pay for a failed clinical chain. A candidate must close:

1. initial service by the declared time;
2. operation without an intact fixed receiver;
3. `A0` continuity;
4. `A1/A2` emergency and stabilization;
5. `A3/A4` inpatient and critical care;
6. accepted `A5` transfer;
7. one clinical-island loss;
8. one source loss in each governing utility;
9. oxygen generation loss;
10. infection-zone isolation;
11. disconnected identity and medication control;
12. workforce rotation;
13. supported discharge; and
14. platform-neutral instantiation.

`A2` advances to article constitution. The other architectures remain mission and implementation variants.

Equipment cannot manufacture consent, jurisdiction or a functioning community destination.

## 19. Stocktake

### 19.1 What became authoritative

- beds as the primary medical readiness metric;
- 22 MW as a clinical requirement without a service denominator;
- one large hospital as the universal field architecture;
- patient departure as completed medical movement;
- discharge as free capacity release;
