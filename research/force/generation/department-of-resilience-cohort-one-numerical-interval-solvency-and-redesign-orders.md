## The correction to “2.5% design close”

Pass 219 reduced modeled capacity by 2.5% and called a nonnegative remainder design-close. That was a useful falsification screen, but it moved only one side of the balance. `NIS-1` replaces it as numerical law:

> lower capacity = raw capacity × (1 − capacity downside)  
> upper requirement = raw requirement × (1 + requirement upside)

An account closes only when lower capacity meets upper requirement. With 2.5% on both sides, raw capacity must be at least 1.05 times raw requirement. The screen is a public design rule, not a probability distribution or evidence that every form shares one uncertainty.

The workbook evaluates 120 form–account–world cells: six forms, five accounts and four worlds. Forty-eight cells have inherited capacity and requirement values. Seventy-two remain unbounded. Twenty-seven cells close, twenty-one fail and seventy-two hold. The structure of that result matters:

- all twenty-four nominal nonthermal accounts close;
- only three of twenty-four largest-cell-loss accounts close;
- every fatal-keystone and cold-start cell holds because retained capacity is unknown; and
- all twenty-four thermal cells hold because neither peak duty nor surviving rejection capacity has a finite form-native interval.

No form closes. Their governing largest-loss ratios support only about 1.27–1.44% symmetric uncertainty. All six survive a 1.25% screen; none survives 1.50%. The old 2.5% one-sided result therefore becomes a destructive-test sizing reference, not numerical closure.

## Thirty-six unknown coefficients

Each form now owns six coefficients that another form may not supply: calibrated capacity, upper service requirement, cell-loss propagation, fatal-keystone retention, cold-start retention and peak thermal duty. All thirty-six remain unmeasured.

The thermal question is deliberately mechanism-specific. `ET-A-A` needs connector, drying and refuge calorimetry under peak relative motion. `CT-C-B` must contain cable, smoke and mixed-utility heat inside occupied zones after guillotining a trunk. `HC-A-C` must sustain rotor, battery or working-fluid temperature for 72 hours without a shared cooling dependency. `LF-B-A` must reconcile bore enthalpy, conversion rejection, product cooling and reinjection chemistry. `HU-A-B` must preserve sterile, humidity and cold-chain limits across all ten care lines after a cooling-path loss. `RT-B-C` must retain qualified release under the worst process mix after one trainlet loses its largest energy cell.

Missing coefficients remain blank and order `HOLD`. A nominal electrical surplus cannot substitute for any of these thermal balances.
