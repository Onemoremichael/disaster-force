## Executive finding

The first cross-portfolio common-force experiment should not seek a universal generator. It should create a nested, open power lattice that allows several source sizes and energy technologies to share protection, control, identity, transfer and evidence rules while preserving mission-specific energy custody, heat rejection, receiver geometry and technical authority.

`RBY-1` reaches that conclusion by forcing six reference duties through the same sizing law:

- a denied-infrastructure fire-control set;
- an inland flood-control group;
- a 100 MW power-restoration group;
- a bounded 22 MW hospital-district challenge;
- a carried `PTS-5` hard-access cell; and
- a regenerative water-service cell.

Three universal source sizes fail for opposite reasons. A 1.25 MW class fits small cells but requires 111 source articles in one `PRG-100` and 186 across the six-reference suite. A 5 MW class reduces article count but installs 10 MW against a 1.422 MW continuity duty. A 10 MW class uses only thirty source articles across the suite but installs 20 MW against the same duty and enlarges each loss quantum.

The selected planning architecture uses three nested source classes behind one public interface and assurance grammar:

| Source class | Continuous / short peak | Assigned reference duties |
|---|---:|---|
| `SPC-1` | 1.25 / 1.75 MW | carried `PTS-5`; regenerative water cell |
| `SPC-5` | 5 / 7 MW | fire control; inland flood; bounded hospital district |
| `SPC-10` | 10 / 14 MW | `PRG-100` |

Across one instance of each reference duty, the nested architecture uses thirty-nine source articles and installs 242.5 MW continuous. Its source-only planning screen is 5,040 dry tonnes and USD 8.200B. Adding three storage time-shapes, two protection scales and local control produces a **common electrical-core screen** of 10,390 dry tonnes and USD 11.194B.

Those numbers are not a fleet estimate. They are a controlled comparison across six unlike formation instances. They exclude mission energy or fuel, heat rejection, line material, occupied-building interfaces, installation crews, common spares, bases and regeneration. The excluded systems can govern the force. In particular, a power source cannot be common across fire, flood, medicine and habitation if its cooling, emissions, fuel quality, contamination, acoustic state, connection sequence or return path breaks one mission.

The most important result is architectural rather than financial. `ENG-5/7` should not mature as a sealed universal machine. It should split into a family grammar:

1. source-power clusters at several loss quanta;
2. storage classes separated by power and time;
3. transformation and protection nodes at several voltage and capacity scales;
4. local controls that operate without a national network;
5. primary-energy custody variants;
6. mission-specific heat destinations;
7. route and receiver kits; and
8. a common physical and evidentiary transaction boundary.

Four portfolios can now advance from measured relationship to a candidate interface architecture. The medical case cannot. Its 22 MW value is a power-restoration load allocation, not a clinical utility contract for `H-THC` or `H-PMG`. Medical quantity remains unavailable until acuity, no-break load, power quality, cooling, oxygen, water, infection control, patient flow, staffing and return are bound together.

No `G4` cross-portfolio substitution exists. The `C24` holdings of 1,824 `ENG-5/7` islands and 6,080 `PWR-20` distribution units remain conserved. Admitted cross-portfolio quantity credit and capital release remain zero.

The pass allocates the existing `TCP-01` USD 110B technology line across source clusters, storage, protection, connectors, thermal systems, energy custody, disconnected control, full-scale missions and pilot production. It does not add USD 110B to the Department account.

No exterior rendering is authorized. The next object is `RBZ-1`, a clinical-utility and medical-continuity contract able to replace the bounded hospital-district challenge with an actual medical-service kernel.

## 1. Decision after `RBX-1`

### 1.1 The first seam

`RBX-1` crossed eight operating kernels with eleven national-common families. It found eleven exact fire-family bills, nine measured relationships and no representative substitution evidence. Power and distribution form the best first seam because five parts of the corpus expose real quantities:

- `RBT-1` specifies six 5/7 MW energy islands and twenty distribution units per fire set;
- `HCG-100` specifies a 30 MW carried-power allocation for the inland flood formation;
- `PRG-100` specifies net load, gross dispatch, source, storage, transformation, routes, fuel, mass and workforce;
- `P4` specifies cell-local source, storage, thermal, control, energy custody and repair for a water formation; and
- `PTS-5` specifies continuous and peak electricity, seven-day energy, thermal effect, heat rejection and black-start time at the occupied edge.

The medical portfolio exposes a service doctrine but not a complete electrical bill. That absence is included in the experiment rather than repaired with an analogy.

### 1.2 Research question

Which parts of a projected power system can become a Department-wide common family, at what physical scales, and under what evidence can one serialized article serve more than one mission over time without weakening either service contract?

### 1.3 Scope

This pass:

- reconciles six reference duty profiles;
- separates continuous, peak, reserve, derating, loss and storage requirements;
- compares three universal source classes and one nested architecture;
- decomposes the selected electrical core into source, storage, protection and control articles;
- identifies energy, thermal, route, receiver and professional functions that remain mission variants;
- defines cross-portfolio failure worlds and a representative substitution campaign;
- allocates the inherited `TCP-01` technology envelope;
- defines workforce and industrial-root requirements; and
- establishes the next medical research object.

It does not:

- infer clinical power from staffed beds;
- declare current `ENG-5/7` or `PRG-100` articles equivalent;
- convert target mass or cost ceilings into bids;
- select a fuel, reactor, battery chemistry, voltage, connector or vehicle;
- sum the six reference duties into national demand;
- retire any `C24` article;
- authorize procurement or fielding; or
- authorize exterior rendering.

## 3. Sizing law

### 3.1 Source count

For duty `d` and source class `s`:

\[
n_{d,s}=\max\left[
\left\lceil\frac{P_{cont,d}(1+r_d)}{a_dP_{cont,s}}\right\rceil,
\left\lceil\frac{P_{cont,d}}{a_dP_{cont,s}}\right\rceil+L_d,
\left\lceil\frac{P_{peak,d}}{a_dP_{peak,s}}\right\rceil+L_d
\right]
\]

where:

- `Pcont,d` is duty continuous load;
- `Ppeak,d` is duty peak or start load;
- `r` is owned-capacity reserve;
- `a` is the declared available-output factor;
- `Pcont,s` and `Ppeak,s` are source-class ratings; and
- `L` is the largest-source loss count, currently one.

The first term preserves the reserve. The second preserves continuous service after loss. The third preserves the peak state after loss. A designer may not count the same spare once in each term and then subtract it.

### 3.2 Control reproduction

Applied to `D-PRG` and `SPC-10`:

\[
\left\lceil\frac{103.89477\times1.20}{0.90\times10}\right\rceil=14
\]

The loss screen requires thirteen; the peak screen requires eleven. Reserve and derating govern at fourteen, reproducing the parent configuration.

Applied to `D-FIRE` and `SPC-5`, the peak-loss term governs:

\[
\left\lceil\frac{35}{1.00\times7}\right\rceil+1=6
\]

The resulting six clusters reproduce the inherited 30/42 MW installed state and 25/35 MW state after one loss.

### 3.3 What the law excludes

The scalar law cannot establish:

- transient stability;
- motor-start sequence;
- fault current and protection selectivity;
- harmonic and unbalanced behavior;
- black-start chronology;
- source ramp, minimum load or part-load consumption;
- storage state-of-charge and degradation;
- thermal performance;
- fuel or feedstock mass;
- connection and restoration safety; or
- crew workload and repair time.

Those are separate gates, not coefficients hidden inside a reserve percentage.

## 5. The selected common electrical core

### 5.1 Source-power clusters

The three source classes share a common transaction boundary:

- declared net continuous and time-bounded peak output;
- dead-bus start input and sequence;
- AC and optional DC output envelopes;
- grounding and protection behavior;
- heat and emissions boundary;
- primary-energy input class;
- physical support, lift, restraint and isolation states;
- local manual operation;
- signed offline configuration and event evidence;
- maintenance, repair and module exchange;
- safe stop and residual-energy state; and
- return-to-ready evidence.

The classes may use unlike internal machines. A turbine, reciprocating engine, fuel cell, advanced fission plant, closed-cycle system, battery-dominant source or future converter may satisfy the same class if the complete boundary closes. The common force should preserve at least two physically meaningful roots within each scale.

### 5.4 Protection and transformation

Two node classes enter the planning reference:

| Class | Rating | Dry mass target | Capital target | Reference ancestry |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `PTN-5` | 5 MVA | 35 t | USD 20M | small and middle island protection |
| `PTN-25` | 25 MVA | 172 t | USD 80M | `PRG-100` mobile transformation scale |

Node count is based on the greater of source or peak apparent-power duty, rounded up, plus one node where the parent requires `N-1` transformation.

For node rating `S`, planning power factor `phi`, selected continuous source capacity `P_src`, and peak duty `P_peak`, the screen is:

```text
n_PTN = ceil(max(P_src, P_peak) / (phi * S)) + 1
```

The last term is a discrete loss allowance, not percentage reserve. A later mission contract may replace the scalar planning power factor with a time-resolved reactive-power and fault-current envelope; this pass does not claim that closure.

This yields nine `PTN-5` nodes for fire, nine for flood, eight `PTN-25` nodes for `PRG-100`, eight `PTN-5` nodes for the medical challenge and two each for `PTS-5` and the water cell: thirty-eight nodes in all. Flood and medical counts are governed by the larger source-capacity basis after the duty-specific power factor is applied, not by peak load alone. The extra node remains additive because the planning case preserves one-node loss.

The node is not a cable reel. It contains transformation where required, switchgear, grounding, relay or converter protection, metering, fault recording, local isolation, safe tie behavior and configuration evidence. Route material and occupied-building connection remain separate.

### 5.5 Local control cells

`CLC-1` is a twelve-tonne, USD 12M planning article for local dispatch, protection coordination, evidence, manual control and safe-state operation. The six-reference suite uses twenty-seven.

The article must function without national identity, remote optimization or cloud telemetry. It may synchronize records later. It may not defer safe stop or local service release until reconnection.

## 6. What remains mission-specific

### 6.1 Primary energy and custody

Identical electrical output does not make fuel or feedstock common. Fire may need a short, high-rate and smoke-tolerant source. A water cell may need twenty-one days of isolated energy. A `PRG-100` may exploit grid, gas, liquid fuel and storage in one district. A medical plant may prohibit emissions, noise or maintenance near clinical air intakes. A carried cell may accept less efficient conversion to reduce handled mass.

Every energy variant must publish:

- usable energy at the electrical bus;
- conversion efficiency by load and ambient state;
- auxiliaries and start energy;
- feedstock composition and contamination limits;
- tank, transfer, vent, fire and spill systems;
- upstream production and delivery roots;
- replenishment and denied-delivery endurance;
- residuals, emissions and environmental release;
- crew and maintenance; and
- post-mission quarantine and reset.

A nuclear source replaces recurring fuel transit with reactor, shielding, security, shutdown heat, licensing, maintenance and end-state obligations. It does not remove the energy chain.

### 6.2 Heat rejection

Heat is not a support detail. It includes source waste heat, electrical conversion loss, storage heat, enclosure cooling and useful thermal-service rejection.

For `PRG-100`, 20 MW electrical input producing 60 MWth of useful cooling creates approximately 80 MWth of final heat rejection before distribution losses. The parent report specifies the cooling effect but not a complete destination. For `PTS-5`, useful cooling is 1.965 MWth and final rejection is explicitly 4.8 MWth. The `P4` water cell specifies 1.8 MWth process rejection.

These values cannot share one thermal article by multiplication. Air temperature, humidity, smoke, salt, ash, water availability, plume, noise, occupied-area separation, temperature lift and receiver location change the technology. Dry, hybrid, evaporative, liquid-loop, ground, water-body, thermal-store and export-to-user systems should compete behind a common measurement and isolation boundary.

No power article receives mission credit until its heat destination closes.

### 6.3 Route material

`PRG-100` carries 60 circuit-km in the bypass case. The fire set carries twenty distribution units but does not yet assign their circuit length, voltage or protection zones. `PTS-5` has ten occupied-building interfaces. The flood and water cells connect large motors over much shorter but harsher routes. The medical challenge has no registered route topology.

The common family should standardize:

- voltage and insulation classes;
- fault and protection zones;
- connectors and terminations;
- physical identity and test points;
- section isolation and bypass;
- robotic placement and recovery interfaces; and
- sanitary and occupied-area separation.

It should not force every mission into one conductor, route length or installation method.

### 6.4 Receiver and public release

Power becomes service only after a qualified receiver accepts it. The receiving boundary includes survey, source impedance, grounding, protection, load inventory, accessible public use, building condition, meter, authority, safe energization and transfer back to civil operation.

Medical, potable-water, high-hazard pump, occupied-building and general utility receivers need different technical releases. Common equipment cannot merge those authorities.

## 7. Transport and physical composition

### 7.1 Mass comparisons

The nested source architecture is the lightest declared source comparator across the six-reference suite, but the difference is modest:

| Architecture | Source articles | Continuous MW installed | Source dry t | Source capital USD B |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| universal `SPC-1` | 186 | 232.5 | 4,650 | 9.300 |
| universal `SPC-5` | 51 | 255.0 | 5,610 | 9.180 |
| universal `SPC-10` | 30 | 300.0 | 6,000 | 9.600 |
| nested `SPC-1/5/10` | 39 | 242.5 | 5,040 | 8.200 |

The selected common core reaches 10,390 tonnes after storage, protection and control. This remains below the sum of complete formation masses because the screen excludes routes, heat, energy, support and mission equipment.

### 7.2 Handled states

Each class must define at least five physical states:

1. stored and maintained;
2. transport safe;
3. handled and cross-loaded;
4. installed and energized; and
5. isolated, contaminated, damaged or returning.

Mass, center of gravity, lift, restraint, residual energy, coolant, fuel, fire separation and evidence change across those states. A nominal module mass is insufficient.

### 7.3 Access constraint

`SPC-1` is the only source class assigned to the air- and terrain-carried cell. Its 25-tonne target is below one governed 30-tonne load position, but storage and protection remain separate loads. Three source articles, four storage articles, two protection nodes and three control cells total 381 tonnes before heat, energy and routes. That is a major share of a 580-tonne dry `PTS-5` cell and therefore a direct challenge to the inherited payload allocation.

This is an important falsification. The current `TAFG-1` cell assigns 82 tonnes to firm power conversion and 42 tonnes to black start. The nested common core assigns 75 and 200 tonnes respectively before heat rejection. The storage target does not fit the inherited 42-tonne allocation.

`RBY-1` does not hide the conflict. The representative campaign must either:

- demonstrate much higher specific energy;
- reduce the six-hour black-start service duty through staged load pickup;
- qualify a non-battery dark-start source;
- allocate more payload mass and recompile the carrier; or
- abandon common storage for a mission-specific high-specific-energy architecture.

The source translation advances. The carried storage translation remains held.

## 8. Failure worlds

### 8.1 Conjunctive worlds

| World | Challenge | Required result |
|---|---|---|
| `PW0` | normal cold start | stable local service and complete evidence |
| `PW1` | largest source cluster removed | continuous and peak service remain within contract |
| `PW2` | storage article isolated | safe source operation and declared reduced ride-through |
| `PW3` | one protection node unavailable | protected loads remain served without unsafe tie |
| `PW4` | primary energy lot contaminated | unlike energy root or lawful controlled service reduction |
| `PW5` | final heat destination blocked | no unsafe operation; protected service or safe stop |
| `PW6` | national network and time source absent | local control, identity, protection and records remain valid |
| `PW7` | wrong-scale or wrong-version article presented | physical and logical rejection before energization |
| `PW8` | mission article transferred after fire contamination | receiver rejects until verified decontamination and recertification |
| `PW9` | two portfolios request the same serialized cluster | one warrant issued; the uncovered obligation remains visible |
| `PW10` | one supplier root and one depot unavailable | retained obligations close through independent roots and repair |
| `PW11` | utility reconnection attempt during island service | safe synchronization, transfer and separation without public-service cliff |
| `PW12` | exhausted crew or missing technical authority | no energization credit; substitute qualified team or lawful stop |

| `PW14` | flood motor-start and wet fault | peak, protection, isolation and safe recovery close together |
| `PW15` | carried-cell mass or carrier loss | remaining payload still produces a complete public-service chain |

Capacity arithmetic closes only `PW1` at planning level. Every other world requires configuration and physical evidence.

### 8.2 Cross-portfolio sequence

The decisive experiment is not five separate demonstrations. One serialized `SPC-5` article should complete a controlled sequence:

1. fire pump and smoke duty;
2. withdrawal, contamination assessment and return;
3. flood pump and wet-fault duty;
4. return and reconfiguration;
5. medical-quality electrical and no-break challenge;
6. return and reconfiguration; and
7. a second fire or flood mission while another article occupies the first return lane.

The sequence tests whether exchange time, contamination, repairs, software, connectors, crew qualification and evidence permit the same physical article to serve several missions over time. A clean new article in each test proves interface compatibility, not substitution.

### 9.4 Admission rule

A configuration reaches `G4` for one named mission pair only when:

- both missions have complete service contracts;
- the same serialized article performs both missions in the required order;
- every mission-specific variant and adapter is listed;
- transition and return fit the reservation clocks;
- the crew and technical authorities are qualified and not double counted;
- one supplier root and one return site can be withheld;
- contamination and software state are reconstructed independently;
- all invalid attempts remain in the denominator; and
- the next simultaneous-demand schedule still closes.

`G4` is granted per mission pair, class, configuration and revision. It is not a family-wide marketing label.

## 11. Industrial architecture

### 11.1 Roots by decisive function

Supplier plurality is insufficient. The Department must map physical roots for:

- prime conversion machines;
- power semiconductors and controls;
- storage cells, packs and fire barriers;
- magnetic materials and transformers;
- switchgear, relays and sensors;
- high-power connectors and cable;
- cooling, pumps and heat exchangers;
- fuel or feedstock processing;
- software toolchains and signing;
- specialty maintenance labor; and
- full-scale test facilities.

Two integrators drawing from one converter, one cell plant, one relay library and one test range form one root for those failures.

### 11.2 Government-owned interface

The government should own the testable interface and its reference implementation. Suppliers may compete on internal source physics, storage chemistry, thermal systems, manufacturing and service support. No supplier should control the only legal configuration key, fault model, maintenance data, connector definition or evidence decoder.

### 11.3 Product ecology

The scale classes support an industrial ecology rather than one flagship:

- small carried clusters for islands, hospitals, water cells and remote communities;
- middle clusters for fire, flood, ports, public works and industrial restart;
- district blocks for utilities, large water plants and metropolitan service;
- storage articles selected by power and time rather than one fixed ratio;
- transformation, protection and connection systems;
- heat-destination and energy-custody variants;
- common test and evidence equipment; and
- allied modules qualified to the same public interface.

The export opportunity is an open service system that recipient institutions can own, maintain and combine with local sources. It is not permanent dependency on an American remote operator.

A Power Common Program Office inside the National Common Force should own:

### 13.2 Mission power detachments

Fire, flood, infrastructure, health and access services should retain mission power detachments. They own duty profiles, mission variants, operating qualification and effect release. Their crews may share a common professional base while holding mission endorsements.

A common electrical operator is not automatically qualified for contaminated fire return, hospital transfer, a flood pump field or a carried aircraft load.

### 13.3 Independent authority

The National Resilience Evaluation Authority should own blind faults, reference loads, destructive inspections, sealed software defects, evidence originals and external reconstruction. The common program office may not grant itself `G4` status.

## 15. Stocktake

### 15.1 What became authoritative

- A single universal generator size is rejected as the common-force objective.
- The planning reference is a three-scale source lattice with one interface and assurance grammar.
- Source count is governed separately by reserve, derating, largest-module continuous loss and peak loss.
- Across the six-reference suite, the nested source architecture uses thirty-nine articles, 242.5 MW installed, 5,040 dry tonnes and USD 8.200B of target capital.
- The source, storage, protection and control screen totals 10,390 dry tonnes and USD 11.194B.
- Storage is divided by both power and time; one fixed power-to-energy ratio is rejected.
- Primary energy, heat destination, route and receiver remain mission-qualified variants.
- The carried-cell storage translation fails its inherited mass allocation and remains held.
- No cross-portfolio quantity or capital credit is admitted.
- `TCP-01` is allocated as USD 110B inside, not in addition to, the Pass 160 technology envelope.

### 15.2 What was falsified

- common output rating as common article identity;
- one generator scale for sub-2 MW cells and 100 MW districts;
- source capacity as proof of black start, protection or public service;
- storage megawatts as storage endurance;
- a generator without its heat destination;
- a hospital load allocation as a medical-force bill;
- air-carried power commonality without a complete mass reconciliation;
- supplier plurality without root separation; and
- target cost as admitted saving.

The source-size problem is now bounded. The decisive work moves to:
