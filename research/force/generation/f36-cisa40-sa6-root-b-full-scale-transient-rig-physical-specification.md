# F36 `CISA-40` `SA6` Root B full-scale transient-rig physical specification

## Executive judgment

The preceding [Root B two-phase relief and deployable-retention technology program](f36-cisa40-sa6-root-b-two-phase-relief-and-deployable-retention-technology-program.md) established `RHT-1`: three materially different architectures, two unlike builders per architecture, two operational replication sites, one destructive site, forty-eight controlled articles, twenty-four protocols and 306 events. It also exposed a problem that cannot be resolved by another calculation. The inherited single 500 mm route misses the cold half-area transfer target under the declared screen, but the alternative candidates interact differently with source blowdown, flashing, line inventory, receiver state, containment, heat rejection and terminal disposition. A useful experiment must reproduce the full transient while remaining neutral among those alternatives.

This pass creates `RHP-1`, the physical specification for that experimental institution. Its governing decision is:

> Build six publicly owned, full-scale transient rigs—two at each of the three proving campuses—with a common source state and measurement coordinate system, but no common downstream geometry. The public boundary ends at a 0.90 m source flange. Each candidate supplies its own nozzle cassette, route topology, receiver and terminal hardware.

That boundary is the central scientific choice. A common pipe, separator or receiver would appear economical but would silently select assumptions about path area, pressure recovery, liquid carryover and terminal state before the competition began. `RHP-1` instead standardizes what must be comparable: source mass, initial state, external heat history, datum, time, evidence identity, safety independence and facility loads. It leaves candidate physics physically unlike where they must remain unlike.

The six-rig federation gives every campus two complete instruments. `PTC-A` carries destructive and rebuild duty. `PTC-B` and `PTC-C` are operational siblings capable of independent replication. A candidate article moves between public interfaces; the source vessels and public evidence system do not move between sites. Site identity therefore remains visible rather than being averaged away.

Each rig begins with a 2,000 kg source vessel. The planning geometry is a 1.50 m internal diameter cylinder with a 1.76 m tangent length and paired 2:1 ellipsoidal heads. The resulting gross volume is approximately 3.994 m³. At the inherited liquid density of 668 kg/m³, source liquid occupies approximately 2.994 m³, leaving approximately 1.000 m³ of initial vapor space and a fill fraction near 0.750. The vessel design basis is 3.0 MPa, proof basis 4.5 MPa, and temperature envelope 243–373 K.

A transparent thin-wall screen using a 120 MPa material allowable and 0.85 weld efficiency gives approximately 22.46 mm. Adding 2.0 mm corrosion and 2.5 mm manufacturing allowances gives a planning shell near 26.96 mm. A uniform-area mass screen produces approximately 2.99 tonnes of shell and approximately 4.33 tonnes after a 1.45 outfitting factor. With source and a declared 1.50-tonne fixture, the operating assembly is approximately 7.83 tonnes; a two-gravity vertical screen is approximately 153.6 kN. These figures establish facility scale and procurement questions. They are not code calculations, material selections or fabrication release.

The thermal boundary is deliberately over-instrumented. Sixteen independently metered 0.5 MW zones supply 8 MW per rig and 48 MW across the six-rig federation. The purpose is not merely to reproduce one fire number. Independent axial and circumferential zones allow the program to discover whether a candidate survives local heat, asymmetric vessel response, sensor loss, boundary mischaracterization and active-energy denial. A single common heater would produce a nominal total while hiding the spatial cause.

Downstream of the public flange, `RHP-1` accepts three candidate families without converting them into one nominal pipe. `RC-1` supplies one 0.60 m path, `RC-2` two 0.45 m paths and `RC-3` three 0.35 m paths. Their aggregate areas are approximately 0.2827, 0.3181 and 0.2886 m². Relative to the 0.6362 m² public flange, open-area ratios are approximately 44.4, 50.0 and 45.4 percent. The contraction between flange and candidate path is a removable, serialized nozzle cassette owned by the candidate but measured by the public system. There is no shared downstream plenum.

Every rig provides three independent 80 m route bays. Each bay contains eight replaceable 10 m segments and twenty-one support stations at 4 m spacing. Across the federation, the program owns eighteen bays and 144 full-scale segments. A bank of fifty-four calibrated restriction cassettes—open, one-half area and two-thirds area for each bay—creates a known geometric challenge without changing the article invisibly. Candidate route volumes are approximately 22.62 m³ for `RC-1`, 25.45 m³ for `RC-2` and 23.09 m³ for `RC-3`.

Each rig terminates in a common structural pad, not a common receiver. The pad admits up to 60 tonnes of installed candidate equipment and establishes 1.2 MN vertical, 1.2 MN axial and 1.5 MN-m overturning interface bases. Candidate-specific liquid-equivalent receiver allocations are 2,500 kg for `RC-1`, 6,500 kg for `RC-2` and 5,344 kg for `RC-3`. At 80 percent fill and the inherited density, their nominal liquid-equivalent volumes are approximately 4.68, 12.16 and 10.00 m³. Candidate backpressure limits remain 0.15, 0.28 and 0.25 MPa, beneath a 0.30 MPa public hard ceiling.

The facility is a causal instrument rather than a long pipe with sensors. Each rig requires an approximately 750 m² source court, 1,800 m² route gallery, 7,200 m² receiver and holder field, and 1,500 m² control, rescue and evidence zone. Direct occupied area is therefore about 11,250 m² or 1.125 hectares per rig, 6.75 hectares across six rigs. Six non-overlapping 250 m planning exclusion circles would require at least 117.81 hectares before terrain, access, weather and consequence analysis. That is an order-of-magnitude land screen, not a site boundary.

Evidence preserves the eighteen `RHT-1` measurement functions and 504 native channels. The public record rate remains approximately 288.736 MB/s. A 300-second full-rate interval, six-hour one-percent tail, two native originals and 1.35 custody factor produce approximately 402.267 GB per event. The inherited 306-event campaign plus thirty-percent reserve requires approximately 160.022 TB. Sixteen named measurement planes per rig produce ninety-six physical plane-calibration objects. Plane identity, calibration state and time lineage survive every candidate change.

Mass closure is designed before the first source operation. One-sigma allocations are 0.70 kg for initial source, 0.80 kg for vessel residual, 0.80 kg for route inventory, 1.00 kg for receiver inventory, 1.18 kg for holder or condensed inventory and 0.80 kg for destroyed material or products. Root-sum-square combination is approximately 2.19 kg; expansion at (k=2) is approximately 4.38 kg against the inherited 5 kg limit. Passing the allocation does not close an event. It establishes the calibration and recovery performance the rig must demonstrate.

Safety is triply independent at each rig: a passive mechanical or thermal path, an analog hardwired direct-current path, and a diverse programmable path with local stored energy. The eighteen paths may not share a final element or depend on a remote network to reach a safe state. Six site-specific rescue, casualty-extraction, contaminated-recovery and evidence-custody packages are also physical acceptance objects. A campus emergency plan cannot stand in for a rig rescue design.

The source sequence begins with empty mechanical inspection, hydrostatic work, dry nitrogen function, inert two-phase surrogate and cold-soak characterization. A 500 kg `C1Q` quarter-source event, the 2,000 kg `C1` event, compound or destructive work and independent replication follow only after their own authority decisions. Workbook completeness grants none of them.

`RHP-1` requires 372 mission positions and a thirty-month realization program. Its design allocation is USD 410M capital and USD 72M/year. USD 300M capital and USD 48M/year overlap the already constituted `RHT-1` infrastructure and evidence program, leaving a gross net addition of USD 110M and USD 24M/year. The corrected CISA ownership increment is USD 80M and USD 14M/year; shared technology ownership adds USD 30M and USD 10M/year. The resulting `BRD-6R` view is USD 1.378B capital and USD 260M/year. It exceeds the inherited CISA capital boundary by USD 418M while exactly consuming the annual boundary. The uncredited physical-use view, `BRD-6U`, is USD 1.881B and USD 369M/year.

The workbook passes 420 planning gates, leaves all 522 physical acceptance gates open and blocks nine downstream authorities. Its 971 terminal observers and forty-nine sensitivity tests pass with no formula error. Exterior rendering remains blocked. The next useful pass is an `RHE-1` execution constitution that turns the physical rig into exact empty, hydrostatic, dry, surrogate, cold-soak and source-release event cards with abort logic, calibration holds and event-recovery rules.

## 2. Why neutrality must be physical

### 2.1 The false economy of a common downstream train

A conventional test program would be tempted to build one source vessel, one discharge line, one separator and one receiver, then attach candidate terminal devices. That arrangement would be cheaper and scientifically misleading. The shared line would set route area and heat transfer. The shared separator would impose a slip and droplet-removal model. The shared receiver would impose entry geometry, liquid residence time, pressure recovery and initial fill. The candidate would then be tested inside the incumbent architecture.

`RHP-1` standardizes the source-side conditions that make comparison meaningful and permits candidate diversity in the downstream conditions that constitute the hypothesis. This is analogous to a wind tunnel with a controlled freestream and interchangeable articles, except that the article begins at the nozzle contraction and includes an 80 m route, receiver and terminal system.

### 2.2 Common variables

The public authority controls:

- source mass, composition lot and initial fill;
- initial temperature and pressure state;
- vessel geometry and thermal inventory;
- external heat command and measured boundary flux;
- source flange datum and bolt pattern;
- public clock, trigger lineage and evidence identity;
- upstream safety and source isolation;
- challenge-cassette identity;
- structural support stations and public load measurements; and
- event custody, recovery accounting and acceptance authority.

### 2.3 Candidate variables

The candidate owns:

- nozzle contraction and transition;
- number, diameter and construction of paths;
- internal route devices, insulation and drainage;
- receiver entry, separation and pressure history;
- holder, condenser, recovery or terminal-disposition system;
- candidate control logic below the public safety hierarchy;
- reset and regeneration process; and
- candidate-specific failure containment.

### 2.4 Prohibited couplings

The rig constitution prohibits:

- a common downstream plenum;
- a shared candidate receiver;
- one candidate using another candidate's terminal volume;
- hidden changes to challenge area;
- candidate control of the public source clock;
- safety paths that share the candidate controller as their sole decision element;
- averaged calibration across rigs or sites; and
- post-event reassignment of an article, challenge or acceptance rule.

Neutrality is therefore an interface property and a custody property, not a statement of intent.

## 4. Source vessel and preparation system

### 4.1 Inventory and gross volume

The inherited source mass is (m_s=2{,}000\) kg. At declared density (\rho_l=668\) kg/m³:

\[
V_l=\frac{m_s}{\rho_l}=2.994\,\mathrm{m}^3.
\]

For internal diameter (D=1.50\) m, cylindrical tangent length (L=1.76\) m and paired 2:1 ellipsoidal heads, the planning gross volume is:

\[
V_g=\frac{\pi D^2L}{4}+\frac{\pi D^3}{12}=3.994\,\mathrm{m}^3.
\]

The head term represents the pair. Initial vapor space is therefore approximately 1.000 m³ and the fill fraction is approximately 0.750. The geometry permits source-state instrumentation and controlled flashing without designing immediately to a nearly liquid-full vessel.

### 4.2 Wall screen

The transparent cylindrical shell screen is:

\[
t_s=\frac{PD}{2SE-1.2P},
\]

where (P=3.0\) MPa, (D=1{,}500\) mm, allowable (S=120\) MPa and efficiency (E=0.85\). It gives approximately 22.46 mm. The planning specified thickness is:

\[
t_p=t_s+2.0\,\mathrm{mm}+2.5\,\mathrm{mm}\approx26.96\,\mathrm{mm}.
\]

The additions are declared corrosion and manufacturing allowances. This calculation omits head thickness, nozzle reinforcement, external loads, cyclic duty, thermal gradients, local support loads, buckling, fracture, low-temperature toughness and code details. It is a dimensional screen that tells the program it is buying tonne-class experimental vessels, not repurposing shop tanks.

### 4.3 Mass and restraint screen

Using a planning heated area near 14.1 m², steel density 7,850 kg/m³ and the specified thickness produces a uniform shell mass near 2.99 tonnes. A 1.45 factor for heads, nozzles, insulation, instrumentation attachments and frame raises the outfitted vessel screen to approximately 4.33 tonnes. Adding 2.00 tonnes of source and 1.50 tonnes of fixture gives approximately 7.83 tonnes operating.

At two vertical gravities, the restraint demand is approximately:

\[
F_v=7{,}829\,\mathrm{kg}\times9.80665\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\times2\approx153.6\,\mathrm{kN}.
\]

The accepted design must also resolve nozzle thrust, asymmetric thermal strain, route reaction, liquid redistribution, local support load and compound external events. The two-gravity figure is a check against implausibly light foundations, not the governing structural load.

### 4.4 Source preparation and custody

Each rig requires a closed preparation path with:

- verified empty mass and heel state;
- serialized source lot and composition sample;
- independently traceable fill mass;
- controlled temperature conditioning;
- public initial-state acceptance;
- no candidate command over fill, isolation or release clock;
- post-event vessel residual measurement;
- quarantined recovery routing; and
- evidence identity through cleaning and reuse.

The source system must support water, dry nitrogen, inert two-phase surrogate, thermal simulant, quarter source and full source without ambiguous cross-contamination. A change of medium is a configuration change with cleaning, sample and custody evidence.

## 5. Sixteen-zone thermal boundary

### 5.1 Why 8 MW must be spatially controlled

The fire-heated `RHT-1` source state carries an 8 MW external heat screen. `RHP-1` implements this as sixteen 0.5 MW zones rather than one 8 MW command. Across six rigs, the installed public capability is 48 MW and ninety-six separately accepted zones.

The zone architecture can impose:

- uniform axial heating;
- lower-shell liquid-wetted heating;
- vapor-space heating;
- local impingement;
- one-sided environmental heating;
- progressive axial heating;
- selected zone loss;
- sensor-command disagreement; and
- a measured decay after independent trip.

These are experimental capabilities, not promises that every event will use 8 MW. Their purpose is to create known boundary conditions and identifiable departures from them.

### 5.2 Zone independence

Each zone requires its own commanded output, native electrical or process measurement, surface response measurements, local isolation and calibration history. A supervisory system may coordinate commands, but a shared supervisory value does not replace zone-native power evidence. Failure of one measurement channel must not cause the remaining zones to be reconstructed from the common command.

### 5.3 Heat-flux screen

Dividing 8 MW by the approximately 14.1 m² planning surface gives about 0.567 MW/m². The actual program must distinguish delivered heater output, incident heat flux, absorbed vessel heat, environmental loss and source enthalpy change. A successful energy balance must not equate electrical input with absorbed source heat.

### 5.4 Technology-development opportunity

The thermal plant should be treated as a national research asset. Candidate technologies include conformal radiant arrays, induction sectors, robotic burner or radiant positioning, fiber-optic distributed temperature fields, heat-flux calibration tiles, self-diagnosing insulation and high-speed inverse estimation of local absorption. These technologies have broader use in wildfire hardening, industrial-fire response, thermal-storage qualification and hazardous-material recovery.

## 7. Route gallery and controlled restriction

### 7.1 Gallery geometry

Each of the six rigs has three candidate-neutral bays. Each bay is 80 m long, divided into eight 10 m replaceable segments. Supports occur at stations 0, 4, 8 through 80 m, twenty-one frames per bay. The federation therefore includes:

- eighteen route bays;
- 144 physical route segments;
- 378 support stations before any candidate-specific duplicate frame; and
- capacity for up to three simultaneous path centerlines within a bay.

The public support envelope accepts path diameters to 0.60 m. The first 20 m retains a 3.0 MPa planning basis; the remaining gallery uses a 1.0 MPa planning basis pending transient results. All segments admit the 243–373 K envelope.

### 7.2 Candidate route volumes

Ignoring fittings and receiver entry:

\[
V_r=n\frac{\pi d^2}{4}L.
\]

For 80 m, route volumes are approximately 22.62 m³ for `RC-1`, 25.45 m³ for `RC-2` and 23.09 m³ for `RC-3`. These are not negligible compared with source and receiver volumes. Route inventory must therefore be measured or reconstructed in event mass closure, including liquid films, trapped pockets and condensed residue.

### 7.3 Segment purpose

Replaceable segments permit:

- local material or insulation variation;
- known roughness and internal-device changes;
- destructive removal without scrapping an entire route;
- embedded strain and temperature fields;
- controlled low points and drainage;
- transparent inspection after a transient; and
- retention of a witness segment under evidence custody.

Segment joints are part of the article. Their cold contraction, seal performance, axial restraint, internal step, dead volume and residual retention require acceptance.

### 7.4 Restriction bank

Each bay receives open, one-half-area and two-thirds-area calibrated cassettes, fifty-four physical objects. The challenge is imposed by installing a known object, not by partially closing a normal isolation valve. That distinction removes stem position, actuator state and ambiguous flow coefficient from the geometric challenge.

The restriction must be recoverable and measurable after the event. If ice, debris, deformation or residue changes its effective area, the event records the changed article rather than preserving the nominal state by assertion.

### 7.5 Support-frame basis

The largest candidate aggregate area is approximately 0.3181 m². At 3.0 MPa, the simple closed-end reaction screen is approximately 954 kN. The public route frame basis is 1.2 MN. Dynamic amplification, two-phase slug load, branch asymmetry, water hammer, thermal restraint and failure redirection remain physical research questions.

The receiver pad is the downstream analogue of the public flange: a controlled structural and spatial interface that does not impose candidate process physics. ### 8.3 Holder and terminal field

The 120 m by 60 m field must admit deployable holders, refrigeration packages, heat sinks, recovery tanks, terminal oxidation equipment, anchor grids and robotic recovery systems. Public utilities enter through controlled disconnects. Candidate systems must remain safe after loss of campus grid, supervisory network and primary time source.

Wind and anchor design remain open. A declared 1.5 kPa wind screen over 400 m² gives 600 kN, enough to show that a large flexible holder is a civil and structural system rather than a fabric accessory. `RC-1` in particular must demonstrate deployment, compartmentalization and recovery under weather before its apparent mechanical simplicity can be credited.

### 8.4 Recovery as a design requirement

A candidate is incomplete if it can receive the source but cannot determine where the mass went, make the site safe, recover retained material, inspect contaminated components and return the rig to a controlled state. Recovery equipment therefore belongs inside the article interface and event schedule. The last measurement is not the instant of source isolation; it is accepted custody of residual, retained and transformed material.

Storage capacity is the easiest part. The hard requirements are clock identity, sample loss visibility, calibration ancestry, sensor replacement history, candidate and rig configuration, source lot, challenge identity, command history and two native originals under separate custody.

## 11. Safety, rescue and recovery

### 11.1 Three unlike safeing paths

Each rig has:

1. a passive mechanical or thermal path using stored mechanical state;
2. an analog hardwired path with a separate direct-current source; and
3. a diverse programmable path with local stored actuation energy.

All three paths require separate initiation logic, wiring or transmission, energy and final action. “Independent” cannot mean three software votes commanding one powered valve. A shared final element would turn all three paths into observations of the same failure.

### 11.2 Candidate control below public protection

Candidates may operate valves, condensers, holders and recovery equipment within their admitted envelope. Public protection observes candidate commands and may force an independently powered safe state. Candidate logic cannot suppress public measurement or redefine an abort after initiation.

### 11.3 Rescue package

Each rig has a site-owned package covering:

- cold, flammable, toxic and structural hazards;
- independent communications;
- remote atmosphere and thermal reconnaissance;
- casualty location and extraction;
- contaminated approach and retreat paths;
- respiratory and skin protection;
- remote isolation and de-energization;
- recovered-source quarantine;
- evidence preservation during rescue; and
- medical handoff.

The package is accepted at the rig, not once per campus. Differences in gallery layout, receiver field, access and candidate equipment change the rescue problem.

### 11.4 Recovery authority

Rescue authority protects life without waiting for evidence approval. Evidence authority cannot delay a necessary rescue. After life safety, recovery command transfers through a declared sequence so candidate personnel, public operators, investigators and waste handlers do not create uncontrolled state changes. Every emergency alteration becomes part of the event record.

### 12.2 Stage logic

Passing a stage means its event packet is accepted for the exact configuration tested. It does not automatically qualify the next inventory or energy. The source-release board evaluates whether the result supports the next step, requires a repeat, requires a changed article or falsifies a candidate.

The program may retreat to an earlier stage after any hardware change. Replacement of a seal, nozzle insert, route segment, receiver internal, safety element, sensor family or recovery method triggers a declared regression decision.

### 12.3 Abort philosophy

An abort is a designed experimental outcome. The event card must define:

- sensed and independently inferred abort variables;
- thresholds and persistence;
- which safeing path acts;
- expected source and route state after action;
- rescue posture;
- evidence required to establish why the abort occurred; and
- criteria for recovery versus destructive hold.

An event that reaches a safe state after a correct abort may be a successful safety experiment and an unsuccessful candidate performance event. The two judgments remain separate.

## 14. Industrial and technological program

### 14.1 A new class of public experimental plant

`RHP-1` is intentionally more ambitious than contracting for six pressure-test stands. It creates a public competence in full-scale hazardous transient science. The prime integrator is accountable for facility configuration and interfaces, while multiple specialist industries compete in:

- high-rate multiphase measurement;
- large-diameter rapidly reconfigurable routes;
- severe-transient vessels and fixtures;
- spatially programmable thermal boundaries;
- deployable gas and aerosol containment;
- high-rate condensation and cold recovery;
- robotic inspection and contaminated recovery;
- independent safety actuation;
- evidence-native control systems; and
- calibrated failure insertion.

The Department of Resilience should own the public data, interface definitions, reference source states and acceptance methods. Builders may retain protected manufacturing methods, but no candidate should own the only means to interpret public evidence.

### 14.2 Technology pull

The military analogy is strongest here. A serious acquisition system does not wait for every enabling technology to appear commercially. It states an operationally meaningful physical problem, funds competing approaches, builds public test infrastructure and buys down uncertainty through destructive evidence.

`RHP-1` can pull technologies that do not yet exist at sufficient scale:

- nonintrusive phase-fraction tomography for large opaque routes;
- high-temperature, cryogenic-tolerant distributed strain and temperature fibers;
- autonomous receiver inventory estimation during flashing flow;
- self-deploying, self-anchoring segmented holders with native shape proof;
- solid-state or stored-energy isolation for very large paths;
- robotic internal inspection immediately after hazardous transients;
- rapid source recovery with kilogram-class mass closure;
- metamaterial acoustic and impulse protection for evidence hardware;
- digital calibration artifacts whose identity survives network denial; and
- causal event reconstruction across hundreds of asynchronous sensor families.

These are not speculative adornments. Each responds to an explicit acceptance burden in the rig.

### 14.3 Reuse beyond Root B

The plant can later support hazardous ammonia and hydrogen systems, contaminated-water and vapor recovery, industrial-fire source isolation, high-rate desalination process safety, thermal-storage failure, compressed-gas emergency handling and deployable containment. Reuse is a consequence of controlling first principles, not a reason to dilute the initial Root B source case.

## 18. Falsification, redesign and stop rules

### 18.1 Rig falsification

The public rig concept must be redesigned if:

- the source vessel cannot safely span the required state envelope;
- the public flange dominates all candidate transients;
- candidate transitions cannot be exchanged without changing upstream state;
- route bays cannot contain and measure credible failure;
- receiver pads impose candidate process geometry;
- independent safety paths converge on a common failure;
- mass closure cannot fit within the inherited limit;
- native evidence cannot preserve clock and configuration identity;
- one campus cannot reproduce the full protocol family; or
- recovery cannot restore a known configuration.

### 18.2 Candidate-neutrality test

Before candidate hardware is procured, an independent review should ask of every public component:

> If this component were different, could the relative ranking of `RC-1`, `RC-2` and `RC-3` change for reasons unrelated to their hypotheses?

If yes, the component must either become candidate-specific, receive a bounded variation program or be shown insensitive through analysis and surrogate testing. Neutrality is not achieved by giving all candidates the same hidden bias.

### 18.3 Stop conditions for source escalation

Source escalation stops on unexplained mass discrepancy, unbounded receiver state, loss of two independent safety paths, time-lineage failure, challenge-state ambiguity, nonrecoverable route inventory, uncontrolled migration beyond the rig boundary, rescue infeasibility or cross-site disagreement without identified cause. Schedule cost is not a reason to waive a stop.

### 18.4 Emergent redesign

The program should expect the accepted rig to differ from this planning specification. The flange may require another diameter; the vessel may require another material or aspect ratio; route lengths may need extension; measurement technologies may fail; receiver pads may need higher loads; exclusion geometry may dominate the site. A mature research constitution makes those changes traceable and compares them against the candidate-neutrality principle.

## 19. Stocktake and next research pass

### 19.1 What this pass resolves

`RHP-1` resolves the gap left by `RHT-1`: the competition now has a physical public instrument rather than an abstract event matrix. It specifies source scale, thermal zoning, candidate boundary, route length, replaceable segments, known restrictions, receiver loads, site area, measurement identity, uncertainty, evidence volume, safety independence, rescue, workforce, schedule and cost.

It also makes three deeper institutional points.

First, test infrastructure can select an architecture as surely as a procurement score. Physical neutrality must be designed.

Second, full-scale research plant is a strategic industrial asset. The Department should own the reference conditions, interface grammar, evidence and destructive-learning capacity rather than renting isolated demonstrations from candidate vendors.

Third, recovery is part of performance. A resilience system that controls the first two minutes but cannot account for material, protect responders and regenerate the site has not completed its mission.

### 19.2 What remains unresolved

The largest unresolved objects are:

- exact event-card timing and command logic;
- calibration sequences for every medium;
- hydrostatic, dry and surrogate acceptance limits;
- source-lot preparation and admission;
- abort thresholds and post-abort classifications;
- quarter-source progression rules;
- compound-failure pairings;
- exact recovery and reset acceptance;
- cross-site equivalence bounds; and
- criteria for selecting, continuing or rejecting candidates.

### 19.3 Next pass

The next pass should create `RHE-1`, the Root B transient-rig execution and source-escalation constitution. It should produce exact event cards for `SQ-00` through `SQ-09`, with initial conditions, commands, expected causal sequence, independent abort channels, required sensors, uncertainty checks, recovery states, evidence packet contents, repeat rules and authority holds.

That pass should continue to defer exterior rendering. The program now knows the scale and anatomy of the experimental plant, but it does not yet possess physical results sufficient to determine candidate form. The next image worth making is still an internal experimental sequence and measurement drawing, not a vehicle silhouette.
