# RBG-1 — Root A AM-06 source–water–ignition entry-ladder reset

## Decision abstract

`RBG-1` defines the next experimental system after the bounded `AM-05 QE-04` transfer in [RBF-1](f36-cisa40-sa6-root-a-qe04-class-execution-and-architecture-transfer-constitution.md). It does not treat ignition as one more event gate on the unignited architecture. `AM-06` introduces three coupled state variables—energetic source, candidate or public water, and ignition opportunity—and four distinct truths: source custody, water custody, flame state and thermal consequence.[1–7]

The inherited six-class `RAS-1` concept was a safety-case envelope, not an earned escalation sequence. The intervening `AM-05` campaign changed route, receiver, return and evidence architecture while leaving `AM-06` behind at the low-source flame-state frontier. A 500-kilogram unignited class therefore cannot release a 500-kilogram fire. Nor can a prior 100-kilogram flame result establish the new source-contact geometry, the 0.80-cubic-metre receiver, water mixing, thermal exposure or post-fire return.

RBG-1 resets the method as ten twelve-cell physical rungs preceded by one documentary prior audit. The sequence separately introduces ignition energy, water hydraulics, blinded dual ancestry, source inventory, coupled candidate water, larger source inventory and the casualty-area case. Required execution is 120 valid credits; four invalidity reserves per rung create 160 maximum attempts. Valid adverse results remain in the rung and cannot be repeated away.

The ladder uses six rigs, two truth roots, two builder lineages, three campuses, twelve thermally qualified active modules, four operational spares and two sealed forensic modules. The source-exposed module receives at least 1,008 hours of post-fire return. Host alternation may occur at 504 hours only after a new thermal module-exchange campaign; the 336-hour unignited credit does not transfer.

At two starts per week, a twelve-credit rung uses six issue weeks. The last primary returns by week eleven and the rung board closes by week thirteen. Ten strictly nested rungs therefore require 130 weeks. Concentrated invalidity can add twenty issue weeks. RBG-1 adopts a thirty-six-month planning boundary and refuses to borrow future-rung authority to preserve a calendar.

The required ladder provisions 26.4 tonnes of event source and 1,641.6 cubic metres of controlled water. The maximum reserve envelope raises those values to 35.2 tonnes and 2,188.8 cubic metres. Water is not credited as source disappearance. Every introduced litre receives origin, command, route, destination, source carriage and environmental disposition.

The proposed `RAS-3R` amendment adds USD 1.1 billion capital, USD 67 million per year, USD 235 million in mission-led technology and 160 public positions. The revised formation is 720 positions, with USD 1.9 billion capital, USD 330 million annual support and USD 1.4 billion technology. Thirty-six-month stage exposure is USD 1.301 billion. These values fund a new thermal and coupled-fluid test institution rather than disguising fire work inside the unignited class.

RBG-1 authorizes only constitution of `RBH-1`, the first dry igniter-reference rung. It does not discharge an igniter, introduce water or source, create flame, qualify any rung, admit a drainage candidate, authorize production, release a fleet system, permit export or permit exterior rendering.

## 1. Why the ladder must restart

`AM-05` asks whether source can be admitted, stopped, captured, accounted for and returned without ignition. `AM-06` asks whether a fire opportunity can be created, observed, controlled, reconstructed and recovered while source and water remain attributable. The second proposition contains the first but adds mechanisms that can invalidate its evidence:

- heat changes seals, liners, sensors, retained films and module geometry;
- combustion transforms source into gas, residue and unmeasured intermediates;
- candidate water can cool, entrain, spread, emulsify or hide source;
- public water can save people while making candidate attribution adverse;
- ignition success, flame persistence and thermal field are separate observations;
- smoke, steam and aerosols can remove optical access;
- post-fire debris and wastewater become physical evidence and regulated inventory; and
- a safe stop can occur before the candidate proposition becomes decidable.

The reset is not regression. It is the scientific cost of adding dimensions to the experiment.

## 3. Nested release logic

Every physical rung has twelve exact rig–root cells. A rung closes only when all twelve cells credit, every issued module completes return, both builder lineages close, three campus boards agree, two truth-root decisions remain reconstructable and fixed rung accounts close.

Release is conjunctive:

1. `ER-01` requires only `ER-00` acceptance.
2. `ER-02` may prepare in parallel but cannot issue until dry safeing and water custody are current.
3. `ER-03` requires accepted water return and two independent ancestry roots.
4. `ER-04` requires the dry igniter reference, the inherited low-source flame-state record or a declared repair, and the new module baseline.
5. Every water-bearing fire rung requires the immediately lower dry-source rung and the immediately lower water-bearing rung.
6. `ER-10` requires `ER-08`, `ER-09`, casualty receiver capacity, degraded-service trials and outside-campus replay.

A no-flame result after a valid opportunity can credit source admission and safeing, but it leaves the flame-state cell open. It cannot be repeated under the same proposition merely to obtain flame. A valid adverse thermal, ancestry, runoff or intervention result denies the exact cell.

## 5. New module and exchange law

The selected `AM-05` module is evidence for geometry and source handling, not a fire-qualified article. RBG-1 creates eighteen `TMX` modules:

- twelve active modules, one per exact rig–root cell;
- four same-lineage operational spares; and
- two sealed forensic modules.

Every module includes the selected route and receiver architecture, thermally qualified seals and liners, protected source and water ancestry readers, removable residue traps, local heat-flux and atmosphere witnesses, robotic drain and sample interfaces, and a serialized debris/waste kit.

Post-fire return is at least 1,008 hours. The module passes observations at 72, 336, 672 and 1,008 hours; completes twenty-eight work packages and twenty-four independent decisions; closes source, water, gas, residue, filter, debris and waste inventories; and produces two outside-origin reconstructions.

Host reuse at 504 hours is a separate claim. Six rigs execute both ordered module directions across eight challenges: nominal exchange, datum edge, wet residue, thermal distortion, one service denied, network/time loss, blinded cross-talk marker and interrupted rollback. Ninety-six valid trials are required. Failure removes the 504-hour host credit and invokes the 1,008-hour fixed-boundary calendar.

## 6. Source, water and transformed-material accounts

Required energetic source is 26.4 tonnes. Four reserves per rung add 8.8 tonnes, creating a 35.2-tonne maximum. Required controlled water is 1,641.6 cubic metres; reserves add 547.2 cubic metres, creating a 2,188.8-cubic-metre maximum.

Every event closes seven conserved destinations: untransformed source, combustion products, surface and route residue, recovered liquid, wastewater-carried source, retained samples and environment. Water closes supply, candidate return, casualty return, retained liquid, vapor, sample and environment. Source ancestry and water ancestry each require at least 0.995 recovery and no more than 0.005 cross-talk.

Environmental limits remain fixed by rung credit, not inflated by reserve attempts. A replacement adds no allowance. Public intervention has its own ledger and causal clock. Correct public action may deny candidate performance while preserving a valid safety and source-account record.

## 10. Stop conditions and bounded authority

The ladder stops or narrows if the prior audit cannot reconcile the selected architecture; the new thermal module lacks independent lineage; igniter opportunity cannot be distinguished from flame; water origin or volume is unknown; ancestry roots share a decisive reader; transformed source lacks a terminal destination; public intervention is credited to the method; a valid adverse result is repeated; a reserve crosses rung, root or cell; host or module reuse occurs before its accepted interval; thermal fields depend on one sensor family; environmental source or wastewater exceeds its fixed rung account; outside replay requires origin services; or fiscal authority is absent.

An accepted `ER-01` dry reference may authorize planning the first water-only rung. It grants no source or fire authority. Only the complete accepted ladder can establish bounded `AM-06 QE-04` method transfer. `AM-07`, drainage-candidate admission, production, operational procurement, export and exterior rendering remain outside RBG-1.

## 11. Research stocktake

RBG-1 changes the Department design in six ways.

First, adding a hazard dimension resets the experiment. Previous scale success becomes prior evidence, not automatic authority.

Second, water is a second deployed mass system. At the casualty rung it outweighs source by a factor of ninety-six and can dominate logistics, environmental custody and recovery.

Third, fire recovery is part of force generation. The formation is sized around 1,008-hour returns, wastewater, debris, samples and outside reconstruction rather than the visible event alone.

Fourth, safe innovation needs nested fallbacks. The program can learn from igniter, water, ancestry and low-source rungs without issuing the next state.

Fifth, the technology agenda is created by missing measurement and recovery capability. It does not wait for a commercial catalogue to define what is possible.

Sixth, ambition is expressed through institutions that can repeatedly establish difficult truth. The Department becomes a lead customer for coupled-fluid sensing, robotic recovery, thermal evidence and source-preserving control—capabilities that later belong in deployable disaster systems.
