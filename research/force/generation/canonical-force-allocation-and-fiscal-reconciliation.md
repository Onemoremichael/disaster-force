# The Canonical Force Allocation

## Reconstructing national concurrency, workforce, capital and entry around forty-eight complete formation families

### Research state

Pass 58 is a first canonical successor to the Pass 23 joint force-allocation screen. It is a governed planning model, not an independent cost estimate, table of organization, budget recommendation or procurement baseline. Its purpose is to make the mature force architecture quantitatively contestable without hiding missing formations or counting common systems several times.

The accompanying [`canonical-force-allocation-model.xlsx`](../../../models/force/generation/canonical-force-allocation-model.xlsx) is the arithmetic authority for this pass. The earlier [`joint-force-allocation-model.xlsx`](../../../models/force/generation/joint-force-allocation-model.xlsx) remains the predecessor and reconciliation control.

## Executive judgment

The [National Formation Registry](national-formation-registry.md) changed the force problem. Pass 23 had thirty-four quantitative rows, but only thirty-three described distinct canonical families. Its utility projection group, `I-UPG`, aggregated water, power and grid bridging. Fifteen of the forty-eight mature family hypotheses had no distinct national allocation line, including all seven Civil Mobility and Continuity families.

Pass 58 retires that aggregate operationally, adds the fifteen absent families and preserves the ten simultaneous campaigns. The resulting reference screen contains:

| Measure | Pass 23 | Canonical successor | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Canonical family lines | 33 | 48 | +15 |
| Ten-campaign formation-equivalent commitments | 1,750 | 2,420 | +670 net after retiring 76 `I-UPG` commitments |
| Affiliated billet-equivalents | 3.945M | 10.484M | +6.539M |
| Capital with 20% program reserve | $12.049T | $15.179T | +$3.130T |
| Mature annual operations | $858B | $1.736T | +$878B |

The scale is deliberately uncomfortable. It does not prove that the Department needs exactly 10.484 million affiliations or $15.179 trillion. It proves that a mature architecture cannot retain the old fiscal and workforce headline merely by naming fifteen additional obligations. Once household collection, continuity transfer, water, power, grid bridging, patient movement and return become owned service chains, their people, equipment, readiness depth and entry burdens must appear somewhere.

The strongest finding is not fiscal. **Civil mobility is a force-generation system, not a transport fleet.** The household collection and transfer-node hypotheses dominate incremental labor because they own work at buildings, households, queues, damaged streets, civil records and receiving interfaces. A purpose-built aircraft or vessel can be technically spectacular and still be unusable if the force cannot reach a person, preserve care and custody, or admit that person to a working destination.

The second finding is that projection capacity must be rebuilt around the enlarged force. After a declared 25-percent prepositioning credit, the canonical package presents 6.677 million tonnes of early mass against 2.463 million tonnes of inherited 72-hour effective throughput. Every campaign also exceeds the responder-basing capacity associated with its inherited theater-opening commands. These failures do not mean the formations should be deleted. They mean strategic movement, prepared receiving capacity and zero-host-utility basing are part of the force requirement.

Renderings remain blocked. The model has improved organizational and resource closure, but none of the fifteen additions has representative formation evidence and every major shared-capacity deduction remains a hypothesis.

## 1. What is being sized

### 1.1 The unit is a complete service obligation

The model does not add vehicles. It adds formation obligations. Each line represents an indivisible force-generation object that must own a public effect, people, equipment, readiness, deployment, sustainment and transfer.

The forty-eight lines divide among:

- six permanently joint families;
- five Land Engineering families;
- six Air Response families;
- six Maritime Response families;
- six Infrastructure Restoration families;
- six Health Protection families;
- six Community Stabilization families; and
- seven Civil Mobility and Continuity families.

The word “family” is important. A line does not declare one final organization or one vehicle class. It identifies the enduring professional and acquisition boundary inside which competing configurations can be built and tested. `I-WRG` can contain several water-production, distribution and sanitation configurations. `CM-AMG` can contain rival civil-air architectures. National allocation gives those competitions a demand envelope; it does not decide their form.

### 1.2 Formation-equivalent commitments are heterogeneous

The 2,420 commitments cannot be summed as if each delivered the same output. One `J-RIG` maintains degraded information; one `I-WRG` is provisionally referenced to `WRG-50`; one `CM-HCG` is referenced to `NCAC-35`; one `H-THC` regulates a theater health system. The count measures simultaneous organizational obligations, not water volume, megawatts, seats, beds or protected people.

That boundary prevents a common modeling error. A model can compare inventory burdens across families while remaining agnostic about conversion among their outputs. Future campaign work must replace the package coefficients with time-phased service deficits and formation-specific output functions.

## 2. Reconciliation method

### 2.1 Preserve the predecessor before changing it

The thirty-three retained canonical lines inherit Pass 23 people, crew sets, availability vectors, early shares, mass, daily supply, unit capital, component shares and ten-campaign packages. Applying the same 20-percent reserve and weakest-readiness calculation reproduces the prior capital headline to $0.02 billion and the prior workforce headline to less than one billet-equivalent.

That reproduction matters. A successor should change the result because its force logic changed, not because a hidden formula or baseline moved.

### 2.2 Add all fifteen absences

The successor adds:

|---|---|---|
| `J-RIG` Resilience Information Group | shared civil, infrastructure and readiness picture under degraded communications | bounded registry hypothesis |
| `M-MPG` Maritime Production Group | offshore production and transfer of water, power, oxygen, cold chain or care | nuclear water–energy proxy |
| `I-WRG` Water Restoration Group | source-to-safe-use water, sanitation and residual custody | `WRG-50` configuration proxy |
| `I-PRG` Power Restoration Group | safe islanded generation, distribution, protection and thermal service | `PRG-100` configuration proxy |
| `I-GBG` Grid Bridge Group | damaged-substation bypass and utility reconnection | power-system architecture proxy |
| `H-THC` Theater Hospital Command | multi-facility clinical regulation and referral | bounded registry hypothesis |
| `H-PMG` Patient Movement Group | origin-to-definitive-care regulation, custody and clinical transport | health–mobility architecture |
| `C-ERG` Economic Restart Group | payment, contractor, market and local supply-channel operation | bounded registry hypothesis |
| `CM-HCG` Household Collection Group | household contact, building access and complete accessible pickup | `NCAC-35` configuration proxy |

Every new quantity is marked as a declared design-study input. Configuration proxies provide a more disciplined starting point than generic unit labels, but they do not validate national quantities.

### 2.3 Inventory is governed by the weakest complete-readiness factor

For formation `f`, the reference readiness factor is:

\[
r_f = \min(r_{material}, r_{personnel}, r_{movement}, r_{reception}, r_{sustainment}).
\]

With total simultaneous demand `D_f`, reserve share `q = 0.20` and global duplicate multiplier `g = 1.50`, owned inventory is:

\[
I_f = \max\left(\left\lceil\frac{D_f(1+q)}{r_f}\right\rceil,
\left\lceil gD_{f,global}\right\rceil\right).
\]

This is deliberately stricter than adding independent probabilities. If reception is the weakest dimension, an otherwise serviceable formation is not ready. The model still understates correlated loss because several formation lines can share the same base, supplier, carrier, data service, receiving site or licensed occupation.

## 3. Disaggregation without multiplication

### 3.1 Why `I-UPG` had to retire

Water, power and grid bridging are not three labels for one generic utility unit.

Water is governed by source quality, treatment, storage, pressure, pipe construction, testing, point-of-use distribution, wastewater and residuals. Power is governed by load, generation, storage, fault duty, protection, conductor, transformation, fuel and synchronization. Grid bridging depends on damaged-network topology, safe bypass, protection coordination and utility acceptance.

Their equipment, licenses, test programs, industrial suppliers and readiness failures differ. One combined certificate would hide which service is actually available.

### 3.2 Why the children cannot simply inherit the parent three times

Pass 23 already held the National Utility Projection System once as a common enterprise: $1.698 trillion in capital and about 198,198 affiliated billet-equivalents. Copying that line into `I-WRG`, `I-PRG` and `I-GBG` would manufacture more than $5 trillion of utility capital before any operational difference was established.

The successor therefore uses a gross-and-credit ledger:

\[
C_{net,f}=I_f(C_{gross,f}-C_{shared,f}).
\]

The common NUPS capital remains once. New utility and maritime-production formations carry gross reference capital, then receive visible embedded-system credits for plant, anchor or production capacity already represented in NUPS. Patient and civil mobility formations similarly receive explicit credits where they depend on shared carriers, rail stock, road fleets, maritime capacity, bases or utility modules.

People use the same rule:

\[
P_{net,f}=I_f(P_{gross,f}-P_{shared,f})k_f,
\]

where `k_f` is the crew-set factor. A credit is not free capacity. It is a claim that another governed object supplies the people or equipment at the same time and place. Every credit must eventually be supported by an item-level allocation, authority and readiness record.

### 3.3 What the credit ledger currently says

Before reserve, the complete capital ledger contains $14.917 trillion of gross formation and common-system claims. It deducts $2.268 trillion of explicit shared-system credit, leaving $12.649 trillion. The 20-percent program reserve adds $2.530 trillion, producing the $15.179 trillion successor.

Expressed as a bridge from Pass 23, the fifteen families create about $5.852 trillion of gross capital additions with reserve. Explicit credits remove $2.722 trillion, leaving a net increase of $3.130 trillion.

This is a better result than either extreme. Counting nothing would preserve a false $12.049 trillion ceiling. Counting every platform and common system inside every formation would create a false $17.901 trillion successor. The present value sits between them because the model shows exactly which capacity is claimed as shared.

## 4. The workforce consequence

### 4.1 The establishment is much larger than the deployable front

The workforce reconciliation is:

| Block | Affiliated billet-equivalents |
|---|---:|
| Thirty-three retained formation lines | 3,345,320 |
| Fifteen new family lines after shared-crew credits | 5,838,642 |
| NUPS common enterprise, held once | 198,198 |
| Twelve-percent institutional depth | 1,102,075 |
| **Canonical establishment** | **10,484,235** |

The institutional line represents schools, depots, doctrine, test, replacement and technical depth outside deployable formations. It is applied once to formation-direct billets. The NUPS enterprise remains a separate common line and does not receive another percentage increment.

The 10.484 million figure is not a federal employee proposal. The component model distributes work among permanent federal, federal reserve, state and territorial, certified industrial and partner affiliations. “Affiliated” still requires a named authority, activation rule, employer-release mechanism, qualification, currency standard, home-station obligation and replacement path.

### 4.2 Household continuity controls the result

`CM-HCG` is the largest single incremental workforce line. The reference uses the `NCAC-35 A2` formation as a configuration proxy: 7,587 gross deployed people, reduced by a declared 2,000-person shared district-service credit, across 128 owned formations and 3.4 crew sets. It produces about 2.431 million direct billet-equivalents after the credit.

This should not be mistaken for precision. It identifies a unit-of-account problem that must be attacked next:

- if one Household Collection Group truly owns a complete `NCAC-35` chain, national labor is enormous;
- if protected-district operations, utilities, route assurance or civil data are pooled above the group, the shared-service allocation must remain simultaneously available and locally reachable;
- if a 35,000-person demand envelope requires several smaller cells under one group headquarters, the national unit count may increase even as headquarters labor falls; and
- if prepared buildings and local workforces substitute for mobile labor, their readiness, maintenance and authority become fixed-force capacity rather than savings.

The model therefore does not prove the preferred echelon. It proves that household access cannot remain a staff-free dependency of vehicles and shelters.

### 4.3 Other large labor lines

The new direct-billet screen also includes approximately:

- 712,800 for Power Restoration;
- 591,480 for Water Restoration;
- 540,000 for Continuity Transfer Nodes;
- 348,600 for Grid Bridge;
- 310,050 for Civil Road Mobility; and
- 159,120 for Theater Hospital Command.

These values reflect readiness depth, not simultaneous deployed headcount. They expose the occupations the institution must generate: lineworkers, protection engineers, treatment operators, pipe crews, clinical regulators, accessible-mobility crews, building-access teams, rights and records officers, route certifiers, maintainers, instructors and depot specialists.

The Department’s industrial strategy must therefore include labor institutions as deliberately as shipyards or aircraft lines. A plant without licensed shifts is inventory, not power. A train without accessible operations and lawful railroad integration is rolling stock, not continuity.

## 5. Capital and mature operations

### 5.1 The capital result is a portfolio screen

The capital ledger preserves six common lines once:

| Common capacity | Pre-reserve capital |
|---|---:|
| National Utility Projection System | $1.698T |
| Bases, depots and ranges | $0.650T |
| Science, technology and proving | $1.500T |
| Education and workforce infrastructure | $0.350T |
| Information and common digital systems | $0.400T |
| Strategic industry and materials | $0.500T |

These lines are not re-added from later subsystem studies. The model also avoids treating existing civil-mobility and deployment papers as independent budgets; it extracts bounded formation assumptions and credits common carriers and facilities.

Among the new formation lines, the largest reserved net capital is Household Collection at about $1.055 trillion. Civil Road Mobility is about $286 billion, Power Restoration $259 billion, Continuity Transfer Nodes $240 billion, Civil Air Mobility $209 billion, Water Restoration $167 billion and Grid Bridge $149 billion. The ranking is provisional because each result is the product of an unvalidated unit definition, unit count, capital allowance and shared credit.

No escalation, construction schedule, learning curve, prototype retirement, recapitalization, financing, environmental mitigation, reference-class estimate or confidence distribution is included. The number is suitable for deciding where configuration and independent cost work must concentrate. It is not suitable for an appropriation.

### 5.2 Annual operations nearly double

The annual bridge deliberately holds the integrated Pass 23 mature operating screen at $858 billion. It then adds only the burdens created by the fifteen new family lines:

| Increment | Mature annual value |
|---|---:|
| Component-weighted new direct personnel | $538.8B |
| New permanent institutional depth | $176.3B |
| Net new formation O&M | $163.3B |
| **Total increment** | **$878.4B** |
| **Canonical mature annual operations** | **$1.736T** |

This method avoids pretending that the predecessor’s complete operating decomposition has been re-estimated. It also reveals the strategic implication of coequal power more clearly than a capital headline: the mature institution is an enduring labor, maintenance, exercise, stock-rotation and readiness economy.

The result exceeds the original defense-parity metaphor. That is not an argument to reduce the public guarantee until the spreadsheet fits. It is an instruction to test the demand standard, formation echelon, component structure, prepared-local substitution, technology productivity and shared-capacity claims with much greater rigor.

## 6. The entry contradiction

### 6.1 Early mass overwhelms the inherited screen

Across all forty-eight families, demand times early share times deployable mass produces 8.903 million tonnes of early requirement. A declared 25-percent prepositioning credit leaves 6.677 million tonnes to move. The inherited effective modal capacities provide 2.463 million tonnes in seventy-two hours:

| Mode | Effective daily throughput |
|---|---:|
| Air | 53,627 t/day |
| Ocean | 198,800 t/day |
| Land and rail | 394,400 t/day |
| Inland water | 174,150 t/day |

The resulting 72-hour deficit is 4.214 million tonnes. Inherited capacity covers about 36.9 percent of the post-preposition requirement.

This does not yet assign individual cargo to routes, modes or deadlines. It does something more basic: it rejects the proposition that the enlarged formation inventory can inherit the old deployment enterprise unchanged.

### 6.2 Reception fails before sustainment does

Each campaign’s deployed people are compared with the independently placeable responder-base modules attached to its inherited `J-TOC` quantity. All ten campaigns fail. Deficits range from approximately 24,695 people in the inland flood to 81,740 in the allied megacity earthquake.

The provisional supply screen passes because each inherited `J-TSC` receives 18,000 tonnes per day of gross onward capacity and a 0.70 route factor. Margins range from roughly 14,115 to 36,740 tonnes per day. This apparent success is the least trustworthy result in the workbook. Formation supply coefficients remain top-down and omit several dominant flows: water, fuel, energy carriers, medical consumables, membranes, filters, construction materials, maintenance replacements, packaging, wastewater, hazardous residuals and damaged equipment.

The ordering is nevertheless useful. The first visible national constraint is not daily cargo arithmetic. It is where hundreds of thousands of responders can live and operate without drawing on damaged communities, followed by how several million tonnes of early formation mass can cross broken geography.

### 6.3 Projection is a coequal force family

The Department must therefore treat strategic movement, prepared reception and responder basing as force-generating programs with their own growth paths. Adding mission formations without adding their carriers, prepared nodes, base modules, crews, fuel systems, maintenance and route-restoration capacity creates a paper force.

This is the civil analogue of a familiar military truth: force structure and lift must be designed together. The difference is that civil entry also depends on public access, utility safety, environmental release, host authority and the ethical rule that responders cannot consume the scarce services they arrived to restore.

## 7. What technology must change

The model is not an argument to reproduce today’s labor and mass at larger scale. It identifies the coefficients that a defense-peer innovation system must attack.

### 7.1 Household and building productivity

The largest labor term invites competition in disconnected household records, building-system observability, vertical-access machinery, supervised autonomous collection, accessible curb interfaces, distributed communications and prepared-building adapters. Success is not fewer workers in the abstract. It is more complete, rights-preserving household contacts and safe releases per shift under heat, darkness, water, smoke, device dependence and communications loss.

### 7.2 Utility mass and labor

Water and power formation mass is dominated by distribution, construction, storage, protection, fuel and residual systems—not only plants. The technology program should compete rapid pipe and conductor corridors; autonomous survey, trenching, stringing and connection; self-proving protection systems; compact storage and treatment; source-flex energy; and prepared utility anchors.

A floating nuclear water–energy platform becomes valuable only if it changes the complete delivered-service equation after marine access, licensing, grid and water transfer, concentrate, security, maintenance and receiving geography. Its promise cannot be measured at the reactor or desalination outlet.

### 7.3 Crew-sharing without common-mode collapse

The current model deducts shared crews provisionally. Technology can make those deductions real through common interfaces, cross-qualified maintainers, automated inspection, digital technical orders, condition-based logistics and modular training. It can also create a catastrophic common mode if several services depend on one software stack, credential, supplier or communication path.

Every productivity claim must therefore be tested under loss of the shared enabler. A common crew is a valid saving only if allocation, travel, fatigue and simultaneous demand still close.

### 7.4 Prepared capacity as an industrial product

The 4.214-million-tonne early-mass deficit cannot be solved only with faster carriers. Prepared sites can hold anchors, utilities, stocks, records, inspected occupied space, material-handling systems and trained local affiliations. This converts transported mass into maintained geographic capacity.

The industrial program should treat prepared receiver and continuity sites as a product line: standardized interfaces, regional construction, recurring inspection, local workforce agreements, replacement stocks and adversarial exercises. Fixed capacity is not free; it must be funded and certified against the hazards likely to create demand.

## 8. What the model does not establish

Twelve evidence gates remain open:

1. campaign packages have not been calibrated to observed time-phased service deficits;
2. utility child missions have not proved distinct demand and shared-system boundaries;
3. person-to-receiver simulations do not yet connect household, node, vehicle and receiving capacity nationally;
4. occupation-level rosters do not yet validate shared-crew deductions;
5. item-level work breakdowns do not yet validate shared-capital deductions;
6. readiness does not yet model common suppliers, bases, routes, data and crews jointly;
7. strategic transport is not an item-level, routed, time-phased fleet model;
8. reception and responder basing lack occupied representative trials;
9. sustainment and residual flows remain incomplete;
10. capital lacks independent reference-class, schedule and confidence analysis;
11. schools, licenses, attrition and employer release do not yet generate the modeled occupations; and
12. no full regeneration trial begins a second campaign before the first force returns to readiness.

These gates bound every numerical claim. A formula can be internally correct while its unit definition is wrong. A shared credit can reconcile a work breakdown while creating an operational conflict. A national total can close while one region, protected cohort or receiving chain fails.

## 9. The next connected research program

Pass 58 changes the order of work.

### 9.1 Configure the dominant unit-of-account

The next force-generation configuration should decide what `CM-HCG` actually is. It must separate functions that remain organic from those embedded in prepared districts or pooled at higher echelon; derive a shift-complete occupation roster; map every shared crew and equipment set; and test one group across several simultaneous neighborhood cells. This is the largest leverage point in both workforce and annual operations.

### 9.2 Build a shared-capacity allocation ledger

Every carrier, base, depot, utility anchor, information service, licensed crew and prepared receiver claimed by more than one family needs a common identity, readiness state and time-phased allocation. The successor must deny capacity to the second claimant when the first has consumed it. Correlated loss must propagate across every dependent formation.

### 9.3 Rebuild deployment around canonical cargo

The [National Resilience Deployment and Distribution Enterprise](../projection/joint-deployment-and-distribution-enterprise.md) should be rerun with the forty-eight-family item manifests. It must distinguish prepositioned fixed capacity, urgent air cargo, heavy sealift, land and rail closure, inland-water access, cargo compatibility, nodes, queues, responder bases, daily sustainment and reverse flow.

### 9.4 Replace package counts with service-deficit curves

Each campaign should express demand in public-service units over time: people without safe thermal conditions, cubic metres of compliant water at use, megawatts of released critical power, hospital shifts, households not reached, route capacity, residuals awaiting custody and receiving places ready for admission. Formation quantities should then be solved from those deficits rather than declared directly.

### 9.5 Only then mature the industrial and fiscal case

Once configurations and shared boundaries stabilize, the Department-wide work breakdown can add program schedules, learning, workforce pipelines, supplier capacity, regional investment, recapitalization, exports and uncertainty. The resulting 25-year economy should be evaluated as a national production and readiness system, not one purchase price.

## Conclusion

The mature Department of Resilience cannot be a larger version of the current emergency-management apparatus, and its force cannot be a list of impressive platforms. It is a national capacity to generate complete public-service formations, move them through broken geography, sustain them without burdening survivors, connect them to households and institutions, transfer service safely and regenerate for the next campaign.

The canonical allocation makes that proposition materially more expensive and organizationally more demanding than Pass 23. That is a useful result. It moves the study from aspirational parity toward the real structure of coequal power.

The successor’s $15.179 trillion capital and 10.484 million affiliated-billet screens should not be defended as answers. They should be attacked as explicit hypotheses. If technology, prepared capacity, formation redesign or shared systems can reduce them without weakening the public guarantee, the model shows where the gain must occur. If they cannot, the scale is not an accounting error to hide. It is the institutional consequence of deciding that rescue, stabilization and civil continuity deserve first-principles national power.
