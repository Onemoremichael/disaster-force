# RBT-1 — Root A production-equivalent fire-control group, set, crew, industrial and regeneration constitution

## Decision abstract

`RBT-1` converts the bounded `DFC-72` operational mission in [RBS-1](f36-cisa40-sa6-root-a-denied-infrastructure-fire-control-mission-envelope-and-architecture-competition.md) into a production-equivalent public system. It does not select a winning architecture without observations. It defines the common physical, human, industrial and evidence burden that any architecture admitted by `RBS-1` must carry through new production lots.

The central correction is organizational. The six-set rotation proposed in `RBS-1` does not close. A set is occupied for at least 450 hours: eighteen hours from regional order through establishment, seventy-two hours on mission, twenty-four hours for relief, withdrawal and transfer, and 336 hours for regeneration. Continuous seventy-two-hour starts therefore require seven line sets. A separate ready reserve raises the production-equivalent group to eight complete sets. Six sets would require the first set to return after 432 hours, eighteen hours before its earliest lawful regeneration release, while preserving no casualty reserve.

One set is not a truck fleet with attached equipment. It is a serialized public-service system containing sixteen intervention cells, six 500 m³/h treatment trains, three 1,000 m³/h raw-water intake paths, twelve kilometres of 0.45 m trunkline, twenty-four route robots, fifteen 100 m³ ready-storage cells, four 1.85 m³/s recovery trains, thirty-six 100 m³ surge cells, six 5/7 MW continuous/peak energy islands, twenty field-distribution units, local and set command, rescue, repair, sustainment, mission energy, initial water and transport interfaces.

The registered set carries 7,086.848 tonnes before locally acquired ready water. Its 312 governed mission-load positions provide 9,360 tonnes of payload capacity, or 75.7 percent planned utilization. Three hundred fifty prime movers preserve thirty-eight unassigned vehicles. The cargo, 312 five-tonne frames and 350 twenty-four-tonne prime movers produce a 17,046.848-tonne regional rolling mass. The force must divide that system into fourteen independently released packets rather than claim that one 26.25-kilometre road column is a usable deployment method.

The water, recovery and energy topology is based on residual service rather than nominal nameplate capacity. Six 500 m³/h treatment trains retain 2,500 m³/h after one loss. Three 1,000 m³/h intake paths retain 2,000 m³/h after one loss. Four 1.85 m³/s recovery trains retain 5.55 m³/s after one loss. Six 5 MW continuous and 7 MW peak energy islands retain 25/35 MW after one loss. Fifteen 100 m³ ready cells retain the 1,200 m³ requirement after three cell losses. Six isolated 600 m³ surge zones retain 3,000 m³ after one complete-zone quarantine. These are entry requirements, not observed performance.

The crew is derived from continuous stations. Twelve active intervention cells, water production, intake, trunkline, recovery, energy, local control, evidence, safety, environment, medical care and public rescue require 237 people on watch. Four watches require 948 people. Deployment engineering, maintenance, repair, logistics, transport, planning and crew support add 492, producing 1,440 positions per set. Eight sets require 11,520 people. Group command, three arsenals, training, outside evaluation, industrial readiness and public configuration authority add 2,700, producing a 14,220-position operational group.

Regeneration is industrial capacity. A 336-hour set return with a seventy-two-hour start interval occupies 4.667 regeneration bays on average. Three regional arsenals therefore receive three complete-set bays each. Nine bays operate at 51.9 percent aggregate utilization. Loss of one arsenal leaves six bays at 77.8 percent utilization and preserves the cadence. Two bays per arsenal would fail after one-site loss.

Production-equivalent qualification assumes the upper `RBS-1` branch of two surviving architectures. Two independent system builders construct three consecutive complete sets for each architecture, producing twelve new-parent-lot systems. The third set in each architecture–builder cell becomes the terminal full-system article after completing all prior trials. Seventeen manufactured hardware families each require two production roots, four process-window units per root and two destructive units per root. The program therefore controls thirty-four module-root cells, 136 process-window units, sixty-eight component destructive units, four terminal full systems and twelve complete production-equivalent set builds.

Each complete set executes fourteen registered trials: commissioning, regional and national deployment, four raw-water missions, two service-casualty missions, two public-control cases and three repeated seventy-two-hour missions. This creates 168 complete-system trials and 7,704 operating hours. At a 25-percent average issue factor, the water screen is 3,328,128 m³. Seven full-duration missions per set also create 28,224 regeneration-bay hours across the twelve-set program.

The set-level equipment and interface cost hypothesis is USD 7.6111 billion. Assigned and reserve transport adds USD 1.1246 billion, producing USD 8.7357 billion per complete set before regional arsenals and central spares. An eight-set operating group, central module reserve, three arsenals and common training, evidence and configuration assets creates an illustrative USD 103.592 billion post-qualification capital floor. It is not a procurement authorization.

The twelve-set production-equivalent campaign, two system-root factories, dual module tooling, three arsenals, destructive infrastructure, public evidence and controlled receiver corridors require USD 175 billion capital. Peak program support is USD 9.5 billion per year and mission-led technology is USD 20 billion. The proposed 220/240-week program fails its own work-package reconciliation; the governing schedule is 256/280 weeks. The program uses 10,500 public execution positions. During its registered overlap with the trailing `RBS-1` campaign and retained reference estate, peak public formation is 29,850.

`RBT-1` issues `RAS-3R10`: 1,900 weeks, 32,000 public positions, USD 275 billion capital, USD 18 billion per year and USD 60 billion technology. Through this pass, primary and maximum calendar use is 1,570/1,654 weeks, capital use is USD 233.68 billion and technology use is USD 43.235 billion. Peak annual support is USD 15.17 billion. No value is a national fleet quantity or Department total.

An accepted `RBT-1` campaign may authorize `RBU-1`, a representative production-line and eight-set operational-acceptance program. It does not authorize operational procurement, deployment, export or an exterior rendering. Sectional engineering diagrams remain blocked until one architecture and both production roots close their interfaces in observed work.

## 2. Production-equivalent object

A **production-equivalent set** is one complete serialized system built from released drawings, released processes, new parent material lots, production tooling, production software and production inspection. It includes the transport and support state required to reach the mission receiver.

It is not:

- an upgraded `RBS-1` development system;
- a selected collection of successful modules;
- a demonstration assembled by the design team;
- a set completed with test-site utilities;
- an average bill lacking serial identity;
- a collection of nominal ratings without residual-service tests; or
- an operational asset merely because it resembles the intended product.

Every set receives one configuration identity joining system architecture, builder, module roots, software, evidence schema, transport packet, crew qualification and regeneration history. Substitution creates a new state. Undocumented cannibalization is prohibited.

## 5. Water and energy closure

### 5.1 Treatment and intake

Six 500 m³/h treatment trains supply 3,000 m³/h at installed rating. One-train loss leaves 2,500 m³/h, or 125 percent of the 2,000 m³/h mission floor. Three raw-water paths each carry 1,000 m³/h; one-path loss leaves exactly the mission floor.

The candidate must close treatment output after intake screen, pretreatment, concentrate, fouling, cleaning, corrosion and water-quality exclusions. Gross pump throughput does not establish usable output.

### 5.2 Initial and acquired water

`DRY-576` carries 574.848 tonnes, enough for one simultaneous twelve-cell reference salvo. It is an entry reserve, not a seventy-two-hour supply. `WET-READY` adds at least 1,200 m³ of measured local buffer. At registered density, that water adds 1,197.6 tonnes to the deployed site state but not to regional transport mass.

### 5.3 Mission energy

Twenty megawatts for seventy-two hours requires 1,440 MWh delivered. A 20-percent planning allowance raises the set energy service to 1,728 MWh. `MES-400` is a transport-mass screen for a candidate energy system; it does not prescribe fuel chemistry or conversion efficiency. Every candidate must separately declare stored, generated and replenished energy, conversion loss, cooling, exhaust, fire consequence, maintenance and terminal waste.

Six energy islands provide 30 MW continuous and 42 MW peak. One-island loss leaves 25 MW continuous and 35 MW peak. Distribution must prevent one bus, control image, protection setting or cooling path from disabling all remaining islands.

### 5.4 Recovery and surge

Four 1.85 m³/s recovery trains supply 7.4 m³/s installed capacity. One loss leaves 5.55 m³/s against the 5.5 m³/s floor. Thirty-six 100 m³ surge cells form six physically isolable 600 m³ zones. Quarantine of one zone leaves 3,000 m³.

Candidate water, public water, source-bearing liquid, treatment concentrate, agent, rain and unknown liquid retain separate accounts. Recovered water earns no supply credit until its eligible chemistry closes.

## 12. Production-root constitution

### 12.1 System roots

Each surviving architecture receives two unrelated system builders. If two architectures survive, four architecture–builder cells enter. Each cell builds three consecutive complete systems from new parent lots:

| Serial position | Primary purpose | Terminal status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `PE-1` | first production-equivalent assembly and correction discovery | retained for mission trials |
| `PE-2` | repeated build after disclosed correction | retained for mission and regeneration trials |
| `PE-3` | third consecutive build and terminal casualty article | destroyed after all prior eligible trials |

Three consecutive builds expose build-order and correction dependence. They do not estimate mature yield or reliability.

### 12.2 Module roots

Seventeen manufactured hardware families require two production roots each, creating thirty-four root cells. Each root cell builds:

- four production-process-window units;
- two destructive units;
- calibrated witness material;
- released tooling and inspection state;
- one outside-build package; and
- a public repair and safeing package.

The campaign therefore contains 136 process-window units and sixty-eight component destructive units before ordinary set quantities. Consumable water and energy stocks receive independent source and quality programs but are not counted as hardware roots.

### 12.3 Line-rate claim

After acceptance, each selected-architecture system root must demonstrate a set completion interval no greater than sixteen weeks across the three-build sequence without hidden design-team labor. Two roots at that interval imply 6.5 sets per year before downtime and learning loss. This is a demonstrated line-rate target, not a national production forecast.

An eight-week interval may be studied only after two additional consecutive lots. It cannot be inferred from parallel work on one set.

## 20. Stop and release law

The program stops, branches or narrows if:

- `RBS-1` admits no architecture;
- a development system is relabeled production-equivalent;
- a candidate omits transport tare, reserve vehicles or packetization;
- local water is credited before intake, treatment and custody close;
- one module loss breaks required residual service;
- six sets are retained by administrative waiver;
- a regeneration bay is counted without complete-set tooling and release authority;
- a builder controls its own decisive reference or terminal reconstruction;
- process corrections are hidden between consecutive builds;
- first-pass yield is inferred from fewer than the registered independent set builds;
- a terminal article is spared because earlier results are favorable;
- a crew qualification substitutes hours worked for observed role performance;
- a private process restriction prevents public repair or second-source qualification;
- a later trial overwrites an adverse valid result;
- one arsenal, software image or specialist remains a group-wide common cause;
- annual or capital capacity exceeds the parent; or
- exterior form is selected before architecture and root acceptance.

Acceptance authorizes only the next production-line and operational-acceptance constitution. It does not turn any trial system into a fielded asset.
