# Pass 166 — Joint Mission-System Appropriation, Reservation and Accountability Constitution

**Research object:** `JMSA-1`

**Decision level:** Department acquisition and force-generation constitution

**Verdict:** `CLOSE`

## Resolution test

Reversing this rule would change Department-wide budget ownership, program offices, contracting, common-capacity accounting and the force credited to every joint mission.

The live branch is `RCC-1R → JMSA-1`: the second pass at the returned first-order acquisition question. It passes because allocating money by platform, service or public effect would produce materially different institutions and incentives.

## Judgment

The Department shall appropriate and account for force generation through five mutually exclusive classes:

1. **Joint mission-system programs** own integrated public-effect requirements, dependency models, representative campaigns and outcome budgets.
2. **Service professional and article-family bases** own schools, doctrine, workforce pipelines, depots, configuration competence and lifecycle stewardship that persist across missions.
3. **National common capacity** owns scarce mobility, sustainment, prepared receivers, open interfaces and reserves only when those resources are separately titled and operationally reservable.
4. **The sovereign technical estate** owns independent test, public standards, causal evidence, safety and rights assurance, and industrial-mobilization options.
5. **A competitive failure reserve** remains unavailable to incumbents until a rival fails, a second source is needed or a program must be recompeted.

One dollar belongs to one account. A service may administer a mission-system work package, but that executor view is the same money—not a second service budget. A common asset may be titled nationally, configured to a shared standard and physically assigned to a service; none of those facts earns quantity credit. Credit requires an exclusive time-and-place reservation, a complete service chain, valid reset and recall clocks, and demonstrated technical equivalence.[^1][^2]

This closes the cross-portfolio allocation rule left open by Pass 165.[^4]

## Why neither service budgets nor mission budgets are sufficient

Pure service budgeting rewards article-family completeness but allows each service to stop at its interface. Pure mission budgeting rewards complete effects but encourages nine mission offices to duplicate schools, depots, standards and technical cadres. `JMSA-1` separates the two forms of accountability.

A mission-system office is accountable for a complete effect such as medical continuity, water and sanitation, or hazard control. It holds the integrated requirement and can withhold acceptance when one contributor delivers an impressive component but the chain fails. The generating service retains professional judgment, technical release and responsibility for safe lifecycle support. Independent assurance can deny evidence credit without becoming a program manager.

The practical control is a three-authority transaction:

- the mission-system executive accepts outcome integration;
- the generating service releases the article family and workforce;
- the sovereign technical authority accepts evidence, safety and interface compliance.

No one authority may substitute for the other two. This is a stronger architecture than either a lead-service memorandum or a new omnibus service.

## Reference allocation—not a Department cost estimate

The companion model tests the constitution against a **USD 1.00T, twenty-five-year, constant-2026-dollar, undiscounted technology and proving envelope** inherited from the convergence case.[^3] It is not the Department's total capital requirement, annual operating budget or an appropriation recommendation.

The reference case assigns 55 percent to nine mission-system programs, 15 percent to seven service bases, 15 percent to five national-common accounts, 10 percent to five sovereign-technical accounts and 5 percent to the failure reserve. This produces twenty-seven unique accounts and conserves the full envelope. Two rival mixes move 10 percentage points from mission systems to service depth, or 5 points from mission systems to failure reserve; both preserve the same accounting law.

The mission share is also crossed to the seven generating services as executable work packages. That crosswalk totals USD 550B exactly. Adding it to the nine mission-office budgets would falsely report USD 1.55T. Only the USD 150B service-base column is distinct from those mission dollars. The model therefore makes a common analytical error mechanically visible: organizational administration is not a second economic resource.

Portfolio weights are provisional research allocations. They show that the constitution works; they do not determine what the mature force should buy. The national concurrency scenario remains the proper source of quantities and therefore the next decision.

## Industrial consequence

The clean-sheet industrial analogue is not seven vertically integrated primes and not nine captive platform monopolies. The Department buys rival **mission-integration roots** against public service outcomes while services sustain rival technical roots for article families. Government-owned fluid, power, data, mechanical and evidence interfaces allow a water train, clinical island, carrier or power lattice to compete without surrendering the whole mission to one vendor.

Failure reserve changes market structure. It protects the option to build a losing prototype far enough to preserve manufacturing knowledge, qualify a second source, or replace a failed integrator. The sovereign estate holds test articles, design standards, configuration evidence and technical rights so that competition can recur after initial award. A contractor may integrate; it may not define the requirement, certify its own evidence and control the only production route.

## Common-capacity reservation test

The model applies five cases. Overlapping claims of six articles each against ten available articles require twelve at peak and earn no credit. Sequential claims may reuse the same articles only when reset and recall are valid. Common title without technical equivalence earns no credit. Two simultaneous twelve-article claims against twenty available articles expose four uncovered articles. Until the national scenario fixes concurrency, geography, travel, maintenance and recovery intervals, admitted cross-portfolio quantity reduction remains zero.

This preserves Pass 159's distinction among title, family, reservation and assignment and Pass 160's prohibition on savings by naming convention.[^2][^3]

## Closure basis

`JMSA-1` closes the allocation question because it now has a conserved account register, an executor crosswalk, an authority matrix, common-capacity denial tests, a twenty-five-year phasing schedule and funding-mix sensitivities. The workbook contains thirteen sheets; all twenty-six audit checks pass, no formula errors are present and a live case change restores the reference exactly.
