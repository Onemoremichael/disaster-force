# F36 First Campaign Wave and Complete-Campus-Loss Rehearsal Constitution

## Executive judgment

The first F36 campaign wave should not be a miniature version of the full protocol or a ceremonial demonstration that happens to use the new range. It should be the first controlled operation of the range as a national evidence-production system. Its purpose is to establish whether registered articles, physical stations, people, clocks, laboratories, blind truth, samples, waste, evidence and decision authority remain one coherent process when a complete campus disappears during active work.

The recommended wave runs for ninety days. It seeks eighteen admitted closure decisions and six admitted full-bore damage decisions, balanced across the three technical roots. Prospective invalid-event rates of fifteen percent for closure work and twenty percent for damage work expand those credit objectives to P90 reservations of twenty-four closure slots and ten damage slots. The calendar protects all thirty-four slots even though only the eighteen closure primaries, six damage primaries and any causally required replacements may start.

At day 38, the rehearsal removes `NRC-B` as a complete operational island. Both closure bays and both damage cells are active. A recently completed blinded closure packet is also still in independent replay outside the campus. The loss denies power, data, people, physical access, laboratories, lifting, water, local originals and stored material from that campus for the rest of the wave. It is not a brief utility drill and it is not modeled as four isolated equipment failures.

Four active events at `NRC-B` become range-caused invalid events: two closure tokens and two damage tokens. Their physical articles, local records, samples, water and waste remain in registered safe custody at the inaccessible campus. They are not moved merely to make a conservation ledger look complete. Outside originals, blind commitments and national token records survive. The recently completed post-run packet remains creditable only because its forensic package left `NRC-B` under seal before the loss and an outside original can complete independent replay without the campus.

Within twelve hours, a separated residual-schedule authority must freeze a new calendar. Within ninety-six hours, two new closure articles and two new damage cassettes must begin under new token identities at `NRC-A` and `NRC-C`. No event resumes under its old identifier. No lost-campus person, station, sample, original or consumable counts toward residual capacity. If all unaffected and replacement events produce valid evidence, the wave reaches eighteen closure and six damage credits by day 64, holds four additional closure and two additional damage contingency slots through day 83, and closes integrated adjudication by day 90.

The first-wave maximum load consumes 65.1 percent of closure capacity and 61.5 percent of damage capacity under the declared day-38 loss. Supporting loads remain below 65 percent of their corresponding residual capacities. The range therefore preserves a real invalidity reserve rather than producing a nominal schedule that succeeds only if every event works.

The first wave uses the existing Pass 104 institution. It requires no additional capital program. Its ninety-day operating draw is USD 0.098137B, the exact `90/365` share of the USD 0.398B annual range-support boundary. Planning can authorize a rehearsal script. It cannot constitute sites, name qualified people, accept articles, seal a live randomization seed, execute the loss or award a credit.

## 1. The operational question

Pass 104 established the mature F36 range complex as three complete and unlike campuses, each carrying two closure bays, two damage cells, two sanitary laboratories, two metrology detachments, two replay cells, one forensic line, one blind/configuration shop and a complete utility and custody island. That architecture preserves the full campaign at P90 after one campus is lost.

Architecture is not operation. A diagram showing three campuses does not answer six decisive questions:

1. Which exact article, station, shift, reference, blind state and evidence authority owns the next event?
2. How much work may be active before reserve becomes fictional?
3. What happens to an event that loses its campus after the stimulus begins?
4. Can a completed but unadjudicated packet survive without the campus that created it?
5. Who may restart work, and what evidence is required before that decision?
6. Can the remaining force finish the registered wave without hidden borrowing from the lost campus?

The rehearsal exists to answer those questions as one causal system. A campus-loss exercise that tests only emergency shutdown proves safety response, not national proving continuity. A schedule exercise that assumes clean shutdown proves neither. A data-recovery exercise that ignores physical articles, samples, water, waste, blind truth and workforce creates an evidence cloud detached from the experiment it purports to preserve.

The first-wave constitution therefore joins five objects that ordinary program planning often separates:

- a finite and immutable token set;
- a station calendar with protected unused capacity;
- a person-and-authority roster;
- physical and digital conservation ledgers; and
- a loss-and-recovery decision sequence.

No object can substitute for another. Spare bays do not compensate for missing blind custody. A second original does not rescue an uncontained article. A replacement article does not continue the evidence identity of the interrupted one. A complete ledger does not authorize a scheduler to award credit.

## 2. What the wave is allowed to claim

The wave has three legitimate outcomes.

First, it can admit individual event packets that satisfy the Pass 102 and Pass 103 rules. Second, it can demonstrate that the range operates its token, custody, replay, reset and authority systems through a declared campus loss. Third, it can generate observed invalidity, cycle-time, backlog and staffing data that replace the prospective assumptions used in Pass 104.

It cannot establish production reliability. Eighteen closure decisions and six damage decisions are far smaller than the registered campaign banks. The conditions intentionally differ, so even a larger count would not be a homogeneous reliability sample. The wave also cannot select a production root, authorize serial manufacture, release an exterior form or prove the complete ten-kilometre mission. Those decisions remain downstream of the full root campaigns, independent reproduction and mission-scale evidence.

The wave may fail constructively. A failed safe-state transition, lost original, broken blind commitment, unexplained sample balance, unresolved clock discontinuity, excessive backlog or missed residual deadline is evidence about the institution. The correct response is to preserve the failure and redesign the system. Retrospective schedule edits, reconstructed timestamps or replacement of a failed event under the same token would destroy the most valuable information the wave can produce.

## 3. Deriving the ninety-day scope

The credit objective is not an arbitrary fraction of the full campaign. It is the smallest balanced wave that exercises every closure evidence class and both damage disclosure states for all three roots.

Each root receives six primary closure intentions:

| Closure class | Events per root | Wave purpose |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| declared nominal | 1 | verify the root's ordinary evidence path |
| declared process edge | 1 | expose a registered low- or high-margin condition |
| known defect | 1 | confirm calibrated rejection and causal recovery |
| blind physical defect | 1 | test physical defect discrimination |
| blind configuration defect | 1 | test recipe, material or software ancestry |
| clean blind control | 1 | test false rejection under challenge handling |
| **Total** | **6** | **eighteen primary closure intentions** |

Each root receives two primary damage intentions:

| Damage class | Events per root | Wave purpose |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| disclosed `D01` clean transection | 1 | establish the complete detect-to-potable-release chain |
| sealed permitted combination | 1 | exercise blind injury truth and full forensic closure |
| **Total** | **2** | **six primary damage intentions** |

The wave does not choose the native low or high process factor, known defect, physical defect, configuration defect or sealed damage combination in this paper. Each root's registered factor space remains supplier-neutral. The live randomization package must choose the exact articles and conditions after registry closure and seed commitment.

The P90 reservation uses the same transparent negative-binomial approximation as Pass 104. If `n` credits are required and `q` is the prospective probability of a range-invalid event, expected attempts and variance are:

`E[A] = n / (1 - q)`

`Var[A] = n q / (1 - q)^2`

The planning count is:

`P90 attempts = ceil(E[A] + 1.2816 × sqrt(Var[A]))`

| Lane | Required credits | Invalid-rate assumption | Expected attempts | P90 reserved attempts | Complete cycle | Reserved station-days |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| closure | 18 | 15% | 21.176 | 24 | 8 days | 192 |
| damage | 6 | 20% | 7.500 | 10 | 14 days | 140 |

The six closure reserve slots and four damage reserve slots are calendar reservations, not permission to repeat until success. Two closure and two damage reserves are activated by the declared campus-loss scenario. The remaining four closure and two damage slots stay held unless an independent range-cause board validates another eligible invalid event. A valid article failure receives no automatic repeat.

## 4. The token as the indivisible unit of work

A campaign token is a signed permission to expose one registered physical article to one declared event in one station during one time interval. It is not a work order that can be moved or edited after disruption.

Every token must bind at least:

- immutable token identifier and schema version;
- lane and primary, replacement or contingent role;
- root and article or cassette identifier;
- condition family and disclosure class;
- parent credit intention and, for a replacement, parent invalid token;
- campus, station, start boundary and complete station-cycle boundary;
- predecessor packet hashes;
- pre- and post-event metrology token identifiers;
- water-assay, forensic and two replay token identifiers;
- blind commitment and permitted unblinding event;
- builder, operator, safety, evidence, witness and acceptance rosters;
- required native records and two-original destinations;
- permitted terminal dispositions; and
- the authority signatures required for each state transition.

An operational token follows one forward-only state machine:

```text
RESERVED
   │ registry, references, roster and predecessors complete
   ▼
ELIGIBLE
   │ schedule authority commits station and time
   ▼
COMMITTED
   │ operator accepts custody and start authority
   ▼
ACTIVE
   ├── safety interruption ──► SAFE_HOLD
   │                              │ independent causal finding
   │                              ├──► RANGE_INVALID
   │                              └──► ARTICLE_FAILED / INDETERMINATE
   ▼
SEALED
   │ originals, samples and configuration close
   ▼
ADJUDICATING
   ├──► CREDITED
   ├──► ARTICLE_FAILED
   ├──► RANGE_INVALID
   └──► INDETERMINATE
```

No transition returns to `ACTIVE`. A range-invalid token stays in the record. A replacement receives a new identifier, new article, new station allocation, new commitments and an explicit parent link to the invalid token. This prevents an institution from laundering a disrupted event into a clean result.

## 7. The loss injection

The loss begins at day 38, 10:00 range time. Its declared physical boundary is complete denial of `NRC-B` for the remainder of the wave. The scenario removes:

- every person rostered to `NRC-B`;
- both closure bays and both damage cells;
- local power, water, hydraulic plant, controls and time sources;
- both sanitary laboratories, both metrology teams and both replay cells;
- the forensic line and blind shop;
- every local original, physical article, sample, waste store and reset item; and
- all road, rail, air and network access to the campus.

The injection does not corrupt `NRC-A`, `NRC-C` or the independently held national commitment registry. It does not grant access to `NRC-B` for convenient retrieval. The complete denial lasts long enough that recovery of the campus cannot contribute to the ninety-day result.

The response sequence is clocked from the injection:

## 8. Four event classes created by the loss

The injection distinguishes events by causal state, not merely by campus address.

### 8.1 Active and interrupted

`CW-C014`, `CW-C015`, `CW-D005` and `CW-D006` are active at `NRC-B`. They stop at the last trustworthy observation and enter safe custody. The independent causal board may classify them as `RANGE_INVALID` because the registered experiment cannot finish after the campus disappears. Their partial records remain permanent. They receive no article credit and no negative article finding unless evidence independently supports such a finding.

### 8.2 Completed and terminal before loss

Earlier `NRC-B` events whose packets are already credited remain credited. Campus loss does not rewrite a terminal decision. Their two-original and provenance obligations continue under normal retention rules.

### 8.3 Completed but still adjudicating

`CW-C010` ends its physical cycle on day 35 and remains in adjudication until day 41. Its forensic package transfers under seal to `FL-A` on day 36, and the outside original is complete before the injection. When `NRC-B` disappears, the packet must finish replay, unblinding and decision without the local campus.

This is a harder and more valuable test than backing up a finished report. If the outside evidence is incomplete, if the transferred physical package cannot be reconciled or if replay silently consults a campus service, `CW-C010` becomes indeterminate or invalid. It cannot be declared complete because its station work happened before the loss.

### 8.4 Reserved but not started

Unstarted slots remain permissions, not assets in motion. A reserved slot may receive a root only through its registered contingent rule. It does not inherit the identity, truth or acceptance status of a failed parent. If no eligible invalid event occurs, it stays held.

## 9. Work-in-process law

The range protects reserve by limiting work before it becomes a queue. The first wave applies the following national limits:

| Control | Normal limit | Degraded limit after loss | Reason |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| simultaneous active closure events | 4 | 4 | leaves two national bays unused before loss and uses all four survivors only during replacement concentration |
| simultaneous active damage events | 3 | 3 | preserves one surviving damage cell after loss and limits sanitary consequence |
| replacement token identities committed inside 24 hours | 4 | 4 | permits the four loss replacements while preventing a broader schedule surge |
| sealed closure packets awaiting terminal decision | 6 | 4 | bounds forensic and replay backlog |
| sealed damage packets awaiting terminal decision | 3 | 3 | bounds sanitary, forensic and replay backlog |
| events sharing one blind-truth manufacturing root | 2 | 1 | limits common-cause disclosure and handling error |
| station with overlapping reserved intervals | 0 | 0 | one station cannot carry two identities |
| unexplained conservation balance | 0 | 0 | no amount of schedule progress compensates for missing material or evidence |

The post-loss closure peak of four occurs on days 42–50 when two clean-control primaries and two replacements use all surviving bays. The post-loss damage peak of three occurs while `CW-D004` continues at `NRC-A` and the two replacement events run at `NRC-A` and `NRC-C`. Any further valid failure or range invalidity holds until a protected contingent interval opens.

A scheduler may reduce these limits. It may not increase them during the wave. A safety stop, unknown configuration, missing reference, lost custody handoff, late assay, replay divergence or workforce shortfall automatically reduces admissible work in process. It never creates a reason to start more work elsewhere.

Each campus has five named roster cells: `ALPHA`, `BRAVO`, `CHARLIE`, `DELTA` and `ECHO`. The first four support a two-watch, twelve-hour rotation with two teams off-cycle at any moment. `ECHO` owns relief, qualification, casualty replacement and mobile reserve. A person may appear in only one roster cell and one primary billet for the wave. Overtime cannot be used as an unbounded capacity input.

The exact individual roster is a physical predecessor, not a planning invention. Before admission, the registry must bind each billet to a qualified person, current credential, medical and fatigue state, campus, watch, alternate and conflict-of-interest disclosure. The workbook freezes billet quantities and watch identities only.

When `NRC-B` is lost, all 410 of its people become unavailable. The residual plan counts only the 820 people assigned to `NRC-A` and `NRC-C`. It may not import the lost campus's workers by telepresence, presume rapid relocation or replace public authority with the 1,850-person industrial affiliate pool. Affiliates may perform only the work permitted by their standing qualifications and may not take public evidence, blind-truth, safety, acceptance or admission powers.

The rehearsal fails if either surviving campus cannot staff its own station, laboratory, evidence and safe-state chain through day 90 without `NRC-B` personnel. This is the personnel equivalent of a complete campus island.

## 12. Authority choreography

The loss creates pressure to collapse authority into whoever can restore schedule fastest. The constitution instead preserves distinct powers:

| Authority | May decide | May not decide during loss |
| --- | --- | --- |
| requirements | whether the registered event still answers the intended question | whether a particular article passed |
| national schedule | freeze, reserve and reissue station intervals | classify a range cause or award credit |
| campus command | protect people and local plant | continue an invalid event for schedule convenience |
| safety | stop any work and attest safe-state evidence | convert a safe stop into an accepted result |
| configuration | validate article, station and software ancestry | reveal blind truth or judge performance |
| metrology | validate reference brackets and measurement uncertainty | substitute a command for a measurement |
| water-release | control sanitary sampling and receiver release | waive evidence because pressure proof passed |
| evidence | seal originals, clocks, hashes and native records | decide whether the article met the requirement |
| witness | preserve the second original and independent event account | become the canonical original by preference |
| blind truth | preserve commitments, unblind and issue fresh challenge identities | operate or initially accept the article |
| invalidity board | distinguish range cause, article cause and unresolved cause | erase the invalid event or assign replacement credit |
| forensics | preserve, open and report causal physical evidence | alter the frozen initial disposition |
| acceptance | apply the registered decision rule to the sealed packet | rewrite thresholds after unblinding |
| admission board | award the terminal event credit and wave finding | cure a missing predecessor by institutional vote |

At least requirements, safety, evidence, blind truth, invalidity, acceptance and admission votes must remain outside the lost campus. No one person may occupy two incompatible powers for the same event. The loss roster must identify alternates before the campaign seed is revealed.

## 13. Conservation through denial

Conservation means every registered object has one explainable state. It does not mean every object moves to a surviving campus.

For each material or evidence class, the event ledger applies:

`opening custody + registered creation = authorized consumption + transferred custody + retained custody + safe-hold custody + terminal waste + unexplained balance`

The unexplained balance must equal zero. Transfers require matching release and receipt records. Authorized consumption requires an event and method identifier. Waste remains an identified state with composition, container, location and disposition authority. A campus-loss condition can move an object from available custody to inaccessible safe-hold custody without moving it physically.

At the day-38 injection, the expected reconciliation is:

| active closure articles | 2 | 2 | 0 | 2 from surviving reserve | 0 |

Replacement draw is not part of the opening affected-object balance. It is a separate withdrawal from surviving registered reserve, linked here to expose the operational consequence. The reserve ledger must fall by exactly the number of new physical objects admitted.

No evidence system is allowed to report a healthy zero by excluding inaccessible objects. `NRC-B` safe-hold items remain active obligations until the campus is recovered, transferred under authority or terminally disposed. The ninety-day wave can finish without them, but the Department cannot forget them.

## 14. The post-run packet survival experiment

`CW-C010` is intentionally placed at the boundary between physical completion and terminal admission. The event ends on day 35. Its sealed forensic package leaves `NRC-B` for `FL-A` on day 36. The outside original, clock map, configuration package and initial disposition are complete before day 38. Blind truth remains sealed.

After the loss, two replay implementations at the surviving campuses must independently reconstruct the disposition. `FL-A` completes the registered physical opening. The blind authority then releases truth to the acceptance board. The local `NRC-B` original remains inaccessible and is not silently replaced. If the outside original and transferred physical package are sufficient under the preregistered evidence rule, the event may reach terminal decision on day 41. If the protocol required an unavailable local object, the result is indeterminate.

This event tests whether two-original architecture creates operational independence rather than duplicate storage. A design that needs the lost campus's database, signing root, analyst, time service or physical coupon has not produced an outside original in the relevant sense.

The plan passes only if it uses no `NRC-B` capacity, creates no station overlap, respects the four-closure and three-damage WIP ceilings, keeps every support service at or below 70 percent of residual productive capacity and places every possible P90 packet inside day 90. A plan that meets the event count by drawing down an unexplained stock, overloading a laboratory or postponing replay beyond the horizon fails.

## 16. Day-90 terminal decision

The integrated board may issue `PASS`, `FAIL` or `INDETERMINATE` for the wave-and-loss rehearsal. `PASS` requires all of the following:

1. every physical station reaches its registered safe state within its safety-case limit;
2. the national start freeze occurs within five minutes;
3. all affected tokens enter immutable causal states without erasure or resumption;
4. the outside originals for all affected events preserve continuous trustworthy records;
5. `CW-C010` reaches a defensible terminal result without consulting `NRC-B`;
6. blind truth remains sealed until each initial disposition is frozen;
7. every replacement uses a new physical identity, token and commitment;
8. the residual plan is signed within twelve hours;
9. replacement closure and damage work starts within ninety-six hours;
10. no `NRC-B` person or asset contributes to the residual plan;
11. eighteen closure and six damage packets reach terminal admission by day 64 under the modeled loss path;
12. all protected P90 contingency packets could finish by day 83 or day 78 as scheduled;
13. every material, sample, waste, evidence and reserve ledger has zero unexplained balance;
14. no station overlap, WIP breach, roster conflict or support-capacity breach occurs;
15. the ninety-day operating draw remains inside its inherited boundary; and
16. two independent reviews reproduce the complete wave finding from native records.

A missing or contradictory record produces `INDETERMINATE` unless it independently proves failure. A safety-case exceedance, lost blind integrity, unexplained balance, hidden dependency on `NRC-B`, unauthorized restart or retrospective token edit produces `FAIL`. The board cannot average failures against successful event counts.

Passing the rehearsal admits the range to continue the registered F36 campaign under its approved configuration. It does not admit any root that has not completed its own campaign. Failing the rehearsal freezes new credit-bearing starts until the causal corrective experiment passes.

## 18. Failure modes the rehearsal is designed to expose

The exercise is most valuable when it prevents plausible shortcuts.

**The equipment-only loss.** Removing four stations while retaining campus people, laboratories, data and stores would test spare equipment, not a complete campus island.

**The pause-and-resume fiction.** Continuing an interrupted event under the same token would erase the causal boundary between two physical histories.

**The backup-as-original fiction.** A copied summary or database replica that cannot independently replay the native packet does not satisfy the outside-original requirement.

**The invisible forensic dependency.** A completed blind event may still depend on a physical coupon, unblinding authority or analyst located at the lost campus. `CW-C010` is positioned to expose that dependency.

**The workforce teleport.** Moving `NRC-B` people instantly to surviving campuses would convert geographic separation into a paper exercise.

**The unbounded queue.** Starting work because a bay is empty while metrology, assay, forensic or replay queues are saturated would create apparent throughput and delayed truth.

**The repeat-until-success rule.** A reserve token activates only after an independent range-cause finding. A valid article failure remains a failure.

**The conservation erasure.** Inaccessible samples, water, waste and originals remain registered obligations. They do not become zero because the campus is absent.

**The authority collapse.** Emergency command may stop work. It may not declare invalidity, unblind, rewrite the schedule and award credit.

**The calendar victory.** Finishing physical cycles by day 90 while packets remain unresolved is failure. Terminal evidence, not station release, is the output.

## 19. What this pass establishes

### Established in planning

- eighteen closure and six damage credits are the smallest root-balanced wave that exercises all closure classes and both damage disclosure states;
- P90 invalidity protection requires twenty-four closure and ten damage station reservations;
- the day-38 scenario removes one complete campus for the rest of the wave;
- four active events become range-caused invalids and receive new replacement identities;
- one completed but unadjudicated blinded event tests true cross-campus packet independence;
- normal and degraded work-in-process ceilings preserve reserve and support queues;

- the residual plan counts 820 surviving public personnel and no lost-campus staff;
- material and evidence conservation includes inaccessible safe-hold custody;
- the full P90 schedule closes by day 83, leaving seven days for integrated review; and
- the ninety-day operating draw is USD 0.098137B inside the standing annual boundary.

### Falsified as acceptable planning

- treating the loss as four independent station outages;
- resuming an interrupted event under its original token;
- using a valid failure to justify an automatic repeat;
- assuming copied data equals an independent original;
- omitting forensic and blind-truth work after physical station release;
- borrowing people, stores or services from the denied campus;
- treating unavailable custody as zero inventory;
- allowing emergency command to award evidence credit; or
- declaring the wave complete before terminal packets close.
