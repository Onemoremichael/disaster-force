## Decision abstract

`RBN-1` defines the seventh physical rung of the `AM-06` entry ladder created by [RBG-1](f36-cisa40-sa6-root-a-am06-source-water-ignition-entry-ladder-reset.md). Its question is:

> What public interface architecture can introduce 12 cubic metres of reference water into a verified 250-kilogram flame over the selected five-square-metre field, preserve both qualified water morphologies, observe source–water contact rather than infer it, control at least 7.884 cubic metres of necessarily liquid water, close wet combustion and atmosphere accounts, end safely and return after 1,008 hours?

The global ratio appears familiar. Twelve cubic metres is 0.048 cubic metre per kilogram of source, the same nominal proportion used at `ER-05`. The facility state is not similar. Water inventory rises by 2.5 while field area rises by two. Applied water per square metre, volume flux and mass flux therefore rise by 25 percent. For an equivalent circular field, water presented per metre of boundary rises by approximately 76.8 percent. The selected dry field also has 25 percent greater source depth and areal source inventory than the 100-kilogram field. Equal global water-to-source ratio does not preserve local contact, drain demand, surface impulse, steam path or root visibility.

The 12-cubic-metre issue contains 11,976 kilograms of water at the registered density. A 120-second issue requires 99.8 kilograms or 0.1 cubic metre per second. Heating the complete inventory from the declared 20-degree-Celsius reference to 100 degrees and vaporizing it would require approximately 31,040.35 megajoules, 2.915 times the 10,647-megajoule source-energy screen. Even the impossible ceiling in which all source energy enters the water can vaporize only approximately 4,107.83 kilograms or 4.116 cubic metres. At least 7.884 cubic metres remains liquid before public water, rain, wash water, foam, retained inventory or uncertainty enters the account.

`RBN-1` creates four rival `WIX-12` field-interface architectures: a zoned matrix lattice, a boundary-fed radial manifold, a distributed passive accumulator field and an overhead isolated canopy. They execute 288 water-field trials and 144 blinded matched comparisons before one interface architecture may populate an eighteen-cassette `AQC-12` bank. A winner must carry both morphologies. ## 5. Four `WIX-12` interface architectures

### 5.1 `ZML-12`: zoned matrix lattice

`ZML-12` maps independently metered water zones to the selected source-field subregions. Each zone carries local isolation, route-state measurement, field witnesses and a dedicated drain identity. `BFR-12` keeps major hydraulic equipment outside the hot field and feeds morphology heads from a surveyed perimeter ring. ### 5.3 `DPA-12`: distributed passive accumulator field

`DPA-12` places isolated local accumulators around the field so the registered water action can survive loss of the primary pump, grid or network. Each accumulator has independent stored-energy, isolation and empty-state witnesses. Its principal risks are unequal discharge, thermal exposure, many stored-energy hazards, maintenance burden and difficult synchronization between dispersed and coherent heads.

### 5.4 `OHC-12`: overhead isolated canopy

`OHC-12` suspends protected hydraulic distribution and field witnesses above the source plane while keeping wet supply and public quench physically separate. It offers short, observable paths to a wide field and leaves floor drains unobstructed. Its principal risks are overhead mass, steam and plume intrusion, falling contaminated water, access after heat exposure and a common structural failure affecting all heads.

## 11. Enthalpy, phase and atmosphere discontinuity

Using the registered 20-degree-Celsius water reference, 4.186 kilojoules per kilogram-kelvin sensible heat and 2.257 megajoules per kilogram vaporization screen, heat plus vaporization requires 2.59188 megajoules per kilogram. Thus:

\[
11{,}976\times2.59188=31{,}040.35\ \text{MJ}.
\]

The source-energy screen is 10,647 megajoules. The maximum source-energy-only vapor mass is:

\[
10{,}647/2.59188=4{,}107.83\ \text{kg}.
\]

That is 4.116 cubic metres or 34.3 percent of candidate-path water. The ratio matches ER-05 because source and water both rise by 2.5. The spatial and rate problems do not match because area and boundary grow more slowly.

RBN-1 does not convert vapor mass into a plume volume without observed temperature, composition and phase state. The dry 47.108-cubic-metre-per-second exhaust screen also cannot be assumed adequate after rapid steam generation, droplet carryover, wet-filter loading and condensation. Supply, exhaust, bypass, condensate and atmosphere safe state remain measured accounts.

## 29. Research stocktake

RBN-1 changes the program's understanding in nine ways.

First, a conserved global water-to-source ratio does not conserve the experiment. Area, boundary, path length, rate and source depth scale differently.

Second, the wet interface is a distinct architecture beneath water morphology. It owns supply, final elements, field truth, contact, drainage, roots, safeing, evidence and return.

Third, both public morphologies must cross one selected interface. Otherwise morphology and host architecture become inseparable.

Fourth, source–water contact is its own proposition. Neither issued water nor changed flame behavior establishes where water met source.

Fifth, the unavoidable liquid remainder makes runoff a primary system. At least 7.884 cubic metres remains under the source-energy-only ceiling.

Sixth, field boundary grows more slowly than liquid inventory. Drains and receivers require sector-resolved evidence rather than geometric scaling.

Seventh, the wet atmosphere system is not the dry exhaust system with added humidity. Steam, condensate, droplet carryover, filter loading, bypass and entry state require separate observations.

Eighth, the scale bridge needs deliberately nonproportional states. Fixed-water and field-expansion controls reveal interactions that a straight 2.5-times test would hide.

Ninth, ambitious technology funding is a condition of knowability. High-flow morphology control, multiphase tomography, source-bearing-water separation and remote recovery are required to decide the event at all.
