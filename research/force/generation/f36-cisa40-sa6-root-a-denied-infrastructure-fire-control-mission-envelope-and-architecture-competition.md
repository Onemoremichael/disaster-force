# RBS-1 — Root A denied-infrastructure fire-control mission envelope and architecture competition

## Decision abstract

`RBS-1` is the first operational candidate program permitted by the transfer ceiling in [RBR-1](f36-cisa40-sa6-root-a-am06-whole-ladder-closure-transfer-ceiling-and-candidate-entry-constitution.md). It does not start from a vehicle. It starts from a bounded public mission:

> After regional grid, water, communications and normal drainage fail, establish first independent fire-control effect within six hours of receiver access, create a one-kilometre protected control edge with twelve independently safeable intervention cells within eighteen hours, prevent registered crossing for seventy-two hours, control contaminated runoff and preserve public rescue, evidence and regeneration without host services.

The planning environment is a 500-by-500-metre urban-industrial casualty box with a 1,000-metre protected edge. It is a design stress state, not a claim about typical incidents. The force must establish twelve 25-square-metre high-intensity intervention cells. Each cell retains the `ER-10` reference issue capability of 48 cubic metres in 120 seconds, but no operational performance credit transfers from that reference.

A simultaneous twelve-cell salvo is 576 cubic metres at 4.8 cubic metres per second. Three complete salvos per hour require 1,728 cubic metres per hour. Continuous use for seventy-two hours would require 124,416 cubic metres. No mobile formation can treat that mass as carried stores. The mission therefore makes water acquisition, treatment, storage, distribution, capture and reuse part of the operational weapon system.

The `DRY-ENTRY` state carries one complete 576-cubic-metre salvo and must establish a raw-water path within two hours of first effect. The `WET-READY` state accepts raw freshwater, brackish water, saltwater or contaminated floodwater through separately qualified treatment modes. It provides at least 2,000 cubic metres per hour of fire-control-grade output, two independent five-kilometre trunklines and a 1,200-cubic-metre ready buffer. Recovered water receives no supply credit until chemistry, source ancestry and equipment-protection limits close.

At the 120-metre hydraulic-head screen, twelve simultaneous reference cells require approximately 8.056 megawatts of pump input. `RBS-1` therefore registers 20 megawatts of continuous and 35 megawatts of peak independent power for water production, distribution, recovery, robotics, atmosphere, communications, evidence and safeing. The numbers are system requirements for competition, not a selected generator technology.

The recovery system must accept 5.5 cubic metres per second of mixed arrival and provide 3,000 cubic metres of isolated surge storage. It separately accounts for candidate water, public water, source-bearing water, treatment concentrate, foam or agent, rain and unknown liquid. A goal of returning 60 percent of chemically eligible captured water to fire-control grade is tested; the governing raw-water supply assumes zero reuse.

Four rival `DFC-72` architectures enter. `LATTICE-12` uses a rapidly deployed cellular pipe, sensor and collector grid. `RING-12` uses a dual perimeter main with passive sector diversion. `SPAN-12` separates elevated delivery from ground and subsurface recovery. `SWARM-12` distributes water, power, sensing and capture among autonomous mobile cells. Each may use novel energy, materials, robotics and treatment; none may inherit operational effectiveness from its `AM-06` analogue.

Two independent builders at three campuses create twenty-four quarter-edge articles. They execute 288 architecture trials. All six pairs execute 192 matched comparisons. Two surviving architectures then produce eight full mission systems—two builder lineages at two proving regions—and execute 144 mission trials spanning short, intermediate, full-day and seventy-two-hour cases. The full-system campaign governs 2,592 operating hours. At the registered 30-percent average issue factor, the development-water screen is approximately 1.344 million cubic metres before quarter-edge work.

Every candidate must provide the twelve objects required by `OCE-1`: mission effect, environment, service contract, nine-root mechanism, public-rescue separation, service islands, safeing, logistics and regeneration, native evidence, industrial rights, cost and production hypotheses, and falsification campaign. Candidate selection is conjunctive. No score can compensate for failure of public rescue, water custody, independent energy, crossing control, runoff containment, safeing, reconstruction or return.

The mission clock has four distinct boundaries: national order to receiver, receiver access to first effect, first effect to full control edge and seventy-two-hour endurance. For the registered 500-kilometre regional case, first effect is required within eighteen hours of national order. A 1,500-kilometre national reinforcement case requires first effect within thirty-six hours. Strategic transport remains an explicit candidate burden.

The operational formation hypothesis is a six-set `Denied-Infrastructure Fire Control Group`. One set is alert, one mobilizes, one operates, one relieves, one regenerates and one remains in deep maintenance or training. The group is not yet a procurement quantity. It is the smallest rotational structure that prevents a single successful deployment from consuming national readiness.

The 280-week primary and 300-week maximum program requires 12,000 execution positions, USD 38 billion capital, USD 4.2 billion per year and USD 15 billion in mission-led technology. Retaining the 7,350-position reference and closure estate produces 19,350 concurrent positions and USD 5.67 billion concurrent annual support. `RBS-1` issues `RAS-3R9`: 1,600 weeks, 21,000 positions, USD 75 billion capital, USD 6 billion per year and USD 35 billion technology.

An accepted competition can authorize one or two full-system architectures for production-equivalent design. It does not authorize fleet production, national quantity, deployment, export or exterior rendering. The next admissible object is `RBT-1`, the production-equivalent system, crew, logistics and regeneration constitution.

## 2. Water system from first principles

### 2.1 Salvo arithmetic

One intervention cell retains the reference capacity:

\[
48\ \text{m}^3 / 120\ \text{s}=0.4\ \text{m}^3/\text{s}.
\]

Twelve simultaneous cells require:

\[
12\times48=576\ \text{m}^3
\]

and

\[
12\times0.4=4.8\ \text{m}^3/\text{s}.
\]

At three salvos per hour:

\[
576\times3=1{,}728\ \text{m}^3/\text{h}.
\]

The seventy-two-hour continuous-use screen is 124,416 cubic metres. A 2,000-cubic-metre-per-hour production path can supply 144,000 cubic metres over that interval, leaving a 19,584-cubic-metre planning difference before downtime, treatment reject, line loss or unusable raw water. Those losses require physical allocation; the difference is not a reserve credit.

### 2.2 Two entry states

`DRY-ENTRY` provides one 576-cubic-metre salvo. At 998 kilograms per cubic metre, that is 574.848 tonnes of water before tanks, pumps or vehicles. The mass requires a distributed train. It is not a plausible onboard load for one apparatus.

`WET-READY` begins only after two unlike intake paths, treatment, 1,200 cubic metres of measured ready storage, both trunks and terminal capture are accepted. One trunk can sustain reduced operation after loss of the other; full twelve-cell concurrency may depend on both.

### 2.3 Raw-water modes

The candidate must declare separate modes for:

- freshwater with low solids;
- brackish or saline water;
- floodwater with suspended solids;
- chemically contaminated water;
- biologically suspect water; and
- source-bearing recovered water.

One treatment setting cannot be assumed safe for all modes. Each mode binds intake exclusion, pretreatment, corrosion, nozzle compatibility, aerosol consequence, concentrate, worker exposure and terminal discharge.

## 3. Rapid trunkline requirement

The mission requires two independent five-kilometre water paths from raw source to the casualty box. A notional 0.45-metre internal diameter line carrying half of 2,000 cubic metres per hour has mean velocity:

\[
v=\frac{0.27778}{\pi(0.45)^2/4}\approx1.747\ \text{m/s}.
\]

The calculation establishes a geometric screen only. Terrain, elevation, bends, fittings, material, temperature, solids, deployment joints and transient closure remain candidate-specific.

Each line must:

- deploy five kilometres within six hours of route release;
- cross damaged roads, debris and shallow water without hidden civil works;
- isolate any 250-metre section;
- identify leak location and mass loss;
- permit robotic inspection and repair;
- survive one complete line loss without unsafe reverse flow; and
- return through cleaning, decontamination, inspection and requalification.

Robotic spool, articulated segment, fabric composite, self-forming conduit and locally manufactured pipe are all admissible technology paths.

Twelve cells at 0.4 cubic metre per second and the 120-metre head screen require:

\[
12\times671.340\ \text{kW}=8.056\ \text{MW}
\]

of pump input. The operational energy budget registers:

| --- | ---: | ---: |
| intervention hydraulics | 8 MW | 12 MW |
| intake and treatment | 4 MW | 6 MW |
| recovery and separation | 3 MW | 5 MW |

Candidates may use microreactors, turbines, fuel cells, high-temperature storage, batteries or unlike combinations. Technology choice does not change the required black start, emissions account, fuel or energy custody, human exclusion, shutdown and 72-hour endurance evidence.

## 5. Recovery and environmental control

The recovery architecture accepts 5.5 cubic metres per second, 14.6 percent above simultaneous cell issue. It provides 3,000 cubic metres of zoned surge custody—slightly more than five full salvos—before continuous treatment or transfer.

Seven liquid classes remain separate:

1. candidate source water;
2. public rescue water;
3. source-bearing captured liquid;
4. chemically eligible recovered water;
5. treatment reject or concentrate;
6. rain, groundwater and unknown inflow; and
7. cleaning and decontamination water.

The 60-percent reuse goal applies only to captured water that independently meets the registered chemical and equipment-protection envelope. The mission supply requirement assumes no reuse. A higher observed recovery fraction may reduce logistics in later force design; it cannot cure missing water during qualification.

## 6. Twelve-cell operational geometry

The protected edge divides into twelve named sectors of approximately 83.3 metres. The 25-square-metre reference cell is a local high-intensity intervention state, not the sector area. Each sector includes:

- two independent water feeds;
- local morphology and flow truth;
- source, flame, energy and topology observation;
- a contact and diversion boundary;
- public-rescue access;
- active and passive safeing;
- local command and time;
- two native evidence stores; and
- isolated recovery custody.

Cells can concentrate, separate or reposition. Every change produces a new geometry state. A candidate may not claim a continuous one-kilometre edge by adding the nominal reach of twelve cells without measuring gaps and crossing.

## 7. Four operational architectures

### 7.1 `LATTICE-12`

A rapid cellular grid distributes water, sensing, local power and recovery across the protected edge. Each cell can isolate from its neighbors, while surveyed cross-links support reconfiguration.

The primary advantage is graceful local loss. Principal unknowns are installation time, joint count, damaged-ground fit, cross-link common cause and regeneration burden.

### 7.2 `RING-12`

Two unlike perimeter mains feed twelve sectors. Gravity-first gutters and sumps direct captured liquid to separated receivers. Active pumps provide issue and transfer; passive geometry preserves a safe terminal route.

The primary advantage is a clear edge and simple hydraulic hierarchy. Principal unknowns are terrain dependence, obstruction, long-path transients and interior blind regions.

### 7.3 `SPAN-12`

Elevated delivery spans place water and sensing above debris while independent ground and subsurface collectors own recovery. Delivery and capture have different support and failure paths.

The primary advantage is access over blocked ground. Principal unknowns are wind, structural stability, span deployment, overhead hazard and coordinate dependence between the two planes.

### 7.4 `SWARM-12`

Autonomous mobile cells carry local water, energy, sensing, compute and capture. A mission coordinator assigns zones but cannot be the sole safeing or evidence authority.

The primary advantage is reconfiguration and direct field translation. Principal unknowns are navigation, congestion, cooperative hydraulics, exhaust or battery consequence, cyber-physical common cause and maintenance population.

## 9. Quarter-edge competition

Each architecture enters through two builders at three campuses, creating twenty-four quarter-edge articles. A quarter edge has three intervention cells, 250 metres of control boundary, 1.25 kilometres of each trunkline, 300 cubic metres of ready storage and 750 cubic metres of surge custody.

Each article executes twelve trials:

1. dry deployment and geometry;
2. freshwater acquisition;
3. saline-water mode;
4. sediment-laden mode;
5. three-cell simultaneous salvo;
6. one trunk lost;
7. grid, network and primary time denied;
8. recovery path restricted;
9. public intervention;
10. cell reposition and gap challenge;
11. interrupted safeing and rollback; and
12. return after contaminated operation.

The 288 trials receive no operational mission credit. All six architecture pairs then execute sixteen blinded common-truth comparisons under two raw-water modes, two service states and four repetitions, producing 192 comparisons.

## 10. Downselection and full mission systems

At most two architectures advance. A candidate fails if any hard gate fails:

- first effect and full edge miss the clock;
- a protected-edge crossing is hidden or misclassified;
- one water or energy loss collapses all cells;
- a raw-water mode lacks safe treatment and terminal reject custody;
- public rescue requires candidate permission;
- source-bearing runoff lacks destination;
- one safeing function lacks unlike active and passive paths;
- native evidence depends on the coordinator or cloud;
- three outside parties cannot reconstruct the mission;
- return cannot restore the set without cannibalizing the alert set; or
- public interface and repair rights are absent.

Eligible candidates are ordered by worst-case mission loss, then regeneration time, raw-water burden, energy burden, workforce, capital and a sealed public draw.

Two candidates, two builders and two proving regions create eight full mission systems. Each executes eighteen trials:

| Trial class | Count per system | Duration each | Purpose |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| segment | 6 | 2 h | isolated mechanisms and denial changes |
| intermediate | 6 | 12 h | shift, treatment, capture and repair behavior |
| full-day | 4 | 24 h | endurance, public interface and evidence |
| full mission | 2 | 72 h | complete service loss, relief and regeneration entry |

Each system accumulates 324 operating hours; the eight-system population totals 2,592 hours. At 30 percent of the 1,728-cubic-metre-per-hour issue screen, full-system development uses 1,343,692.8 cubic metres. Reconditioned water may reenter only after complete custody closure.

## 11. Mission trial population

The eighteen cases collectively cover:

- both 500-kilometre and 1,500-kilometre strategic distances;
- freshwater, saline, sediment and chemical raw-water modes;
- both trunk directions and one complete trunk loss;
- zero host grid, water, network, primary time and drainage;
- three-cell and twelve-cell simultaneous issue;
- cell loss, coordinator loss and evidence-store loss;
- receiver restriction and treatment-reject accumulation;
- public rescue with candidate action continuing;
- public rescue with candidate action denied;
- day-night handover and relief formation;
- contaminated return and repair; and
- a repeated mission after the registered regeneration interval.

One favorable seventy-two-hour run cannot replace the population. Short trials isolate mechanism; long trials expose cumulative state.

## 12. Command, public authority and evidence

The deployed system has five separate command chains:

1. incident mission command assigns the protected edge and priorities;
2. candidate operations control water, power, cells and recovery;
3. public rescue commands life-protection action;
4. safety can stop any exposure and order a terminal state; and
5. evidence and reconstruction authorities preserve native truth.

Candidate operations cannot delay public rescue or classify its own performance. Safety cannot award mission credit. Evidence custody cannot decide whether the candidate met the effect. The mission board receives separately signed packets.

Operational evidence is tiered. High-rate cell and root records persist through active transitions. Lower-rate state, custody and environment records persist through seventy-two hours and regeneration. Two independent originals survive coordinator, network and one-store loss. Three outside reconstructions receive no origin code, analyst or conclusion.

## 13. Logistics and rotational force

The candidate must account for:

- 574.848 tonnes of dry-entry water;
- 1,200 cubic metres of ready storage;
- ten kilometres of primary trunkline;
- one kilometre of protected-edge distribution and recovery;
- 20 MW continuous and 35 MW peak power;
- 3,000 cubic metres of surge receivers;
- treatment media, spares, concentrates and waste;
- twelve intervention cells and replacements;
- crew shelter, medical support and contaminated-zone protection; and
- return, decontamination, calibration and repair.

The initial force hypothesis contains six system sets. At any moment the formation can hold one alert, one mobilizing, one operating, one relief, one regenerating and one deep-maintenance or training set. The state assignment rotates. A set cannot occupy two states, and training cannot be canceled in the arithmetic to manufacture readiness.

This six-set construct becomes a trial input. `RBT-1` must derive the actual crew, fleet and arsenal quantity from observed setup, mission, relief, return, repair and reliability distributions.

## 19. Stop and release law

The program stops or narrows if:

- the mission effect is rewritten after candidate data;
- reference-cell qualification is treated as operational performance;
- carried water is counted without its transport and storage mass;
- recovered water is credited before chemical eligibility;
- a single intake, trunk, power island, coordinator or receiver can collapse all cells;
- a one-kilometre edge is claimed from nominal cell reach without gap observation;
- public rescue requires candidate permission or improves candidate credit;
- treatment reject, source-bearing liquid or unknown inflow lacks custody;
- a candidate omits strategic transport or regeneration;
- a full mission consumes the alert or relief set;
- one builder controls the public reference, repair data or acceptance method;
- outside reconstruction needs origin services;
- a valid adverse trial is repeated to seek a favorable result;
- an invalidity reserve crosses candidate, lineage, mission case or region;
- exterior form is selected before production-equivalent requirements; or
- parent authority is absent.

Quarter-edge selection authorizes full-system build only. Full-system qualification authorizes `RBT-1` only. No result authorizes fleet procurement or field deployment.

## 20. Research stocktake after Pass 155

### What became authoritative

- the first operational mission is a denied-infrastructure fire-control and contaminated-runoff problem, not a vehicle category;
- twelve concurrent cells create a 576 m³ salvo and 4.8 m³/s peak issue;
- seventy-two-hour continuous use screens at 124,416 m³, forcing local raw-water acquisition;
- `DRY-ENTRY` requires a distributed 574.848-tonne initial water train;
- the force needs 2,000 m³/h water production, two five-kilometre trunks, 1,200 m³ ready storage, 5.5 m³/s capture and 3,000 m³ surge custody;
- 20 MW continuous and 35 MW peak independent power define the system energy estate;
- four rival operational architectures enter through 24 quarter-edge and eight full mission systems;
- the campaign contains 288 article trials, 192 comparisons and 144 mission trials;
- six rotating sets are the minimum initial readiness hypothesis; and
- the program requires a USD 38B capital and USD 15B technology scale.

### What was falsified or narrowed

- a firefighting vehicle cannot be specified independently of raw water, energy, recovery and regeneration;
- one carried tank cannot sustain the registered mission;
- water reuse cannot enter the governing supply floor before chemistry closes;
- twelve reference cells do not establish a continuous edge by arithmetic;
- autonomous cells cannot depend on one coordinator for safeing or evidence;
- a single successful 72-hour event cannot qualify the system; and
- `RAS-3R8` cannot contain an operational candidate program.

### What remains provisional

- every operational effect threshold and trial result;
- raw-water treatment performance and reject burden;
- trunkline material, deployment method and losses;
- candidate energy technology and emissions;
- cell reach, spacing, reposition and crossing behavior;
- recovery fraction and reuse eligibility;
- strategic transport mode and actual arrival time;
- six-set rotational sufficiency;
- unit staffing, national quantity, production rate and life-cycle cost; and
- all exterior form and renderings.
