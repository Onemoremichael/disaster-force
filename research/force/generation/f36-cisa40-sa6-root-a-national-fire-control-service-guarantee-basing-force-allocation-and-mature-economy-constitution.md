# RBV-1 — National fire-control service guarantee, basing, force-allocation and mature-economy constitution

## Executive judgment

`RBV-1` asks what national institution follows if the first fire-control group defined by [RBU-1](f36-cisa40-sa6-root-a-first-fire-control-group-production-line-transport-and-operational-acceptance-constitution.md) eventually proves physically credible. It converts one representative eight-set group into a national service promise, a force-allocation law, a basing topology, a replacement-production requirement, a public workforce and a twenty-five-year economic account.

The answer is not a multiplication of the research program. `RAS-3R11` is an evidence and acceptance estate. It cannot be relabeled as an operating service merely because its tests succeed. A national fleet requires a separate fielding constitution with separate authorities, accounts, sites and readiness law.

This pass therefore creates `NFCA-1`, the National Fire-Control Allocation planning envelope. Its selected reference is `C24`:

- twelve sector groups, each guaranteeing one continuous domestic response line;
- four national compound-event groups;
- four invited international-assistance groups, subordinate to the domestic floor;
- four protected strategic-reserve groups that cannot be consumed by routine tempo;
- twenty-four groups and 192 operational sets in total;
- 256 system bodies after training, independent evaluation, reference, destructive, depot-float and casualty-replacement articles are added;
- forty-eight launch stations and eighteen arsenals organized into six four-group arsenal cells;
- 84,480 service prime movers and 79,200 governed load frames;
- 345,600 operating positions, 32,000 public industrial positions and a 51,840-person qualified reserve;
- USD 3.6412016 trillion of initial capital, USD 250 billion of technology authority and USD 290.465648 billion per year of mature support in constant 2026 dollars; and
- USD 12.19164112 trillion of undiscounted twenty-five-year public cost after scheduled transport recapitalization, system midlife modernization and facility recapitalization.

These figures are not a demand forecast, a procurement recommendation or a claim that twenty-four groups cover the geography of the United States. They are the least force among four explicit candidates that satisfies the selected four-part service guarantee. The guarantee itself is a constitutional design choice. Later work must test it against a synthetic national demand atlas, access times, receiver locations, correlated hazard worlds and the other Resilience Force portfolios.

The cost result is strategically important. The dedicated `C24` fire-control upper bound consumes about 24.0 percent of the project’s existing USD 15.179 trillion force-capital boundary before its technology account. One mission family cannot be multiplied forty-eight times with its own transport, power, communications, arsenals and public configuration institution. National scale therefore reveals the next first-principles problem: which capabilities must remain dedicated to preserve service, and which can become common-force assets governed by enforceable availability contracts. `RBW-1` must answer that question before geographic sites, production contracts or exterior forms are frozen.

No physical evidence gate closes in this pass. No system is fielded. No city or region is selected. No exterior rendering is authorized.

## 1. Decision object

The decision object is a national service constitution, not a fleet list.

`RBV-1` must answer six questions:

1. What continuous fire-control service does the Department of Resilience promise?
2. How many indivisible one-line groups follow from that promise?
3. Which groups are regionally assigned, nationally allocable, internationally available or strategically protected?
4. What launch, regeneration, training, evaluation and industrial geography preserves that service after selected site and root losses?
5. What replacement rates, workforce and public budget keep the force real after the initial build?
6. Which apparent conclusions remain unearned because no physical campaign or geographic demand model has been executed?

The pass is governed by the project’s service rule:

> A force quantity is meaningful only when it specifies the public effect, response time, duration, damage world, recovery obligation and reserve that the quantity must sustain.

The object inherited from `RBU-1` is one continuous `DFC-72` response line. One line holds a one-kilometre protected edge for seventy-two hours after water, grid, communications and drainage fail. The group rotates seven line sets through a 450-hour occupied cycle and protects an eighth set for a no-notice onset.[1–4]

The group is the smallest readiness-bearing national unit. A system body, convoy, bay or intervention cell cannot receive independent national readiness credit.

## 2. Status law and category correction

### 2.1 The research estate is not the operating force

`RAS-3R11` governs a long chain of source, water, flame, casualty, mission, production and operational-acceptance research. It contains experimental articles, destructive articles, calibration systems, range campuses, frozen protocols and evidence authorities. Those assets exist to reject false claims.

An operating service exists to deliver an accepted service repeatedly.

The distinction creates four separate ledgers:

| Ledger | Owner | What it can claim | What it cannot claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| research | `RAS-3R11` program authority | accepted mechanism, interface and production evidence | national readiness |
| fielding | `NFCA-1` acquisition authority | serialized public assets delivered against an accepted baseline | mission availability |
| readiness | Fire Control Service | complete groups available under the allocation law | experimental validity |
| public effect | civilian effect authority | verified service at a named receiver and population | technical acceptance by itself |

No ledger reads credit from a downstream ledger. A research article does not become a fleet set by reassignment. A fielded set does not become ready without its crew, transport, reserve, launch station and regeneration rights. A ready group does not create public effect until its receiver and civilian chain close.

### 2.2 `NFCA-1`

`NFCA-1` is a planning envelope with five boundaries:

- force boundary: 256 system bodies, of which 192 are operational;
- geography boundary: forty-eight abstract launch stations and eighteen arsenals;
- personnel boundary: 429,440 public personnel across operating, industrial and qualified-reserve accounts;
- fiscal boundary: initial capital, technology, annual support and scheduled recapitalization are separate; and
- authority boundary: planning reconciliation cannot issue procurement, site selection, operational assignment or deployment.

The envelope may be revised when demand, sharing or physical evidence changes. Revision is expected. Treating the first national number as immutable would repeat the exact category error this research chain was built to prevent.

## 3. The service unit

### 3.1 One line

One response line is the continuing ability to place one complete `DFC-72` set into a mission, replace it every seventy-two hours and regenerate every withdrawn set through the inherited 450-hour occupied cycle.

The line arithmetic is:

\[
N_{line}=\left\lceil\frac{450\ \text{hours}}{72\ \text{hours}}\right\rceil=7\ \text{line sets}
\]

The protected onset reserve adds one set:

\[
N_{group}=7+1=8\ \text{operational sets}
\]

The reserve is not an eighth scheduled turn. If routine planning consumes it, the group no longer meets the service constitution.

### 3.2 Two lines

Two indefinite response lines require two complete groups:

\[
N_{two-line}=2\times(7+1)=16\ \text{sets}
\]

Fourteen sets preserve two rotations and zero onset reserve. Fifteen sets preserve two rotations and only one shared reserve. Both arrangements fail the requirement that each line remain able to answer a distinct no-notice onset.

`RBV-1` therefore rejects a permanent fourteen- or fifteen-set “wing.” A two-line theater wing is a command relationship between two eight-set groups. Each group retains its own crew, transport account, launch rights, regeneration share and protected reserve.

### 3.3 What one line does not mean

One line is not:

- one wildfire;
- one county;
- one state;
- one simultaneous public incident of any size;
- guaranteed travel time from an unspecified base;
- a replacement for evacuation, building protection, public health or long-term recovery; or
- a claim that one kilometre is the correct operational edge for every hazard.

An incident may need several lines. Several smaller incidents may share a theater command but cannot share one indivisible group at the same time. Geographic analysis must translate demand into line-equivalents rather than count disaster declarations.

### 3.4 Annual and concurrent capacity

At a seventy-two-hour issue interval, one line can issue approximately 121.67 set orders per year:

\[
365/3=121.667
\]

Twenty-four lines can issue 2,920 set orders per year if every line remains continuously occupied. That number is a mechanical capacity screen, not a desirable utilization target. The selected service law normally protects four strategic lines and may withhold four global lines, so full mechanical use would itself indicate that the reserve constitution has failed.

## 4. Four rival national guarantees

The project has no external hazard-frequency dataset in this pass. It therefore does not pretend to optimize a probability distribution. It compares four clear service constitutions against four explicit entitlements.

### 4.1 Hard entitlements used in this pass

The selected constitutional test requires:

| Entitlement | Required lines | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| domestic sector floor | 12 | one continuous line for each of twelve abstract service sectors |
| compound-event increment | 4 | four additional domestic lines without withdrawing the sector floor |
| invited international assistance | 4 | four lines available by invitation while the domestic compound floor is preserved |
| strategic reserve | 4 | four uncommitted lines protected from routine domestic or international tempo |

These are policy inputs. Twelve sectors do not yet correspond to twelve map polygons. Four compound lines do not assert that four are statistically sufficient. The value of the test is that the obligation is visible and falsifiable.

### 4.2 Candidate set

| Candidate | Sector | Compound | Global | Strategic | Total groups | Result |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `A12` | 12 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 12 | fails compound, global and strategic entitlements |
| `B18` | 12 | 4 | 0 | 2 | 18 | fails global and strategic entitlements |
| `C24` | 12 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 24 | passes all four selected entitlements |
| `D30` | 16 | 6 | 4 | 4 | 30 | passes, but adds six groups without a stated entitlement |

`C24` is selected because it is the least candidate in this set that passes every stated entitlement. It is not called optimal. `D30` may become necessary when geography, travel time, seasonal maintenance, workforce absence or cross-portfolio coupling is modeled.

### 4.3 Why a force larger than the sector floor is necessary

`A12` creates twelve regional capabilities but no national force. Any cross-sector reinforcement would cancel another sector’s guarantee.

`B18` creates limited domestic depth but treats international assistance as spare capacity and leaves only two strategic groups. That would make the international instrument disappear whenever domestic demand rises and would allow routine scheduling to consume the national reserve.

`C24` makes all four obligations visible. The force can sustain sixteen domestic lines, offer four invited lines and keep four strategic groups uncommitted.

`D30` may improve access and failure tolerance, but additional groups need a defined public promise. More assets without a service rule become an untestable claim.

## 5. Allocation constitution

### 5.1 Four categories, one technical standard

All twenty-four groups share the same technical readiness standard. Their allocation rights differ.

| Category | Groups | Routine authority | Recall or withholding rule |
|---|---:|---|---|
| sector | 12 | sector command within national doctrine | may reinforce another sector only if its own line is restored or formally transferred |
| national compound | 4 | National Service Allocation Command | cannot be preassigned to routine local schedules |
| invited global | 4 | Department invitation authority plus receiving-sovereign consent | withheld or recalled when the sixteen-line domestic compound floor would otherwise fail |
| strategic | 4 | Secretary-level strategic release board | cannot count toward ordinary utilization, exercises or recurring international commitments |

The categories are budget and readiness accounts, not paint schemes. A group may change category only through a published baseline amendment that also restores the category it leaves.

### 5.2 Priority order

When demand exceeds immediately available lines, the allocation authority applies this order:

1. preserve life-safety actions already in execution;
2. preserve the twelve-sector domestic floor;
3. activate up to four national compound groups;
4. withhold or recall invited global groups before reducing the domestic compound guarantee;
5. release strategic groups only under the strategic-release rule; and
6. publish every unserved line requirement rather than treating it as zero demand.

This order does not decide which community deserves service. The civilian effect authority still applies rights, vulnerability and consequence criteria within each allocation tier. The order protects the integrity of the national promise.

### 5.3 Strategic-release rule

A strategic group can be released only when all of the following are recorded:

- the event exceeds the sixteen-line domestic compound constitution or causes a named system-root casualty;
- the receiving theater has complete launch, access and receiver rights;
- the release does not consume the last root-balanced strategic pair;
- a replacement or return plan is registered; and
- the public ledger states which strategic capacity becomes unavailable and for how long.

Strategic groups may exercise, but the exercise cannot overlap the response window unless a second group holds the same readiness obligation.

### 5.4 International service

The four global groups are not a humanitarian annex. They are full Fire Control Service groups with the same production, crew, safety, evidence and public-effect standard as domestic groups.

Their use additionally requires:

- invitation and host-sovereign operating authority;
- compatible water, energy, access and waste rules;
- receiver-language and accessibility packages;
- customs and hazardous-material custody;
- public data and evidence terms; and
- an exit condition that does not strand a service population.

International availability is real only if these interfaces exist before a crisis. `NFCA-1` includes interface and prepositioning support but does not select partner states.

| Formation | Quantity | Operational sets | Protected reserves | Continuous lines |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| sector group | 12 | 96 | 12 | 12 |

| strategic group | 4 | 32 | 4 | 4 |
| **total** | **24** | **192** | **24** | **24** |

Each group contains seven rotation sets and one protected onset reserve. The reserve count is included in operational sets.

### 6.2 Task-organized echelons

The national force uses command echelons without creating fractional groups:

| Echelon | Constituent groups | Lines | Sets | Use |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| response group | 1 | 1 | 8 | one continuing public-service edge |
| theater wing | 2 | 2 | 16 | two independent edges or one two-line incident |
| compound brigade | 4 | 4 | 32 | multi-edge domestic or international theater |
| arsenal cell | 4 | 4 | 32 | regeneration, training and industrial support geography |
| national force | 24 | 24 | 192 | four-part service guarantee |

Theater wings and compound brigades are command assemblies. They do not merge reserves or regeneration entitlements. This preserves the ability to divide again without reconstructing readiness accounts.

### 6.3 System-root balance

Every eight-set group contains four system bodies from each accepted production root. Each of its two launch stations holds two sets from each root in the normal posture. No launch-station loss removes all of one root from a group.

Root balance applies to configuration authority, modules, transport interfaces and trained maintenance, not merely builder name. A supplier logo cannot establish technical independence.

## 7. Fleet quantity beyond the 192 operational sets

### 7.1 Why operational sets are not the fleet

The service needs training, evaluation, reference, destructive, overhaul and casualty capacity that cannot be counted as available line sets. `RBV-1` places those bodies in explicit accounts.

| Fleet account | System bodies | Derivation | Readiness credit |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| operational | 192 | 24 groups × 8 sets | 192 |
| training | 12 | one body per two groups | 0 |
| independent evaluation | 6 | one per arsenal cell | 0 |
| cold reference | 4 | two roots × two reference environments | 0 |
| destructive | 6 | one major-block terminal article per arsenal cell | 0 |
| depot float | 24 | 16 expected deep-overhaul occupants plus 8 capacity contingency | 0 |
| casualty replacement | 12 | two root-balanced bodies per arsenal cell | 0 until issued |
| **total** | **256** | reconciled fleet | **192** |

### 7.2 Depot-float derivation

The planning input assumes each of the 192 operational sets receives one ninety-day deep overhaul every three years:

\[
192\times\frac{90}{3\times365}=15.78
\]

Sixteen bodies cover expected occupancy. Eight additional bodies provide a fifty-percent capacity contingency for clustering, overrun or denial. The resulting twenty-four-body depot float is 12.5 percent of the operational fleet.

This is an assumption, not observed maintenance behavior. The workbook exposes overhaul days, interval and contingency so later evidence can replace them.

### 7.3 Casualty replacements

Each of six arsenal cells receives two root-balanced casualty-replacement bodies. These bodies remain preserved and configuration-current. They do not backfill routine overhaul, exercises or schedule gaps.

The account can restore one complete two-root pair in every cell after a destructive or nonrepairable loss. A larger casualty world remains unserved and visible.

### 7.4 Serviceable and destructive populations

The replacement-production calculation uses 250 serviceable bodies:

\[
256-6\ \text{destructive}=250
\]

Destructive articles have their own block-build requirement and are not treated as twenty-five-year service assets.

## 8. Basing topology

### 8.1 Topology before cities

This pass selects no geographic location. It defines four kinds of places:

1. launch station: holds four operational sets and their immediate transport;
2. arsenal: regenerates complete sets and holds governed reserve modules;
3. production campus: builds system bodies, module families or transport;
4. public standards and configuration node: preserves interfaces, software, reference artifacts and release authority.

A location becomes eligible only when later analysis proves access, utility independence, workforce, correlated-hazard separation, environmental custody and receiving relationships.

### 8.2 Forty-eight launch stations

Every group is divided between two launch stations:

| Per group | Station 1 | Station 2 | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| operational sets | 4 | 4 | 8 |
| system root A sets | 2 | 2 | 4 |
| system root B sets | 2 | 2 | 4 |
| service movers | 1,600 | 1,600 | 3,200 |
| governed frames | 1,500 | 1,500 | 3,000 |

The national topology therefore contains forty-eight launch stations. A station loss removes half a group’s immediately based assets but does not remove one entire system root.

Launch stations require at least two independent outbound route families, local black-start energy, secure crew assembly, configuration verification, clean and contaminated holding zones, and enough hardstand for four complete packet sets. Exact land, water and power requirements remain open.

### 8.3 Six arsenal cells

The nation is divided into six abstract arsenal cells. Each cell supports four groups and three unlike arsenals.

The category pattern avoids concentrating all allocable classes in one cell:

| Arsenal cell | Sector groups | Compound groups | Global groups | Strategic groups | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `AC-1` | 2 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 4 |
| `AC-2` | 2 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 4 |
| `AC-3` | 2 | 0 | 1 | 1 | 4 |
| `AC-4` | 2 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 4 |
| `AC-5` | 2 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 4 |
| `AC-6` | 2 | 0 | 1 | 1 | 4 |
| **total** | **12** | **4** | **4** | **4** | **24** |

The pattern is not a map. It is an allocation-diversity requirement that later geography must preserve.

### 8.4 Arsenal-bay correction

One group requires nine complete-set bay equivalents across three arsenals in `RBU-1`. A tempting calculation would multiply the eight sets by the arsenal residence divided by the issue interval:

\[
8\times\frac{336}{72}=37.333
\]

That expression mixes set count and order interval if used directly. The correct steady line demand is the inherited 336-hour arsenal residence divided by the seventy-two-hour issue interval:

\[
D_{group}=336/72=4.667\ \text{bays}
\]

Four groups therefore require:

\[
D_{cell}=4\times4.667=18.667\ \text{bays}
\]

If each three-arsenal cell must survive loss of any one arsenal, the two remaining sites must cover at least 18.667 bays. Eight bays per site would leave sixteen and fail. Nine would leave eighteen and still fail. Ten bays per arsenal is the minimum integer topology:

\[
2\times10=20\ge18.667
\]

Residual utilization is 93.33 percent. The national estate therefore contains:

\[
6\ \text{cells}\times3\ \text{arsenals}\times10\ \text{bays}=180\ \text{bays}
\]

Normal national demand is 112 bays. If one arsenal in every cell is denied at once, 120 bays remain. The eight-bay national margin is deliberately narrow and must be tested against arrival clustering and invalid regeneration.

### 8.5 Arsenal independence

Three addresses do not make three arsenals. Each arsenal must possess independent:

- black-start and sustained energy;
- water production, sanitary custody and discharge authority;
- controlled clean and contaminated paths;
- lifting, inspection, repair and reassembly capacity;
- configuration originals and local verification;
- workforce access and minimum watch;
- inbound and outbound transport paths; and
- authority to return a complete set to service.

A shared utility, bridge, software signer or specialized repair team can collapse the three-site claim into one functional site.

## 9. Production and replacement constitution

### 9.1 Normal body replacement

Using 250 serviceable bodies and a twenty-five-year life gives a normal replacement requirement of ten systems per year:

\[
250/25=10\ \text{systems/year}
\]

The life is a planning input. Earlier retirement, block obsolescence or hazard damage would raise the requirement.

### 9.2 Independent-root capacity

One integration line at the inherited sixteen-week serial interval produces:

\[
52/16=3.25\ \text{systems/year}
\]

The national rule requires either production root to meet normal replacement alone. Three lines yield 9.75 systems per year and fail. Four lines yield thirteen and pass.

`NFCA-1` therefore requires:

- four integration lines per root;
- eight lines total;
- two campuses per root;
- two lines per campus; and
- twenty-six systems per year installed capacity with both roots available.

Loss of one entire root leaves thirteen systems per year, above the ten-system normal requirement. Loss of one campus in one root leaves 19.5 systems per year across the surviving six lines. These are capacity claims only; labor, modules, acceptance and transport must also close.

### 9.3 Initial fielding

At twenty-six systems per year, 256 bodies represent 9.846 years of full-rate output. `NFCA-1` uses a twelve-year fielding horizon to include line qualification, ramp, lot learning, arsenal activation and crew formation.

The schedule does not authorize a crash build before the accepted baseline exists. The two-root learning lots must enter fielding in balanced blocks so early operational groups do not become single-root fleets.

### 9.4 Transport replacement

The mature transport inventory includes a ten-percent strategic reserve above the twenty-four group pools:

| Family | Group pools | Strategic reserve | Total service inventory | Life | Normal annual replacement |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| prime movers | 76,800 | 7,680 | 84,480 | 12 years | 7,040 |
| governed frames | 72,000 | 7,200 | 79,200 | 20 years | 3,960 |

Each of two transport roots must independently meet the normal rate. The reference installs 7,500 mover positions and 4,200 frame positions per year in each root. Total installed rates are 15,000 movers and 8,400 frames per year.

The ten-percent reserve and asset lives are editable planning inputs. They are not measured reliability.

### 9.5 Module campuses

The nineteen dual-root production families remain governed as thirty-eight root cells. National scale does not permit a single campus to become the only process holder for a root.

The planning topology retains ten module campuses, five per root, but enlarges their rate and repair role. Every family must have:

- two independent root process definitions;
- transfer-capable public tooling and metrology;
- qualified surge shifts;
- an accepted substitute-material path or an explicit no-substitute rule;
- reserve replenishment capacity; and
- serialized configuration and custody records.

Exact family rates remain open until common-force decomposition determines which modules are truly dedicated to fire control.

### 11.6 Portfolio collision

The existing research architecture carries a USD 15.179 trillion force-capital boundary across forty-eight force families. The `C24` initial capital alone is approximately:

\[
3.6412016/15.179=23.99\% 
\]

Adding its USD 250 billion technology authority raises the share to approximately 25.64 percent.

This does not prove `C24` is unaffordable. It proves the ownership model cannot be repeated independently across every mission family. The national Resilience Force needs a common-force constitution that distinguishes:

- assets whose availability must be dedicated to one service;
- assets that may be pooled only within one effect portfolio;
- assets that can be shared across water, fire, flood, medical and power missions;
- assets that can be commercially mobilized under an accepted public baseline; and
- assets that must remain sovereign evidence or configuration infrastructure.

The dedicated figure is retained as an upper bound until that decomposition is performed. Reducing it by an arbitrary sharing percentage would hide rather than solve the problem.

## 12. Industrial economy and market structure

### 12.1 Public builder and competing roots

The mature economy needs more than prime contracts. It requires:

- a public technical commons that owns service interfaces and acceptance logic;
- two independent system roots capable of full replacement output;
- two roots for every governed module and transport family;
- public arsenals that can repair either root without supplier permission;
- independent evaluation and forensic institutions;
- supplier entry through public interface data; and
- protected reference articles and process records.

The Department should purchase learning, production rights, tooling, workforce qualification and configuration continuity, not only delivered units.

### 12.2 Competition over decades

A twenty-five-year fleet cannot depend on one source-selection moment. Competition occurs at five levels:

1. concept roots compete before architecture selection;
2. production roots compete while remaining technically unlike;
3. module suppliers compete against public interfaces;
4. overhaul and modernization teams compete against observed fleet evidence; and
5. new entrants reproduce selected families from public data packages.

Competition is lost if both nominal roots depend on the same process recipe, firmware signer, specialty material, metrology chain or acceptance authority.

### 12.3 Export opportunity

The export object is not simply a vehicle. It may include:

- complete service groups;
- smaller receiver-compatible service packages;
- arsenal process licenses;
- public interface standards;
- training and certification;
- root-diverse module families;
- partner production lines; and
- multinational reserve agreements.

No export is authorized here. Export eligibility must preserve domestic replacement capacity, recipient rights, end-use custody, safety evidence, configuration support and withdrawal obligations.

### 12.4 Civil use between catastrophes

Routine civil work can maintain proficiency only when it exercises the actual service chain. Appropriate uses may include:

- reservoir and treatment bypass exercises;
- prescribed-fire boundary protection;
- industrial water-contingency drills;
- grid-island and black-start service;
- international partner qualification; and
- scheduled arsenal regeneration campaigns.

Commercial rental that separates equipment from its certified crew, transport, evidence and return chain does not maintain readiness.

## 13. Technology at force scale

### 13.1 The innovation problem changes with quantity

The prototype question is whether a system can work. The national question is whether thousands of modules, tens of thousands of vehicles, hundreds of thousands of people and decades of configuration change can remain one safe service.

The hardest technology programs may therefore be institutional technologies:

- machine-readable physical interfaces;
- distributed configuration signing that survives network loss;
- automatic inventory and contamination state;
- root-neutral diagnostic equipment;
- public process twins tied to physical measurements;
- scalable convoy autonomy with human public authority;
- robotic arsenal handling and sanitation;
- local manufacture of certified repair parts;
- high-energy mobile utility protection; and
- receiver discovery and service negotiation before arrival.

### 13.2 Autonomy law

Autonomous systems may drive, inspect, meter, route, lift or monitor. They may not independently:

- allocate service among populations;
- waive safety or environmental limits;
- certify their own configuration;
- erase an interrupted mission record;
- change the public-effect standard; or
- convert an unavailable service into a nominal success.

Every autonomous action requires a recoverable command record and a safe degraded mode.

### 13.3 Open architecture without false interchangeability

Public interfaces should make substitution possible, but substitution must be earned at the service boundary. Two pumps with matching flanges are not interchangeable if controls, contamination state, energy quality, repair tools or evidence differ.

The architecture therefore distinguishes:

- physical fit;
- utility compatibility;
- control compatibility;
- safety compatibility;
- maintainability;
- evidence compatibility; and
- mission-effect equivalence.

Only the last state permits one family to replace another in readiness accounting.

## 15. Readiness measurement

### 15.1 Group readiness vector

A group receives readiness only when all dimensions are current:

\[
R_g=\min(S,C,T,L,A,E,U,P)
\]

where:

- (S) is eight accepted system sets;
- (C) is complete qualified crew;
- (T) is accepted transport and packet capacity;
- (L) is launch-station availability;
- (A) is arsenal and regeneration entitlement;
- (E) is energy, water and consumable state;
- (U) is configuration, software and evidence currency; and
- (P) is receiver and public-authority preparedness.

The minimum operator prevents a surplus of one dimension from hiding a deficit in another.

### 15.2 National readiness report

The national report must publish:

- ready groups by category;
- ready line-hours by category;
- protected reserves consumed or encumbered;
- systems in overhaul and expected return;
- transport packet shortfalls;
- arsenal bays available and occupied;
- qualified crew shortfalls by occupation;
- production-root and campus status;
- receiver agreements current or expired; and
- unserved line-equivalent demand.

An aggregate percentage is secondary. The category and complete-chain deficits are the governing facts.

### 15.3 False readiness states

The following do not count:

- seven ready sets plus one set expected soon;
- eight bodies without complete transport;
- equipment with borrowed uncertified operators;
- a launch station whose only outbound route is closed;
- a regenerated set awaiting configuration release;
- a strategic group scheduled into routine work; or
- an international group without host authority and receiver interfaces.

## 16. Command and civil authority

### 16.1 Department of Resilience

The Department owns:

- the four-part public service guarantee;
- rights, consent and allocation law;
- national and international activation authority;
- capital and lifecycle budgets;
- public technical and configuration standards;
- independent investigation; and
- publication of deficits and failures.

### 16.2 Fire Control Service

The Service owns:

- professions and career systems;
- twenty-four group readiness;
- launch stations and operating doctrine;
- arsenals and regeneration;
- training and evaluation;
- integration with other Resilience Force services; and
- mission execution after civilian authorization.

### 16.3 National Service Allocation Command

The allocation command maintains the national line ledger. It can task national compound groups and recommend global withholding or strategic release. It cannot certify technical readiness, change a civilian priority rule or declare its own mission effect.

### 16.4 Independent public-effect authority

The effect authority verifies whether the promised public service reached the stated receiver, population, time and duration. It remains independent of the operational chain and cannot be directed to convert partial delivery into full credit.

### 16.5 Local and sovereign receivers

Local, tribal, state or partner-sovereign authorities retain public-health, land, environmental and civil-rights powers defined by law and compact. National capacity does not erase receiver authority. Prepared agreements specify emergency exceptions and due process before deployment.

## 17. Acquisition path

### Phase 0 — evidence handoff

- close every predecessor physical gate;
- freeze the accepted service and production baselines;
- separate research articles from fielding lots; and
- establish `NFCA-1` public configuration authority.

### Phase 1 — common-force decomposition

- assign every module, vehicle, utility and facility to dedicated, portfolio-common or national-common ownership;
- define availability contracts and conflict rules;
- remove double-counted capital; and
- preserve complete-group readiness.

### Phase 2 — national demand and geography

- build a synthetic line-equivalent demand atlas;
- test travel times and receiver access;
- select candidate arsenal cells and launch regions;
- model correlated infrastructure and workforce loss; and
- revise `C24` if entitlements fail.

### Phase 3 — balanced-root fielding

- qualify eight integration lines and transport rates;
- build training and evaluation bodies first enough to prepare crews;
- field operational groups in root-balanced blocks;
- activate arsenals before their supported groups; and
- preserve strategic bodies from schedule substitution.

### Phase 4 — service declaration

- certify complete groups, not deliveries;
- declare category guarantees only after matching groups close;
- publish every geographic and temporal exception; and
- retain the research estate for block changes and failure inquiry.

### Phase 5 — block renewal

- replace ten serviceable system bodies per year;
- execute mover and frame recapitalization;
- modernize systems without splitting the fleet into incompatible blocks;
- requalify reserve personnel; and
- reopen evidence whenever a service boundary changes.

## 20. What this pass changes

### 20.1 It creates a service constitution

The national number now follows from a stated promise rather than an analogy to military units or an arbitrary procurement total.

### 20.2 It refuses fractional two-line readiness

Two lines require sixteen sets and two reserves. The national force pairs intact groups rather than creating a cheaper wing that silently shares the onset reserve.

### 20.3 It separates research from fielding

`RAS-3R11` remains an evidence estate. `NFCA-1` becomes a separate planning envelope, preventing experimental and destructive assets from appearing in national readiness.

### 20.4 It finds a basing threshold

Four groups require 18.667 average arsenal bays. Three sites with nine bays each fail the one-site-loss rule; three sites with ten bays each pass with only 6.67 percent residual margin.

### 20.5 It makes replacement production a sovereign requirement

Each system root needs four lines because three lines yield only 9.75 systems per year against a ten-system normal replacement requirement.

### 20.6 It reveals the portfolio ownership problem

A fully dedicated fire-control service reaches USD 3.6412 trillion of initial capital. This is a valid upper-bound architecture and an invalid template for forty-eight separately duplicated families. The next pass must redesign ownership, not apply an unsupported discount.
