# Joint Force Allocation and National Order of Battle

## Executive judgment

A Department of Resilience equal in seriousness to the Department of Defense cannot be a loose federation of specialized fleets. It requires a **single national force-management system** that decides which complete formations exist, which component owns them, which campaigns may plan against them, which commander receives them, how they reach a damaged theater, and when they have genuinely returned to readiness.

This is the institutional middle between strategy and machinery. Without it, the Department can buy aircraft, ships, pumps, hospitals, power systems and robots while still being unable to answer the decisive question: **which ten disasters can it fight at once without promising the same people, transport, receiving nodes or repair capacity several times?**

The reference design applies the established `3C + 5M + 2G + 20R` promise to ten simultaneous campaigns: three catastrophic domestic operations, five major domestic operations and two invited global operations, with a 20 percent residual immediately employable reserve. It composes thirty-four permanent formation families across joint command, land engineering, air response, maritime response, infrastructure restoration, health protection and community stabilization.

Under the workbook's explicit and still-unvalidated coefficients, that promise implies:

- about **714,000 people simultaneously deployed**;
- **1,750 formation-equivalent commitments** across the ten campaigns;
- about **3.95 million affiliated billet-equivalents** after rotation, components, non-utility institutional overhead and the integrated National Utility Projection System are included;
- a provisional **$12.05 trillion 25-year capital frame**, including a 20 percent program reserve;
- about **$858 billion per year** in mature operations, 89 percent of the FY2026 Department of Defense request used only as a scale comparator; and
- an average capital-plus-operations burden of about **$1.11 trillion per year** during the build, peaking near **$1.46 trillion** in the reference phasing.

These figures are not an estimate, recommendation or forecast. They are the consequence of making the institutional promise explicit with one set of assumptions. The model deliberately produces a contradiction with the earlier 302,000-active/396,500-reserve screen. That earlier result was a top-down order-of-magnitude envelope. It does not generate the formations, rotations and institutional depth required by the new bottom-up case.

More importantly, buying the modeled formations does not close the force. Four of five strategic-movement channels are short, and all ten damaged-theater reception screens are short. Daily sustainment screens close only under coarse coefficients that exclude several likely dominant burdens. The binding design problem is therefore not simply how many specialized units to own. It is how to create a **Joint Deployment and Distribution Enterprise for public service under infrastructure failure**.

The accompanying [joint force allocation workbook](../../../models/joint-force-allocation-model.xlsx) exposes every coefficient, component credit, formation demand, readiness vector, double-count boundary and evidence gate. No platform rendering is authorized by this study.

## 1. The missing layer

The project has developed water, power, fire, flood, utility-anchor, proving-network and technology-enterprise concepts in increasing detail. Each thread has reached the same limit: a capable formation is not a national force.

A national force must solve five problems simultaneously.

1. **Ownership:** every unit has one generating service, one home-station obligation and one readiness authority.
2. **Planning access:** several commands may plan against a scarce formation without pretending that each owns it.
3. **Execution allocation:** one authority decides which named formation goes to which operation, for how long and at what readiness cost.
4. **End-to-end employment:** lift, route, staging, reception, communications, sustainment, host support and technical release arrive with the operating unit.
5. **Regeneration:** personnel, equipment, stocks, data and certification return to standard before the formation re-enters the available pool.

The U.S. military calls the comparable discipline global force management: assignment, apportionment and allocation are distinct decisions, and the system is meant to expose worldwide availability and risk rather than allow every plan to assume the whole force.[^jcs-gfm] U.S. Transportation Command likewise treats transportation, deployment and distribution as an integrated enterprise and serves as the joint force provider for mobility forces and enabling packages.[^ustranscom-ucp] The Department of Resilience needs an equivalent logic designed around civilian public effects, damaged infrastructure and civil authority.

This does not mean copying combatant-command culture. It means recognizing the same conservation law: a formation, runway slot, berth, licensed operator, repair line or mobile substation cannot be in two places at once.

## 2. The national force promise

The Department should publish one operational promise before it publishes a fleet plan:

> Sustain three catastrophic, five major and two invited global campaigns concurrently; retain twenty percent immediately employable reserve in every nationally scarce effect; and regenerate the committed force without abandoning the communities it is supporting.

The promise is a stress case, not a forecast of ten statistically independent disasters. It exists to reveal common dependencies and policy choices. The campaigns combine infrastructure failures rather than sort neatly by hazard.

| Campaign class | Reference operations | Force-driving condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Three catastrophic | continental grid failure during temperature extreme; sequential Gulf–Atlantic hurricane; Cascadia earthquake and tsunami | Multiregional command, prolonged public-service deficits, damaged access, mass movement and national reallocation |
| Five major | western wildfire–heat–smoke complex; urban heat emergency; inland flood; regional tornado sequence; territorial drought and water emergency | Simultaneous distributed operations that cannot be treated as leftovers after the catastrophic campaigns |
| Two global | allied coastal megacity earthquake; regional flood and displacement mission | Invited access, sovereignty, customs, standards, host reception and long strategic lines of communication |

Each campaign is represented by a formation package. The package is an editable hypothesis about complete operating increments, not a prediction of one future incident. Its purpose is to force all domains into the same ledger.

## 3. The order of battle

The force is organized into seven formation systems. The names describe public effects and operational threads, not preferred vehicles.

| System | Permanent formation families | What the system must finish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Joint integration | civil integration and effects; theater opening; theater sustainment; replacement and reconstitution; transfer and demobilization | Convert civil priorities into executable missions, open and sustain the theater, manage scarce force, and return service and formations safely |
| Land engineering | access and route opening; flood and hydraulic control; debris and materials; temporary works and crossing; ground fire and landscape | Create movement and utility routes, control water and fire, process debris, build temporary infrastructure and maintain durable access |
| Air response | strategic mobility; austere air access; aerial hazard control; tactical distribution; sensing and relay; aeromedical movement | Move urgent force, reopen air access, sense and influence hazards, distribute time-critical cargo and move patients |
| Maritime response | port opening; littoral access; inland waterways; offshore support; ocean sustainment | Reopen maritime nodes, bridge shorelines and rivers, support offshore systems, and sustain heavy overseas movement |
| Infrastructure restoration | utility projection; communications utility; thermal safety; utility reconstruction | Establish power, water, communications and survivable thermal service, then rebuild fixed networks |
| Health protection | community health continuity; mobile acute care; public-health control; health-workforce replacement | Preserve routine care, create surge care, control population health risks and replace exhausted or displaced medical labor |
| Community stabilization | population support; essential goods; civil access; displacement transition; community institutions | Translate technical output into accessible shelter, food, sanitation, information, movement, housing and functioning local institutions |

The **formation** is the smallest unit that receives national readiness credit. It must have a commander, certified people, owned or contractually controlled equipment, communications, movement data, initial sustainment, safety and technical authorities, and a defined public-effect output. A warehouse kit, contractor list, aircraft tail or hospital module is an input to a formation, not a formation by itself.

FEMA's resource-management doctrine provides a useful lower-level precedent. Typed resources have minimum capability, personnel, equipment, training and availability criteria; Mission Ready Packages add mission parameters, conditions, logistics and cost.[^fema-rmp][^fema-mutual] The Department extends this idea from deployable resources to permanent national force structure.

## 4. Force management as a constitutional system

The Department should distinguish five states that ordinary coordination systems often blur.

| State | Meaning | Authoritative decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Assigned | A formation has an enduring command relationship and generating-service home | Secretary of Resilience |
| Apportioned | A campaign may use the formation for planning under specified assumptions | Chief of the Resilience Staff |
| Allocated | A named formation is transferred for a bounded execution period | Secretary or delegated national allocator |
| Committed | The formation is deployed, staged or reserved for a named operation | Supported commander, recorded in the national ledger |
| Available | The complete formation is certified inside its employment window | Generating service and independent readiness authority |

These distinctions prevent two kinds of fiction. The first is **planning duplication**, in which every regional plan assumes the same high-demand fleet. The second is **administrative availability**, in which equipment is counted even though its crews, movement, receiving node, fuel, technical authority or recovery capacity are missing.

The Department should establish a permanent **Joint Force Coordinator** under the Chief of the Resilience Staff. It validates requests, develops sourcing options, identifies shortfalls, records readiness cost and recommends allocation. It does not own the formations and cannot waive technical safety conditions.

Standing integrating headquarters are preferable to assembling a new national staff after every event, but they do not eliminate augmentation. Joint doctrine warns that headquarters built from large numbers of individual augmentees impose integration costs and that sourcing must be addressed before execution.[^jcs-jp333] The Resilience answer is a permanent command nucleus, rehearsed service components and whole-unit augmentation packages—not a database of impressive résumés.

## 5. Readiness is a vector

The model treats readiness as the minimum of five complete-formation dimensions:

```text
ready capacity = minimum(
  material,
  qualified personnel,
  movement,
  theater reception,
  sustainment and network support
)
```

These fractions are correlated capacities, not independent probabilities. A formation at 82 percent material, 80 percent personnel, 78 percent movement, 80 percent reception and 82 percent sustainment has 78 percent complete readiness—not an average of 80.4 percent and not a product of unrelated chances.

This rule changes inventory. For each formation family, selected inventory is the maximum needed to meet four tests:

1. simultaneous demand plus 20 percent reserve under complete readiness;
2. the share required inside the first 72 hours;
3. enduring demand after component mobilization; and
4. globally eligible demand.

The resulting inventory is not a procurement recommendation. It is a way to identify which assumption binds. In the reference case, complete readiness usually dominates. That result is partly structural: movement availability is the lowest dimension for most formations, while reception is lowest for the health system.

The model also keeps **committed**, **preparing**, **regenerating** and residual **available** pools separate. This addresses a common reset failure: deployment ends in the accounting system before people, equipment and stocks have actually recovered. GAO has repeatedly emphasized that reset and reconstitution require goals, plans and resources rather than an undefined return to normal.[^gao-reset]

## 6. Components are different forms of capacity

The establishment contains five components. They are not interchangeable percentages of one workforce.

| Component | Primary role | Reference establishment | Structural limit |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| Permanent federal | immediate response, scarce systems, command, technical authority and global force | about 1.97M billet-equivalents | Cannot alone provide national mass or local legitimacy |
| Federal reserve | rotation, enduring campaigns and specialist replacement | about 917,000 | Mobilization and employer support limit early credit |
| State and territorial guard | domestic mass, place knowledge and local-authority relationships | about 552,000 | Receives no automatic global credit |
| Certified industrial force | production, repair, construction, carriers and licensed surge labor | about 494,000 | Commercial capacity is not ready force without contracts, priority, crews and movement |
| Partner and allied | host access, co-production and invited global capacity | about 12,500 U.S.-supported equivalents | Cannot be counted as U.S.-owned reserve |

The figures are **affiliated billet-equivalents**, not a federal civilian headcount. They include different levels of annual paid readiness and availability. A permanent operator and an industrial surge worker are not costed or credited identically.

This component architecture is strategically important. A resilience force is partly a uniformed operating service, partly a reserve institution, partly a compact with states and territories, and partly an industrial mobilization system. Treating contractors as an invisible procurement input would miss the fact that licensed utility crews, shipyards, carriers, construction firms, hospitals, manufacturers and repair networks are themselves force elements when availability is contractually and physically assured.

The Coast Guard's force-management process is a relevant civil-military analogue: limited assets are allocated against operational priorities using resource-capacity estimates and explicit risk.[^uscg-gfm] The Department should go further by giving industrial, state and reserve capacity formal component records and mobilization evidence.

## 7. Mobility and reception are part of the weapon system

The reference package contains about 11.58 million tonnes of deployable mass. The workbook divides this mass provisionally across strategic air, tactical air, ocean sealift, inland water and land routes. This is only a screening partition; it is not an item-level load plan.

The result is decisive:

| Channel | Required mass | Reference capacity in window | State |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Strategic air / 72 hours | 1.39M t | 0.18M t | short |
| Tactical air / 7 days | 0.93M t | 0.31M t | short |
| Ocean sealift / 14 days | 4.05M t | 4.14M t | closes narrowly |
| Inland water / 14 days | 1.74M t | 1.57M t | short |
| Land route throughput / 14 days | 3.47M t | 2.85M t | short |

The numbers should not be read literally until loads, distances, cycles, weather, fuel, maintenance, port conditions and return flow are modeled. They should be read structurally: **the movement enterprise must be designed at the same time as the operating force**. No Department of Defense lift is credited in the reference case, because a coequal institution cannot make its national promise from capacity that may be committed elsewhere.

Reception is even more constraining. The ten campaigns require roughly 64,000–252,000 tonnes per day of opening throughput during their reference buildup windows. The provisional theater-opening commands deliver only 19,500–78,000 tonnes per day after availability. Every campaign is short.

This is not a reason to inflate a headquarters count until the cells turn green. It reveals that a theater-opening formation is a system of ports, airfields, inland terminals, heavy handling, surveys, traffic control, responder bases, repair, alternate nodes and agreements. It must be configuration-modeled like water or power. A disaster force cannot consume the last functioning airport, port, road, power circuit or hospital in order to arrive.

## 8. Sustainment and regeneration

The package coefficients imply about 148,000 tonnes per day of direct formation sustainment across the ten campaigns. Reference theater-sustainment commands close that signal under the workbook's simplified 7,500-tonne/day unit assumption.

This apparent success should be treated skeptically. The signal omits or incompletely represents:

- utility-source fuel and cooling burdens already carried in the National Utility Projection System;
- water, wastewater, contaminated debris and reverse-logistics flows;
- aviation and marine fuel by actual cycle;
- clinical oxygen, pharmaceuticals, blood, sterile supply and regulated waste;
- food, sanitation and accessible distribution by person-day;
- repair parts and replacement equipment under correlated failure; and
- the flow required to regenerate formations while other campaigns continue.

The mature force therefore needs a **Sustainment and Reconstitution Command** with authority over end-to-end distribution, depots, medical and environmental logistics, repair, replacement pools and reverse flow. Its output is not tonnes shipped. It is a formation returned to certified readiness while public service remains bounded.

## 9. Workforce consequence

The reference workforce is derived formation by formation:

```text
direct billets = selected inventory × on-duty people × crew sets
reference establishment =
  non-utility direct formations
  + National Utility Projection System integrated workforce
  + non-utility institutional overhead
```

The result is approximately 3.95 million affiliated billet-equivalents. About 3.35 million arise in non-utility formations, 198,200 in the integrated utility enterprise, and 401,000 in non-utility schools, headquarters, acquisition, safety and depots. The institutional factor is deliberately explicit and excludes the utility system's own already-counted overhead.

This result is more than five times the earlier 698,500 active-plus-reserve screen. The correct interpretation is not that 3.95 million has been proved. It is that the earlier workforce number must no longer be used as though it were generated by the force design.

The next workforce model must convert billet-equivalents into occupations, qualification time, annual accessions, instructor and examiner capacity, geographic labor markets, attrition, medical standards, state and industrial contracts, and home-base minimum staffing. GAO's review of Air Force force generation is directly relevant: fragmented unit constructs and unknown minimum home-station staffing make it difficult to know whether apparently available units are hollowing the base that generates them.[^gao-afforgen]

## 10. Capital and fiscal parity

The provisional capital work breakdown is constructed once to prevent additive storytelling.

| Work-breakdown line | 25-year capital |
| --- | ---: |
| Non-utility formation acquisition | $4.943T |
| National Utility Projection System, integrated | $1.698T |
| Non-utility bases, depots and ranges | $0.650T |
| Science, technology and proving outside NUPS | $1.500T |
| Education and workforce infrastructure | $0.350T |
| Information, forecasting and common digital systems | $0.400T |
| Strategic industry and materials outside NUPS | $0.500T |
| Pre-reserve capital | $10.041T |
| Explicit 20% program reserve | $2.008T |
| **Total reference capital** | **$12.049T** |

The values are top-down configuration assumptions. They do not come from independent designs, schedules or cost estimates. Their purpose is to force every major program family to name one authoritative owner and to expose overlap.

The mature annual operating screen is about $858B: roughly $436B in component-weighted personnel cost, $267B in formation and enabling-system maintenance, $35B in training and stock rotation, $45B in deployment and exercises, $25B in industrial readiness and $50B in continuous research and test.

The FY2026 Department of Defense budget request is $961.6B, including $194.7B for military personnel, $339.5B for operation and maintenance, $153.3B for procurement, $142.0B for research, development, test and evaluation, and $18.9B for military construction and family housing.[^dod-fy26] It is a then-year political and accounting object, not a directly comparable cost basis. It is used here for one purpose: to test whether the proposed institution is being imagined at genuinely coequal scale.

Mature Resilience operations alone reach 89 percent of that comparator. Mature operations plus average 25-year capital reach about $1.34T per year, or 139 percent. The build profile averages $1.11T and peaks at $1.46T in the reference phasing. Equal seriousness does not mechanically mean equal annual spending in every year. It means the mission promise, force structure, industrial system and evidence standard are not silently compressed to fit a symbolic parity line.

## 11. The industrial object

This order of battle would create several Lockheed-scale industrial sectors, but they would not be organized around a handful of prime platforms.

The durable economic object is a **readiness network**:

- purpose-built air, maritime, land, health and utility systems;
- strategic lift, ports, airfields, inland terminals and responder-base packages;
- large-bore pipe, cable, transformers, pumps, storage, crossings and temporary works;
- modular clinical, laboratory, shelter, sanitation, cooling and communications systems;
- depots, overhaul lines, shipyards, airworthiness and industrial second sources;
- sovereign forecasting, communications, configuration and logistics software;
- professional schools, licensing pipelines, ranges and independent test; and
- contracted commercial capacity with mobilization priority, audited readiness and public fallback production.

The export opportunity is correspondingly larger than selling rescue vehicles. Allies could buy compatible formations, prepared nodes, training, standards, depot support and co-production rights. The Department's global force would then operate through known interfaces rather than arrive as a self-contained American enclave.

## 12. Failure doctrine

A mature resilience force must fail visibly and safely.

1. A second allocation of the same formation is rejected by the authoritative ledger.
2. A personnel-only or equipment-only package does not receive formation certification.
3. Home-base minimum functions remain staffed when deployable units leave.
4. If strategic lift is unavailable, movement is delayed by public priority rather than hidden in optimistic arrival times.
5. If reception fails, the force activates an alternate node, smaller entry package or later effect promise.
6. If sustainment exceeds the corridor, public service is reduced before stocks are exhausted.
7. A state formation receives no global credit without specific authority and agreement.
8. A damaged or delinquent industrial supplier triggers sovereign fallback and alternate production.
9. Technical authorities may stop unsafe operation even when the campaign commander needs the output.
10. The first campaign that exhausts replacement pools causes later commitments to be reduced or refused.

The discipline is deliberately anti-heroic. It substitutes a bounded, truthful service promise for the appearance of unlimited national capacity.

## 13. What must be proved next

Twenty evidence gates remain open. The next research program should concentrate on four connected models.

### 13.1 Formation configurations

Select one representative unit from each system—heavy land engineering, strategic mobility, theater opening, mobile acute care and community stabilization—and produce item-level people, equipment, mass, volume, power, communications, maintenance, movement, supply and output records. The current unit costs and crew sizes remain placeholders until these configurations exist.

### 13.2 Joint deployment and distribution

Pass 24 establishes the institutional, time-phased and first-order capacity architecture for this enterprise. It replaces the all-mass-at-once screen with anticipatory, 72-hour, 14-day, 45-day and 90-day closure bands; reconciles eleven formation lines; and tests movement, reception, onward distribution, responder bases and sustainment as one weakest-link chain. Pass 25's [Cascadia Joint Resilience Campaign](../../campaigns/cascadia-joint-resilience-campaign.md) supplies the first item-level origin–destination–load–cycle test: nine service islands, twelve paths, sixty-four loads, ninety-day sustainment and reconstitution. It closes under declared coefficients but leaves asset-level damage, queues, weather, fuel, maintenance, cost and performance unvalidated.

### 13.3 Occupation and institutional force

Translate billet-equivalents into occupations, qualifications and generating institutions. Measure annual accessions, instructor ratios, attrition, licensing, medical fitness, employer support, geographic supply and the minimum workforce that cannot deploy.

### 13.4 Independent cost and campaign trial

Create a single Department-wide work breakdown connecting the original force, technology frontier, proving network, NUPS and this order of battle. Then run a full campaign-and-regeneration exercise under independent data custody. No formation, budget or rendering receives validation from workbook arithmetic alone.

## 14. Strategic implications

The mature concept is not “FEMA with more equipment” and not “the military, but benevolent.” It is a different instrument of national power with its own theory of victory.

Victory is not the arrival of federal assets. It is the controlled reduction of population-service deficits, the preservation of legitimate local authority, the restoration of essential systems and the return of the national force to readiness without creating a second collapse.

That theory produces three design conclusions.

First, the Department's center of gravity is **joint force generation and distribution**, not a flagship fleet. A visible platform is strategically irrelevant if its complete formation cannot be allocated, moved, received, sustained and regenerated.

Second, the active–reserve distinction is insufficient. State, territorial, industrial and allied components must be treated as governed readiness systems with different time, legal and geographic credit.

Third, parity with defense is not achieved by assigning the same dollar figure. It is achieved when rescue and stabilization receive the same quality of force planning: declared concurrent obligations, permanent organizations, owned infrastructure, technology competition, industrial depth, independent test, visible risk and the authority to say that a mission cannot be supported.

That is the point at which disaster response becomes force projection.

## Method and evidence boundary

The workbook is a deterministic reference case in real 2026 dollars. Campaign-package quantities, formation sizes, crew sets, readiness fractions, component shares, employment credits, movement partitions, throughput, capital, operations and phasing are design-study assumptions unless explicitly sourced. Arithmetic closure establishes consistency only.

The model integrates the $1.698T and 198,198-billet National Utility Projection System once. It excludes utility-projection-group direct capital and billets from non-utility formation totals, then adds the integrated NUPS work-breakdown line. It excludes Department of Defense lift, ordinary disaster reconstruction, host-government capital, uncompensated private capacity and uncontracted allied capacity.

The force is `R1/R2` institutionally with selected `R2+` arithmetic. Mobility, reception, workforce, cost, configuration and campaign-trial gates prevent an `R3` claim. Renderings remain blocked.
