# F36 `CISA-40` `SA6` Root A full-scale fire/hydraulic-rig physical specification

## Executive judgment

The [Root A drainage, firewater and recovery technology constitution](f36-cisa40-sa6-root-a-drainage-firewater-and-recovery-technology-constitution.md) created three unlike complete-chain candidates, six builder roots, forty-eight articles, ten methods, eight challenges and a 306-event campaign. It also left a hard institutional problem unresolved. A test facility that supplies a common drain, sump, cooling loop, robot controller or recovery train would appear neutral while quietly completing whichever candidate fits it best. A facility that refuses to intervene when a candidate fails would preserve conceptual purity by sacrificing people, land and evidence.

This pass creates `RFP-1`, the Root A full-scale fire/hydraulic public-rig physical specification. Its governing decision is:

> Build six complete public instruments that standardize source, terrain datum, challenge, clock, safety and evidence while leaving source capture, surface behavior, drainage, water logic, heat rejection, robotics and recovery physically unlike. Give the public plant enough independent casualty capacity to survive candidate failure, but make every public intervention visible as candidate-performance evidence.

Two rigs sit at each of the three proving campuses. `PTC-A` receives a destructive fire/hydraulic position and a destructive recovery position. `PTC-B` and `PTC-C` each receive two operational siblings capable of the full method ladder. Every position is complete. A destructive rig is not a reduced burn cell, and an operational sibling is not a witness stand.

Each rig has a 30 m × 20 m, 600 m² experiment field. One hundred fifty independently serialized 2 m × 2 m terrain cassettes carry 600 replaceable 1 m² panels. Each cassette has 150 mm total vertical travel and a 1.5-percent local-grade envelope. The federation therefore owns 900 cassettes and 3,600 panels. This is not a permanently graded concrete apron. It is a measurable, damageable and replaceable terrain instrument able to introduce adverse grade, local settlement, drain blockage, thermal damage and debris without rebuilding the facility after every event.

Twenty-four candidate cartridge bays per rig connect the field to four longitudinal galleries. The public structure defines load, datum, access and casualty isolation; the candidate supplies its own surface inserts, drain cartridges, source receiver, water-return logic and fractionation equipment. There is no common source plenum. Across six rigs, 144 bays can accept passive slots, underdeck cells, robotic hoses or future architectures that do not resemble any current candidate.

The public source dispenser prepares, weighs and releases the inherited 2,000 kg `C1` inventory. At 800 kg/m³, the source occupies 2.500 m³. A 120-second release averages 16.667 kg/s or 1.250 m³/min. Replaceable 40–200 mm adapters permit different release geometries while preserving a common origin volume, mass, temperature, start time and aperture identity. The physical structure reserves an 8,000 kg `C2` interface, but no `C2` authority is created.

The field accepts configurable pool boundaries at 5, 10, 25, 50 and 100 m². The 25 m² inherited screen produces approximately 58.559 MW of heat release and 20.495 MW of radiant power. `RFP-1` allocates a 72 MW zoned thermal boundary per rig—about 23 percent above that 25 m² screen—not to predict the fire but to impose spatially known heat, asymmetric shielding and instrument-loss challenges. Twenty-four independently measured thermal zones, sixteen shielding datums, four protected instrument galleries, a 1.5 kPa wind field and two unlike public quench paths make the boundary a causal instrument rather than an ignition demonstration.

Each rig provides 3 m³/min of metered water at 1.2 MPa and a 20 MW heat-rejection envelope. Normal supply, candidate return, public casualty capture and quarantine transfer are separate physical paths. The candidate retains its inherited 40 m³ firewater vault. The public facility has a separately isolated 120 m³ casualty-water vault and a 50 m³ casualty-source tank. The casualty systems are one-way safety receivers. They are dry and isolated in a nominal event, cannot be counted toward candidate capacity, and record their use as an adverse candidate outcome.

This distinction resolves the central contradiction. `AF-1` can require 48 m³ after twenty minutes at 2.4 m³/min and therefore exceed the inherited 40 m³ vault by 8 m³. The public plant must catch that failure without turning the extra 120 m³ into an invisible design allowance. A rig that protects the site while preserving the fact of overflow is scientifically superior to either an unsafe facility or an overhelpful one.

Three 4 m-wide robot corridors per rig separate hot approach and extraction, candidate service and drain access, and clean return with decontamination. Independent witness beacons establish public position truth. Candidate autonomy, localization, tools and mission logic remain candidate-owned. The rescue package supports remote reconnaissance, 150 kg casualty extraction through two unlike mechanical paths, decontamination, stabilization of two simultaneous patients, 120 minutes of independent breathing air, three unlike communication bearers and evidence retention during rescue.

The inherited twenty scientific measurement functions are conserved at 480 channels per rig. Ninety-six facility-witness channels are added for utilities, environmental state, casualty systems and independent field video. At the declared sampling and byte allocations, each rig records approximately 53.404 MB/s. A 600-second full-rate interval, six-hour one-percent tail, two native originals and 1.35 custody factor produce approximately 117.659 GB per event. The complete 306-event campaign with thirty-percent reserve requires approximately 46.805 TB. It does not prove that an event will close.

Safety is triply independent at every rig: passive mechanical or thermal action, hardwired analog direct-current action, and diverse programmable action with local stored energy. Independence includes sensing, logic, transmission, energy and final element. Eighteen paths across the federation may not collapse into three commands acting on one valve, one controller or one utility feed.

## 2. Physical neutrality is an engineered property

### 2.1 The false-neutral facility

A conventional proving program might pour one sloped apron, install one trench drain, connect one separator and invite candidates to attach their equipment. Every candidate would receive the same facility, but the comparison would not be neutral. Apron grade would favor gravity capture. Trench spacing would favor one surface topology. The separator would assume a source–water mixture and residence time. Shared pumping would favor architectures able to use that suction state. A site navigation network would favor robots designed around that map.

Equal access to a biased plant is not neutrality. Neutrality exists only when common elements establish a comparable cause and candidate-specific elements retain the causal mechanism being tested.

### 2.2 Public invariants

The public authority owns:

- source mass, lot, initial condition and origin volume;
- release start, duration, aperture identity and event time;
- six-axis terrain datum and recorded cassette state;
- challenge identity, blind insert and weather history;
- load, water, thermal and utility service envelopes;
- independent safeing and casualty capacity;
- public measurement functions and native evidence;
- environmental custody outside the candidate boundary; and
- event configuration, intervention and recovery records.

### 2.3 Candidate-owned mechanisms

Each candidate owns:

- contact surface and local grading mechanism;
- source entry and collection geometry;
- drain cartridges and internal ancestry;
- source sump, receiver or cartridge inventory;
- water application, recirculation and return logic;
- barriers, shielding and agent use;
- robot autonomy, tools and recovery sequence;
- fractionation and candidate-side environmental treatment;
- candidate controller and declared software; and
- restoration of its own serialized state.

### 2.4 Prohibited hidden assistance

The rig may not supply a common source plenum, shared candidate sump, shared candidate vault, common heat exchanger, common robot controller, common navigation solution or unrecorded overflow path. A public casualty action is allowed for life, site and evidence protection; it cannot erase or improve the candidate score. The event packet must identify when it began, why it began, which inventories it changed and what would have occurred without it.

## 4. Reconfigurable terrain field

### 4.1 Geometry

The active field is 30 m long and 20 m wide. Its 600 m² area is six times the largest 100 m² pool screen and twenty-four times the 25 m² reference pool. The extra area is not assumed clean standoff. It carries candidate equipment, known debris, thermal boundaries, rescue routes, measurement planes and controlled failure regions.

One hundred fifty 2 m × 2 m cassettes form a fifteen-row by ten-column field. Each cassette carries four 1 m² replaceable panels. A 150 mm total actuator interval gives ±75 mm around a nominal datum. A 1.5-percent grade across a 2 m cassette requires 30 mm of differential elevation, leaving travel for settlement and discontinuity challenges.

### 4.2 Why the field is modular

A permanent apron would convert thermal cracking, chemical sorption and local deformation into unmeasured site history. Replaceable panels allow destructive sampling, cleaning verification, known-roughness substitutions and blind defect insertion. Replaceable cassettes allow a damaged load path or actuator to leave the evidence population without forcing demolition of the complete rig.

Every cassette carries a serial, material lot, surface finish, pre-event geometry, thermal history, contamination history, load proof and post-event disposition. A replacement is a configuration change, not routine housekeeping.

### 4.3 Load and damage questions

The field must accept candidate modules, mobile tanks, robotic loads, concentrated supports, thermal barriers, debris impact and water inventory while preserving local geometry evidence. `RFP-1` deliberately does not select the cassette support technology. At least two unlike mechanisms should enter prototype work: a mechanically locked adjustable reaction frame and a replaceable sacrificial crib with independently measured settlement. The program needs failure evidence before it decides whether active leveling is worth its energy and complexity.

## 5. Public source dispenser

### 5.1 `C1` source state

The inherited source mass is 2,000 kg. At 800 kg/m³:

\[
V_s=\frac{2{,}000}{800}=2.500\ \mathrm{m^3}.
\]

For the 120-second release screen:

\[
\dot m_s=\frac{2{,}000}{120}=16.667\ \mathrm{kg/s},
\]

\[
\dot V_s=\frac{2.500}{2}=1.250\ \mathrm{m^3/min}.
\]

The dispenser conditions and weighs at least 3.5 m³ of source-side working volume so the 2.5 m³ liquid inventory can be prepared without filling every internal void. Native weighing targets 0.8 kg standard uncertainty. Residuals in the preparation vessel, manifold and release adapter remain in the final mass ledger.

### 5.2 Public boundary and replaceable adapters

The public source boundary ends at a 1.2 m-diameter structural manifold. Replaceable adapters create equivalent release apertures from 40 to 200 mm at sixteen controlled locations. The candidate may supply the final adapter shape, but the public system measures its geometry and retains custody before and after the event.

This arrangement standardizes what must be known without requiring a single spill shape. A gravity cascade may need a low source origin. A cellular deck may need a distributed inlet. A robotic field may need a damaged or offset origin. The rig can vary those conditions as registered challenges while preserving mass and time identity.

### 5.3 Isolation and casualty diversion

The public isolation train must stop new source release inside the inherited thirty-second terminal-source target using final elements independent of candidate control. A separate casualty path leads to a 50 m³ isolated tank. That tank is much larger than the 2.5 m³ `C1` source because it must accept purge media, mixtures, damaged-route inventory and future bounded escalation without immediate transfer.

The 8,000 kg `C2` structural and custody interface is reserved, not activated. A future pass must determine whether Root A requires a different source field, fire boundary and site exclusion before `C2` can exist physically.

## 8. Water, heat rejection and the casualty firewall

### 8.1 Normal water service

Each rig provides 3 m³/min at 1.2 MPa. The hydraulic-power screen is:

\[
P_h=\Delta p\,Q=(1.2\times10^6\ \mathrm{Pa})(0.05\ \mathrm{m^3/s})=60\ \mathrm{kW}.
\]

At 70-percent pump efficiency, input would be approximately 86 kW before distribution losses. The value sizes utility and stored-energy questions; it is not a pump selection.

The supply exceeds the largest current candidate demand of 2.4 m³/min by 25 percent. That margin belongs to the public service envelope, not to candidate performance. The candidate is scored on its declared gross use, recovered fraction, net retained volume, heat transfer, source entrainment and terminal custody.

### 8.2 Heat-rejection envelope

The public plant accepts up to 20 MW from a candidate return through a candidate-owned exchanger. This is larger than the `RFA-1` simplified transfer screens of approximately 7.394–14.788 MW. It allows transient margin and future candidate development without imposing exchanger geometry or return temperature.

A candidate cannot claim closed-loop operation merely because the public plant accepted heat. It must prove that its return is water, that the source path remains separate, that net retained volume is measured and that its exchanger state does not hide source inventory.

### 8.3 Capacity contradiction and public response

The inherited candidate vault remains 40 m³. For `AF-1` at 2.4 m³/min with no recirculation:

\[
t_{40}=\frac{40}{2.4}=16.667\ \mathrm{min}.
\]

If the challenge begins half full, usable capacity is 20 m³ and time to geometric capacity becomes 8.333 minutes. At twenty minutes, total applied water is 48 m³. These are precisely the cases in which the public plant must distinguish candidate failure from facility failure.

The public casualty vault holds 120 m³ and accepts up to 4 m³/min. At that full casualty rate it has thirty minutes of geometric capacity from empty. Its isolation boundary, level, mass, chemistry and actuation time are public evidence. It is not connected until a separately powered safety path acts.

### 8.4 Four physically separate paths

Every rig carries:

1. metered normal supply;
2. candidate-owned return to a public heat-rejection interface;
3. isolated public casualty capture; and
4. independent quarantine transfer after the event.

Color, labeling or software tags are insufficient separation. The paths require distinct physical routing, valve lineages, inventory measurement and post-event verification.

The configurable pool field supports 5, 10, 25, 50 and 100 m² boundaries. The inherited 25 m² screen uses 0.055 kg/m²/s burning flux and 42.588 MJ/kg lower heating value:

\[
\dot Q=25(0.055)(42.588)\approx58.559\ \mathrm{MW}.

With radiative fraction 0.35, the screen produces approximately 20.495 MW radiant power. The 72 MW public thermal allocation creates approximately 13.441 MW of margin above the screen. Twenty-four zones permit a localized or asymmetric history rather than one undifferentiated total.

The rig also supplies four unlike remote-ignition modes so ignition dependence can be separated from candidate response. A 1.5 kPa wind boundary realizes `A07`. Two public quench paths are part of safeing, not candidate suppression. If they operate, the event can remain valid evidence of candidate failure and public safety success. Quench water enters its own ledger.

## 11. Robotics, rescue and clinical capability

### 11.1 Three corridors

Each rig has three 4 m-wide, 60 m-long corridors:

- hot approach and casualty extraction;
- candidate service and drain access; and
- clean return and decontamination.

The corridors include independent public witness beacons but no common candidate navigation system. A candidate may use map-based autonomy, local perception, tethered control or another approach. Its localization failure remains its own evidence.

### 11.2 Rescue package

The rig-native rescue object includes remote thermal, gas, acoustic and visual reconnaissance; 0.25 m casualty-location target; extraction of one 150 kg casualty through two unlike mechanical paths; hot and warm decontamination lanes; stabilization of two simultaneous patients; 120 minutes of independent breathing air; three unlike communication bearers; and two evidence originals during rescue.

Life-safety authority can intervene immediately. Evidence authority cannot delay rescue. After the immediate rescue state, every valve, robot, sample, moved fragment and decontamination action enters the event history.

### 11.3 Why casualty capability belongs inside research design

Ambitious disaster-response technology will fail during development. Treating rescue as an external campus service would make the most important access geometry, atmosphere, communication and decontamination assumptions invisible. The public research force must learn how to rescue through damaged terrain and active fluid hazards with the same seriousness it applies to the candidate mechanism.

## 13. Independent safeing

1. passive mechanical or thermal action using stored mechanical state;

## 17. Technology pull and industrial strategy

`RFP-1` is intentionally beyond the current catalogue of industrial test equipment. It creates acquisition pull for capabilities that a Department of Resilience would need across flood, fire, chemical, energy and infrastructure missions.

### 17.1 Self-mapping high-temperature terrain

The terrain field demands cassettes that report geometry, thermal damage, chemical exposure and residual inventory after fire and heavy loading. A mature version could become rapidly deployable ground for rail derailments, port fires, damaged substations, temporary hospitals and contaminated flood logistics.

### 17.2 Source-selective refractory drainage

The candidate bays create a market for drains and membranes that distinguish fuel, water, firefighting agent and solids under heat, blockage and deformation. The objective is not a better trench. It is liquid ancestry that survives a damaged surface.

### 17.3 Megawatt closed-loop cooling

A 20 MW exchange envelope with proof of water ancestry supports compact mobile cooling that can later serve data centers, industrial fires, thermal storage, district cooling and reactor casualty response. The innovation target combines heat transfer with verifiable low net water use.

### 17.4 Robotic liquid custody

Robots must clear drains, map pool depth, sample unknown mixtures, place barriers, skim source, recover debris and extract casualties without inheriting a perfect site map. This is a new industrial class between hazardous-material robotics, earthwork autonomy and process control.

### 17.5 Causal fire instrumentation

The rig requires public reconstruction of source, spread, heat, intervention and custody at subsecond resolution. The resulting measurement systems could transform aviation, energy-storage, urban-fire and industrial-accident learning by replacing narrative timing with native causal records.

### 17.6 Stored-energy public safeing

Three truly independent paths create demand for valves, gates, diverters, quench devices and evidence recorders able to act after network and utility loss. The system must prove not merely redundant commands but independent final actions.

### 17.7 Rapid environmental custody

Candidate fractionation and public quarantine paths can drive mobile separation, assay and disposition systems for disaster water and contaminated soil. The goal is a field chain whose material identity remains auditable from collection through reuse, treatment or final disposal.

### 17.8 Transferable public test grammar

The deeper innovation is institutional. A common grammar for source, datum, challenge, safeing, evidence and candidate-owned mechanism can support desalination, power, flood, wildfire and medical-response acquisitions. The government owns the experiment without owning one answer in advance.

## 20. Release, falsification and redesign

### 20.1 Three ledgers

The workbook separates 335 planning gates, 2,244 physical gates and nine downstream decisions. Physical gates include six rigs; geometry and load acceptance for 900 terrain cassettes; eight source-dispenser objects; 144 drain bays; twenty-four water paths; eighteen robot corridors; twenty-four sensor functions; eighteen safeing paths; ten method admissions; 120 drawings; and seventy-two interfaces.

The nine blocked downstream decisions are water-only campaign, surrogate campaign, quarter-source campaign, full-source fire campaign, destructive campaign, candidate selection, Root A freeze, production release and exterior rendering.

### 20.2 Candidate-neutrality falsification

Before construction release, independent review should ask of every public component:

> Could changing this component alter the relative ranking of `AF-1`, `AF-2` and `AF-3` for reasons outside their declared hypotheses?

If yes, the component must become candidate-specific, enter a bounded public variation program or be demonstrated insensitive. Cost or procurement convenience is not evidence of neutrality.

### 20.3 Rig redesign conditions

The public rig must be redesigned if:

- terrain state cannot be reconstructed after heat and liquid exposure;
- candidate surfaces cannot be exchanged without changing public datum;
- drain ancestry collapses before measurement;
- casualty systems aid nominal performance;
- a common water or heat exchanger determines candidate behavior;
- public quench cannot protect the site without destroying causal evidence;
- robot corridors favor one localization method;
- independent safeing converges on a shared final failure;
- mass-closure uncertainty cannot remain below the inherited ceiling;
- the native record cannot survive source, power and network denial;
- rescue requires unplanned human entry to an uncertain state; or
- one campus cannot reproduce the complete method ladder.

### 20.4 Source stop conditions

Source progression stops on unexplained mass discrepancy, challenge-state ambiguity, source–water crossover without ancestry, failed casualty capacity, loss of two public safeing paths, unmeasured public intervention, invalid clock lineage, unbounded migration, rescue infeasibility or unresolved cross-rig disagreement. Schedule and sunk cost cannot waive the stop.

## 21. Stocktake and next research pass

### 21.1 What this pass resolves

`RFP-1` converts six abstract rig roles into a physical public institution. It establishes how a 2,000 kg source enters a 600 m² configurable field; how unlike terrain, drain, cooling and robotic hypotheses remain unlike; how the public plant catches failure without improving the candidate result; how thermal, wind, water and service-denial challenges are imposed; how people are rescued; how material remains in custody; and how the event remains interpretable.

The deepest result is architectural: public safety and candidate neutrality are not opposites. They coexist when casualty systems are physically isolated, separately metered, independently controlled and scored as interventions. That principle can generalize across the Department of Resilience.

The pass also advances the larger project beyond a catalogue of assets. It defines a type of industrial institution capable of driving new terrain, drainage, cooling, robotics, environmental and evidence technologies through repeatable full-scale competition.

### 21.2 What remains unresolved

The rig still lacks:

- an exact Root A event identity and event-card schema;
- a clocked command sequence for source, ignition, water and casualty action;
- calibration and blank-event requirements for every inventory;
- exact acceptance rules for `CQ-00` through `CQ-07`;
- quarter-source and full-source escalation boards;
- command precedence between candidate, public safeing, rescue and evidence;
- valid-event classification after public intervention;
- exact recovery, sampling, cleaning and regression rules;
- cross-rig equivalence limits; and
- a Root A-native causal estimator tied to these physical intervention states.

### 21.3 Next pass

Pass 125 should create `RFE-1`, the Root A fire/hydraulic-rig execution and source-escalation constitution. It should turn the 306-event population into exact event identities and define the common event clock from configuration freeze through source admission, release, ignition, water command, candidate action, public intervention, casualty recovery, evidence seal and restoration. It should distinguish candidate failure with valid public evidence from facility-caused invalidity; conserve every event and matched pair; specify blank, water, tracer, surrogate, quarter-source and full-source progression; and predeclare regression after physical change.

Exterior rendering should remain blocked. The project now knows the research plant's internal physical anatomy, but it has not yet established the executed source, intervention and recovery sequence from which a credible operational asset could be derived.
