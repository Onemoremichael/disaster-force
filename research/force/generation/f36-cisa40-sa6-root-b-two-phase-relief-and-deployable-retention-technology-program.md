# F36 `CISA-40` `SA6` Root B two-phase relief and deployable-retention technology program

## Executive judgment

The preceding [internal cell and source-boundary design constitution](f36-cisa40-sa6-internal-cell-and-source-boundary-design-constitution.md) corrected a fundamental architectural error: a source is not independent if its emergency destination is shared. It assigned every two-tonne `SA6` source pod its own relief, receiving, retention, disposition, safety-energy and evidence train. For Root B, however, it retained a single 500 mm gas route as a transparent sizing screen. That route showed the order of magnitude of the system; it did not establish that a flashing dimethyl ether (`DME`) release could be controlled.

This pass creates `RHT-1`, a first-principles technology program for that unresolved Root B problem. Its governing finding is immediate and consequential:

> The inherited single 500 mm route misses the 120-second terminal-source target in the cold, half-area case. Under the declared choked-flow screen, transfer takes approximately 161.4 seconds. Root B therefore cannot advance as a fixed pipe-and-holder design. It must become a controlled competition among materially different physical architectures.

`RHT-1` admits three candidates without selecting a winner:

- `RC-1`, direct segmented low-pressure capture, uses one 600 mm path, a 2,500 kg receiver allocation and a 2,200 m³ deployable holder. It minimizes active process equipment but asks the hardest questions about rapid containment deployment, membrane compatibility and adverse weather.
- `RC-2`, staged pressure recovery and condensation, uses two 450 mm paths, a 6,500 kg receiver, a 600 m³ holder, 1,200 MJ of stored thermal capacity and 2.5 MW of condensation. It reduces deployed volume but introduces backpressure, refrigeration, active-control and regeneration dependencies.
- `RC-3`, hybrid recovery and measured last-resort destruction, uses three 350 mm paths, a 5,344 kg receiver, a 1,200 m³ holder, 650 MJ of stored thermal capacity, 1.5 MW of condensation and a limited 0.25 kg/s oxidation path. It offers a mixed response to uncertain phase behavior but must prove ignition control, emissions accounting and a one-percent maximum source loss.

All three clear the declared planning screens. In the cold challenged case, modeled transfer times are approximately 112.1 seconds for `RC-1`, 99.7 seconds for `RC-2` and 82.3 seconds for `RC-3`. Their long-horizon holder margins are approximately 769, 43.5 and 166.5 m³ respectively. These numbers do not rank the candidates. They establish only that each is coherent enough to build and attempt to falsify.

The distinction matters because the source physics are not yet known at design authority. `RHT-1` uses four declared source states from 258 K and 0.35 MPa absolute to a fire-heated 353 K and 2.0 MPa absolute condition with 8 MW of external heat. A first-order sensible-plus-external-energy calculation produces screened vapor fractions from about 5.4 percent to 100 percent. A homogeneous ideal-gas choking expression, reduced by a declared 0.15 two-phase mass-flux factor, makes route scale visible. Neither equation resolves flashing fronts, void fraction, slip, aerosol entrainment, metastability, ice formation, acoustic coupling, vessel blowdown, line inventory, receiver pressure history or structural response. Those are the research program.

The program is designed to resist premature convergence. Each architecture receives two unlike builders, two sibling operational sites and a destructive witness path. Forty-eight controlled articles comprise twelve scaled routes, twelve full-scale integrated trains, twelve destructive witnesses, six regeneration spares and six calibration references. Twenty-four protocols cross three architectures with eight challenges. Each protocol receives two builders, two sites and a challenge/control pair, producing 192 paired full-scale events. Seventy-two development events, twenty-four destructive events and eighteen reset or site-loss events raise the registered campaign to 306 events. The campaign consumes 288 tonnes of controlled DME source: 240 tonnes in paired events and 48 tonnes in destructive work.

The eight challenge conditions are not a generic environmental matrix. They target causal weaknesses: cold start and icing; one-half equivalent route area; wrong-side receiver pressure; fifty-percent initial receiver fill; hot full-source release; fire heat with active safety-energy denial; partial holder deployment in adverse wind; and site-network plus primary-time loss. The first four use a 500 kg quarter source. The final four use the full two-tonne source. Every challenged event has a clean sibling control under the same architecture, builder and site lineage.

Evidence is a physical subsystem. Sixteen measurement planes follow source ancestry from vessel interior to terminal disposition and evidence custody. Eighteen measurement functions create 504 channels and an aggregate native rate of approximately 288.736 MB/s. A five-minute full-rate interval, six-hour one-percent tail, two native originals and 1.35 protocol overhead produce about 402.267 GB per event. The whole campaign with a thirty-percent reserve requires approximately 160.022 TB, well within the inherited 576 TB public vault. Capacity does not settle identity, calibration, time, packet loss or independence; those remain separate acceptance objects.

Selection is conjunctive rather than compensatory. Each candidate must meet twelve rules: cold challenged transfer, holder margin, receiver allocation, no cross-source path, passive first stage, accounted fraction, retained fraction, backpressure, reset time, unlike-builder replication, site replication and eight accepted protocol dispositions. No weighted score may offset a failed life-safety, propagation, mass-accounting, evidence or replication requirement. The independent architecture board may select one or more candidates, reject all three, or continue the competition. It cannot convert an unexplained failure into a tradeable preference.

`RHT-1` requires 426 mission positions over forty-two months. Its gross capital allocation is USD 875M; gross annual support is USD 148M/year; capital plus forty-two months of support creates USD 1.393B of program exposure. CISA-specific ownership is USD 530M/90M per year and the shared technology program owns USD 345M/58M per year. After replacing USD 172M/18M per year of the existing Root B allocation, the net new CISA increment is USD 358M/72M per year. The corrected `BRD-5R` boundary becomes USD 1.298B capital and USD 246M/year. It exceeds the USD 960M CISA capital boundary by USD 338M while retaining USD 14M/year of annual room. The gross physical-use view reaches USD 1.771B/345M per year and exceeds both parent boundaries. These are findings, not spreadsheet defects.

The capital consequence changes the institutional thesis. A serious Department of Resilience cannot promise unprecedented rescue technology while requiring every research question to fit a platform budget written before the physics were known. It needs a mission acquisition account and a shared technology account, each with explicit ownership, public technical data and independent test infrastructure. `RHT-1` is expensive because it creates a reusable national competence in hazardous multiphase capture, rapid containment, high-rate condensation, controlled disposition, remote recovery, native evidence and regeneration. Those capabilities apply to mobile hydrogen and ammonia systems, high-rate desalination, post-fire chemical recovery, reactor auxiliaries, flood-borne industrial releases and other future force families.

The model closes 483 planning gates, leaves 398 physical gates open and blocks nine downstream decisions, including architecture selection, Root B freeze, `C2` source admission, production and exterior rendering. Its 910 terminal audits and fifty-three sensitivity tests pass with no formula error. Accepted articles, protocols, events and physical sources remain zero.

Exterior rendering remains blocked. The next decisive work is not a vehicle silhouette. It is `RHP-1`: an exact full-scale route, nozzle, receiver and transient-rig physical specification that can produce traceable two-phase data for all three candidates without embedding one candidate's assumptions into the test apparatus.

## 1. Decision, scope and epistemic boundary

### 1.1 Decision

`RHT-1` answers:

> What technology competition, article bank, source-state envelope, measurement system, challenge campaign, selection logic, public authority, workforce, schedule and fiscal commitment are required to discover a physically credible Root B relief and retention architecture for one two-tonne `SA6` source train?

The answer is not “install a larger pipe.” Diameter is one variable in a coupled source–nozzle–route–receiver–separator–holder–disposition system. A path that transfers mass quickly can impose unacceptable receiver pressure. A receiver that limits pressure can create intolerable holder volume. Condensation can reduce volume while creating heat-rejection, power and icing dependencies. Destruction can limit inventory while creating ignition, products and public-trust obligations. The design object is therefore the whole source train through terminal custody.

This pass constitutes the search space and the evidence needed to reduce it. It does not select materials, final wall thickness, certified relief area, production equipment, field doctrine or exterior form. It does not admit `C1`, `C2` or any live-source operating state. It does not claim that the declared DME properties are certified. It makes those absences visible and assigns each to a physical authority.

### 1.2 Inherited requirements

`RHT-1` inherits:

- a 2,000 kg Root B source per compartment;
- a 120-second terminal-source target;
- four independent source trains per operational lane;
- two sibling operational sites and one destructive site;
- a maximum 5 kg mass discrepancy per two-tonne event;
- two independent native evidence originals;
- the source-protection hierarchy established by the [maximum credible release constitution](f36-cisa40-sa6-maximum-credible-release-and-source-preserving-containment-constitution.md);
- the no-common-path discipline and sixteen measurement planes established by `SBD-1`;
- the public-site and evidence architecture established by the [public test complex](f36-cisa40-public-test-complex-and-phase-zero-commissioning-constitution.md); and
- the prohibition on exterior rendering.

It corrects one inherited planning object: the single 500 mm Root B route is no longer a presumptive design. It is a falsified cold half-area reference case.

## 2. Governing correction: why 500 mm is not enough

### 2.1 Reference flow screen

For an ideal gas with stagnation pressure \(P_0\), stagnation temperature \(T_0\), specific heat ratio \(\gamma\), specific gas constant \(R\) and discharge coefficient \(C_d\), the ideal choked mass flux screen is:

\[
G_i = C_d P_0 \sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{R T_0}}
\left(\frac{2}{\gamma+1}\right)^{\frac{\gamma+1}{2(\gamma-1)}}.
\]

`RHT-1` applies a declared two-phase factor \(\phi=0.15\):

\[
G_e = \phi G_i.
\]

The factor is not a material property. It is an explicit conservatism used to prevent an ideal-gas expression from being treated as a flashing-flow result. The effective route capacity is:

\[
\dot m = n\frac{\pi d^2}{4}aG_e,
\]

where \(n\) is path count, \(d\) is internal diameter and \(a\) is the challenged available-area fraction. Transfer time is \(t=m_s/\dot m\).

At `RB-S1`—258 K and 0.35 MPa absolute—the ideal flux screen is approximately 841.2 kg/m²-s and the reduced effective flux is approximately 126.2 kg/m²-s. One 500 mm route at fifty-percent available area passes approximately 12.39 kg/s. Moving 2,000 kg therefore requires approximately 161.4 seconds, forty-one seconds beyond the inherited target.

The result does not prove that a 500 mm system fails in nature. It proves that the previous planning basis cannot demonstrate compliance under its own conservative screen. That is sufficient to reopen the architecture.

### 2.2 Why adding diameter alone is an incomplete answer

Increasing route area changes at least six coupled quantities:

1. vessel blowdown rate and flashing history;
2. transient loads on the nozzle and supports;
3. line inventory and two-phase distribution;
4. receiver fill rate, pressure and thermal state;
5. holder deployment rate and gas temperature; and
6. consequence if isolation or terminal retention fails.

A larger line may improve terminal time while worsening receiver impulse, liquid carryover or downstream overpressure. Multiple smaller lines may offer fault tolerance while multiplying valves, supports, cold surfaces and common-mode maintenance errors. `RHT-1` therefore asks each architecture to own its route topology and its terminal mass-energy closure.

### 2.3 Falsification of the reference architecture

The prior 500 mm arrangement is removed from the candidate set unless a builder proposes it as a separately identified fourth architecture and meets the same entry terms. It cannot return through a silent change to the two-phase factor, source state, available-area challenge or terminal clock. Any such change requires a new controlled source argument and independent approval.

This is a general rule for the Department of Resilience: when a conservative screen exposes an architectural contradiction, the institution should widen the technology search rather than tune the assumption until the inherited design passes.

## 3. Source-state constitution

### 3.1 Four states

| State | Temperature | Pressure absolute | external heat | Intended question |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| `RB-S1` cold | 258 K | 0.35 MPa | 0 MW | cold flow, icing, seal response and weak driving pressure |
| `RB-S2` nominal | 293 K | 0.58 MPa | 0 MW | reference operation and receiver initial conditions |
| `RB-S3` hot | 323 K | 1.20 MPa | 2 MW | full-source rate, thermal demand and holder load |
| `RB-S4` fire-heated | 353 K | 2.00 MPa | 8 MW | external heat, active-energy denial and full vaporization screen |

The values are declared program hypotheses. A source-characterization campaign must establish composition, saturation state, impurities, dissolved gases, initial fill, vessel heat capacity and boundary heat flux before live-source acceptance. The model preserves the declared values so every downstream result can be traced to them.

### 3.2 First-order flash screen

The screened vapor mass is:

\[
m_v = \min\left[m_s,\frac{m_s c_p\max(T-T_b,0)+\dot Q\Delta t}{h_{fg}}\right].
\]

With \(m_s=2{,}000\) kg, \(c_p=2.5\) kJ/kg-K, \(T_b=248\) K, \(h_{fg}=467\) kJ/kg and a 120-second heat interval, the screen gives:

| State | sensible energy | external energy | screened vapor mass | flash fraction |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `RB-S1` | 50 MJ | 0 MJ | 107.1 kg | 0.054 |
| `RB-S2` | 225 MJ | 0 MJ | 481.8 kg | 0.241 |
| `RB-S3` | 375 MJ | 240 MJ | 1,316.9 kg | 0.658 |
| `RB-S4` | 525 MJ | 960 MJ | 2,000 kg capped | 1.000 |

The calculation has four deliberate omissions. It assumes one effective boiling reference rather than a pressure-dependent saturation path. It does not solve vessel wall and source heat transfer. It converts available energy to phase change without slip or nonequilibrium delay. It says nothing about where vaporization occurs. A source that initially flashes only five percent may continue boiling in the route, receiver or holder; a source screened as fully vaporized may still transport droplets. `RHT-1` uses the result to size observation and heat handling, not to certify phase state.

### 3.3 Required source characterization

Before `C1Q` quarter-source work, the public physics authority must accept:

- source composition and impurity bounds;
- pressure–temperature–density relation with uncertainty;
- saturation and metastable behavior over the admitted range;
- liquid and vapor heat capacity, latent heat and viscosity;
- compatibility with seals, membranes, coatings and sensor windows;
- decomposition and oxidation products;
- vessel and nozzle thermal inventory;
- traceable mass preparation and post-event recovery; and
- a source lot identity that follows every event packet.

The accepted property set may change calculated route scale. That is expected. The article bank is modular so nozzle inserts, path sections, receiver entry devices and separator elements can be changed without rewriting evidence identity.

## 4. Candidate `RC-1`: direct segmented low-pressure capture

### 4.1 Physical proposition

`RC-1` attempts to preserve the source by transferring it through one 600 mm route into a modest pressure receiver and a rapidly deployed low-pressure holder. Its first stage is passive. The holder is segmented so a local tear, ignition or anchor loss cannot release the full retained inventory.

```text
source vessel
  → passive full-bore isolation and 600 mm route
  → droplet-tolerant 2,500 kg receiver
  → distributed low-pressure header with flame and liquid breaks
  → segmented 2,200 m³ holder cells
  → recovery manifold under terminal custody
```

The modeled cold challenged capacity is approximately 17.84 kg/s and the transfer time is 112.1 seconds. Ten percent of source mass is allocated to pressure capture. The remaining 1,800 kg sets a long-horizon holder demand of 1,431 m³ using the inherited 0.53 m³/kg reference volume and 1.5 thermal allowance. The 2,200 m³ holder leaves 769 m³ of planning margin.

### 4.2 Research burden

`RC-1` is mechanically simple only in a block diagram. Its decisive questions are:

- Can a 600 mm deployable path preserve geometry under cold shock, liquid slugging and external fire?
- Can the receiver remove droplets without imposing unstable backpressure?
- Can 2,200 m³ of segmented containment deploy inside the event clock and remain anchored in adverse wind?
- Can holder materials tolerate DME, cold surfaces, heat, folding cycles and decontamination?
- Can inter-cell isolation stop flame, pressure and liquid transfer without active energy?
- Can autonomous handling recover, fold, inspect and regenerate contaminated cells?
- Can the system maintain source ancestry when mass occupies several flexible volumes?

These questions justify novel technology. Candidate work includes robotic membrane deployment; self-verifying anchor networks; distributed optical shape sensing; conductive composite layers that reveal tears; passive phase separators; self-closing inter-cell throats; and mobile recovery skids that close mass without human entry.

### 4.3 Failure signature

`RC-1` should be rejected if low-pressure containment cannot meet the source clock under wind, if a single tear recruits adjacent cells, if receiver backpressure approaches 0.15 MPa without traceable margin, if material absorption defeats mass closure, or if regeneration cannot return an article to controlled state within 72 hours.

## 5. Candidate `RC-2`: staged pressure recovery and condensation

### 5.1 Physical proposition

`RC-2` accepts greater pressure-vessel mass to shrink the deployed gas volume. Two independent 450 mm paths feed a 6,500 kg receiver allocation. The train captures a declared 65 percent of source mass in the pressure stage. A 2.5 MW condenser and 1,200 MJ thermal store process the downstream fraction before a 600 m³ holder.

```text
source vessel
  → two 450 mm paths with independent final elements
  → staged 6,500 kg receiver and phase separator
  → 2.5 MW condenser against 1,200 MJ stored thermal capacity
  → 600 m³ segmented holder
  → liquid and gas recovery under a common event ledger
```

At fifty-percent available area, the two-path arrangement provides approximately 20.07 kg/s in the cold screen and transfers 2,000 kg in 99.7 seconds. The 700 kg assigned to the holder requires 556.5 m³, leaving only 43.5 m³ of planning margin. Condensing that 700 kg requires approximately 326.9 MJ under the latent-heat screen, leaving 873.1 MJ of stored thermal margin.

### 5.2 Research burden

The small holder is attractive, but its apparent compactness depends on several coupled systems working during the event. `RC-2` must answer:

- How does receiver pressure evolve under vapor, droplets and liquid slugs?
- Can parallel paths avoid unstable flow division and cross-path oscillation?
- What separator geometry works across cold, nominal and fire-heated states?
- Does condensation occur at the intended surface or as uncontrolled frost and blockage?
- Can stored thermal capacity accept the event without grid power or cooling water?
- Can refrigeration and valves fail safe under independent safety-energy denial?
- How is a 6,500 kg receiver regenerated, inspected and returned to service within 96 hours?
- Is the 43.5 m³ holder margin meaningful after line volume, noncondensable gas and measurement uncertainty are included?

The program should pursue high-flux phase-separation surfaces, additive-manufactured droplet knockdown structures, passive thermosyphon transfer, phase-change thermal cartridges, embedded acoustic ice detection, and receiver liners that can be removed robotically. The requirement is not to use familiar industrial equipment. It is to create a resilient train that remains intelligible when active systems are absent.

### 5.3 Failure signature

`RC-2` should be rejected if receiver pressure exceeds 0.28 MPa in an accepted event, if parallel paths couple into a common failure, if the thermal store cannot absorb the event without external utilities, if residual gas exceeds holder capacity, or if the system depends on unmeasured condensation to close source mass.

## 6. Candidate `RC-3`: hybrid recovery and measured last-resort destruction

### 6.1 Physical proposition

`RC-3` distributes risk among pressure capture, condensation, low-pressure storage and a tightly limited oxidation path. Three 350 mm routes feed a 5,344 kg receiver. Thirty-five percent of mass is assigned to pressure capture. A 1.5 MW condenser and 650 MJ thermal store reduce load on a 1,200 m³ holder. A 0.25 kg/s oxidizer is permitted to destroy no more than one percent of source mass under independent authorization.

```text
source vessel
  → three 350 mm paths
  → 5,344 kg receiver and separator
  ├── retained liquid recovery
  ├── 1.5 MW condensation against 650 MJ stored thermal capacity
  ├── 1,200 m³ segmented holder
  └── independently authorized 0.25 kg/s terminal oxidizer
```

At a challenged available-area fraction of 0.667, the train passes approximately 24.29 kg/s and completes the cold screen in 82.3 seconds. The 1,300 kg holder-source allocation requires 1,033.5 m³, leaving 166.5 m³. The latent-heat screen requires 607.1 MJ and leaves 42.9 MJ of thermal margin. A one-percent destruction allowance equals 20 kg and requires eighty seconds at 0.25 kg/s.

### 6.2 Why measured destruction remains in the competition

A rescue institution should prefer source preservation, but an absolute ban on destruction can create a less safe system. Under fire heat, loss of holder geometry or receiver pressure excursion, controlled oxidation of a small, measured fraction may prevent uncontrolled ignition of the whole source. The ethical and engineering requirement is not “never destroy.” It is:

1. preserve life and prevent source recruitment;
2. retain and recover the source whenever the protected state allows;
3. authorize destruction independently from the builder and operator;
4. measure feed, combustion state and products with unlike methods;
5. cap destroyed mass at a predeclared fraction;
6. make any exceedance a failed event; and
7. preserve public evidence of the decision and outcome.

The oxidizer is therefore not a routine exhaust device. It is an emergency terminal state whose use is itself a reportable physical event.

### 6.3 Research burden and failure signature

`RC-3` must prove stable ignition over the admitted composition range, no flame return into recovery paths, complete products accounting, independent shutoff, acceptable heat and acoustic fields, and mass closure across retained and destroyed fractions. Its 42.9 MJ thermal margin is narrow and must not be rounded into adequacy. The candidate fails if destruction exceeds one percent, if products cannot be reconciled, if ignition creates source recruitment, if any route loses physical independence, or if the train cannot reset within 84 hours.

## 7. Competition design and anti-convergence rules

### 7.1 Two unlike builders per candidate

Six architecture–builder teams are funded. “Unlike” means more than separate corporate names. The public technical authority must record differences in flow topology, receiver internals, actuation, sensing, holder construction, thermal storage and software. A common critical supplier, identical controller or shared proprietary flow model can make two nominal builders one epistemic root.

Each team receives the same source-state envelope, interface boundary, evidence schema, challenge classes and acceptance rules. It may innovate inside those constraints. The government owns interface definitions, calibration data, failure data, event packets and regeneration procedures with sufficient rights to qualify a second source and reproduce the public model.

### 7.2 No early winner

The program prohibits an architecture down-select before completion of:

- all scaled known-truth route work;
- one full-scale cold and inert commissioning sequence for each builder;
- accepted challenge apparatus at both sibling sites;
- a minimum evidence-survival demonstration; and
- the first destructive witness for each architecture.

Early attrition is allowed only for a clear life-safety contradiction, inability to meet source independence, refusal to provide required technical data, or a physically unrecoverable test configuration. Cost and schedule alone do not erase a candidate before its governing physical proposition is tested.

### 7.3 Selection outcomes

The board has four legitimate outcomes:

1. select one candidate as the Root B reference architecture;
2. select two complementary candidates for different formations or source states;
3. continue one or more candidates with a bounded additional campaign; or
4. reject all candidates and reopen the technology space.

A multi-architecture result may be strategically valuable. Direct low-pressure capture may suit open expeditionary sites; staged condensation may suit dense coastal or urban logistics nodes; hybrid disposition may suit high-consequence fixed magazines. Standardization should occur at interfaces, evidence, source custody and training where possible, not by forcing one physical answer across incompatible environments.

## 8. Article bank and build strategy

### 8.1 Forty-eight articles

Each architecture–builder pair receives eight controlled articles:

| Article class | per pair | Campaign role |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| scaled route articles | 2 | coefficients, phase observation, restriction and material work |
| full-scale integrated trains | 2 | one installed at each sibling operational site |
| destructive witnesses | 2 | fracture, support loss, ignition and terminal-loss work at `PTC-A` |
| regeneration spare | 1 | replacement, rebuild and configuration-control proof |
| calibration reference | 1 | public known-truth geometry and instrument comparison |
| **total** | **8** | **48 across six architecture–builder pairs** |

The destructive witness is not a lower-quality production article. It must reproduce the safety-critical path, supports, materials, sensors and interfaces of its operational sibling. Any intentional simplification is recorded as a test limitation.

### 8.2 Modular physical interfaces

The full-scale rig should separate five replaceable modules:

1. source vessel and thermal boundary;
2. nozzle and primary isolation;
3. route geometry and controlled restriction;
4. receiver, separator and thermal process; and
5. holder, recovery or terminal disposition.

Mechanical joints, evidence connectors and authority boundaries must allow a failed module to be replaced without erasing the identities of the remaining train. This enables learning across architectures while preventing a common rig component from dictating all outcomes.

### 8.3 Manufacturing learning

The article program is also an industrial policy instrument. It should expose manufacturability, material lot variation, inspection burden, field repair, packaging, transport, deployment, contamination and regeneration. A candidate that performs once in a bespoke laboratory but cannot be rebuilt from controlled data is not a national force capability.

## 9. Challenge campaign

### 9.1 Twenty-four protocols and 192 paired events

Each of three architectures receives eight protocol identities. Every protocol is executed by two builders at two sibling sites as both a challenge and a clean control:

\[
3\ \text{architectures}\times8\ \text{challenges}\times2\ \text{builders}\times2\ \text{sites}\times2\ \text{pair states}=192.
\]

Pairing is causal, not merely statistical. The clean event establishes the architecture–builder–site response without the named challenge. The challenged event changes one registered condition or a tightly specified compound condition. Both retain independent event identities. If either is invalid, the pair is not silently repaired; a replacement pair receives new identities.

### 9.2 Eight challenges

| Challenge | Source | Primary perturbation | Protected-state metric |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| `CH-01` cold start and icing | 500 kg | cold source and ice-prone surfaces | terminal time ≤120 s |
| `CH-02` route-area loss | 500 kg | fifty-percent equivalent area | terminal time ≤120 s |
| `CH-03` wrong-side pressure | 500 kg | 0.20 MPa receiver-side initial pressure | reverse mass ≤0.5 kg |
| `CH-04` receiver initial fill | 500 kg | fifty-percent credited receiver capacity occupied | unretained mass ≤5 kg |
| `CH-05` hot full source | 2,000 kg | hot source and 2 MW external heat | terminal time ≤120 s |
| `CH-06` heat plus safety-energy loss | 2,000 kg | 8 MW external heat and both active safety paths denied | recruited protected source ≤0.5 kg |
| `CH-07` partial holder plus weather | 2,000 kg | incomplete holder and adverse wind | unretained mass ≤5 kg |
| `CH-08` network and time loss | 2,000 kg | site network and primary time denied | two evidence originals survive |

Challenges six through eight are compound because real public emergencies remove several supports together. The clean pair makes the incremental consequence visible. Additional unregistered perturbations invalidate the causal comparison unless the challenge authority accepts them as part of a replacement protocol.

### 9.3 Support events

Seventy-two development events provide twelve per architecture–builder pair: water and nitrogen coefficients; inert two-phase surrogate; cold and hot seal work; known-restriction truth; reverse load; receiver fill; holder weather; safety-energy denial; recorder/time denial; and reset/decontamination. “Known-restriction truth” refers to a metrologically measured obstruction, not an operator estimate.

Twenty-four destructive cases provide four per pair: route fracture at full flow, receiver support loss, holder ignition and overpressure, and terminal-disposition loss. Eighteen reset and site-loss cases provide a twelve-month cold start, sibling-site denial and complete train rebuild for every pair.

The total campaign is 306 events. Event count is not a reliability demonstration by itself. The campaign is a structured attempt to reveal distinct causal weaknesses, cross-site differences and builder-specific assumptions.

## 10. Measurement and evidence architecture

### 10.1 Sixteen planes

`P00` begins inside the source vessel. `P01` and `P02` observe the primary wall and interstitial boundary. `P03` through `P08` cover nozzle, isolation, route entrance, midpoint, restriction and receiver inlet. `P09` through `P13` cover receiver, separator or condenser, holder inlet, holder volume and last-resort disposition. `P14` observes the protected source and environmental field. `P15` is the evidence boundary itself.

This sequence prevents a frequent experimental failure: measuring the facility well while leaving the initiating source or terminal destination inferred. Mass, energy and event time must be traceable across every boundary.

### 10.2 Eighteen functions and 504 channels

The model allocates channels to source mass; source and route pressure; wall and source temperature; differential and wave pressure; void fraction; phase velocity; two-phase mass flow; acoustic emission; route and support strain; receiver state; holder state; gas concentration; flame and heat; weather; safety energy and final elements; event time; composition and products; and evidence-system state.

Unlike methods are required for decisive quantities. Two-phase mass flow combines direct flow instruments, momentum inference and inventory closure. Void fraction combines attenuation and capacitance. Temperature combines contact and optical methods where possible. Time uses two independent local clocks. Source mass is not accepted solely from a commanded fill value.

### 10.3 Data volume

For function \(j\), native rate is:

\[
r_j=\frac{N_j f_j b_j}{10^6}\ \text{MB/s}.
\]

The aggregate is approximately 288.736 MB/s. With full-rate time \(t_f=300\) s, tail time \(t_t=21{,}600\) s, tail fraction \(q=0.01\), two originals and overhead \(k=1.35\), event volume is:

\[
V_e=\frac{r(t_f+qt_t)2k}{1000}\approx402.267\ \text{GB}.
\]

Across 306 events, native volume is approximately 123.094 TB. A thirty-percent reserve yields 160.022 TB. The 576 TB vault gives a capacity ratio of approximately 3.60.

The reserve is not permission to discard data. It covers replacement events, calibration, hashes, metadata, public replay products and evidence replication. Native originals remain separately identified and locally complete.

## 11. Mass, energy and terminal custody

### 11.1 Event closure

Every accepted source event must close:

\[
m_0=m_r+m_h+m_c+m_d+m_{line}+m_{loss}\pm u_m,
\]

where receiver, holder, condensed, destroyed and line inventories remain separately observed. The unexplained term must not exceed 5 kg for the full source. “Accounted fraction” in the design model is therefore a minimum 0.9975. It is not a license to assign the missing quantity to an unmeasured category.

Energy closure includes source sensible energy, phase change, external heat, receiver work, stored thermal absorption, heat loss, oxidation and residual chemical energy. The reduced-order workbook checks holder and condensation scale. The physical campaign must establish the transient terms.

### 11.2 Terminal source condition

Terminal source condition requires more than an empty vessel indication. It requires:

- source pressure and mass below accepted limits;
- no continuing uncontrolled phase change;
- isolated route and known line inventory;
- stable receiver and holder pressure;
- authorized terminal disposition;
- two surviving native originals;
- no recruited adjacent source; and
- a declared safe entry or continued exclusion state.

The event clock stops only when the physical authority accepts those observations. A software state flag cannot stop the clock by itself.

### 11.3 Recovery and regeneration

Retained source is a controlled material, not waste. Recovery equipment must preserve lot identity, composition samples and mass through transport or reprocessing. Contaminated route sections, membranes and receiver liners receive individual disposition records. Regeneration must demonstrate inspection, cleaning, replacement, calibration and configuration return within the candidate's 72-, 96- or 84-hour target.

## 12. Safety, rescue and human entry

The test program uses three distinct safety layers: a passive first stage within the source train, an independent site stop and isolation system, and a remote rescue formation. The builder cannot command the site stop or declare its own rescue boundary acceptable.

Human entry is not the default response to an uncertain DME state. Remote systems should provide thermal imaging, gas mapping, acoustic localization, valve observation, debris manipulation, sampling and casualty extraction. A protected refuge must retain independent breathing air, local event time and native evidence. Decontamination and clinical transfer are planned before source admission.

The rescue package is itself tested under smoke, cold surfaces, failed lighting, communication denial and obstructed access. The protected state may require continued exclusion rather than rapid entry. Schedule pressure never changes that decision.

## 13. Decision rules and independent board

### 13.1 Twelve conjunctive rules

Each candidate must demonstrate:

1. cold challenged transfer at or below 120 seconds;
2. nonnegative holder margin;
3. receiver allocation at least equal to its claimed pressure-captured mass;
4. no cross-source path;
5. a passive first stage;
6. accounted fraction at or above 0.9975;
7. retained fraction at or above 0.99;
8. backpressure at or below 0.30 MPa and its tighter candidate limit;
9. reset at or below 96 hours and its tighter candidate target;
10. accepted results from two unlike builders;
11. accepted results from two sibling sites; and
12. accepted dispositions for all eight candidate protocols.

The board may add a stricter safety rule before protocol freeze. It may not weaken a failed rule after observing which candidate benefits.

### 13.2 No scalar winner

A weighted score obscures noncompensable failures. A lower capital estimate cannot offset source recruitment. Faster transfer cannot offset loss of both evidence originals. High retained fraction cannot offset a single common relief header. `RHT-1` therefore uses a feasibility frontier after conjunctive acceptance. Only candidates that pass every mandatory rule may be compared on deployability, regeneration, logistics, footprint, lifecycle resource use and mission fit.

### 13.3 Public decision record

The selection record includes native event identities, exclusions, invalidations, uncertainty, builder differences, site differences, unresolved mechanisms, fiscal consequences and minority technical opinions. The government model must be reproducible from public technical data even where vendor manufacturing details remain protected.

## 16. Technology spillovers and industrial economy

`RHT-1` is not a one-platform expense. It creates product families and supplier competence that a national resilience force can reuse:

- **high-rate hazardous capture:** modular receivers, phase separators and deployable holders for chemical releases, damaged pipelines and industrial floods;
- **stored thermal absorption:** phase-change cartridges and passive heat-transfer systems for mobile cooling, medical cold chains and reactor shutdown support;
- **autonomous containment deployment:** robotic membrane, anchoring and manifold systems for flood barriers, smoke exclusion and temporary water storage;
- **evidence-grade process control:** native recorders, independent clocks and public replay for high-consequence civil operations;
- **remote recovery:** sampling, manipulation, decontamination and material-custody systems that reduce human entry;
- **measured terminal disposition:** tightly governed oxidation or neutralization systems for cases where preservation would increase public danger;
- **regenerable pressure systems:** removable liners, self-instrumenting supports and rapid inspection for expeditionary utilities; and
- **public technical standards:** interfaces, challenge methods and data rights that allow a competitive export industry rather than vendor lock-in.

The military has historically driven innovation by sustaining demand, accepting early technical risk and building test institutions. A Department of Resilience can do the same around public protection. The export product would not merely be equipment. It could be an integrated package of articles, training, event protocols, evidence software, spares, site design and public certification.

The program also changes how industrial value is measured. A technology may be worthwhile even if no single vehicle absorbs its full development cost. Shared thermal stores, containment cells or evidence systems can serve wildfire, flood, drought, medical, power and hazardous-material formations. Portfolio accounting should expose that reuse without pretending a shared asset can serve incompatible deployments at the same time.

## 19. Research stocktake

### 19.1 What this pass resolves

The project no longer treats Root B as a large pipe attached to a gas bag. It has a controlled source envelope, an explicit failure of the inherited reference route, three coherent physical alternatives, six industrial roots, forty-eight articles, eight causal challenges, 306 event identities, sixteen planes, 504 channels, conjunctive selection, independent authority and a visible fiscal consequence.

It also resolves a methodological question for the broader project: radical resilience technology should not be introduced as a polished platform concept. It should enter through competing physical propositions, public test infrastructure, destructive work, source custody, evidence independence and regeneration. Only after that process should exterior form be allowed to make one option feel inevitable.

### 19.2 What remains unknown

The largest unknown is the coupled transient. The program still lacks accepted data for:

- vessel blowdown and nozzle phase state;
- nonequilibrium flashing and slip;
- line pressure waves and unstable flow division;
- ice, deposition and controlled restrictions;
- receiver impulse, pressure and thermal history;
- separator efficiency and liquid carryover;
- condensation location and heat-transfer decay;
- membrane deployment and gas compatibility;
- wind loads and segmented-holder behavior;
- terminal oxidation and product accounting;
- structural loads under route or support failure; and
- time-resolved mass-energy closure.

The declared architecture values will almost certainly change. The program succeeds if those changes are caused by accepted evidence and remain traceable.

### 19.3 Implication for the force thesis

The Department of Resilience is becoming legible as an innovation institution, not an enlarged emergency-management office. It needs public arsenals and proving grounds, mission formations, source authorities, technical-data rights, destructive-test competence, independent evidence and a shared technology budget. It must be able to create technologies that markets would not develop at national scale because their value appears primarily during rare, severe events.

That ambition is consistent with the user's original premise: equal first-principles investment in rescue and stabilization should create industrial capabilities comparable in seriousness to military force projection. `RHT-1` shows what “equal” means below the headline. It means funding two builders when one is cheaper, destroying representative articles, preserving failed evidence, carrying public technical rights, training recovery and rescue personnel, and accepting that the honest program can exceed a provisional budget.

## Conclusion

`RHT-1` turns an unqualified 500 mm reference route into a national technology competition. The correction is simple to state and deep in consequence: the cold half-area case requires about 161.4 seconds, so the inherited route cannot satisfy the 120-second planning target. Three alternatives now enter on equal authority—direct low-pressure capture, staged pressure recovery and condensation, and hybrid recovery with measured last-resort destruction.

The program is sized to learn rather than to decorate a preferred answer. Six unlike builder roots produce forty-eight articles. Twenty-four protocols produce 192 paired full-scale events. Development, destructive, reset and site-loss work raise the campaign to 306 events and 288 tonnes of controlled source. Sixteen planes and 504 channels preserve source ancestry at approximately 288.736 MB/s. Conjunctive rules prevent a favorable score elsewhere from erasing a failed safety fact.

The price is USD 875M capital and USD 148M/year, with USD 1.393B of forty-two-month exposure. The corrected CISA ownership view exceeds its provisional capital boundary. That is what first-principles ambition sometimes reveals: the institution must change its budget architecture to match the technological truth, not compress the truth to protect an early number.

No architecture is selected. No physical source is accepted. Production and exterior rendering remain blocked. The work now advances where it should—into the exact full-scale transient rig that can show which parts of these architectures survive contact with physics.
