# F36 `CISA-40` `SA6` maximum-credible-release and source-preserving containment constitution

## Executive judgment

Pass 116 established that the `SA6` energy–ballast reference articles may contain four independently identified two-tonne source compartments. It then used one compartment as the planning maximum credible release. That was useful for finding the governing hazard, but it was not yet an earned physical boundary. Four compartments containing eight tonnes are installed on one rack. Until the barriers between them have survived representative failure, fire, fragment, drainage, relief and support conditions, the defensible unconfined upper bound is the whole rack.

This pass creates `MCR-1`, the maximum-credible-release and source-preserving containment constitution. Its primary correction is simple and consequential:

> A two-tonne maximum credible release is not an input. It is the result of proving that one two-tonne compartment cannot recruit the other three.

`MCR-1` therefore replaces one assumed source state with three governed states. `C0` contains no hazardous medium. `C1` admits one live two-tonne compartment while all adjacent compartments remain inert and independently verified. `C2` admits all four compartments only after every passive propagation claim, a separate full-rack authority decision and the physical source record have closed. `C2` remains prohibited. The eight-tonne rack remains the bounding analysis source in the meantime.

The two roots are physically unlike and cannot share one safety story. Root A contains a liquid fuel with a declared planning lower heating value of 42.588 MJ/kg and density of 800 kg/m³. One compartment contains 85.176 GJ in 2.500 m³; the full rack contains 340.704 GJ in 10.000 m³. Root B contains dimethyl ether (`DME`) with a planning lower heating value of 28.512 MJ/kg, liquid density of 668 kg/m³ and reference-state gas volume of 0.530 m³/kg. One compartment contains 57.024 GJ, 2.994 m³ of liquid and 1,060 m³ of reference gas. A 1.5 thermal expansion allowance raises the retention design quantity to 1,590 m³. The full rack contains 228.096 GJ and expands to a 6,360 m³ design quantity.

Those differences produce different source-preservation systems. Root A requires separated liquid collection, firewater retention, thermal shielding and controlled product recovery. Root B requires pressure recovery, outdoor relief, dense-gas detection, low-point control, a remote gas holder and a measured last-resort destruction route. A 12 m³/s emergency ventilation system can dilute only about 0.171 kg/s of DME to the declared 0.75-volume-percent target. A one-compartment, 120-second release would require about 1,169 m³/s of dilution air. Ventilation is therefore a small-leak control, not the full-compartment safety boundary.

The resulting institution is not a shed with a larger fan. It is a three-cell public test system. Two root-capable, source-preserving lane cells sit at `PTC-B/LN-11` and `PTC-C/LN-12`. Each has a 12.5 m³ segregated liquid sump, 120 m³ of separately credited firewater retention, a 2,000 m³ gas holder, a 200 m provisional exclusion domain and remote control from at least 250 m. A third off-lane consequence cell at `PTC-A` carries destructive ignition, blast, fragment and thermal characterization without consuming either operational lane. It has a 40 m³ sump, 200 m³ firewater retention, a 250 m provisional exclusion domain and remote control from at least 300 m.

The cell system must prove twenty-four passive propagation-denial claims: six functions, two roots and two operational sites. It must execute eighty root- and site-specific safety events: forty-eight inserted faults, twenty-four clean controls and eight cross-site replays. Eighteen independent measurement functions, four root–site safety-logic cases, four source-recovery packages, three rescue packages and a nonpausing evidence clock determine whether a physical event can close.

The fiscal correction is also material. `MCR-1` requires a USD 260M capital allocation and USD 25M/year of annual support. Thirty months of support produces a USD 322.5M program exposure. The net-new `CISA-40` test allocation becomes USD 462M capital and USD 73M/year when the USD 168M/44M `RSP-1` source package and a protected USD 34M/4M remainder are included. The reconciled `BRD-3R` boundary becomes USD 780M capital and USD 157M/year after the inherited `BRD-1` build-record allocation. The uncredited physical-use view is USD 908M and USD 198M/year. Both remain inside the USD 960M/260M `CISA-1` parent boundary, leaving USD 180M/103M under the reconciled view.

Seventy-two planning gates close. One hundred nineteen physical gates remain open. Seven downstream decisions remain blocked: `C2` admission, `SDEC-1` execution, complete-article admission, `CISA-RB1`, `CRC-E01`, production and exterior rendering. No hazardous source, cell, passive barrier, instrument package, safe-state path or recovery system has been physically accepted.

The pass changes what may be drawn next. Internal cell sections, flow paths, relief and drainage topology, electrical one-lines, sensor maps and consequence domains can now be developed against a stated safety constitution. Exterior vehicle form remains blocked. The next work is not a silhouette. It is the exact cell and source-boundary design that could falsify the two-tonne claim.

## 1. Decision and inherited boundary

### 1.1 Decision

`MCR-1` answers:

> What source-state law, root-specific consequence model, passive containment, public test-cell architecture, fault campaign, instrumentation, recovery system and independent authority are required before a two-tonne `SA6` maximum credible release can be claimed?

The answer has four parts.

First, the maximum credible release is earned by propagation denial. A rack containing four two-tonne compartments may be analyzed at two tonnes only when every path by which one compartment could recruit another has been physically tested and accepted.

Second, the two fuel roots remain separate safety cases. Similar installed mass does not create similar release physics. Liquid pool formation and dense-gas expansion lead to different sensors, routing, recovery, ignition controls and terminal source conditions.

Third, source preservation is a subordinate objective. The order is human safety, prevention of propagation, preservation of direct evidence, recovery of material, protection of public infrastructure and only then preservation of schedule. Any architecture that saves evidence by delaying a necessary safe-state action fails the constitution.

Fourth, the test system must preserve causality. An operational lane should recover the source and evidence when safe. A separate consequence cell should accept destructive characterization. Combining both purposes in one cell would repeatedly force the program to choose between representative consequences and retention of the source object under judgment.

### 1.2 Evidence classes

Four evidence classes remain separate:

| Class | Meaning | Credit in `MCR-1` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| inherited requirement | controlled boundary from `BPT-1`, `CRET-1`, `SDEC-1`, `PTC-0` or `RSP-1` | retained unless explicitly corrected |
| declared planning hypothesis | physical property, source state, response time, capacity or consequence relationship proposed here | planning only |
| calculated screen | arithmetic consequence derived from inherited values and declared hypotheses | sizes the test domain; not a validated hazard prediction |
| physical source | accepted cell, article, event, instrument, recovery and authority record | zero |

This distinction matters most in the consequence screens. The thermal and scaled-distance calculations are deliberately transparent planning tools. They define where the public program must begin its design and measurement. They are not a facility siting approval, an operating limit or a substitute for higher-fidelity analysis and physical trials.

### 1.3 Inherited boundary

The pass preserves:

- the `CISA-40` common envelope at 12.0 × 3.6 × 3.8 m;
- the 32-tonne dry and 40-tonne operating mass states;
- four two-tonne `SA6` source compartments;
- unlike Root A and Root B material, joint, service, energy and evidence ancestry;
- `PTC-B/LN-11` and `PTC-C/LN-12` as the sibling `SA6` operational lanes;
- two locally complete evidence originals;
- a thirty-month `SDEC-1` execution horizon;
- the USD 960M capital and USD 260M/year `CISA-1` parent boundary; and

## 2. The maximum-credible-release correction

### 2.1 Why installed mass and credible release differ

Installed source is the hazardous inventory physically present. Event source is the inventory intentionally admitted to one test. Maximum credible release is the largest source that can reach one uncontrolled consequence path after accounting only for accepted passive boundaries and accepted initiating events. These quantities may be equal, but they are not synonyms.

The `SA6` rack carries eight tonnes. A two-tonne event can be planned by loading one compartment and leaving the other three inert. That proves a two-tonne event can be conducted. It does not prove that a fully loaded rack has a two-tonne maximum credible release. The latter requires evidence that heat, pressure, fragments, common lines, drainage, support collapse and emergency actions cannot recruit adjacent compartments.

An active valve is not automatically a passive boundary. A software command is not a physical separation. A check valve connected to a common manifold may prevent normal reverse flow while still transmitting pressure or heat. A drain that collects four compartments in one sump can turn independent vessels into one common source. A shared support can collapse otherwise separate containers into the initiating fire. The MCR case must therefore be a topology claim, not a component list.

### 2.2 Provisional bounding rule

Until all twenty-four propagation-denial claims have accepted physical sources:

- the full eight-tonne rack is the unconfined analysis upper bound;
- only `C0` or `C1` may be physically admitted;
- adjacent compartments in `C1` must contain no hazardous medium;
- the inert state must be independently observed, not inferred from a fill command;
- the cell must be able to reach safe state without recruiting a second source;
- no `C1` success counts as evidence for `C2` unless the specific propagation claim was under test; and
- full-rack consequences remain a facility and authority design condition.

This approach is intentionally asymmetric. A test can reduce uncertainty about a named propagation path. It cannot reduce the source bound merely because no propagation happened in an event that did not stress that path.

### 2.3 What earns the two-tonne claim

The two-tonne MCR becomes physically supportable only when all of the following are true at both operational sites and for both roots:

1. every compartment has an independent vessel or liquid boundary;
2. no common fuel manifold can transmit the initiating release;
3. relief is directional and has no common return that can backfeed an adjacent source;
4. drainage and knockout remain segregated through the bounding event;
5. a thermal barrier prevents adjacent-source escalation for the accepted exposure; and
6. fragment shielding and independent support prevent mechanical recruitment.

Each claim requires a design package, an executable protocol, a physical event, an accepted result and an authority record. Twenty-four accepted results are therefore the minimum, not the total test population. Repeats, clean controls, boundary conditions and invalid replacements remain separate.

## 3. Source-state constitution

### 3.1 The three states

| State | Hazardous configuration | Permitted purpose | Admission condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `C0` | no hazardous source | cold commissioning, calibration, controls, recovery rehearsal | cell and evidence package accepted for cold work |
| `C1` | one two-tonne live compartment; all neighbors inert | bounded live-source commissioning and propagation trials | root–site cell, safety logic, instruments, recovery and source packet accepted |
| `C2` | four two-tonne live compartments | full-rack propagation and consequence qualification | all twenty-four passive claims, separate `C2` authority and accepted full-rack source record |

`C0` is not a low-value state. It is where the force proves valves, routing, receiver operation, instrument timing, evacuation, evidence survival, communications denial and recovery logic without using hazardous media. A failed `C0` event is a stop condition, not an opportunity to proceed because no harm occurred.

`C1` is the only live state initially available. One compartment may contain the designated source. The other three must be physically inert, isolated from the live source, represented in the mass ledger and subject to independent pre-event verification. Water or an inert surrogate may create its own interactions and therefore requires an explicit configuration record; “empty” is not sufficient.

`C2` is a new experiment, not a larger `C1`. It changes total energy, gas inventory, fire duration, relief competition, support loads, drainage demand, emergency access and consequence domains. A separate authority owns the transition. No accumulation of successful `C1` events silently authorizes it.

### 3.2 Admission packet

Every source-state admission packet must contain:

- article, rack, compartment and media identity;
- measured source mass and uncertainty;
- neighboring-compartment state and independent witness;
- line and trapped-volume inventory;
- root-specific material and compatibility record;
- designated primary and last-resort routes;
- gas-holder, pressure-receiver or sump starting state;
- firewater-retention starting state;
- weather and exclusion-domain release;
- rescue posture and human-entry prohibition;
- two evidence-original destinations;
- source recovery and terminal disposition plan; and
- independent operating and acceptance signatures.

No arithmetic flag in the companion model stands in for this packet. Model inputs state the planning constitution; the source packet creates the physical event.

### 3.3 State regression

Admission is revocable. A cell returns to `C0` after any unexplained mass discrepancy, barrier damage, calibration lapse, unauthorized configuration change, common-path discovery, evidence loss, emergency action outside the registered logic or source exposure beyond the accepted envelope. A previous `C1` acceptance does not survive repair or a material topology change unless the authority record explicitly preserves it.

## 4. Root A liquid-source physics

### 4.1 Declared planning source

Root A uses a liquid-fuel planning case:

| Quantity | `C1` | `C2` |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| source mass | 2,000 kg | 8,000 kg |
| liquid density | 800 kg/m³ | 800 kg/m³ |
| liquid volume | 2.500 m³ | 10.000 m³ |
| lower heating value | 42.588 MJ/kg | 42.588 MJ/kg |
| chemical energy | 85.176 GJ | 340.704 GJ |

The property values are program hypotheses selected for range sizing. Procurement of a real medium requires a controlled property envelope, composition, temperature range, contamination limits and measured batch certificate. A nominal fuel name cannot replace those data.

### 4.2 Capture is not one volume

The local liquid-capture rule is five times the source liquid volume. It gives 12.5 m³ for `C1` and 50.0 m³ for `C2`. The inherited 40 m³ local allowance therefore supports the `C1` planning case with a 3.2 capacity ratio but fails the `C2` rule at 0.8.

Firewater is separately conserved. Each lane cell receives 120 m³ of segregated firewater retention in addition to the 12.5 m³ source sump. The same cubic metre cannot be claimed once as fuel capture and again as firewater capacity. Root A `C1` therefore requires 132.5 m³ of combined segregated retention. The consequence cell receives 40 m³ of source capture and 200 m³ of firewater retention because it is expected to accept destructive exposures.

Segregation serves four purposes. It preserves the source for mass reconciliation. It prevents suppression water from spreading fuel. It distinguishes original medium from runoff and thermal decomposition. It gives the evidence authority a physical basis for saying where the source went.

### 4.3 Pool-fire screen

The planning thermal screen assumes a 25 m² bounded pool, a 0.055 kg/m²/s burn rate and a 0.35 radiative fraction. For `C1`:

- mass burning rate is 1.375 kg/s;
- heat-release rate is 58.559 MW;
- radiant power is 20.495 MW; and
- the simple point-source distance to 1.6 kW/m² is 31.927 m.

Scaling the same simplified relationship to the full rack produces 63.855 m. The result is not a siting distance. It omits flame geometry, smoke, atmospheric transmission, surface effects, shielding, wind, combustion efficiency and transient behavior. It establishes that thermal measurement and shielding must be designed at tens of metres and that the full-rack domain is materially larger than the one-compartment domain.

### 4.4 Root A terminal source condition

The Root A safe-state clock does not stop when a valve receives a close command. It stops when independent measurements establish that:

- flow from the source has terminated or entered the accepted recovery path;
- no neighboring compartment has been recruited;
- the sump and firewater inventories remain inside their accepted limits;
- pool growth has stopped;
- pressure and temperature remain bounded;
- ignition state is known;
- evidence continues to record; and
- the source mass is either retained, recovered or measured in a governed disposition path.

The planning terminal-source target is thirty seconds for Root A. That target is ambitious by design. It is a technology requirement for fast passive routing, high-integrity isolation, rapid inventory estimation and instrumentation that remains credible through fire and loss of normal power.

## 5. Root B DME-source physics

### 5.1 Declared planning source

Root B uses a DME planning case:

| Quantity | `C1` | `C2` |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| source mass | 2,000 kg | 8,000 kg |
| liquid density | 668 kg/m³ | 668 kg/m³ |
| liquid volume | 2.994 m³ | 11.976 m³ |
| lower heating value | 28.512 MJ/kg | 28.512 MJ/kg |
| chemical energy | 57.024 GJ | 228.096 GJ |
| reference gas volume | 1,060 m³ | 4,240 m³ |
| gas volume with 1.5 thermal allowance | 1,590 m³ | 6,360 m³ |

The gas-volume relationship is a declared reference-state conversion, not a transient two-phase release solution. The actual source depends on vessel pressure, liquid temperature, flashing, heat transfer, aerosol formation, relief geometry and surface contact. Those variables must become controlled test inputs in the next design pass.

### 5.2 Why ventilation does not close the event

The declared planning lower flammable limit is 3 volume percent. The ventilation target is one quarter of that value, or 0.75 volume percent. At 12 m³/s of emergency airflow, the largest source rate that can be diluted to the target is approximately 0.171 kg/s.

A 1 kg/s release requires about 70.137 m³/s of dilution air. Releasing one compartment in 120 seconds produces 16.667 kg/s and requires approximately 1,168.944 m³/s. The full rack over the same period produces 66.667 kg/s and would require approximately 4,675.778 m³/s.

These comparisons falsify a tempting design shortcut. More conventional room ventilation does not transform a full-compartment DME release into a bounded indoor event. The architecture must prevent indoor accumulation by passive outdoor routing, pressure recovery, remote retention and exclusion. Ventilation remains valuable for seal seepage, purging, occupied-space protection and post-event clearance. It is not the governing `C1` containment function.

### 5.3 Remote retention

Each operational lane receives a 2,000 m³ remote gas holder. Against the 1,590 m³ thermally allowed `C1` volume, it retains a 410 m³ planning reserve. It cannot retain the `C2` 6,360 m³ quantity; the shortfall is 4,360 m³.

The gas holder is not a soft bag attached to a relief pipe. It is a governed source destination with:

- independently measured starting volume and pressure;
- compatible wetted materials;
- a route that cannot backfeed the article;
- isolation independent of the article controller;
- local mass or volume measurement;
- temperature and pressure histories;
- controlled access and ignition protection;
- a recovery or last-resort disposition route; and
- two evidence originals capable of surviving article and site-network failure.

Root B also receives 5,344 kg of pressure-receiver capacity at each lane. That is not counted as 5,344 kg of guaranteed recovered DME until the transient pressure, temperature, two-phase flow and receiver-fill cases are accepted. It is a planning capacity for liquid-rich and compressed-source recovery.

### 5.4 Dense-gas behavior and low points

DME release creates a three-dimensional cell problem. Low areas, trenches, sumps, cable routes, pits and drainage conduits can collect a flammable cloud even when the main cell volume appears clear. The cell must therefore avoid unmonitored depressions, place unlike detection principles at low and representative locations, make the primary relief path visible and prevent water or drainage systems from becoming unregistered gas routes.

The requirement drives innovation in fast optical concentration fields, distributed catalytic sensing, remotely deployed boundary sensors, robotic low-point inspection and real-time source reconciliation. These are not accessories to a finished test cell. They are part of the force technology program that makes the source state knowable.

### 5.5 Root B terminal source condition

The planning terminal-source target is 120 seconds. It is longer than Root A because the source may occupy vessel, line, pressure receiver, gas holder and outdoor route simultaneously. Terminal condition requires measured isolation, bounded receiving inventories, no growth of the unretained cloud, no adjacent-source recruitment, known ignition state and a closed mass ledger within the declared uncertainty.

If recovery becomes unsafe, an independently commanded last-resort route may destroy the source. That action is permitted only to protect life and prevent escalation. It remains measured source disposition, not disappearance and not recovery credit.

## 6. Ventilation applicability constitution

### 6.1 Three ventilation missions

Ventilation has three legitimate missions:

1. keep normally occupied or intermittently accessed spaces below the declared target during trace leakage;
2. clear residual gas after the source is isolated and accounted for; and
3. direct minor leakage toward an instrumented outdoor destination without crossing egress or evidence routes.

It has no planning credit for containing a full `C1` release. The 0.171 kg/s calculated limit is a source-rate ceiling for the declared target, not an equipment nameplate. Actual acceptance requires measured airflow through the governing geometry, detector response at adverse locations, failure of one fan or power path and confirmation that the discharge does not create a secondary hazard.

### 6.2 Active and passive distinction

An active fan can improve normal conditions but may fail with power, controls, heat or fragments. Passive relief geometry remains available without a successful command. `MCR-1` therefore gives active dilution no propagation-denial credit unless the claim under test explicitly includes its failure and proves a separately accepted passive boundary.

### 6.3 Innovation burden

The program should pursue ventilation technology beyond conventional building practice:

- high-rate, short-duration outdoor induction systems;
- blast-tolerant, low-sparking flow machinery;
- morphing relief apertures that fail open to a protected direction;
- sensor-informed flow fields with independent local control;
- rapid deployable outdoor dilution towers;
- two-phase knockdown and recovery before gas enters the holder; and
- computational models continuously corrected by distributed physical measurements.

Innovation does not lower the burden of proof. A novel system must expose more of its state, not ask the authority to trust a hidden algorithm.

## 7. Fire and thermal screening

### 7.1 Screening purpose

The thermal screen establishes three design needs: protected standoff for people and critical systems, heat-flux measurement over the complete domain, and thermal barriers capable of preventing one compartment from recruiting another. It does not predict the final flame or authorize occupancy.

Root A uses the bounded-pool relationship above. Root B uses a 900-second energy-release screen. Under the declared assumptions, Root B `C1` produces 63.360 MW of heat release and 15.840 MW of radiant power. The point-source distance to 1.6 kW/m² is 28.068 m. The corresponding `C2` distance is 56.136 m.

### 7.2 Barrier acceptance

A thermal-escalation barrier passes only if the adjacent source boundary remains below its accepted pressure, temperature, seal, support and material limits throughout the registered exposure and cooldown. A surviving outer panel is insufficient. The program must measure the protected-side heat flux, internal temperature field, support deformation, penetrations and post-event material condition.

The barrier should be deliberately modular. Replaceable heat shields, phase-change layers, sacrificial intumescents, actively cooled frames and high-temperature transparent sensor paths can be pursued in competing roots. The public interface is the admitted exposure and protected-side state, not a mandated material.

### 7.3 Firewater as a source term

Suppression media can cool the article while increasing liquid spread, contaminated inventory, electrical interaction, support load and DME vaporization. Firewater is therefore an inserted hazard world, not an automatically beneficial action. Each event card declares whether water, foam, inert gas, dry agent or no active suppression is allowed; the system measures both thermal benefit and secondary source creation.

## 8. Explosion and consequence domain

### 8.1 Deliberately conservative screen

The explosion worksheet uses TNT energy equivalence and scaled distance only to define a provisional analysis and instrumentation domain. TNT energy is set at 4.184 MJ/kg. Three participation hypotheses—one, five and ten percent—are carried rather than one preferred answer. Five-, three- and one-psi scaled distances are 4.5, 6 and 12 m/kg^(1/3).

At five-percent participation, the simple one-psi distances are:

| Root and state | TNT-equivalent participating mass | One-psi distance | Provisional exclusion domain |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Root A `C1` | 1,017.9 kg | 120.711 m | 160 m |
| Root B `C1` | 681.5 kg | 105.599 m | 140 m |
| Root A `C2` | 4,071.5 kg | 191.617 m | 240 m |
| Root B `C2` | 2,726.2 kg | 167.628 m | 210 m |

At ten-percent participation, the `C2` one-psi distances grow to 241.421 m for Root A and 211.198 m for Root B. Their rounded provisional exclusion domains become 310 and 270 m.

These numbers are hypotheses, not predictions. Congestion, confinement, mixing, ignition location, venting, flame acceleration, obstacles and two-phase behavior determine whether TNT equivalence is conservative, nonconservative or simply inapplicable. The consequence cell must create the evidence needed to replace this screen.

### 8.2 Why the cells use 200- and 250-metre domains

The two operational cells begin with a 200 m exclusion domain because they admit only `C0` and `C1`; this exceeds the rounded five-percent `C1` domains for both roots. The consequence cell begins at 250 m because it exists to explore larger and more destructive hypotheses. Remote control begins outside the exclusion domain: at least 250 m for lane cells and 300 m for the consequence cell.

This is not final siting. The actual public estate must reconcile topography, neighboring operations, fragment trajectories, thermal radiation, gas travel, wind, evacuation time, emergency access and cascading infrastructure. The numbers define the minimum analytical burden for the next internal site design.

### 8.3 Instrument the hypothesis, do not hide it

Dynamic-pressure arrays, high-speed optical records, distributed gas concentration, heat flux, structure response, weather and synchronized event time must extend beyond the predicted boundary. An instrument field ending at the model boundary can only confirm that the model was used to place the instruments. It cannot observe an underestimated domain.

The destructive cell should support deliberately varied congestion and venting inserts, remote ignition location, measured cloud inventory and replaceable fragment shields. Its role is to turn uncertain consequence relationships into public physical data before an operational lane is asked to survive them.

## 9. Passive propagation-denial constitution

### 9.1 Twenty-four claims

Six functions are asserted for each root at both `PTC-B` and `PTC-C`, creating twenty-four independent claims:

| Function | Physical question | Failure that must be inserted |
| --- | --- | --- |
| independent vessel or liquid boundary | can one source boundary fail without opening the neighbor? | local rupture, seal loss and mounting distortion |
| no common fuel manifold | can released mass or pressure enter a shared distribution path? | line severance and wrong-side pressure |
| directional relief without common return | does relief move away without backfeeding another vessel? | restricted primary route and reverse pressure |
| segregated drainage and knockout | can liquid, aerosol and firewater remain assigned to one source cell? | primary-retention loss and suppression intrusion |
| thermal-escalation barrier | does the adjacent source remain below accepted limits? | registered fire and delayed ignition exposure |
| fragment shadow and independent support | can rupture or collapse mechanically recruit a neighbor? | representative fragments, support release and combined load |

Root and site are not interchangeable replications. Material, joining, relief implementation, support behavior, local terrain, weather and emergency routes differ. A Root A result at `PTC-B` cannot close Root B at `PTC-C`.

### 9.2 No-common-path register

Before construction release, each cell receives a no-common-path register that traces:

- source vessel and compartment walls;
- fill, drain, transfer, purge and sample connections;
- pressure relief and emergency vent paths;
- sumps, trenches, separators and runoff;
- supports, anchors and fragment shields;
- normal and emergency power;
- control, vote and isolation logic;
- measurement, time and evidence retention;
- fire detection and suppression;
- access, egress and rescue; and
- recovered-source custody.

For every pair of adjacent compartments the register asks whether one initiating event can cross the path. If the answer depends on an active device, common power, common software, common structure or one operator action, the path is not passively independent.

### 9.3 Physical acceptance standard

Each propagation claim requires five objects:

1. a frozen design package with the relevant boundaries highlighted;
2. a protocol defining stimulus, measurement, stopping rule and expected protected state;
3. a physical execution under the registered root–site configuration;
4. an evidence packet with two originals, calibration, custody and unresolved anomalies; and
5. an independent authority disposition.

The result is PASS, FAIL or INDETERMINATE. An invalid event remains an event but supplies no acceptance credit. A repaired failure receives a new configuration identity and a new trial; the failed evidence remains public to the program.

### 9.4 Compound paths

Single-path trials are necessary but not sufficient. The hazard worlds include combinations such as thermal exposure plus safety-power loss, line severance plus primary-retention loss, or delayed ignition after detector disagreement. The two-tonne claim must survive representative compound paths because propagation often occurs when the second barrier is consumed by the first response.

The program should maintain a live causal graph rather than a static fault tree. Physical observations update link strengths, reveal common causes and determine which combinations must move from analytical coverage to direct test. The graph may prioritize events; it may not waive a registered passive function.

## 10. Three-cell public test architecture

### 10.1 Two operational lane cells

`MC-B1` at `PTC-B/LN-11` and `MC-C1` at `PTC-C/LN-12` are complete root-capable lane cells. Each must be capable of receiving either root without sharing the root-native source hardware. Their common public services include:

- an instrumented mounting and reaction bed;
- remote article transfer and connection;
- 12.5 m³ segregated source-liquid collection;
- 120 m³ separate firewater retention;
- a 2,000 m³ remote gas holder;
- Root B pressure-receiver capacity;
- directional relief and instrumented outdoor discharge;
- replaceable thermal and fragment protection;
- local safety energy and independent logic;
- two evidence-original routes;
- robotic inspection and sample recovery;
- a 200 m provisional exclusion domain; and
- control from at least 250 m.

The cell does not become root-neutral by using one set of internal lines for both fuels. Public structure, coordinate systems, sensors and command boundaries may be common. Root-native wetted components, connections, purge logic, recovery paths and custody remain physically segregated.

### 10.2 One consequence cell

`MC-A1` at `PTC-A` is an off-lane destructive consequence cell. It carries ignition, blast, fragment, thermal-barrier and high-participation characterization that would otherwise monopolize or damage an operational lane. It includes:

- a 40 m³ source sump;
- 200 m³ of segregated firewater retention;
- replaceable congestion, vent, fragment and thermal inserts;
- an extended dynamic-pressure, heat-flux and gas field;
- a 250 m provisional exclusion domain;
- remote control from at least 300 m; and
- no credited gas holder in the current design.

The absence of credited gas-holder capacity is deliberate. `MC-A1` is not used to claim source recovery. It measures destructive consequence and disposition. If later design adds retention, that capability must be accepted without reducing the cell's ability to observe the unretained case.

### 10.3 Why three cells are the minimum useful institution

One cell would entangle operational qualification, destructive characterization and schedule. Two cells would restore sibling-site operation but still force consequence work into a lane needed for root–site evidence. Three cells separate the purposes:

- `MC-B1` and `MC-C1` establish reproducible source-preserving operation at unlike sites;
- either lane can continue when its sibling is unavailable;
- `MC-A1` destroys articles and tests boundary physics without consuming the only source-recovery path; and
- all three campuses participate in the `SA6` safety case.

This is a public research installation, not merely a facility procurement. Its permanent outputs are accepted methods, calibrated consequence relationships, trained professions, reference articles, physical data, root diversity and the ability to investigate failure without depending on a vendor.

These drawings are permitted because their purpose is to test the safety constitution. They remain internal functional artifacts, not exterior vehicle design.

## 11. Source-preserving control hierarchy

### 11.1 Order of obligations

The control hierarchy is:

1. protect human life;
2. prevent recruitment of additional hazardous source;
3. preserve direct causal evidence;
4. recover the source medium;
5. protect the test cell and surrounding infrastructure; and
6. preserve program schedule.

The hierarchy resolves apparent conflict. If retention could create escalation, the source must be routed to a safer destination or destroyed. If a person would have to enter an uncertain domain to preserve a specimen, the specimen is abandoned. If an emergency action destroys the precise failure surface, all surviving direct measurements and the action history remain evidence; the physical surface does not outrank life.

### 11.2 Preferred source paths

Root A follows the preferred sequence:

```text
live compartment
      │
      ├── normal isolation succeeds ──> retained in compartment
      │
      ├── released liquid ──> segregated sump ──> remote recovery vessel
      │
      └── unsafe escalation ──> registered suppression or controlled burn
```

Root B follows:

```text
live compartment
      │
      ├── normal isolation succeeds ──> retained in compartment
      │
      ├── liquid-rich release ──> pressure receiver / knockout
      │
      ├── gas release ──> passive outdoor route ──> remote gas holder
      │
      └── unsafe escalation ──> measured last-resort flare or destruction
```

Every branch is instrumented. “Vented” and “burned” are dispositions, not missing mass categories.

### 11.3 Independent safety logic

The safety system uses at least three independent logic paths. Pressure or flow can trip in 0.10 s. Gas-detection `T90` is no more than 2.00 s. Safety voting takes no more than 0.25 s. Root A isolation is targeted at 2.00 s and Root B at 5.00 s. A passive route opens within 10.00 s. Terminal source is targeted at 30 s for Root A and 120 s for Root B. Evidence seals within 30 s.

These clocks remain separate. Fast command does not imply fast isolation. Fast isolation does not imply terminal source. Evidence seal does not imply physical safety. Each time is measured from a defined physical observation to a defined terminal observation with synchronized unlike clocks.

### 11.4 Human entry

Planning human-entry prohibitions are 1,800 s for Root A and 3,600 s for Root B, and they are minimums, not automatic entry permissions. Entry also requires known source disposition, stable gas and oxygen fields, bounded heat and pressure, structural clearance, rescue readiness and an independent entry authority.

Remote recovery technology should be treated as a strategic research line: heat-tolerant mobile manipulators, intrinsically safe low-point crawlers, aerial concentration mapping, automated sample isolation, remote hose and receiver connection, and robotic debris stabilization. The goal is not to make early human entry safer. It is to make early human entry unnecessary.

## 12. Twelve hazard worlds

### 12.1 Root-specific world cards

Twelve world classes are instantiated separately for Root A and Root B, creating twenty-four source cards:

| World | Initiating condition | Live-source fraction | Governing question |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| `W01` | seal seep | 0.01 | can small leakage be detected, diluted and reconciled? |
| `W02` | transfer-line severance | 0.05 | does isolation prevent continuing source and common-path spread? |
| `W03` | full compartment loss | 1.00 | can the cell contain or safely route one complete source? |
| `W04` | adjacent-source exposure | 1.00 | does one event recruit an independently represented neighbor? |
| `W05` | restricted relief | 0.10 | does the alternate passive route preserve the boundary? |
| `W06` | immediate ignition | 0.10 | do fire, isolation and evidence remain causal? |
| `W07` | delayed ignition | 0.25 | can a formed cloud or pool be measured before ignition? |
| `W08` | safety-power loss | 0.05 | do passive paths and independent energy reach safe state? |
| `W09` | primary-retention loss | 1.00 | does secondary segregation prevent uncontrolled spread? |
| `W10` | suppression-media intrusion | 0.10 | does the response create a larger secondary source? |
| `W11` | detection or clock disagreement | 0.05 | can the authority preserve truth under conflicting observations? |
| `W12` | evidence blackout and recovery | 0.05 | can local originals reconstruct the event without the main network? |

The fraction controls admitted live source for the planning card. It does not imply that consequences scale linearly. `W04` is conducted initially with one live compartment and an instrumented inert neighbor; the question is boundary survival. A truly live adjacent source remains a `C2` experiment and is prohibited.

### 12.2 Clean controls

Six worlds—`W02`, `W03`, `W06`, `W07`, `W08` and `W09`—receive clean controls at both roots and both operational sites. A clean control reproduces the event sequence without the registered fault. It distinguishes an inserted causal mechanism from normal cell variability and operator action.

Clean controls are not favorable demonstrations selected after a failure. Their identities, roots, sites, source masses and expected dispositions are frozen before the inserted-fault result is known.

### 12.3 Cross-site replays

Eight cross-site replays move sealed hazardous-event packets between `PTC-B` and `PTC-C`. They test whether the outside site can reconstruct the terminal disposition from a surviving original without using the producing site's narrative, network or operator memory. A replay result is MATCH, MISMATCH or INDETERMINATE; it does not alter the original event.

## 13. Eighty-event safety campaign

### 13.1 Event count

The campaign contains:

- forty-eight inserted faults: twelve worlds × two roots × two operational sites;
- twenty-four clean controls: six selected worlds × two roots × two sites; and
- eight cross-site hazardous replays.

These are eighty separate evidence objects. A replay is not another physical source exposure. A clean control is not a replacement for its inserted fault. An invalid event remains in the register and receives a new identity if repeated.

### 13.2 Expected dispositions

Expected dispositions include SAFE STATE, SAFE STATE AND REPLAY, QUARANTINE, PASS and MATCH. QUARANTINE is not failure. It is the expected outcome when the event intentionally destroys a boundary or makes the source condition unsuitable for further use. A lane that reports PASS because it reached safe state after the article crossed a quarantine threshold would erase important evidence.

### 13.3 Event admission

Before arming, each event needs:

- a root-specific world card;
- an accepted source state;
- accepted cell, propagation, instrument, safety and recovery packages;
- measured source and receiver starting inventories;
- source-independent fault insertion;
- a frozen expected disposition;
- a declared stop and rescue posture;
- two ready evidence-original paths; and
- independent operating and acceptance authorities.

An event closes only when it was executed, its required originals exist, the observed disposition matches the frozen rule or is formally adjudicated, and no unresolved anomaly undermines the inference.

### 13.4 No success aggregation across mechanisms

Eighty events do not create an “80 for 80” platform score. Each event bears on named mechanisms and authority questions. A successful seal seep cannot offset a failed thermal barrier. Four clean controls cannot erase one unexplained common-path release. The program may summarize schedule and workload, but technical admission remains claim-specific.

## 14. Instrumentation and evidence constitution

### 14.1 Eighteen measurement functions

The minimum measurement constitution includes:

1. compartment mass;
2. liquid transfer mass flow;
3. gas mass flow;
4. vessel pressure;
5. vessel temperature;
6. interstitial state;
7. high-range gas concentration by one physical principle;
8. low-range gas concentration by an unlike principle;
9. oxygen displacement;
10. thermal radiation;
11. flame onset;
12. dynamic pressure;
13. structure and support response;
14. sump inventory;
15. gas-holder state;
16. three-dimensional weather field;
17. safety-energy state; and
18. event and evidence time.

Every function requires declared channels, expanded uncertainty target, independent power, local retention, coordinate or volume map, calibration bracket and physical acceptance. Listing a sensor supplies none of those objects.

### 14.2 Unlike principles

Critical quantities are observed through unlike physical principles wherever practicable. Compartment mass combines local load cells with an independent platform scale. Liquid flow combines Coriolis measurement with gravimetric change. Gas flow combines Coriolis measurement with receiver mass change. Pressure combines strain-diaphragm and resonant methods. Gas detection combines infrared absorption and catalytic oxidation across high and low ranges. Time uses three unlike clocks.

The purpose is not redundant display. It is disagreement that can reveal a failed assumption. The safety logic may act on conservative observations while the evidence authority retains both values and the disagreement history.

### 14.3 Local evidence survival

The primary cell network, article controller and facility recorder are all plausible failure sources. Each instrument package therefore keeps local native data under independent energy. Two complete originals leave by physically or logically separate routes. Derivative plots, synchronized videos and event summaries remain reproducible from the originals and do not replace them.

Evidence seal is targeted at thirty seconds after the registered terminal trigger. “Seal” means write closure, identity, hash, time provenance and custody transition; it does not mean erasing late-arriving channels. Late material becomes a separate append-only object.

### 14.4 Spatial measurement

Source terms are fields, not single points. Gas concentration needs vertical, horizontal and low-point coverage. Heat flux and dynamic pressure need radial and angular arrays beyond the predicted domain. Structure requires support and barrier response. Weather requires three-dimensional wind and stability rather than one mast reading. Every sensor location belongs to the configuration record so moving a sensor creates a new measurement state.

### 15.1 Root–site timing cases

Four safety-logic packages exist: Root A and Root B at each of the two operational sites. Each carries the same sensing and voting targets but root-specific isolation, terminal-source and human-entry clocks. No site inherits a timing result from its sibling.

### 15.2 Rescue packages

Each cell has a separate emergency and rescue package. The three packages cover lane cell B, lane cell C and consequence cell A. They define remote casualty location, decontamination, burn and inhalation care, protected staging, transport, hospital interface, communications loss and responder withdrawal.

The rescue team does not sit inside the likely consequence domain to improve response time. Remote observation, protected refuge, pre-positioned medical capability and independently operable access preserve both speed and responder survival.

### 15.3 Entry is an authority decision

Elapsed time is only one entry condition. Independent safety authority must also see stable or declining gas, bounded oxygen, known ignition status, acceptable structure, controlled source paths, viable egress, rescue cover and continuing instrumentation. If any required measurement is absent, the conservative condition governs.

### 15.4 Technology campaign

The program should use the same ambition applied to military test and recovery ranges:

- autonomous firefighting and cooling systems able to work inside the exclusion domain;
- robotic casualty extraction mannequins and instrumented rescue trials;
- wearable physiology and location systems isolated from the cell network;
- rapid deployable clean-air corridors;
- remotely operated decontamination and sample transfer;
- high-temperature, low-visibility navigation; and
- digital casualty and exposure records that survive communications denial.

These technologies should be qualified as complete rescue effects, not purchased as isolated robots or sensors.

## 16. Recovery, custody and terminal disposition

### 16.1 Four recovery packages

Root A and Root B each receive a recovery-and-evidence package at both operational sites. Each package begins with a two-tonne target source and a five-kilogram maximum mass discrepancy. Two independent originals and eighteen required packet fields are the minimum record.

Root A uses the 12.5 m³ source sump and remote liquid recovery. Root B uses 5,344 kg of pressure-receiver capacity and the 2,000 m³ gas holder. These capacities describe the planned hardware; recovery credit still depends on measured source transfer and a closed ledger.

### 16.2 Closed mass ledger

The source ledger includes:

```text
initial compartment mass
+ line and trapped inventory
+ separately measured introduced media
= final retained source
+ recovered liquid or pressure inventory
+ gas-holder inventory
+ measured released or destroyed mass
+ samples and residues
+ discrepancy
```

Every term carries measurement uncertainty and custody identity. A value inferred solely from a commanded flow or nominal vessel size cannot close the ledger. The five-kilogram discrepancy is a program target, not yet an achieved performance.

### 16.3 Evidence preservation without unsafe retention

Direct source evidence is unusually valuable because it connects a failure surface to actual media, pressure, temperature and chronology. The program should preserve vessels, lines, seals, barrier surfaces, sump fractions, gas-holder samples, receiver residue and relevant control states when safe.

Preservation ends where escalation begins. A last-resort flare, controlled burn, remote dump or suppression action may alter or destroy the source. The action remains fully instrumented and the lost evidence is named. Safety authority does not need permission from the evidence authority to act within the frozen emergency hierarchy.

### 16.4 Custody and disposition

Recovered media enters a root-specific custody path. Reuse, reprocessing, destruction, retention as reference material or transfer to forensic examination each needs identity, quantity, compatibility and authority. Mixing across events is prohibited until the evidence authority releases both sources.
The final disposition record distinguishes:

- retained in original compartment;
- recovered as substantially original liquid or pressure inventory;
- retained as gas-holder inventory;
- contaminated or mixed recovery;
- sampled residue;
- measured atmospheric release;
- measured controlled destruction;
- unreconciled discrepancy; and
- unknown.

Unknown is an admissible physical finding. It is not an acceptable basis for closing the recovery gate.

## 17. Workforce and institutional composition

### 17.1 A 216-position mission establishment

`MCR-1` uses 216 positions from the inherited 448-position `CISA-40` campaign establishment. The work is sequentially scheduled, so these are not added to the parent headcount. The 216 positions form an identifiable safety institution even while remaining inside the larger force.

The establishment must cover:

- lane and consequence-cell operations;
- independent safety authority;
- fire, hazardous materials and environmental control;
- instrumentation, metrology, time and native evidence;
- source recovery, chemical custody and waste disposition;
- structures, fragments, heat and consequence analysis;
- control logic, independent energy and cyber-physical assurance;
- rescue, medicine and responder protection;
- separate Root A and Root B engineering and material teams;
- configuration, event and acceptance authorities; and
- logistics, maintenance and regeneration.

### 17.2 Authority separation

At minimum, the following decisions cannot be held by one person or organization for the same event:

- source preparation and source acceptance;
- cell operation and cell acceptance;
- fault insertion and expected-disposition custody;
- safety command and evidence disposition;
- instrument operation and calibration acceptance;
- event execution and final technical acceptance;
- financial management and physical result; and
- program schedule and `C2` admission.

The Department of Resilience should treat these as professions with career paths, schools and statutory independence. Safety-case competence cannot be improvised by assigning ordinary facility operators an additional approval box.

### 17.3 Force-generation implication

A mature Resilience Force needs hazardous-source test formations in the same sense that advanced military systems need ranges, test pilots, weapons officers, safety boards and independent evaluation. These formations deploy less often than response units, but they determine whether the response force's energy, water, thermal and chemical systems can be trusted under damage.

Their outputs include doctrine, accepted interfaces, hazard libraries, trained recovery teams, calibrated models, public technical data and supplier-independent qualification. That is strategic infrastructure: it allows the Department to pursue novel high-energy capabilities without asking operational disasters to serve as the first full-system experiment.

### 18.1 Nine phases

| Phase | Months | Exit object | Source-state limit |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| source and authority freeze | 0–3 | root cases, `C0/C1/C2` law and independent review | `C0` |

Overlap is conditional. A phase may start early only where its predecessor physical and authority gates have closed for the affected source state. Civil works can proceed while source details are refined; hazardous commissioning cannot borrow schedule credit from unfinished containment.

### 18.2 The sequence is a learning system

The early months create the authority and topology needed to recognize later evidence. Cold-flow work deliberately exercises failure, evidence loss, recovery and rescue before live media arrive. Limited-scale work calibrates source and consequence relationships. Full-compartment work follows only after component, cell and safety paths are accepted.

The calendar contains no automatic production milestone. Month thirty ends with an independent decision among acceptance, conditional acceptance, redesign, additional evidence, or termination. Retained dissent is an output, not a defect in consensus.

### 18.3 Schedule threats

The largest threats are not ordinary construction delay. They are:

- discovery of a common source path after civil works;
- inability to measure two-phase Root B inventory;
- gas-holder or receiver dynamics outside the planning envelope;
- thermal or fragment propagation at lower exposure than expected;
- evidence loss during genuine safety action;
- recovery discrepancy beyond five kilograms;
- cell-specific results that do not reproduce at the sibling site; and
- authority pressure to interpret `C1` success as `C2` permission.

Each threat has a planned stopping rule. The correct response to an unfavorable discovery is to preserve the failed architecture and change the next build, not to redefine the gate.

## 20. Technology and industrial consequences

### 20.1 A civilian high-energy test economy

If resilience capability is funded as a coequal instrument of national power, hazardous energy and process safety become major public technology sectors. The likely industrial base includes:

- rapid, high-integrity isolation and passive routing;
- high-capacity flexible gas retention;
- compact pressure recovery and two-phase separation;
- firewater–fuel segregation and automated runoff custody;
- explosion-tolerant instrumentation and local data survival;
- robotic hazardous-source recovery;
- replaceable thermal and fragment barriers;
- mass-accounting digital twins constrained by physical measurement;
- intrinsically safe distributed sensing; and
- modular public test cells deployable across the national range estate.

These technologies have direct value beyond `CISA-40`: disaster energy systems, mobile hospitals, desalination plants, wildfire bases, temporary utility plants, industrial accidents, damaged ports and partner stabilization missions all need energy dense enough to be useful and containment strong enough to be trusted.

### 20.2 Innovation should attack the governing physics

The program should not begin by specifying commercially familiar equipment. It should create competing technical roots around the most restrictive functions:

- passive valves and rupture routing that reach safe state without facility power;
- lightweight gas holders with rapid deployment and verified leak integrity;
- receiver systems that capture two-phase media across uncertain thermodynamic states;
- real-time multi-sensor mass reconciliation;
- optically and chemically unlike dense-gas field measurement;
- autonomous barrier cooling without uncontrolled runoff;
- sacrificial, replaceable fragment and thermal cells;
- self-powered evidence nodes with deterministic time recovery; and
- robots that can connect, isolate, sample and recover source under heat, gas and debris.

The Department can fund these as mission campaigns with rival roots, public test data and protected challenge teams. The goal is not to select one clever component. It is to create a complete, repeatable source-control effect.

### 20.3 Export and alliance value

A mature containment ecosystem would be useful to any country operating temporary energy, water or medical systems under disaster conditions. Exportable value would reside not only in hardware but in public interface standards, training, reference cells, hazard libraries, source ledgers, instrument packages and independent acceptance methods.

The strategic product is confidence that high-capacity civil systems can be deployed near vulnerable populations without shifting unmanaged hazard onto them. That is a form of power projection distinct from coercion: the ability to bring industrial-scale service while carrying one's own safety, evidence and recovery institution.

### 20.4 Limits on innovation credit

Novelty earns no safety credit. A new material, sensor, algorithm, gas holder or robot enters through the same source-state law, root identity, physical test, evidence survival and independent acceptance as a conventional system. Simulation can reduce the experiment space and improve instruments. It cannot declare propagation impossible.

## 22. Falsification and automatic stop rules

`MCR-1` is falsified or returned to redesign if:

- any compartment shares an uncontrolled source path with a neighbor;
- one passive propagation function fails at either root or site;
- the inert neighbor in a `C1` test cannot be independently established;
- Root A source and firewater inventories cannot remain segregated;
- Root B requires active ventilation to control a full-compartment release;
- the gas holder, pressure receiver or relief system cannot establish a bounded terminal source;
- a safety action requires early human entry;
- one logic, power or communications failure defeats all safe-state paths;
- the instrument field cannot distinguish source behavior from cell behavior;
- the two evidence originals share a hidden failure path;
- recovery discrepancy exceeds five kilograms without a governed explanation;
- the observed consequence domain crosses the protected operating boundary;
- `PTC-B` and `PTC-C` cannot reproduce a named root mechanism;
- an invalid or missing event is recoded as success;
- a favorable `C1` event is used to authorize `C2` without the full gate set;
- financial reconciliation depends on counting shared estate twice; or
- external pressure, schedule or visual design is used to relax a source-state rule.

Automatic stop is a capability, not a program embarrassment. The ability to stop, preserve state, publish the contradiction and redirect the next article is one of the central institutional differences between a serious resilience acquisition system and a demonstration program.
