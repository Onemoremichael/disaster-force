# F36 no-notice cold reconstruction campaign and rotational-readiness constitution

## Executive judgment

Pass 107 established the minimum material architecture that could recreate one F36 campus after permanent loss. It did not establish that the architecture could remain ready while proving itself. That omission is decisive. A single 300-person regeneration group and four complete shipsets can conduct a full reconstruction exercise or stand alert for a real campus loss. They cannot do both while preserving the registered guarantee against one industrial-root denial plus one arsenal loss.

This pass therefore supersedes `CRS-1` as the mature planning reference. `CRS-1` remains the one-shot mission minimum. The operational reference becomes `CRS-2`: six complete root-coherent shipsets at six separated arsenals; three shipsets from each of two unlike roots; two 300-person line Campus Regeneration Groups; an independent 120-person commissioning and challenge authority; two retained 1,400-person industrial affiliate pools; and the six dark receiver sites established in Pass 107.

Six shipsets are the minimum rotational inventory. During an exercise, one shipset is committed. If either industrial root is then denied, three shipsets disappear from credit. Loss of any one arsenal can remove another surviving-root shipset. One complete uncommitted shipset must still remain for a real activation. The workbook enumerates all seventy-two combinations of denied root, lost arsenal and exercise shipset. `CRS-1` reaches zero in its corresponding thirty-two-case screen. `CRS-2` retains at least one in all seventy-two.

The associated force is equally indivisible. One line group executes or resets the exercise while the other holds alert. Two 1,400-person affiliate pools preserve the same separation. The independent authority provides one sixty-person commissioning team to the exercise and holds one sixty-person team for an actual event. Exercise staff cannot accept their own assembly work, and the alert force cannot be counted inside exercise output.

The qualification program is `CRC-6`: six full, no-notice cold reconstructions over seventy-eight months. It exercises every receiver identity once, denies each industrial root three times, covers every arsenal, rotates the simulated loss twice across each active F36 campus and gives every shipset at least one selected, denied-root or lost-arsenal exposure. The root, arsenal, receiver, line group, shipset and secondary disruption are sealed until the event begins. Participants know the universe, safety limits and acceptance law but not the selected branch.

Every event imposes the root-plus-arsenal cut. Five also introduce a secondary disruption: grid loss, source-water loss, heavy-route loss, national-network and signing loss, or combined twenty-percent affiliate non-muster and reference-cache loss. Planning completions range from day 162 to day 179. The day-162 internal target remains the core architecture screen. The eighteen-day reserve is deliberately available to absorb the secondary challenge. Every full event must still reach independent admission by day 180.

The campaign carries twenty-four sealed fault injections and twelve clean controls. Eight fault families each appear three times across the six events. They challenge ancestry, executable configuration, geometry, sensor bias, time, containment, original evidence and blind truth. Zero missed faults across twenty-four injections would produce only an 88.3-percent one-sided 95-percent lower bound under a simple independent-trial approximation. `CRC-6` can demonstrate mechanism coverage and institutional behavior; it cannot establish high-reliability fault detection from twenty-four favorable trials.

Rotational readiness changes the financial boundary. `CRS-2` requires USD 13.900B of standing capital and USD 1.086B of annual support. The recognized inherited overlap remains USD 0.100B. The twenty-five-year incremental boundary becomes USD 40.950B and the gross boundary USD 41.050B. The USD 1.650B six-event qualification campaign is cross-classified inside the first six years of the annual support program, not added again. The change from Pass 107 is USD 3.900B of capital, USD 0.466B per year and USD 15.550B over twenty-five years. The increase buys alert capacity during training rather than a different campus mission.

No exercise has occurred. All schedules, detection outcomes and costs are design hypotheses. Planning may authorize `CRS-2`, the `CRC-6` registry, the branch-selection law and the physical campaign package. It may not claim a ready force, a completed reconstruction, fault-detection reliability, production hardware or rendering.

## 1. Why a one-shot reserve is not a mature force

A military force does not become ready by assembling every available unit into one exercise and leaving no alert formation behind. The same principle applies here. Regeneration readiness must survive the activities needed to create, inspect and renew that readiness.

Pass 107's `CRS-1` contains exactly the material needed after a root-plus-arsenal cut:

- two shipsets from Root A;
- two shipsets from Root B;
- one shipset at each of four arsenals; and
- one 300-person public group with one 1,400-person affiliate pool.

If one surviving-root shipset is committed to an exercise, root denial removes the two opposite-root shipsets and an arsenal loss can remove the other surviving-root shipset. No uncommitted complete campus remains. The same problem exists in people: the only reconstruction group and affiliate pool are occupied by the exercise.

Exercise recall is not an adequate answer. A partially assembled shipset at the wrong receiver, with challenge faults still inserted and an open evidence chain, is not an immediately deployable campus. Recall also turns the proving program into a hidden readiness debit. The mature force must hold a separate executable path.

## 2. The rotational-readiness requirement

The added public effect is:

> While one complete campus-regeneration formation and one root-coherent shipset are committed to a no-notice reconstruction exercise or reset, retain an independent alert formation and at least one uncommitted complete shipset after denial of either industrial root and loss of any one arsenal.

This requirement adds four non-substitutable conditions:

1. the alert line group does not share named people with the exercise line group;
2. the alert affiliate pool does not share named mobilization obligations with the exercise pool;
3. the alert shipset remains complete, preserved and outside the exercise custody chain; and
4. an independent commissioning team remains available to admit a real replacement campus.

An exercise may convert to a real mission if its receiver, shipset and configuration are suitable. That conversion is optional capacity. The readiness floor cannot depend on it.

## 3. `CRS-2` force structure

### 3.1 Material structure

`CRS-2` contains six complete shipsets:

| Shipset | Industrial root | Home arsenal | Exercise eligibility |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `A1` | Root A | `ARSENAL-1` | yes |
| `A2` | Root A | `ARSENAL-2` | yes |
| `A3` | Root A | `ARSENAL-3` | yes |
| `B1` | Root B | `ARSENAL-4` | yes |
| `B2` | Root B | `ARSENAL-5` | yes |
| `B3` | Root B | `ARSENAL-6` | yes |

Each shipset carries the complete nine-line campus bill of service and its common utilities, identity, safety, evidence and custody chain. Nothing is fractionally credited. Each arsenal can inspect, preserve and load its shipset without unique equipment from another arsenal.

The six dark receiver identities remain planning objects. `CRC-6` uses each once, but no receiver acquires routine campus capacity through exercise.

### 3.2 Public formations

Two line groups each retain the 300-billet Pass 107 structure:

- forty-five command, loss-authentication and logistics billets;
- fifty civil and receiver-activation billets;
- sixty-five power, hydraulic, thermal and waste billets;
- seventy-five station-assembly and integration billets;
- forty-five metrology and evidence-integration billets; and
- twenty safety, qualification and transfer billets.

The 120-person Independent Campus Commissioning Authority is outside both line groups. It contains two sixty-person teams, each able to establish references, conduct known-truth challenges, replay native records, classify cause and present an admission packet. One team follows the exercise. One remains on alert.

The public total is 720 billets. Builder, line commander, challenge designer, commissioner, cause authority and campus-admission board remain separated.

### 3.3 Industrial formations

Two retained affiliate pools each contain 1,400 qualified people across the two roots, civil and utility partners, heavy transport, instruments, software and references. Pool identities are not simply payroll estimates. Each person has one primary mobilization obligation, one alternate and a conflict declaration. The same specialist cannot satisfy both the exercise and alert roster.

The mature retained pool is 2,800 people. Ordinary subcontract capacity without named qualification, availability and conflict control receives zero muster credit.

## 4. Exact rotational reserve

The relevant quantity is not total shipsets. It is the minimum number of complete, uncommitted, admissible shipsets remaining after three simultaneous conditions:

`available = root-admissible × arsenal-accessible × not-exercise-committed × complete`

For `CRS-2`, the campaign enumerates:

- two choices of denied root;
- six choices of lost arsenal; and
- six choices of exercise shipset.

That creates seventy-two cases. The minimum is one complete uncommitted shipset. Some cases retain two because the exercise shipset or lost arsenal belongs to the already denied root. No case may rely on mixing partial shipsets.

The corresponding `CRS-1` screen contains thirty-two cases: two roots, four arsenals and four exercise shipsets. Its minimum is zero. This is why `CRS-1` remains a one-shot mission minimum but loses mature-force authority.

## 5. The `CRC-6` campaign

Six full reconstruction events occur over seventy-eight months. Event starts are thirteen months apart. Each event has up to 180 days for reconstruction and independent admission, followed by up to 180 days to return its exercised shipset, people, receiver and evidence estate to controlled states. The final program board closes in month seventy-eight.

| `CRC-E02` | 13 | `NRC-B` | Root B | `ARSENAL-1` | `A2` | `DRS-02` | `CRG-B` | receiving grid unavailable | day 168 |

Every receiver appears once. Each active campus is simulated lost twice. Each root is denied three times. All six arsenals are removed once. Both line groups conduct three events. Five shipset identities are selected for reconstruction, while `A3` is exercised through denied and lost custody; both roots supply admitted-build candidates.

The specific event matrix is a registered balanced conformance design. The live challenge authority may permute equivalent identities with a committed seed, but it may not change the coverage totals after observing performance.

## 6. No-notice branch selection

No-notice does not mean unconstrained surprise. It means the executing formation cannot pre-stage to the selected branch.

Before campaign start, the independent challenge authority freezes:

- the valid root, arsenal, receiver and campus identities;
- incompatibility and safety exclusions;
- the balanced six-event coverage constraints;
- the secondary-challenge catalog;
- the fault-injection assignment;
- the event start windows;
- conversion rules for a real disaster; and
- a cryptographic commitment to the branch schedule.

At each event's `T0`, the authority reveals only the information required for the next lawful action. The complete branch becomes public after the event reaches terminal state. The executing group may not survey the selected route, pre-connect the receiver, move the selected shipset, reserve local labor or load site-specific configuration before reveal.

The authority may cancel or modify an inject for safety. Such a change remains in the evidence record and reduces coverage. It cannot be relabeled as a completed challenge.

## 7. Immutable item campaign

Each event begins with a frozen item registry. At minimum it binds all nine campus-service packages, utility and data dependencies, transport units, references, known-truth articles, consumables, clean controls and waste containers.

For each item, the registry records:

- serial, lot, root and home arsenal;
- complete-campus service role;
- preservation and calibration state at reveal;
- release, custody, transport and receiver identities;
- installed mate and interface baseline;
- fault-injection eligibility;
- evidence-original locations;
- physical disposition at event admission; and
- return-to-known-state disposition and deadline.

An item removed for fault insertion remains the same item. A replacement receives a new identity and a causal relation to the original. Missing inventory is not represented as zero, and an inaccessible item is not deleted from the balance.

The workbook carries fifty-four required service-package records: nine service lines for each of six events. The physical receipt, installation, acceptance and reset fields remain zero until execution.

## 8. Workforce rotation and the no-borrow law

At each event, one 300-person line group, one sixty-person commissioning team and one 1,400-person affiliate pool enter exercise duty. The paired line group, commissioning team and affiliate pool retain alert status.

The campaign must prove, not assume:

- unique person identities across exercise and alert rosters;
- current qualifications at `T0`;
- travel and muster inside registered clocks;
- employer, union, licensing and contract availability;
- fatigue and relief coverage through day 180;
- no double assignment to an operating campus;
- no borrowed calibration master, signing key or safety authority; and
- reset and retraining before the next event.

The two surviving F36 campuses retain 820 public personnel, four closure bays and four damage cells. Their synthetic Pass 106 planning utilizations remain 62.0 and 64.0 percent. `CRC-6` may use declared liaison interfaces but receives zero labor, station, reference, evidence, utility or reserve credit from those campuses.

If the alert roster falls below 300 public line billets, sixty independent commissioning billets or 1,400 affiliates, the exercise does not start. The Department does not create readiness evidence by suspending real readiness.

## 9. Alert-force protection during each event

The alert ledger runs continuously from `T0` through event reset. It must show:

- one complete alert line group;
- one complete alert commissioning team;
- one complete alert affiliate pool;
- at least one complete uncommitted shipset after the registered root-plus-arsenal cut;
- at least two currently eligible receiver sites outside the exercise site and simulated loss region;
- one independent evidence and signing path;
- no alert asset inside the exercise fault or custody boundary; and
- a twenty-four-hour real-activation command path.

If a real campus loss occurs, the alert force activates. Experimental fault insertion stops. The exercise team either continues without consuming alert resources, enters safe hold or converts under real-mission authority. The challenge authority preserves all records but loses power to delay the public mission for experimental coverage.

## 10. Fault and clean-control campaign

Each event contains four sealed faults and two clean controls. The allocation is balanced so every fault family appears three times.

Every injected fault must be detected, localized to the correct causal class and preserved in a terminal packet before the affected chain can be accepted. A clean control must not be rejected without a terminal independent cause. Unlimited retries are prohibited. Any replacement event receives a new token and remains linked to the failed or invalid predecessor.

The fault sample supports mechanism coverage, not fleet reliability. Under a simple independent Bernoulli approximation, zero misses in twenty-four injections gives a one-sided 95-percent lower bound of:

`pL = 0.05^(1/24) = 0.883`

Dependence among three repetitions of the same fault family would make even that bound optimistic. No production-reliability claim follows.

## 12. Evidence and event terminal states

Each event can end in only one of six states:

| State | Meaning | Campaign consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ADMITTED_BY_180` | complete campus independently admitted by the deadline | event mission pass; continue after reset |
| `LATE_ADMISSION` | complete campus admitted after day 180 | architecture failure; preserve evidence |
| `INCOMPLETE_SERVICE` | at least one required campus service remains open | architecture failure |
| `EVIDENCE_INDETERMINATE` | physical work exists but the decision cannot replay | evidence failure |
| `SAFETY_STOP` | safety authority terminates exposure | no mission pass; independent eligibility review |
| `REAL_MISSION_CONVERSION` | public emergency supersedes the exercise | exercise coverage incomplete; real authority governs |

An event does not receive partial campus credit. It may produce useful subsystem evidence, but `CRC-6` mission admission requires the complete bill of service, no-borrow proof, alert-force protection, correct fault classification, clean-control disposition and shipset reset.

Campaign completion requires six terminal event packets and six return-to-known-state packets. A favorable reconstruction without reset proves deployment but consumes the reserve force.

## 15. Admission gates

### Campaign package

- six events cover every receiver, each root three times, every arsenal and every active campus twice;
- every event begins from a sealed branch and an unmodified cold state;
- one line group and affiliate pool remain on alert;
- all seventy-two rotational reserve cases retain at least one shipset;
- each of eight fault families appears three times and every event contains two clean controls;
- the baseline event targets day 162 and all challenged events target day 180 or earlier;
- every event includes return-to-known-state; and
- the USD 1.650B campaign draw remains inside six-year support.

### Event start

- the prior exercised shipset is reset and accepted;
- six shipsets, two roots and six arsenals reconcile;
- exercise and alert rosters are unique and fully qualified;
- at least three receiver options remain eligible before reveal;
- the two active campuses retain their full force and workload;
- challenge commitment and safety envelope are signed; and
- no participant has performed branch-specific preparation.

### Event admission

- the complete campus bill of service is accepted;
- the alert-force ledger never falls below its floor;
- all injected faults are detected and correctly classified;
- both clean controls receive terminal disposition;
- native evidence replays independently;
- no operating-campus resource appears in the reconstruction ledger;
- admission occurs by day 180; and
- the exercised shipset returns to known state by its reset deadline.

### Mature force

- all six events reach permitted terminal states;
- all required coverage remains after safety exclusions and real-mission conversions;
- both line groups and commissioning teams reproduce the result;
- observed rework and queue distributions support future P90 planning;
- the alert force remains independently deployable throughout; and
- no fault-detection reliability beyond the earned statistical bound is claimed.

## 16. Failure modes this campaign is designed to expose

`CRC-6` should fail the architecture if:

- exercise preparation quietly pre-activates the selected receiver;
- the alert roster contains any exercise person or affiliate obligation;
- root independence collapses through common software, materials, tooling, signing or references;
- a complete shipset exists only by mixing unqualified parts;
- an arsenal needs another arsenal's unique lift or test equipment;
- the two surviving campuses surrender people, references, utilities or stores;
- a fault is found by its inserter rather than the independent acceptance chain;
- a clean control is discarded because it delays admission;
- schedule reporting omits rework, evidence or reset time;
- a real emergency leaves no independent alert path; or
- a successful assembly is called a campus before independent replay and board admission.

These are not administrative imperfections. Each would show that the program produced a demonstration while consuming the very readiness it claimed to prove.

## 17. Technology consequences

The rotational requirement creates a sharper technology portfolio than rapid construction alone.

### 17.1 Reserve-efficient service cells

Four extra first-generation shipsets are created by indivisible singleton chains and rotational protection. Cellular utility, forensic, custody and blind-truth services could reduce that inventory only if they prove quorum behavior, root isolation and mixed-module acceptance under fault.

### 17.2 Self-declaring cold state

Stored modules should continuously report preservation, calibration, configuration, seal and executable-data status through local, independently verifiable records. The objective is to reduce `T0` inventory uncertainty without connecting the arsenal to a common national dependency.

### 17.3 Autonomous receiver conversion

Robotic survey, grading, anchor finishing, heavy movement and utility-corridor verification can reduce exposure of scarce public specialists. The metric is accepted work under altered site conditions, not machine-hours.

### 17.4 Deployable trust and time

Two commissioning teams require portable time, signing, geometry, metrology and replay systems that remain independent of each other and of the campuses. Common cloud or key infrastructure would erase the rotation.

### 17.5 Exercise-safe fault insertion

The force needs reversible, sealed physical and digital faults that are representative enough to test detection yet cannot create uncontrolled contamination, structural collapse or irreversible loss of the alert estate.

Technology earns a reduction in force only after it changes the minimum across the full rotational-reserve and campaign-admission model.

## 18. Stocktake after Pass 108

### What became authoritative

- `CRS-1` is the one-shot physical minimum, not the mature ready force;
- rotational readiness requires a separate alert line group, affiliate pool and commissioning team;
- six root-coherent shipsets at six arsenals are the minimum under root loss, arsenal loss and one exercise commitment;
- the full qualification campaign requires six balanced cold reconstructions over seventy-eight months;
- the day-162 core target and day-180 challenged target have different roles;
- twenty-four injected faults support mechanism coverage but not high-reliability claims; and
- the current mature planning boundary is USD 13.900B capital, USD 1.086B/year and USD 40.950B incremental over twenty-five years.

### What was falsified or narrowed

- one regeneration group cannot exercise and remain independently alert;
- four shipsets cannot preserve the compound-loss guarantee while one is committed;
- recalling or converting the exercise is optional capacity, not the alert floor;
- one successful reconstruction cannot qualify the full root, arsenal, receiver and team universe; and
- the Pass 107 financial boundary omitted rotational readiness.

### What remains provisional

- every event duration and secondary-challenge allowance;
- the 180-day shipset reset target;
- the USD 0.275B event draw;
- the sufficiency of six events and twenty-four injected faults;
- the roster and receiver-site assumptions; and
- every physical, reliability, production and rendering claim.
