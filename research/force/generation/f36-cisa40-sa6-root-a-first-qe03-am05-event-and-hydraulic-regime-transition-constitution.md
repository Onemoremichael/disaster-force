## Executive judgment

`RBC-1` defines the first lawful 250-kilogram unignited-source event in the F36 `CISA-40` `SA6` Root A research chain. It begins only after the complete `QE-02` source-transfer decision in [RBB-1](f36-cisa40-sa6-root-a-qe02-class-execution-and-source-transfer-qualification-constitution.md) is accepted. It does not assume that the 250-kilogram event is a larger copy of the 100-kilogram event. It asks whether the public reference system can cross a new hydraulic regime while preserving source identity, exact physical state, safe termination, recovery and source-exposed return.[1–5]

The central result is that source escalation is a vector, not a scalar. Relative to `QE-02`, admitted mass rises by 2.5, source volume by 2.5, bounded area by 2, nominal depth by 1.25, mass rate by 2.5, velocity by 2.5, Reynolds number by 2.5, Weber number by 6.25 and the conservative instantaneous surge screen by 2.5. Static route inventory does not rise at all. The line-inventory share falls from about 12.064 percent to 4.825 percent. No single phrase such as “two-and-a-half times larger” describes the experiment.

The existing 40-millimetre reference article remains a conditional research path, but its earlier hydraulic evidence does not transfer automatically. At 250 kilograms over 120 seconds, nominal velocity is approximately 2.0723 metres per second and Reynolds number is approximately 16,579. The Darcy friction screen is approximately 14.370 kilopascals. The conservative 1,000-metre-per-second instantaneous closure screen is approximately 1.658 megapascals. ## 2. Scale is a state vector

The reference hypotheses remain density 800 kilograms per cubic metre, dynamic viscosity 0.004 pascal-seconds, surface tension 0.025 newtons per metre, a 40-millimetre internal diameter, 12-metre route and 120-second source schedule. The `SF-800` density acceptance band remains 800 plus or minus 8 kilograms per cubic metre.[2,4–6]

For admitted mass (m), density (ho), time (t), diameter (D) and length (L):

\[
V_s=\frac{m}{\rho},\qquad
\dot V=\frac{V_s}{t},\qquad
A=\frac{\pi D^2}{4},\qquad
v=\frac{\dot V}{A}.
\]

The transition from `QE-02` to `QE-03` is:

| State | `QE-02` | `QE-03` | Ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| admitted mass | 100 kg | 250 kg | 2.500 |
| source volume at nominal density | 0.1250 m³ | 0.3125 m³ | 2.500 |
| bounded occupied area | 2.50 m² | 5.00 m² | 2.000 |
| nominal mean depth | 0.0500 m | 0.0625 m | 1.250 |
| mass rate | 0.8333 kg/s | 2.0833 kg/s | 2.500 |
| route velocity | 0.8289 m/s | 2.0723 m/s | 2.500 |
| Reynolds number | 6,631 | 16,579 | 2.500 |
| Froude number | 1.324 | 3.309 | 2.500 |
| Weber number | 879.5 | 5,497.0 | 6.250 |
| static line inventory | 12.0637 kg | 12.0637 kg | 1.000 |
| line share of admitted mass | 12.064% | 4.825% | 0.400 |
| instantaneous surge screen | 0.663 MPa | 1.658 MPa | 2.500 |

Several apparently reassuring comparisons can still mislead. Lower line-inventory share improves the fraction delivered beyond one static fill, but higher velocity increases surge and changes hold-up distribution. The five-square-metre plane keeps depth from rising in proportion to mass, but it creates a larger wetted boundary and more recovery locations. The normal receiver still contains the nominal 0.3125 cubic metres inside its 0.40-cubic-metre usable boundary, but margin falls to 0.0875 cubic metres. At the accepted low-density edge of 792 kilograms per cubic metre, volume becomes approximately 0.3157 cubic metres and usable margin approximately 0.0843 cubic metres.

The experiment therefore cannot be accepted through dimensional scaling alone. Each state has its own measurement, limit and authority.

## 3. Conditional retention of the 40-millimetre route

### 3.1 Steady flow and closure surge are separate

Using the Blasius screening relation (f=0.3164/Re^{0.25}), the nominal `QE-03` Darcy factor is approximately 0.02788. The level-route friction screen is:

\[
\Delta P_f=f\frac{L}{D}\frac{\rho v^2}{2}\approx14.370\;kPa.
\]

A two-degree route slope contributes approximately plus or minus 3.286 kilopascals. The result remains far below the 0.1-megapascal normal control boundary and 0.25-megapascal relief set point. Steady pressure is not the governing screen.

At a deliberately conservative 1,000-metre-per-second wave-speed bound, instantaneous closure gives:

\[
\Delta P_s=\rho a v\approx1.658\;MPa.
\]

That leaves approximately 0.342 megapascals below the 2-megapascal design boundary. The remaining margin is about 17.1 percent of the design boundary, or a design-to-screen ratio of approximately 1.206. This is enough to justify a controlled experiment, not enough to inherit the previous rate qualification. The physical closure transient, compliant volume, valve travel, trapped gas and relief response must be observed.

### 3.2 Twenty-four rate-extension trials

## 15. Research stocktake

RBC-1 adds four conclusions to the broader Department of Resilience design.

First, escalation ladders must be multidimensional. Mass, rate, duration, area, receiver state, source uncertainty and recovery do not share one scale factor. A mature force needs public methods that identify which state changed and which claim survived.

Second, earlier structural qualification is not a hydraulic operating license. The 40-millimetre article can remain the right research instrument while requiring new rate and closure trials. This is how public programs preserve continuity without turning predecessor evidence into unlimited authority.

Third, reserve architecture should reflect the uncertainty of the next regime. Designing the `QE-03` bank at a 0.70 planning-validity floor exposes the source, custody and storage institution required before event pressure creates an incentive to improvise.

Fourth, regeneration time is a force-design variable. A larger unignited event may be safer than a fire event in thermal terms while imposing more difficult source recovery, delayed residue, receiver and custody work. The ability to return a contaminated public instrument truthfully is part of projected resilience capacity.
