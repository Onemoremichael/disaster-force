The object is therefore below the resolution of the study. The nearest question that passes the test is ownership of the end-to-end clinical-continuity mission system. ## Epitaph for the returned implementation branch

The branch tried to establish whether a thirty-day clinical service for a declared population could become a buildable object without first choosing a ship, building or vehicle. Across four passes it replaced beds and megawatts with completed clinical episodes; derived care, workforce, patient-flow and utility obligations; decomposed one island into clean, controlled and contaminated domains; created centralized-gallery and federated-cell rivals; and reduced both to serials, packages, interfaces, receiver datums and witnessed build actions.

Its analytical cost was four passes, a hypothetical eight-island development campaign bounded at USD 45B, 339 serial definitions, 674 packages and 2,353 interface records. The work taught three durable lessons: a hospital is a coupled care-and-lifeline system; receiver preparation and patient movement are coequal with the clinical plant; and modularity trades common-cause reach against interface and configuration burden.

It stops here because another interface article, fixture or teardown protocol cannot decide the mature institution, national force composition or industrial economy. The dossier remains useful if a later top-level decision authorizes a clinical demonstrator. It does not spawn a successor now.

## Decision at the returned resolution

Clinical continuity shall be acquired as a **joint mission system**, not as a Health Protection platform program.

The Health Protection Service owns clinical professions, doctrine, licensure, care standards and the lifecycle of clinical equipment. Infrastructure Restoration owns power, water, oxygen, wastewater and technical-release professions. Civil Mobility and Continuity owns patient collection, regulation, transfer and return. Air, Maritime, Land and Sustainment organizations own their carrier, access and replenishment families. None of those owners may claim the end-to-end outcome alone.

A Department-level **Clinical Continuity Mission System Office** owns the integrated requirement, dependency model, program budget, open interfaces and representative campaign. It buys four inseparable capability lanes:

1. prepared local continuity and facility adapters;
2. mobile and relocatable clinical capacity;
3. patient regulation, movement, receiving and supported discharge; and
4. utilities, supply, waste, repair and regeneration through transfer.

This is not a new eighth service. It is the acquisition expression of the existing distinction between services, effects and formations. Services generate professions and article families; the mission-system office makes their combined chain testable; campaign commands employ certified formations; independent assurance denies credit when any decisive dependency is absent.

The industrial consequence is equally first-order. The Department should contract for measurable service-chain increments, with rival mission integrators and government-owned interface/evidence standards, rather than award one hospital-shaped prime a vertically closed monopoly. A clinical module, power source or carrier can be competed separately, but payment and force credit depend on the integrated pathway reaching a person and completing transfer or supported discharge under declared loss.

The clinical-island workbook becomes one candidate design library inside that structure. Its USD 45B campaign boundary is not a recommendation for appropriation and its roots are not programs of record.
