## 2. Method is a public asset

### 2.1 Why hardware neutrality is insufficient

`RHP-1` prevents the rig from embedding one candidate's geometry. Method can reintroduce the same bias invisibly. A builder-favored warm-up sequence, a proprietary channel treated as decisive, a challenge tolerance widened after a failed run, a site-specific clock correction or a post-result choice of averaging rule can select an architecture without changing the public flange.

The state therefore owns the method layer. Candidate teams may challenge a method, propose an amendment and provide additional channels. They may not define the governing observation after the event, suppress a public signal, substitute proprietary inference for missing public evidence or change a pair's scientific proposition between its control and challenge.

### 2.2 A method card is an executable compact

Every method card binds five things:

1. **Physical proposition:** the state or failure being imposed.
2. **Realization rule:** the variables, tolerances and dwell that prove it was actually imposed.
3. **Public command grammar:** who may issue, refuse or stop each consequential action.
4. **Evidence burden:** which calibrated functions, samples, originals and custody records are required.
5. **Disposition rule:** what makes the attempt valid, how candidate outcome is classified and when recovery may begin.

A card is incomplete if it describes only the physical steps. It must also define the evidence needed to prove those steps occurred.

### 2.3 Common grammar, unequal consequence

All ten methods use the same eighteen-phase clock. This does not make them equally hazardous. It makes their administrative objects legible to the same public institution. `MC-00` may scale the recovery proof to a dry mechanical state; `MC-07` requires the full source, exclusion, casualty and forensic systems. The common clock prevents a supposedly simple stage from accumulating undocumented exceptions that later become inherited practice.

## 4. The eighteen-phase public clock

### 4.1 Before hazardous preparation

At `T-72 h`, configuration authority freezes the serialized article, rig, route, receiver, software and method. At `T-48 h`, recovery authority proves receiver, vapor-treatment, residual-store and casualty capacity. At `T-26 h`, source authority obtains the required material splits. At `T-24 h`, lot identity, mass, chemistry and custody must be accepted. At `T-12 h`, candidate authority accepts the as-tested configuration and reset history.

These phases make later forensic work possible. If the source lot, software version or replacement sensor is unresolved after the event, the public cannot know which proposition it tested.

### 4.2 Condition realization

The controlled ramp begins at `T-8 h`. The minimum condition dwell begins at `T-6 h` and is restarted if a decisive variable leaves its tolerance. Rescue and exclusion are independently polled at `T-2 h`. At `T-45 min`, the metrology authority verifies every required function against its accepted calibration chains. At `T-30 min`, the evidence authority proves two native original paths. At `T-10 min`, each console gives an explicit signed response.

The dwell is not waiting time. It is a measured interval over which the initial-state statistics must remain inside the card. A single compliant sample cannot represent a drifting source.

### 4.3 Release and terminal interval

Full-rate recording begins at `T-60 s`. The registered challenge enters at `T-10 s`, far enough before release to prove realization and close enough to prevent an unregistered new equilibrium. At `T0`, the test conductor issues one source or method command. The terminal interval runs through `T+120 s`; observation continues at full rate through `T+240 s`.

The single command matters. Distributed or ambiguous release creates an avoidable cause-order dispute. The command transaction must show issuer, acceptance or refusal, automatic action and physical response on the common clock.

### 4.4 Tail, recovery and disposition

From `T+240 s` through six hours, reduced-rate recording and remote survey continue. Human entry is not granted by the clock. It depends on the ordered recovery criteria. By `T+72 h`, the immutable packet freezes. By day seven, separate boards disposition validity and outcome.

Across ten methods, these phases create 180 individual procedure objects. Each can be amended, trained, accepted or held without turning the whole campaign into one vague procedure.

## 6. Initial-state tolerances and challenge realization

### 6.1 Quarter-source cards

`Q01` requires 258 K ±1 K for 120 minutes and 0.35 MPa ±0.005 MPa for thirty minutes; receiver-fill state is a supporting observation. `Q02` requires an available-area fraction of 0.500 ±0.005, with supporting temperature and source-pressure tolerances. `Q03` requires receiver pressure at 0.80 ±0.01 of the candidate limit. `Q04` requires receiver liquid-equivalent fill at 0.50 ±0.02 for sixty minutes.

The decisive/supporting distinction prevents every imperfect environmental variable from invalidating an event while ensuring the challenge itself cannot be inferred from a command or fixture label.

### 6.2 Full-source cards

`F05` requires 323 K ±1 K for 120 minutes, 1.20 MPa ±0.012 MPa and 2,000 kg ±2 kg. `F06` requires 353 K ±1 K, 2.00 MPa ±0.02 MPa and an incident heat boundary of 8.0 ±0.40 MW; active candidate energy is denied by the method. `F07` requires holder deployment of 0.50 ±0.02, a 1.50 ±0.15 kPa wind screen and 2,000 kg ±2 kg. `F08` requires both campus network and primary-time availability to equal zero for the registered interval, with source mass at 2,000 kg ±2 kg.

These are initial design tolerances, not proven metrology capability. They establish what the program must learn to impose and measure. If later physical work shows that a tolerance is unsafe, unmeasurable or causally uninformative, the amendment system changes the prospective protocol openly.

### 6.3 Challenge realization is conjunctive

A challenge is realized only if every decisive variable remains inside its allowed interval for the declared dwell and its independent witness is accepted. A command to close a screen, deny a network or energize heaters is not the challenge. The observed area, service state or incident heat is the challenge.

## 7. Calibration and sample sovereignty

### 7.1 Two chains for every function

Each of eighteen measurement functions receives a primary and independent witness chain, producing thirty-six calibration objects. The functions cover source mass and composition; pressure and temperature fields; flow and phase; force and strain; vibration; geometry; gas and fire boundaries; weather; electrical energy; control state; public time; imagery; acoustic/leak state; recovery inventory; and site/custody state.

Independence is functional, not documentary. Two certificates from one instrument path do not make two chains. Dynamic functions require a response characterization appropriate to the transient, not only a static zero and span.

### 7.2 Sampling as causal evidence

Every method has three sample records: identity/control, pre-method state and post-method residual. Hazardous-source methods use liquid and vapor triplicates; non-source methods use a method-appropriate witness. Each split is sealed for the origin laboratory, witness laboratory and archive.

This distinguishes three questions that are often conflated: what material the institution received, what material state entered the test and what remained after the method. The archive preserves the ability to revisit analytical assumptions without consuming the only surviving sample.

## 8. Command, abort and safe-state architecture

### 8.1 Sixteen signed transactions

The command sequence begins with configuration freeze and recovery proof, then admits source, candidate and condition. It seals the exclusion area, arms originals, polls consoles, starts the common record, inserts the challenge and issues the single release command. After release it declares the terminal interval, transitions to the tail, authorizes recovery entry and freezes the packet.

Every transaction has one issuer, one required evidence object and one predetermined response to refusal or failure. The transaction records issued, refused, automatic and manual commands. Silence is not assent.

### 8.2 Abort channels

The sixteen abort channels protect:

- source, candidate and public-receiver pressure;
- route axial load, pad vertical load and overturning moment;
- gas and fire boundaries;
- protected thermal state;
- independence of the safety paths;
- continuity of both native originals;
- the 5-microsecond time-alignment ceiling;
- nonsacrificial route integrity;
- capture geometry;
- the 120-second terminal-source ceiling; and
- an unconditional manual stop from any console authority.

Each channel fixes its observation, threshold, vote, debounce, final element and authority. Fast structural and pressure limits act in 10–20 milliseconds; gas and fire rules act in 100 milliseconds; thermal, evidence and timing rules act in one second; loss of a safety path and manual stop act immediately.

### 8.3 Safe state is public

The eight-action safe-state sequence closes two source barriers, isolates the candidate boundary, directs a protected segment into recovery, inerts exposed volume, removes nonessential ignition energy, keeps remote measurement alive, protects people and perimeter, and freezes configuration and custody.

The candidate may reach a more conservative state first. It may not prevent the public rig from reaching its own safe state. Safety intervention is recorded as a candidate outcome, but it does not automatically invalidate the event.

## 9. Feature constitution

### 9.1 Four kinds of feature

The twenty features are not a scorecard. They are typed observations:

- **Mission performance:** terminal-state time and source residual.
- **Conjunctive safety and retention:** receiver pressure, candidate pressure margin, route and pad margins, accounted mass, retained mass, uncontrolled loss, holder utilization and public intervention.
- **Diagnosis:** vapor carryover, thermal impulse and route impulse.
- **Validity:** maximum time skew, usable-channel fraction, mass discrepancy, cause-order confidence and evidence completeness.

Direction is declared before data. Lower is preferred for terminal time, receiver pressure, loss, intervention and skew. Higher is preferred for margins, mass fractions, usable channels, cause confidence and completeness. Thermal and route impulse are two-sided diagnostic variables rather than quantities to minimize blindly.

### 9.2 Governing bounds

The inherited terminal ceiling remains 120 seconds. Public receiver pressure remains at or below 0.30 MPa. Structural margins must remain nonnegative. Accounted mass fraction is at least 0.9975; retained mass fraction at least 0.995. Uncontrolled loss and expanded mass discrepancy remain at or below 5 kg. Holder utilization remains at or below one. Public intervention remains zero for a clean success. Time skew remains at or below 5 microseconds, usable-channel fraction at or above 0.90, cause-order confidence at or above 0.95 and evidence completeness equal to one.

These bounds are conjunctive where assigned. A faster terminal time cannot compensate for uncontrolled loss, and a favorable mass result cannot compensate for an unresolvable clock.

## 10. Causal estimator hierarchy

### 10.1 Event layer

An event record receives observed terminal time, mass discrepancy, receiver peak pressure, public intervention, usable fraction and cause confidence. Those fields do not become admissible merely because they are numeric. The validity board must first accept the registered configuration, source, initial state, challenge, measurement, timing, safety-envelope treatment, mass closure, custody and independent adjudication.

### 10.2 Matched-pair layer

For feature (Y), the first causal contrast is:

\[
\Delta Y_{r,s,c}=Y_{challenge,r,s,c}-Y_{control,r,s,c},
\]

where (r) is the candidate–builder root, (s) the operational site and (c) the challenge. The sign is never reversed to make the candidate appear favorable. Both events must be valid. If either event is missing or invalid, the pair effect remains blank.

For the worked root there are sixteen pair estimators: eight challenges at each of `PTC-B` and `PTC-C`. The control absorbs the root, site and source-tier state nearest to the challenged event. It does not remove every time-varying influence; pair order therefore remains registered and visible.

### 10.3 Root layer

The two site-specific contrasts form the root mean:

\[
\bar{\Delta Y}_{r,c}=\frac{\Delta Y_{r,B,c}+\Delta Y_{r,C,c}}{2}.
\]

The site spread is retained beside the mean. A mean is not produced unless both site pairs are accepted. The workbook does not treat one site as an expendable replicate. A disagreement can reveal site preparation, ambient boundary, rig realization, builder installation or a real interaction; it requires disposition before root acceptance.

### 10.4 Candidate layer

The unlike-builder roots form the candidate mean:

\[
\bar{\Delta Y}_{k,c}=\frac{\bar{\Delta Y}_{k,BA,c}+\bar{\Delta Y}_{k,BB,c}}{2}.
\]

Builder spread remains visible. Both roots must be accepted. No production-weighted or confidence-weighted mean is introduced at this stage because such weights could reward the more familiar builder, hide process sensitivity or create leverage to redefine confidence after results.

The present workbook resolves `RC-1-BA` completely and leaves the `Builder B` candidate inputs blank. That blank is intentional: the candidate estimator is defined but cannot yet issue a candidate effect.

### 10.5 Why the estimator is deliberately modest

The hierarchy is a causal accounting constitution, not the final scientific model. Later physical learning may justify survival models, transient field reconstruction, interval censoring, Bayesian hierarchical terms or nonlinear interactions. Those advances must enter through the amendment system, reproduce all affected events and retain both views. Complexity is welcome when it explains more of the physical system; it is not welcome when it obscures which accepted observations carry a claim.

## 11. Missing data and uncertainty

### 11.1 No decisive imputation

Missingness is classified by function. An absent decisive function, a decisive function outside calibration, both native originals absent, usable fraction below 0.90, time skew above 5 microseconds, an absent challenge witness or failed evidentiary mass closure makes the attempt invalid under its applicable rule. A vendor certificate cannot substitute for a lost source split, and a rendered view cannot substitute for both native originals.

A nondecisive channel may use a bounded redundant estimator only if the method permits it, the missing flag remains and uncertainty expands. An unobserved mass term may be represented as an interval, never as an invented point. If that interval crosses the governing limit, the event cannot receive the desired decision. An outside-site environmental witness is usable only when registered prospectively. A missing proprietary channel does not penalize the candidate when the public measurement functions remain sufficient.

### 11.2 Feature-level uncertainty

Each of the twenty decision features has a declared expanded allocation and physical acceptance field. Contributors include calibration, spatial representation, temporal alignment and estimator transformation. Root-sum-square is the default only when covariance has not been registered; correlated terms require an explicit covariance treatment.

The allocations include 0.25 s for terminal time, 0.50 kg for source residual, 0.002 MPa for receiver peak, 0.003 MPa for candidate margin, 0.02 MN for route and pad load margins, 0.03 MN-m for moment, small ratio allocations for mass and holder metrics, one kilogram for uncontrolled loss, five microseconds for clock skew and approximately 4.383 kg for expanded mass discrepancy. These are method-design allocations, not claims that instruments have achieved them.

### 11.3 Decision at the uncertainty boundary

A decision variable is compared with its bound using the accepted uncertainty treatment specified by the method. A nominal value narrowly inside a limit is not automatically a pass. If the decision interval crosses the limit, the method must specify whether the result is fail, indeterminate or requires another prospectively authorized observation. The program cannot discard uncertainty because the point estimate is attractive.

## 12. Validity before outcome

### 12.1 Ten validity rules

The validity system requires registered configuration, source identity, initial-state realization, challenge realization, measurement sufficiency, time integrity, safety-envelope integrity, mass closure, custody continuity and independent adjudication.

The critical distinction is `VT-07`: a candidate may fail while the public rig remains inside its envelope or enters a measured safe state. That can be a valid and highly valuable event. In contrast, missing decisive evidence can make an apparently benign event scientifically invalid.

### 12.2 Twelve outcome classes

After validity, the outcome board may assign clean success, degraded success, safe refusal, recoverable shortfall, unsafe excursion, public-rig intervention, terminal-time failure or reset-time failure. Evidence-limited, method-limited, site-limited and indeterminate-causal-chain classes describe invalid attempts, not poor candidate performance.

This ordering prevents two institutional errors. First, it prevents an adverse candidate result from being relabeled invalid merely because it is inconvenient. Second, it prevents an evidence-poor result from being credited as success merely because nothing visibly dramatic occurred.

## 13. Recovery, entry and evidence

### 13.1 Eight ordered recovery states

Recovery progresses through source isolation, candidate isolation, inventory capture, atmosphere classification, thermal stabilization, limited-entry authorization, mass/evidence closure and serialized-root restoration. The first three remain remote-only. Instrumented survey precedes entry. Two-person protected entry occurs only after independent acceptance of the route and atmosphere. Controlled work begins only after mass and evidence close.

The final state restores configuration, calibration and damage disposition. Candidate-specific 72-, 96- and 84-hour reset ceilings remain outcome attributes; they do not override safe entry.

### 13.2 Eight-object evidence packet

The immutable packet contains frozen configuration; source and samples; initial and challenge state; two native observation originals; command and intervention history; recovery and mass closure; the causal estimator with exclusions and sensitivity record; and independent validity/outcome decisions including minority opinion and reserve action.

The sufficiency test is institutional: an outside-site board should be able to reconstruct what happened without vendor narration or origin-site services.

## 14. Amendment and anti-erasure law

### 14.1 Twelve change classes

The amendment ledger distinguishes pre-freeze correction, post-freeze clerical defect, safety-critical discovery, source-lot substitution, tolerance change, sensor replacement, estimator-software change, missing-data-rule change, candidate change between paired events, site-procedure change, reserve assignment and outcome correction.

The record treatment is as important as approval. Old versions remain. A new source lot requires a new event identity. A tolerance change after registration creates a new protocol version and pair. A sensor replacement requires a new calibration object. An estimator change after the first event reprocesses all affected events and retains both views. A missing-data change is prospective unless the entire affected campaign is re-adjudicated.

### 14.2 Worked invalid-attempt replacement

The training sequence freezes `RHE-E001`, observes a synthetic maximum skew of 7.2 microseconds, fails `VT-06`, preserves all mechanical observations and both native originals, withholds candidate outcome and convenes the validity board. If authorized, `RHE-R001` is assigned once and the replacement receives new identity `RHM-X001`. The original, cause, corrective action and replacement remain linked.

The example proves three policies. Scientific invalidity does not erase observation. Replacement does not reuse an identity. Reserve consumption does not create two campaign credits. Because the values are synthetic, the example itself creates zero credit and leaves every physical gate open.

## 15. Institution, schedule and industrial purpose

### 15.1 Thirty-month sequence

Months 1–3 freeze method and estimator governance. Months 2–6 realize calibration chains. Sampling and custody qualify in months 3–7. Command and abort integration runs in months 5–9. Empty, hydro and dry methods qualify in months 7–12; surrogate and cold methods in months 10–15; quarter source in months 13–20; full source in months 18–25; destructive, recovery and reconstruction in months 22–28. Months 29–30 are reserved for outside review and the worked-root release packet.

Overlap is allowed only where authorities and physical articles are independent. A schedule bar does not grant permission to advance source.

### 15.2 The 286-position establishment

The institution uses 42 method engineers, 38 metrology and calibration specialists, 32 source and analytical scientists, 48 data and causal analysts, 44 test operators, 36 safety and recovery specialists, 22 configuration/evidence custodians, 14 independent adjudicators and 10 training/site-support positions.

This is not clerical overhead on a rig program. It is the public intellectual and operating capital that makes hundreds of hazardous events comparable across builders, sites and decades. A mature Department of Resilience would retain these competencies as a standing method corps, not disband them after acquisition.

### 15.3 Technology acceleration

The constitution is intentionally compatible with more ambitious tools: distributed traceable time, self-describing instruments, tamper-evident native records, automated configuration recognition, machine-assisted anomaly discovery, physically informed state estimation, robotic post-event survey and isolated cross-site replay. The public requirement is not that every technology already exist at fleet scale. It is that new technology enter through auditable interfaces and never make the scientific decision irrecoverably proprietary.

In this sense, the method institution is itself an industrial policy. It creates demand for dynamic metrology, hazardous-process robotics, evidence-grade timing, source analytics, public scientific compute, recovery instrumentation and independently verifiable control systems—the resilience equivalents of test ranges, wind tunnels and qualification laboratories.
