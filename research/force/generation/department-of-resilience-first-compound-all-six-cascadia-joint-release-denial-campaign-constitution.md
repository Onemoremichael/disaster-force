## Decision: Cascadia goes first because it already breaks the force

The Department will use the `C3` Cascadia earthquake-and-tsunami campaign as the first compound all-six demonstrator. It is not selected because earthquakes are more important than fire, flood, heat or international response. It is selected because the repository already contains two incompatible truths that the new grammar must reconcile.

Pass 25 makes an item-level deterministic architecture close every declared wave and service test. Pass 26 admits common dependencies and reduces the same reference to 5.8 percent campaign closure; even its cellular-mesh challenger reaches only 84.2 percent against a 95-percent target. The first experiment should begin where the strongest current architecture is known to fail, not where assumptions can manufacture success.

Eight noncompensable gates screen all ten national campaigns. The first campaign must activate all twenty-eight service routes; begin without useful warning; contain registered disconnected civil service islands; repeatedly invalidate physical releases after entry; include protected patient, workforce and hazardous reverse flow; carry the force through repair and recertification; have both deterministic and covariance predecessors; and avoid confounding the first grammar test with invited-host translation. Only `C3` passes all eight. `I1`, the allied coastal-megacity earthquake, preserves the no-notice and projection challenge but lacks an equally developed host service-island compiler and introduces invitation, language, standards and sovereignty translation before the domestic grammar has survived once. It remains the necessary second translation case.

## The test object

`C3-JRC-1` preserves the inherited physical boundary: nine civil service islands, eight safe-zone or afloat origin complexes, twelve paths in eleven correlation groups, sixty-four controlled load records, 1.76 million tonnes of formation flow, a 22,500-tonne/day sustainment peak and 362 concurrent formation equivalents. All twenty-eight Pass 172 routes are active. Their system allocation is 62 theater-entry, 48 civil-terrain, 24 hazard-control, 38 lifeline, 128 human-continuity and 62 regeneration formations.

The campaign adds no platform. It changes the unit of observation. Each execution contains ten all-six mission threads: one regional allocation thread and one for each of the nine service islands. Each thread is observed at days 3, 14, 45, 90, 180 and 260. The first four epochs retain the Pass 25 public-service milestones; day 180 tests field-mission termination and withdrawal; day 260 tests the complete Pass 167 repair-and-recertification occupancy boundary.

Every thread–epoch cell contains the complete Pass 172 object: forty-two system-stage judgments and thirty directed exchange records, each resolving six domains and twenty fields. A regional average cannot compensate for an island failure. An equipment arrival cannot compensate for an unaccepted service. A day-90 effect cannot compensate for an ownerless day-180 residual.

## Twelve denials, not one polished demonstration

Each execution receives the same twelve sealed denials, with within-epoch order rotated across stacks and sites:

1. loss of the regional network and any central broker for seventy-two hours;
2. conflicting civil authorities over priority, access or protected people;
3. an aftershock revoking a previously released route and hazard envelope;
4. expiry of utility, receiver or pathway evidence while dependent work continues;
5. a lifeline–human-continuity circular release around clinical power and declared load;
6. an entry–regeneration circular release around route access, fuel and repair;
7. divergent configuration histories merging after offline operation;
8. withdrawal of a patient, household or facility destination’s acceptance;
9. refusal of contaminated waste or damaged-energy custody at the return destination;
10. delayed patient and workforce relief that makes intact machinery unusable;
11. one concealed supplier or energy ancestry shared by nominally unlike systems; and
12. an unresolved repair backlog when another national campaign claims reserve.

`PROVISIONAL` data may plan but never release dependent credit. `EXPIRED` or `REVOKED` records must remove that credit and drive the affected system backward. The two circular cases use the Pass 172 joint-release barrier: both sides prepare; both independent authorities validate; both commit together or neither commits. Loss of the network is not itself failure. Inability to verify locally, operate manually, reconcile without double acceptance or reconstruct the decision afterward is failure.

## Reproduction and evidence scale

Four rotating all-six stacks cross three independent range classes: coastal/littoral, metropolitan/valley and interior/safe-zone. This creates twelve complete campaign executions. Every execution contains sixty thread–epoch cells, 2,520 stage judgments, 1,800 exchange records, 10,800 domain judgments and 13,320 governed decisions. It also instantiates 36,000 mandatory record fields.

The complete campaign therefore contains 144 sealed denial exposures, 30,240 stage judgments, 21,600 exchange records, 129,600 domain judgments, 159,840 governed decisions and 432,000 field instances. Root combinations and denial order rotate so a permanent integration team, site or event sequence cannot masquerade as a system result.

Twelve hard gates govern acceptance. Among them: all twenty-eight routes must act; all sixty state vectors per execution must reconcile; each of the thirty directions must be challenged and recovered; revocation must erase dependent credit; no joint barrier may partially commit; disconnected decisions must merge without duplicate acceptance; all nine islands must close separately; every residual must have an accepted owner; and an independent authority must reproduce the credit history. One failure denies the campaign. Success qualifies only the tested configurations for a `G4` judgment; it creates no fleet-wide readiness.

## Financial boundary

The campaign does not add the inherited physical screens. Pass 25’s USD 373B regional posture, Pass 26’s USD 228B cellular-assurance increment and USD 34.2B annual operating hypothesis describe nested force capacity, not this experiment’s appropriation.

Within the Pass 171 Wave-3 envelope, Cascadia carries 94,100 formation-days, 19.8 percent of the ten-campaign total. Applying that burden share reserves USD 49.5B of the conserved USD 250B `G4` wave: USD 27.3B mission-system work, USD 7.43B service technical bases, USD 7.43B common capacity, USD 4.95B sovereign evidence and USD 2.48B failure reserve. This is a transparent planning rule, not a cost estimate. The workbook keeps it separate from physical-force capital and exposes every source quantity.

`C3-JRC-1` closes selection and constitution of the first compound campaign. It neither repeats Pass 25’s deterministic closure nor promotes Pass 26’s 84.2-percent challenger. It turns both into adversarial starting conditions.
