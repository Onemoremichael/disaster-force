# Department of Resilience Acquisition and Industrial Mobilization Command

## Executive judgment

A coequal Resilience Force cannot be acquired by enlarging the federal emergency market. The desired systems—autonomous civil utilities, high-rate fire aircraft, shallow-water response carriers, distributed medical production, robotic construction fleets, rapid utility corridors, nuclear water-energy plants and evidence systems able to judge them—are too integrated, too capital intensive and too far outside ordinary demand to emerge from episodic grants or conversions of equipment designed for another purpose. The Department of Resilience requires a permanent institution whose product is not purchasing. Its product is **technical possibility made reproducible at national scale**.

This pass defines that institution as the **Acquisition and Industrial Mobilization Command**, or AIMC. It is the Department's sovereign builder, industrial mobilizer and public technical commons. At mature scale it manages the acquisition architecture for all forty-eight force families inside the established USD 15.179 trillion, twenty-five-year force-capital boundary. It sustains 192 concurrent major programs, 576 live technical roots, sixteen primary integrator ecosystems, 4,256 certified industrial nodes, 136 public design, production, test and custody facilities, 542,000 direct personnel and 3.24 million industrial affiliates. Its twenty-five-year enterprise investment is USD 5.25 trillion, nested inside rather than added to force capital. USD 9.929 trillion remains for force production and fielding.

Those quantities are deliberately commensurate with the premise. A department asked to create a new instrument of national power cannot depend on a small acquisition secretariat negotiating with the industrial base that happens to exist. It must create and continuously exercise a new industrial base. It must possess enough internal engineering competence to recognize impossible promises, preserve alternatives, destroy articles, reproduce abandoned designs, start new process lines and replace a failed supplier without replacing the public mission.

The scale creates a constitutional danger. A builder this powerful could convert institutional enthusiasm into self-certified success. AIMC is therefore strong in means and limited in judgment. It may design, compete, prototype, manufacture, reserve capacity and mobilize. It may not redefine the civil guarantee, grade its own evidence, determine operational suitability by itself, conceal opportunity cost or unilaterally release a design into production. Requirements, construction, operation, evidence, cost and serial release remain separable authorities.

That separation is the central finding of this pass. Technological ambition and public discipline are not opposing values. Independent judgment is what permits the Department to pursue reactors, autonomous heavy equipment, novel aircraft, biological production and other high-consequence systems without turning schedule pressure into permission to waive safety or exaggerate readiness.

No platform is selected here. No existing firm, facility or workforce is treated as capable merely because the model contains a line for it. All evidence records remain open, all acquisition and production decisions remain held and rendering authority remains zero.

## 1. The institutional product

Conventional procurement treats acquisition as the transaction between a requirement and a delivered item. That frame is too narrow for a clean-sheet resilience economy. The Department needs to create several things at once:

1. a technical architecture capable of delivering a defined public effect;
2. rival mechanisms whose failures are meaningfully different;
3. instruments and trials capable of distinguishing those failures;
4. production processes that can repeat the accepted behavior;
5. workforce, material, tooling and facilities that can expand under compound demand;
6. legal rights and technical data sufficient to replace a provider; and
7. receiving interfaces that let the resulting capability become ordinary public service.

The acquisition object is therefore a **falsifiable learning path with a reproducible production path**. A platform is one possible result. It is not the starting unit.

This distinction changes the meaning of success. A brilliant prototype that only its original team can operate is not mature capability. A production line whose decisive material comes from one hidden process is not mobilizable. A hospital carrier that cannot enter local referral, utility, workforce and discharge systems is not health force projection. A desalination plant without residual disposal, water-quality release, conveyance and distribution is not a water service. AIMC must govern the complete path from mission effect to repeatable service rather than reward the visible machine at its center.

The command has five sovereign functions:

- translate mission requirements into inspectable technical boundaries without turning them into preferred forms;
- preserve materially unlike technical roots below the prime contractor;
- create public standards, metrology, facilities and destructive-evidence capacity;
- hold exercised industrial options across people, tools, materials, processes and geography; and
- retain the data and legal rights needed to reproduce, repair, modify or replace every decisive root.

The fifth function is especially important. Supplier plurality without reproduction power can be cosmetic. Two integrators may depend on the same controller, membrane, specialty alloy, firmware, calibration source, test facility or beneficial owner. AIMC must understand technical ancestry below corporate labels and preserve an alternate path at the level where common failure actually occurs.

## 2. Powerful means, divided judgment

The command's authority is divided among seven institutions.

| Authority | Sovereign responsibility | Prohibited combination |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Civil guarantee authority | define the population effect, distributional floor, rights and lawful exceptions | selecting a platform or relaxing a test for program convenience |
| Resilience Staff | balance the forty-eight family requirements and campaign priorities | accepting technical evidence or awarding a source |
| AIMC | conduct competitions, build articles, operate public technical infrastructure and mobilize industry | grading its own output or redefining mission success |
| Independent Acquisition Evidence Authority | own test plans, metrology, raw evidence, custody and acceptance findings | managing the builder or granting requirement relief |
| Operating services and jurisdictions | determine field suitability, receiving burden, training and consent | substituting operator preference for technical acceptance |
| Independent cost authority | expose cost, schedule, concentration and opportunity cost | setting mission priority or accepting performance |
| Production Release Board | authorize a bounded serial configuration after all findings | conducting the test or managing the program it releases |

Each authority owns a different kind of truth. The civil authority owns what must become true for people. The requirements system owns how that obligation is allocated across the force. AIMC owns the act of creating technical and industrial options. The evidence authority owns whether a tested claim survived. Operators own whether accepted equipment can be used safely in a real jurisdiction. Cost authority owns the price of choosing this path instead of another. The release board owns the final, configuration-specific permission to reproduce.

No authority can turn its partial truth into total authority. A technically successful system can still fail operational suitability. A locally popular system can still fail containment or evidence. A low-cost design can still be non-reproducible. A strategically important mission can still require termination of a physically failed mechanism.

The institutional split also makes failure usable. A program can be stopped without erasing its test record, rights, facilities, workforce or alternate roots. AIMC is not judged by whether every program survives. It is judged by whether the portfolio learns fast enough, preserves public options and converts the surviving paths into reproducible capacity.

## 3. A continuous portfolio

The mature force contains forty-eight capability families across nine effect portfolios: civil coordination; food, shelter and essential goods; health; energy and thermal continuity; communications and computation; mobility and access; water and sanitation; hazard control; and housing, public works and economic continuity.

Each family carries four overlapping major increments. The result is 192 active programs. A twelve-year lifecycle produces sixteen new starts in each steady-state cohort. The purpose is not perpetual novelty. It is to prevent a national force from becoming one synchronized technical bet.

One increment may be in mission and architecture competition, a second in representative prototype campaigns, a third in initial production and operational learning, and a fourth in mature production or replacement. Their coexistence lets evidence from fielded systems change the next design while preserving continuity of service. It also prevents an incumbent from arguing that replacement is too disruptive because every family is designed around managed overlap.

Every program begins with three roots:

- **Build root A** receives 45 percent of technical attention;
- **Build root B** receives 45 percent; and
- **Challenge root C** receives 10 percent of attention and protected access to a decisive experiment.

The attention shares describe governance, not a universal cash split. Capital intensity differs radically between reactor experiments, biological process lines, flight articles, pipeline systems and software-heavy public interfaces. The model therefore funds USD 0.80 trillion for rival prototyping and independent replication and USD 0.75 trillion for science and challenge portfolios as separate workstreams.

The two build roots must differ below the integrator in decisive physics, process, facility, software, ownership and calibration ancestry. The challenge root is not an inferior third bidder. It exists to attack a registered boundary that the build roots accept: a new separation method, energy conversion system, structural process, flight mechanism, autonomy architecture, biological production method or measurement root. It receives no waiver and no promised follow-on. Its institutional privilege is the right to reach a fair physical experiment before convention excludes it.

The portfolio therefore supports 384 build roots and 192 challenge roots at once. Sixteen primary integrator ecosystems may each hold no more than twenty-four live build roots in the baseline. That ceiling prevents one firm from becoming the Department's de facto systems architecture. It does not prove diversity by itself; the command still traces sub-tier and process ancestry.

## 4. Mission-led technological advance

The Department should use persistent public failure the way a military establishment uses contested mission needs: as a driver of long-horizon invention. AIMC's Science and Challenge Agency organizes twelve frontiers around capabilities the civil guarantee requires but current technology may not provide at the necessary scale, speed, burden or safety.

The frontiers cover nuclear and high-density energy conversion; water and chemical separations; atmospheric and aerospace access; maritime, riverine and amphibious systems; terrain robotics and autonomous construction; medical and biological production; thermal, fire and environmental control; advanced materials and structures; autonomy, sensing and communications; food, shelter and deployable manufacturing; public interfaces and civil safety; and metrology, time, data and custody.

Each frontier sustains eight mission-failure programs and two cross-frontier grand tests: ninety-six focused programs and twenty-four system campaigns. The USD 0.75 trillion challenge portfolio averages USD 62.5 billion per frontier over twenty-five years, but execution need not be equal. A frontier earns continued capital by changing a decisive mission boundary and surviving a whole service-chain test.

This design is more ambitious than waiting for commercial technology. It can commission passively safe mobile reactors whose output is heat, electricity and water-service potential rather than propulsion; new membranes, sorbents, electrochemical cycles and residual systems for changing water sources; aircraft optimized around civil payload transfer and mixed-airspace safety; autonomous earthmoving machines for fire, contamination, collapse and aftershock zones; distributed sterile manufacturing for oxygen, blood products, diagnostics, pharmaceuticals and biologics; rapid joining and inspection for field pipelines and bridges; offline public command under network loss; and portable public metrology able to prove service without becoming hidden mission support.

Ambition remains bounded by consequence. A radical system may change a mass, energy, throughput, time, labor or access coefficient. It may not waive bystander safety, residual disposition, evidence independence, receiving burden, local refusal, failure visibility or configuration control. “Breakthrough” is a hypothesis until representative articles and independent measurement close those conditions.

Grand tests prevent component achievement from hiding system failure. A new desalination process must enter intake, power, heat rejection, concentrate control, water release, pipeline, storage and public distribution. A wildfire aircraft must enter sensing, mixed-airspace command, turnaround, suppressant logistics, maintenance and ground-force coordination. An autonomous construction fleet must expose site survey, exclusion, soil interaction, task planning, recovery, public-works acceptance and human override. AIMC funds both the technology and the machinery required to falsify its relevance.

## 5. The public technical commons

At this scale the government cannot remain merely an intelligent customer. It must remain a competent designer, measurer and reproducer. AIMC therefore contains twelve sovereign design arsenals, twenty-four pilot-production campuses, thirty-two system proving ranges, twelve destructive and forensic campuses, eight strategic material reserves, six public test foundries, sixteen regional integration arsenals, four high-hazard energy and nuclear sites, twelve data and custody centers and ten training campuses.

The network totals 136 nodes and USD 2.3 trillion in twenty-five-year capital. That amount is not additional to the USD 5.25 trillion enterprise budget. It is the physical topology funded across the USD 1.35 trillion sovereign design-and-test workstream and the USD 0.95 trillion pilot-production-and-foundry workstream.

These sites perform four roles that ordinary contracting cannot reliably supply.

First, they preserve **design intelligence**. Eighty-four thousand direct personnel in sovereign design arsenals maintain system models, interface knowledge, alternate processes and second-team build competence. Their purpose is not to displace industry. It is to ensure that the state understands what it is buying and can reopen competition after technical or corporate failure.

Second, they preserve **comparable evidence**. Public standards and metrology make results portable between firms and over time. Traveling calorimeters, flow and pressure references, mass-property standards, structural load fields, chemical and biological assay references, denied-time standards, software challenges and custody fixtures prevent an incumbent's proprietary test system from becoming the definition of success.

Third, they preserve **destructive truth**. Some of the most important information appears only when an article is burned, overloaded, contaminated, dismantled or reproduced by a team that did not design it. Independent forensic campuses and protected article arsenals make that work a planned public function rather than a concession negotiated after failure.

Fourth, they preserve **industrial entry**. Pilot lines and public foundries let new technical teams demonstrate processes without first financing an entire production estate. The government can qualify alternates, reproduce a discontinued component and separate a good design from a weak incumbent process.

Public facilities operate as evidence common carriers. Qualified teams receive access under published methods, capacity rules, custody controls and conflict separation. Geographic and process diversity matter as much as node count. One storm, grid region, transport corridor, ownership group or specialized process must not be able to stop every decisive trial or reproduction path.

## 6. Rights are force structure

Every one of the 576 technical roots carries eight required rights or disclosures: complete bill of materials; process and facility identity; firmware source and software bill of materials; ownership and beneficial-control map; calibration genealogy; destructive teardown right; second-team reproduction right; and perpetual interface and evidence-data right. The companion ledger contains 4,608 records.

These rights are not a documentation appendix. They are the legal layer of industrial maneuver. Without them the Department cannot determine whether two roots share a process, ask an independent team to reproduce a failure, move production after an ownership change, repair a system after a provider exits, or preserve interoperability across increments.

The standard is practical: can a qualified second team reconstruct the decision and reproduce the bounded capability without the original provider's discretionary assistance? Controlled repositories, limited-purpose licenses, escrow and protected access can preserve legitimate secrecy. A promise to negotiate after failure does not satisfy the standard.

Rights attach below the prime and follow serial and process changes. A prime's broad data license is insufficient if the decisive membrane coating, reactor component, control law, calibration chain or joining process remains unreachable. Material changes in facility, firmware, ownership, process or serial configuration reopen the rights and independence finding.

## 7. Industrial mobilization as exercised topology

The mature industrial network contains 4,256 certified nodes and 3.24 million affiliated workers:

| Node class | Nodes | Affiliated workforce |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Primary system-integrator ecosystems | 16 | 320,000 |
| Independent technical-root firms | 96 | 480,000 |
| Subsystem and process firms | 480 | 960,000 |
| Certified production nodes | 2,400 | 960,000 |
| Regional service suppliers | 1,200 | 360,000 |
| Allied lead firms | 64 | 160,000 |
| **Total** | **4,256** | **3,240,000** |

The topology separates integration, decisive technical roots, subsystem knowledge, repeat production, field service and allied capacity. It is not a forecast of company size. It is a force-design claim about how many independent places, skills and ownership centers must exist if compound disasters are not to collapse into a queue behind a few national primes.

AIMC buys readiness through six contract states. D0 design retainers require current product definitions, process plans, alternate sources and restart labor within 180 days. W1 warm-engineering contracts reduce activation to 90 days with exercised teams and fixtures. W2 pilot capacity produces accepted output within 45 days. S1 surge-material contracts reserve decisive feedstocks, tools and logistics for 30-day expansion. H1 hot reserve maintains rotating output and expansion crews with a seven-day response. X1 pays for no-notice activation exercises.

The readiness portfolio allocates USD 60 billion to design retainers, USD 120 billion to warm engineering, USD 180 billion to pilot capacity, USD 120 billion to surge materials, USD 90 billion to hot reserve and USD 30 billion to activation exercises. The USD 600 billion total reconciles to the industrial-mobilization workstream.

These contracts do not purchase a certificate. They purchase measured option value: named labor, maintained tooling, qualified alternates, current process knowledge, rotating materials, acceptance instruments, transport plans and a demonstrated receiving path. Payment depends on restart time, conforming output, recovery from injected loss and delivery into an exercise. A readiness assertion without physical activation earns zero credit.

The model tests six production-equivalent scenarios. They range from a 0.60 steady-demand index against 1.00 available capacity to a 2.40 compound-demand index with 35 percent of industrial domains lost and 0.95 recovery capacity activated. All retain positive planning margin; the narrowest is 0.045. These are hypotheses, not production claims. Their value is to specify what exercises must measure. Each family will eventually require real units, rates, yields, transport, acceptance and receiving throughput.

Mobilization is thus a continuous behavior of the industrial base. It cannot be stored as a plan and discovered during a national emergency.

## 8. Workforce: an engineering state

AIMC's direct workforce is 542,000. The largest elements are 128,000 in system acquisition commands, 84,000 in sovereign design arsenals, 66,000 in test and destructive evidence, 44,000 in public standards and metrology, 42,000 in mission requirements and architecture, and 38,000 in the Science and Challenge Agency. Industrial mobilization, technical-data custody, education, allied production, cost analysis and headquarters complete the structure.

The number is large because the institution performs work that the present state often delegates and later struggles to judge. It must retain people capable of system architecture, reactor and process engineering, airworthiness, naval architecture, construction robotics, civil utilities, clinical systems, manufacturing, materials, metrology, software assurance, economics, logistics, field maintenance and public-service integration.

Career design matters as much as headcount. Engineers rotate through arsenals, proving ranges, production nodes and field campaigns. Advancement depends on accepted physical work, useful failure disclosure and successful reproduction—not only program continuity. Operators spend time in design teams; designers observe receiving burden; cost analysts see production; industrial mobilizers execute no-notice activations. The acquisition corps becomes a profession of public technical judgment rather than contract administration.

The direct workforce is nested inside the established 10.484 million affiliated-force boundary. The model does not add it on top of the canonical force. Future reconciliation must assign which existing acquisition, proving, depot, research, industrial and educational positions migrate into AIMC and which represent genuinely new capacity.

## 9. Financial architecture

The twenty-five-year AIMC enterprise investment is USD 5.25 trillion:

| Workstream | USD trillions |
| --- | ---: |
| Sovereign design and test infrastructure | 1.35 |
| Pilot production and public foundries | 0.95 |
| Science and challenge portfolios | 0.75 |
| Rival prototyping and independent replication | 0.80 |
| Industrial mobilization and material reserves | 0.60 |
| Standards, data rights and configuration | 0.25 |
| Workforce and schools | 0.15 |
| Allied production and export certification | 0.10 |
| Program reserve | 0.30 |
| **Total** | **5.25** |

The budget is intentionally not a percentage surcharge applied after buying platforms. It is the part of force capital that creates the ability to discover, judge, reproduce and mobilize those platforms. Treating it as overhead would recreate the institutional weakness this project is trying to avoid.

The model phases 50 percent of enterprise investment through the first decade, 22.5 percent in years eleven through fifteen, 17.5 percent in years sixteen through twenty and 10 percent in the final five years. The earlier share builds the public technical commons before the force reaches full production. Mature AIMC operations reach USD 310 billion per year after a ten-year ramp. Operations are distinct from the capital envelope and require later reconciliation with the canonical USD 1.736 trillion mature annual force-operating boundary.

These values are allocations, not forecasts. They have no vendor price base, site estimate, learning curve, inflation series or observed productivity. Their purpose is to expose institutional order of magnitude and internal opportunity cost. USD 5.25 trillion devoted to acquisition infrastructure leaves USD 9.929 trillion for production and fielding. If that remainder cannot produce the forty-eight-family force, the Department must change its force mix, lifecycle, assurance level or total premise explicitly.

The USD 300 billion program reserve is equally important. A portfolio designed around frontier technology should expect programs to fail. Reserve allows the state to commission a new root, add a pilot line, recover an industrial domain or repeat a destructive campaign without converting every surprise into emergency waiver pressure.

## 10. Allied production and strategic export

The mature command treats export as the extension of a resilience production network, not the sale of excess inventory. The desired product is a locally operable service family with common interfaces, independent evidence, training, spares, technical rights and a lawful receiving model.

Sixty-four allied lead firms provide potential parallel roots. They count as independent only when ownership, process, material, software, calibration and geography survive ancestry review. A foreign flag does not remove common-mode dependency. Conversely, an allied process that is genuinely independent can be more valuable than a nominally domestic second prime using the same decisive chain.

Export packages should favor licensed local manufacture, public standards, repairability and joint proving. A partner that can build and maintain water, power, medical, hazard-control and mobility systems becomes more capable and less dependent after a campaign. That is a different kind of power projection from maintaining permanent reliance on United States crews or proprietary services.

The Department still needs bounded export controls. High-density energy, biological production, autonomy, sensing and dual-use manufacturing may create serious misuse risks. Export safety authority must determine which technical data, fuel cycles, software permissions and custody systems can be transferred without turning a civil capability into coercive or unsafe power. Those constraints belong in the original architecture, not in a licensing review after the system is complete.

## 11. What AIMC is not

AIMC is not a renamed purchasing office, a national prime contractor or an empire of government factories. It does not preserve every present agency and add a coordinating layer. It reorganizes the acquisition problem around five public capabilities: requirements discipline, technical plurality, independent evidence, industrial mobilization and lawful reproduction.

Industry remains essential. Large integrators assemble unprecedented systems; specialist firms own deep technical roots; production nodes create geographic and process diversity; regional suppliers maintain the fielded force; allies add independent capacity. The public sector owns the interfaces and facilities that keep this ecology contestable.

Nor is AIMC a technology-acceleration program detached from operations. Research begins with a blocked civil effect. It ends only when accepted behavior can be produced, received, maintained and recovered. Scientific novelty that cannot survive the service chain remains useful knowledge but does not become force capability.

Finally, AIMC is not evidence that the mature design is politically or administratively feasible. The project assumes a green light in order to ask what technical state capacity the mission would require. Questions of transition, federalism, labor markets, statutory design and political coalition remain future research objects. They should not be used to shrink the first-principles answer before it is understood.

## 12. Twenty-four gates

The command's architecture is governed by twenty-four stop rules. They cover constitutional authority separation; effect-first requirements; portfolio completeness; two build roots; a protected challenge lane; root independence; technical rights; public standards and facilities; pilot lines; article custody; artifact-based payment; independent cost; workforce and materials; concentration; allied sources; export safety; data continuity; mobilization exercises; representative acceptance; serial release; public accountability; and form authority.

Every gate remains open and every decision remains held. No arithmetic in the workbook closes a physical or legal condition. This matters especially for mobilization. A nonnegative capacity index is a hypothesis that organizes an activation exercise. It is not proof that a line can restart, a workforce will report, a component will yield, a carrier will arrive or a jurisdiction can receive the output.

The final gate preserves the rendering boundary. AIMC authorizes no interior or exterior form before measured sections, mass properties, energy and heat paths, structural and contact behavior, human access, maintenance, production constraints and representative coupled performance exist. The present family names remain functional development lines, not visual designs.

## 13. What this pass changes

MESA-1E established a source-selection experiment for eleven evidence-system families. It showed how two build roots, one challenge root, public standards, root-level rights, serialized lots and artifact payments could preserve competition through the first award. That pattern was still program-sized.

This pass scales the pattern into a mature state institution. It makes the following propositions explicit:

- acquisition and industrial mobilization are part of force structure, not support overhead;
- forty-eight families require 192 overlapping programs rather than one generation of platforms;
- 576 technical roots must remain legible below primes;
- a 136-node public facility estate is required to preserve design, evidence and reproduction sovereignty;
- industrial readiness requires paid and exercised options across 4,256 nodes;
- a direct 542,000-person technical corps is proportionate to the mission;
- frontier research is embedded in every capability family rather than isolated in a small laboratory; and
- USD 5.25 trillion of the force-capital envelope is the machinery that creates and renews the force.

The pass also narrows authority. The Department may now reason about a mature acquisition command, but it may not infer that the named facilities exist, the labor market closes, the industrial network can be assembled, the financial envelope is sufficient or any frontier technology works.

## 14. Model integrity and next research boundary

The companion [acquisition and industrial mobilization command workbook](../../../models/force/generation/department-resilience-acquisition-and-industrial-mobilization-command.xlsx) contains eighteen sheets. It registers forty-eight families, 192 major programs, 576 technical roots, 4,608 rights records, twelve technology frontiers, twelve command elements, ten public facility classes, six industrial node classes, six readiness contracts, nine investment workstreams, twenty-five annual phases, six mobilization stress cases and twenty-four gates.

All 1,839 deterministic controls pass in the restored baseline. The formula-error scan matches zero cells; the Open XML container validates; and every sheet has been rendered and visually inspected. Increasing concurrent increments from four to five without expanding the program, root, start and rights registers produces six expected audit failures before restoration. The sensitivity proves that the core counts are linked rather than decorative.

The next research object should be the **common AIMC program-office operating system**. Department-wide scale is not credible until every program uses one inspectable architecture for requirement custody, root ancestry, earned physical artifacts, serial configuration, technical data, capacity contracts, cost uncertainty, test admission, failure replacement and production release. That pass should define the complete program data package and the transactions between the seven separated authorities.

A following pass should design the national industrial-mobilization exercise series: which force families activate, which industrial domains are removed, how material and workforce calls are made, how conforming output is measured, how transport and receiving enter the clock, and how failed capacity loses payment or readiness credit.

Vehicle and system renderings remain deferred. The command now explains how the Department would create the evidence needed to draw responsibly; it does not yet provide that evidence.
