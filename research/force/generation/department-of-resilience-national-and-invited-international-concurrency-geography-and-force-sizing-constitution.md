# Pass 167 — National and invited-international concurrency, geography and force-sizing constitution

**Research object:** `NIFS-1`  
**Decision level:** mature national force promise  
**Verdict:** `CLOSE`

## Branch and resolution

The deepest live branch is the returned parent chain `RCC-1R → JMSA-1 → NIFS-1`, now in its third pass; no formation-detail successor remains active.

**Resolution test:** Changing the national concurrency and geographic-denial case changes every service inventory, readiness district, international commitment, workforce and capital boundary, so it directly changes the mature-force architecture.

## Decision

The provisional `3C + 5M + 2G + 20R` rule is replaced by **`3C + 5M + 2I + R20 / N−1`**:

- three catastrophic domestic campaigns, five major domestic campaigns and two invited international campaigns are simultaneous allocation claims;
- a protected reserve equal to 20 percent of concurrent demand remains available after the loss of one domestic readiness district;
- seven domestic readiness districts and two international reach networks generate the force;
- territories are domestic obligations, not international missions;
- international capacity is optional before sovereign invitation and U.S. acceptance, but becomes non-recallable unless both authorities release it; and
- partner, host-nation and military capacity receive no planning credit.

This is a stress constitution, not a probability forecast. It asks what the Department promises to survive, not how often ten disasters will coincide. The ten inherited design environments remain planning hypotheses until empirical hazard, duration and arrival distributions can recalibrate them.[^1]

## The force-sizing law

The [canonical allocation](canonical-force-allocation-and-fiscal-reconciliation.md) supplies 48 heterogeneous formation families and 2,420 simultaneous formation-equivalent commitments across the ten campaigns.[^2] Those quantities are not additive measures of effect: a medical group cannot substitute for a route-opening group simply because both count as one.

For each family, `NIFS-1` selects the larger of its invited-mission floor and:

> `ceil[(concurrent demand × 1.20) / (bottleneck readiness × (1 − district concentration cap))]`

The reference concentration cap is 20 percent. Thus a district may disappear while the remaining ready inventory still covers all ten claims **and** the protected reserve. This is stricter than adding a spare base: it constrains the geographic distribution of every family, including scarce clinical, maritime, utility and mobility lines.

The result is **5,251 owned formation equivalents**, 1,042 more than the 4,209 no-geographic-loss predecessor. Every one of the 48 family tests passes. Seven service summaries reconcile exactly, as does the Joint layer. The increase is real crews, maintenance, stock, schools and recertification capacity; it cannot be created by relabeling common assets.

## Campaign composition and time

The three catastrophic domestic cases are national grid/temperature failure, sequential Gulf and Atlantic hurricanes, and Cascadia rupture. The five major cases cover western wildfire/heat/smoke, urban heat, inland flood, tornado, and territorial drought/utility failure. The invited cases are an Indo-Pacific host nation and an Atlantic or Americas host nation. Across a nine-effect completeness matrix, every campaign claims coordination, life support, health, energy, communications, mobility, water, hazard control, and housing/public works. Ordinal scores check composition; they do not multiply formation demand.

Occupancy exposes the reserve’s purpose. Preparation, transit, mission, withdrawal, quarantine, repair and recertification hold campaigns for 56–281 days. No committed campaign completes its occupied interval by day 30. A second no-notice event therefore draws on protected reserve rather than fictional recall. Accepted international formations remain outside the domestic recall ledger until jointly released.

The opening movement claim is itself strategic: 1,409 early-entry formation equivalents, 8.90 million tonnes of deployable mass and about 174,000 tonnes per day of sustainment. These inherited planning factors do not prove a transport fleet. They prove that projection routes, reception nodes and local transfer are coequal force requirements rather than logistics appendices.[^3]

## Fiscal and industrial consequence

Applying the new inventory to the canonical formation inputs produces 11.5 million direct affiliated billets and a **13.0 million** total affiliated establishment after institutional depth and common-utility labor. This is 2.56 million above the predecessor screen. Formation capital reaches $9.41 trillion. Holding $5.10 trillion of common capital once, then applying the inherited 20 percent program reserve, produces a **$17.4 trillion** 25-year capital screen—$2.23 trillion above Pass 58—and **$2.11 trillion** in mature annual operations.[^2]

These are constant-2026-dollar, undiscounted planning screens, not budget estimates. Their value is comparative: the geographic promise alone raises the capital boundary about 15 percent. The mature industrial posture is consequently distributed. Each readiness district needs arsenals, schools, maintenance, recertification and replacement access; the two reach networks need sovereign staging and host-interface kits. A single national mega-base is incompatible with the selected promise.

Pass 166’s fiscal law remains controlling: joint mission systems fund integrated civilian outcomes, services fund professional and article-family depth, common capacity is separately reserved, and no shared title reduces quantity without exclusive availability and equivalent technical evidence.[^4] `NIFS-1` therefore carries zero cross-portfolio savings.

## What this closes

`NIFS-1` closes the sole remaining parent-level sizing question. It does not authorize procurement or exterior form. It establishes the load case against which mission-system portfolios, rival technologies and complete service chains must now be selected. The next admissible work begins from public effects and denial conditions; it may not begin from a desired ship, aircraft or vehicle.
