# Household Collection Group formation design

**Status:** `R2+` unit-of-account configuration; not force-admitted

**Decision:** advance `HCG-35B`, a seven-company origin formation, as the next research baseline. Retain the Pass 58 national workforce, capital and operating headlines until every external support claim has an item identity, receiving owner, readiness state and time-phased allocation.

**Design class:** one group for an approximately 35,000-person disaster-demand envelope, divided into seven `HCC-5` Household Continuity Companies and sustained for seven days

**Companion model:** [Household Collection Group model](../../../models/force/generation/household-collection-group-model.xlsx)

## Executive judgment

The Household Collection Group is neither a small liaison team nor the entire neighborhood service system wearing a formation label. It is the deployable **origin formation** that owns the physical and lawful path from a household to one of two completed dispositions: protected local service or accepted collection into a complete civil-mobility chain.

That distinction corrects the largest unit-of-account problem in the [canonical force allocation](canonical-force-allocation-and-fiscal-reconciliation.md). Pass 58 used the conventional `NCAC-35 A2` configuration as a proxy for `CM-HCG`, credited 2,000 of its 7,587 deployed positions to shared district service, applied a uniform 3.4 crew-set factor and multiplied the result across 128 owned formations. The resulting **2.431 million direct billet-equivalents** became the largest workforce line in the proposed Department of Resilience.

The proxy was valuable because it prevented a staff-free fleet. It was not a settled organization. It combined four different kinds of capacity:

- work that must remain under the origin command because it touches households, buildings, consent, clinical status and custody;
- fixed or locally affiliated capacity that should already exist in prepared districts;
- heavy route, power and water capability generated by other services; and
- common sustainment and information capacity that may be pooled only if allocation and latency are governed.

`HCG-35B` separates those accounts without letting any service disappear. Its organic formation contains **5,076 deployed people** and a role-specific readiness establishment of **12,163 affiliated billets per owned group**. Across the existing 128-group inventory, the organic direct line is **1.557 million billet-equivalents**, about 36 percent below the Pass 58 proxy.

That is not a 36-percent national workforce saving. The complete system still requires about **2,510 external on-duty positions per active group**. At the Pass 58 simultaneous demand and reserve, this is approximately **207,858 on-duty supporting positions**. An illustrative 2.5 generation factor produces another **519,644 affiliated billet-equivalents** before institutional depth. Those occupations have not been assigned to supporting formations, prepared sites or component systems. The legitimate current national total therefore remains the Pass 58 headline, not the organic-only lower screen.

The physical result is even more instructive. The mobile HCG falls from **6,388 to 1,830 packed tonnes**, a 71-percent reduction. Yet the complete assigned system becomes **6,414 packed tonnes**, slightly above the inherited proxy, because prepared districts, route detachments, power blocks, water islands and supporting stocks still exist. The formation becomes easier to project only by moving mass into maintained geography or named supporting forces before the incident.

Capital behaves the same way. Organic unit capital falls from **$6.866 billion to $2.249 billion** under the inherited cost conventions. Restoring every removed capability as an exclusive supporting package yields **$6.986 billion per complete system**, about $120 million more than the inherited unit. Reorganization can improve readiness, specialization and transport sequencing. It cannot manufacture economic savings by moving equipment across an organizational boundary.

The force-design decision is therefore precise:

> Build the HCG as a modular, labor-dense origin formation. Build prepared districts and heavy supporting capacity as separate but binding force objects. Do not credit pooling, pre-positioning or technology until those mechanisms survive the same complete-service mission.

No rendering is authorized. The group has a more coherent mission and echelon, but its vehicles, access systems, company geometry, supporting interfaces and measured work rates remain open.

## 1. The unit-of-account error

The [Neighborhood Continuity and Access Cell](../mobility/neighborhood-continuity-and-access-cell.md) was deliberately configured as a complete service system. It had to verify households, enter buildings, provide assisted vertical access, operate prepared districts, collect people accessibly, release local routes, supply power and water, maintain communications, preserve rights and sustain itself for seven days. That boundary was correct for testing whether the public effect closed.

It did not follow that every element belonged inside one deployable formation.

The original `A2` cell included 34 prepared districts, 300 household-contact teams, 100 vertical teams, five collection squadrons, seven heavy route-assurance detachments, ten power blocks, nine water islands, seven communications nodes, a 720-person command and regulatory allowance, and 990 sustainment personnel. Calling this a “cell” obscured a brigade-scale system of systems. Treating the same object as one canonical formation then created two risks.

The first was **organic duplication**. A Household Collection Group should not own complete heavy route, power and water systems if Land Engineering and Infrastructure Restoration formations already generate those professions and assets.

The second was **accounting erasure**. Removing those systems from the HCG does not prove that another formation can deliver them to the same district, at the same hour, under the same loss. A generic shared credit can make the spreadsheet smaller while making the operation impossible.

The correction must preserve the complete-service boundary while changing the organizational boundary. This paper therefore distinguishes three objects:

| Object | Purpose | Readiness question |
|---|---|---|
| `HCG-35B` | deployable origin formation | can it know, reach, assist, collect and hand off households lawfully? |
| external service contract | prepared and supporting capacity bound to the group | are the required districts, routes, utilities, information and sustainment present by deadline? |
| complete neighborhood system | HCG plus every external contract | does the person receive complete service under the declared damage? |

The complete neighborhood system remains the mission-test object. `HCG-35B` becomes the force-generation object.

## 2. First-principles echelon rules

A function should not become organic because it is important. Every function in a disaster system is important. It becomes organic when separation would break command, time, accountability or the indivisible public effect.

This pass applies six tests.

### 2.1 Continuous command

The origin commander must control functions that make immediate decisions about household status, building entry, assisted service, consent, clinical separation, accessible collection and lawful handoff. These decisions cannot wait for a remote service queue whenever the household state changes.

### 2.2 Deadline and latency

An external function is valid only if its alert, travel, setup, release and productive-work time fit inside the mission clock. The model shows that the seven route detachments can begin no more than **12.29 hours late** and still produce 60 released route-kilometres by hour 48 under the inherited rate. A route formation that exists nationally but cannot cross that time boundary provides no credit to the HCG.

### 2.3 Exclusive allocation

Shared capacity must have a common identity. If two simultaneous HCGs claim the same detachment, block, island, crew or prepared site, the second claim must be denied unless distinct capacity exists. “Available somewhere in the Department” is not a readiness state.

For supporting item (s), region (r) and time interval (t), credit requires:

\[
C_{s,r,t}^{ready}\;\ge\;\sum_h q_{h,s,r,t},
\]

where (C^{ready}) is capacity remaining after readiness and damage, and (q) is the time-phased claim from HCG (h). The same item cannot appear twice in the sum.

### 2.4 Independent failure behavior

Pooling is useful only when the pool does not share the failure that creates demand. A regional data service dependent on commercial power and cloud connectivity cannot replace an organic disconnected record. A route fleet based across the same flooded crossing cannot be treated as independent reinforcement.

### 2.5 Economies of scale

Some capabilities have genuine scale advantages. Heavy pumps, bridge systems, protection engineering, water treatment, grid-forming power, depots and major maintenance should be pooled when one formation can support several origin groups without violating time or exclusivity. Household entry and consent do not exhibit the same scale economy because the work remains distributed across doors, floors, devices and people.

### 2.6 Authority and custody

The HCG may receive technical service from other formations. It cannot transfer its accountability for the household record or the completeness of the journey. Supporting route, utility and district authorities retain technical release; the HCG retains the person-service obligation until a named receiving authority accepts it.

These rules reject both extremes. The monolithic `A2` system carries too much heavy and fixed capacity inside a mobile formation. A thin coordinator carries too little physical labor and cannot produce the origin effect. A fully local model lacks assured common standards, replacement depth and degraded-system capability. The selected design is a deployable origin formation bound to prepared geography and heavy national support through enforceable interfaces.

## 3. The `HCG-35B` mission

The design demand is inherited from Pass 37 so the organizational change can be compared without changing the workload:

| Requirement | Design value |
|---|---:|
| Total demand | 34,956.8 people |
| Protected-local service | 28,671.1 people |
| Complete collection | 6,285.7 people |
| Household records | 14,268.1 households |
| Assisted vertical service | 1,503.1 people |
| Critical route release | 60 route-km |
| Average local power | 7.2 MW |
| Daily local water | 650 m³/day |
| Communications coverage | 100 abstract units |

This remains a disaster-demand envelope, not a resident population or jurisdiction. One group might operate across part of a dense city, several towns, a rural corridor or a mixed territory. The assignment should follow buildings, travel time, vertical burden, access friction, local-service anchors and civil authority—not equal population polygons.

The group owns five non-delegable outputs:

1. every assigned household receives a verified disposition or remains in an explicit exception queue;
2. every vertical-assistance case receives safe service, supported remain or an authorized rescue handoff;
3. every required collection preserves household, caregiver, device, medication, consent and clinical relationships;
4. every locally protected person is bound to an activated district with the necessary service state; and
5. every person leaving the origin enters a named, accepted next service with custody intact.

The group does not receive outcome credit for a completed visit, a bus departure, a cleared street, an energized generator or an open shelter. Those are intermediate activities.

## 4. Seven `HCC-5` companies

The formation contains seven Household Continuity Companies. `HCC-5` denotes a nominal 5,000-demand-person operating envelope. It is not a fixed territorial boundary.

At an even analytical division, each company receives approximately:

- 4,994 demand people;
- 2,038 household records;
- 215 assisted vertical-service cases; and
- 898 collection cases supported by the group fleet.

Actual sectors will be unequal. A tower district can concentrate vertical metres, medical-power dependence and access-control friction into a few buildings. A low-rise district can distribute more doors and road interfaces across a larger area. A campus can create common utility and institutional dependencies. Company boundaries must therefore be built from work accounts and common-mode separation.

Each HCC has 49 two-person household teams, 14 four-person vertical teams and a 24-person headquarters. Collection squadrons, communications reserve, group rights and clinical authority, maintenance and major external interfaces remain at group level. The company must still function when disconnected from the group network, but it does not become a miniature group with every specialist duplicated seven times.

The seven-company structure is selected for four reasons.

First, it creates smaller geographic and command spans than the five large contact companies in `A2`. Second, the loss of one company leaves six coherent sectors rather than removing a cross-cutting specialty from the whole group. Third, it supports unequal reinforcement between high-door, high-rise and high-collection sectors. Fourth, it provides a practical experimental unit below the full group.

The structure does not solve the known information failure. Under commercial-network isolation, all seven companies together reach only **88.8 percent** of the required household verification by hour 36 at the inherited 1.6 households per team-hour and 15-percent offline verification share. With one company also unavailable, closure falls to **78.3 percent**.

That failure is retained intentionally. A new organization must not erase the reason the technology program exists.

## 5. Organic organization and workforce

The shift-complete deployed structure is:

| Element | Deployed people | Readiness factor | Affiliated billets per owned HCG |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Group command, rights, clinical and records | 360.0 | 2.7 | 972.0 |
| Seven company headquarters | 168.0 | 2.7 | 453.6 |
| 343 household access teams | 1,893.4 | 2.2 | 4,165.4 |
| 98 vertical continuity teams | 1,081.9 | 2.4 | 2,596.6 |
| Five collection squadron crews | 662.4 | 2.6 | 1,722.2 |
| Seven route-interface teams | 115.9 | 2.3 | 266.6 |
| Eight communications and field-record teams | 132.5 | 2.5 | 331.2 |
| Organic sustainment and regeneration | 662.1 | 2.5 | 1,655.3 |
| **Total** | **5,076.2** | — | **12,162.9** |

The deployed total is only 9.1 percent below the Pass 58 net proxy because the household, vertical and collection work remains. The larger national reduction comes from replacing the uniform 3.4 crew-set factor with role-specific generation factors.

Those factors are not empirical findings. They express a hypothesis about component design:

- command, rights, clinical and records positions require high continuity and qualification depth;
- household teams can draw more heavily from territorial and reserve affiliations if common training, employer release and local currency are real;
- vertical and collection crews require greater technical, safety and equipment currency;
- communications and sustainment require persistent professional depth but can combine permanent, reserve and certified industrial components.

The national gain is conditional on this personnel system. A state or local worker cannot appear as an affiliated billet merely because the occupation exists in the economy. The Department must know the individual or governed pool, activation authority, employer-release terms, qualification, current training, equipment assignment, home-station duty, replacement path and availability conflict.

The same distinction applies to deployed headcount. The 5,076-person formation already includes a 2.4 shift multiplier, local command and relief, and a 15-percent organic support allowance. It is not the number simultaneously standing in streets and buildings. It is the shift-complete deployed force required to sustain that work.

## 6. What remains organic

### 6.1 Command, rights and clinical authority

The 360-person group headquarters is an operational allowance, not an administrative staff. It must maintain the common operating picture, household and building state, civil priorities, exceptions, consent and lawful authority, accessibility remedies, clinical regulation, collection dispatch, destination acceptance, records reconciliation, safety, public communication and inter-formation support claims.

The command cannot outsource its rights function to a post-incident review office. Refusal, family unity, disability accommodation, service animals, essential equipment, custody and destination suitability change the immediate physical plan.

### 6.2 Household access

The 343 household teams are the largest labor block. They perform local reconnaissance, building entry coordination, welfare contact, needs identification, service-state verification, referral, records closure and follow-up. Their productivity must include travel, waiting, language access, inaccessible doors, conflicting information and incomplete records. Counting door knocks would materially overstate capacity.

### 6.3 Vertical continuity

The 98 vertical teams are not general search-and-rescue companies. They operate at the boundary between supported occupancy, assisted vertical service and rescue escalation. They need building-state information, powered handling systems, medical-device continuity, fall protection, heat controls, refuge procedures and independent safety authority.

At the inherited rate, 98 teams provide a 125.2-percent capacity ratio. Loss of one 14-team company set leaves a 107.3-percent ratio. That narrow degraded margin is appropriate for research; it is not yet evidence for reduction.

### 6.4 Accessible collection

Five group-level squadrons retain the `A2` complete-cycle rate. Four surviving squadrons provide a 119.3-percent ratio. The fleet must serve wheelchairs, stretchers, caregivers, service animals, medical devices, families and contaminated or high-water conditions without turning each category into a separate incompatible fleet.

The five squadrons are organic because dispatch, household readiness, load sequencing, clinical separation and handoff must be synchronized with company work. Long-distance carriers remain external.

### 6.5 Route interface and communications

The seven route-interface teams do not receive heavy route-release credit. They verify company access needs, observe route state, control the local HCG interface, preserve evidence and request work from the supporting route formation.

Eight communications nodes provide one per company and one group reserve. The HCG must operate a disconnected local field network and authoritative reconciliation edge. Regional information formations may supply data products and backhaul, but they cannot be the only place where the current household-service record exists.

### 6.6 Organic sustainment

The group carries first-entry base, food, water, fuel, maintenance, waste, decontamination and regeneration capacity sufficient to begin work and survive delays. It does not carry the full seven-day local population utility system. The organic support account is 15 percent of the direct deployed formation; trials must replace that coefficient with occupations, duty cycles and an itemized manifest.

## 7. What moves outside the group

External does not mean optional. It means a different formation or prepared-capacity owner generates the capability while the HCG mission test continues to require it.

| External contract per active HCG | Quantity | Proposed owner | Mission boundary |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Prepared district packages | 34 | Population Support Group and prepared lattice | 29 must remain active after five are lost |
| Heavy route-assurance detachments | 7 | Access and Route-Opening / Civil Road Mobility | six must release 60 route-km after one is lost |
| Power blocks | 10 | Power Restoration / Grid Bridge | eight must provide 7.2 MW after N-2 loss |
| Water islands | 9 | Water Restoration | eight must provide 650 m³/day after N-1 loss |

Prepared districts belong primarily to Community Stabilization and receiving geography because their capacity should exist before the mobile group arrives. Heavy route work belongs to Land Engineering and Civil Mobility because it requires larger equipment, inspection and network release. Power and water blocks belong to Infrastructure Restoration because their technical authority, maintenance, distribution and residual systems exceed the HCG profession.

The split creates better specialization, but it also creates four hard dependencies. The mission-test model preserves them. It fails when route support begins 24 hours late, when only half the districts activate, or when only half the power and water capacity is present by deadline.

The correct command response is not to redefine those cases as someone else’s failure. The complete neighborhood system failed. Readiness reporting should identify both the failed effect and the formation or interface that caused it.

## 8. The national shared-capacity burden

Pass 58 requires 69 simultaneous HCG-equivalents. Applying the 20-percent readiness reserve produces 82.8 supported equivalents. Using the current service readiness factors produces the following required inventory screen:

| Supporting item | Required inventory |
|---|---:|
| Prepared district packages | 3,754 |
| Route-assurance detachments | 828 |
| Power blocks | 1,183 |
| Water islands | 1,065 |

These are item counts, not added formation counts. The project does not yet know how many packages, detachments, blocks or islands sit inside each canonical supporting formation. It therefore cannot subtract the existing `C-PSG`, `L-ROG`, `CM-RMG`, `I-PRG`, `I-GBG` and `I-WRG` inventories from this requirement.

That missing crosswalk is the point. A shared-capacity ledger needs at least:

- item or governed pool identifier;
- configuration and technical authority;
- home location and transport path;
- readiness and maintenance state;
- time-phased capacity and setup requirement;
- hazard and common-mode exposure;
- current claimant and reservation interval;
- supported formation and service contract;
- substitution and denial rule; and
- regeneration state.

Until that record exists, the supporting inventory is neither a demonstrated addition nor a demonstrated duplication. It is an unresolved force claim.

## 9. Equipment and deployment consequence

The organic HCG retains household and vertical team sets, the five collection squadrons, light route-interface sets, eight communications nodes, command/data/clinical modules and its first-entry sustainment. The prepared and supporting account retains district packages, heavy route detachments, power and water blocks, most startup utility inventory and part of the base, food, fuel, waste and decontamination system.

The mass reconciliation is:

| Account | Functional mass | Packed mass |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inherited `A2` system | 5,323.7 t | 6,388.5 t |
| `HCG-35B` organic | 1,525.1 t | 1,830.1 t |
| External contract | 3,819.8 t | 4,583.7 t |
| **Complete assigned system** | **5,344.9 t** | **6,413.9 t** |

The 25.4-tonne packed increase comes from adding 43 household sets, seven route-interface sets and one communications node while reducing vertical sets by two. This is not a bill of material. It is a conservation test showing that the organizational split did not make the complete system lighter.

The operational value is **echeloning**. The 1,830-tonne organic group can enter through a smaller transport aperture while prepared district capacity already exists and heavy technical formations follow through their own movement plans. The result is better only if three conditions hold:

1. prepared capacity is actually in the affected geography and remains serviceable;
2. the external formations can meet the route and utility deadlines; and
3. the group carries a minimum effect package while it waits.

Future design should divide the organic mass into alert, advance, company entry, collection entry, expansion, seven-day sustainment and relief packages. Each echelon needs a minimum public effect and a stated failure mode. “First aircraft landed” or “first convoy arrived” is not a useful readiness milestone.

## 10. Capital and operating consequence

Under the inherited comparative cost factors, direct equipment capital moves as follows:

| Account | Direct capital | Unit capital after inherited base/depot and reserve factors |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inherited `A2` | $4.225B | $6.866B |
| `HCG-35B` organic | $1.384B | $2.249B |
| External contract | $2.915B | $4.737B |
| **Complete assigned system** | **$4.299B** | **$6.986B** |

The revised complete system costs more because the selected organization adds company-access and interface resilience. This is a useful warning against a common reform narrative. Modularity and specialization do not automatically reduce procurement cost. Their value may be faster entry, clearer ownership, independent failure behavior, deeper industrial specialization and better regeneration.

The national model carries three screens:

| Measure | Pass 58 | Organic-only lower screen | Exclusive-support screen |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Affiliated establishment | 10.484M | 9.505M | 10.087M |
| Capital | $15.179T | $14.470T | $15.197T |
| Mature annual operations | $1.736T | $1.605T | $1.700T |

The organic-only column is inadmissible because it removes external obligations. The exclusive-support column assigns a complete external package to every owned HCG and applies an illustrative generation factor to supporting people. It is deliberately conservative and may overstate what well-designed pools and prepared sites require. Neither column is the new baseline.

The bracket changes the research question. The Department should not ask whether the HCG can be “made smaller.” It should ask what combination of organic depth, prepared geography, shared inventory and technology provides the complete public guarantee with the lowest correlated failure and lifecycle burden.

## 11. Failure architecture

`HCG-35B` preserves the eight serial `NCAC` capability accounts. The base case has an 118.6-percent minimum margin. The eight declared physical component-loss cases pass, with the power N-2 case exactly at 100 percent and the five-district-loss case at 101.1 percent.

Five cases fail:

| Case | Minimum ratio | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Commercial network and cloud unavailable | 88.8% | household verification remains the bottleneck |
| Isolation plus one household company unavailable | 78.3% | organization alone cannot replace disconnected knowledge and productivity |
| Route support begins 24 hours late | 67.2% | shared inventory without timely arrival is not capacity |
| Half of prepared districts active by deadline | 59.3% | nominal fixed sites do not equal released occupied service |
| Half of power and water active by deadline | 62.3% | external utility promises remain serial mission gates |

These failures demonstrate why the HCG must own the support request and its consequence even when it does not own the supporting equipment. A mature command system should show the denied service, the affected households, the failed interface and the supporting capacity conflict in the same record.

## 12. Technology program created by the formation

The organization creates mission-driven technology requirements rather than a vehicle wish list.

### `HC-01`: disconnected household continuity graph

With one company unavailable and conventional field productivity retained, the HCG requires a **36.7-percent lawful offline verification share**, compared with the current 15-percent hypothesis. The system must preserve household, caregiver, device, consent, language, destination and exception relationships without creating a permanent surveillance registry.

### `HC-02`: building-access mission system

If offline verification remains at 15 percent, household productivity must increase from 1.60 to **2.15 completed households per team-hour** under isolation and one-company loss. The numerator must be an authoritative completed disposition, not a contact attempt. The safety case must include residents, field workers and anyone whose risk is shifted by faster work.

### `HC-03`: powered vertical continuity

The 98-team organization depends on maintaining at least the 0.50-person-per-team-hour inherited rate across occupied, hot, dark and damaged building states. Novel powered stair, hoist, device-support and temporary-lift systems should compete against changes in staffing, prepared-building interfaces and supported-remain methods. A throughput gain that obstructs egress or increases injury fails.

### `HC-04`: common-cabin collection family

The group needs a cabin and restraint architecture that can cross road, high-water and clinical mission sets while preserving accessibility and decontamination. The requirement is not one universal vehicle. It is a common human-service interface that allows several mobility platforms to share training, loading, securing, records and handoff.

### `HC-05`: route request-to-release system

The route-support chain has a 12.29-hour maximum start-delay screen. Autonomous reconnaissance, work-package compilation, vehicle-specific route proofs, small robotic clearance systems and pre-positioned interface kits are valuable only if they reduce the complete alert-to-lawful-release time.

### `HC-06`: prepared district adapter

Thirty-four planned districts must yield at least 29 active districts after five are lost. The adapter program should standardize electrical, water, communications, clean-air, sanitation, accessibility and civil-release interfaces across existing buildings. It is a construction, maintenance and inspection industry, not a box stored in a warehouse.

### `HC-07`: shared-capacity allocation and denial engine

The Department needs a system that reserves physical assets, licensed crews, prepared sites and transport slots across formations and time. It must deny double claims, propagate common-mode loss and preserve civil priorities. Optimization that silently reallocates a promised household service is unacceptable.

### `HC-08`: human force-generation system

The largest technology opportunity may be institutional. Modular qualification, simulation, remote supervision, common technical orders, wearable safety systems, employer-release mechanisms, cross-state credentialing and rapid replacement could make the role-specific readiness factors real. These systems must improve competent available labor, not merely digitize training records.

## 13. Proving sequence

The formation cannot advance through separate demonstrations of a contact application, stair device, accessible vehicle, communications node or microgrid. The evidence sequence should preserve the service chain.

The first stage measures tasks. Household teams, vertical teams, collection crews, company headquarters and sustainment elements need observed work distributions across building and household archetypes, not expert point rates.

The second stage operates one `HCC-5` company in a representative occupied district under power, elevator, communications, water and route degradation. It tests work, rights, safety, records and shift endurance together.

The third stage operates all seven companies with the group fleet and command. One company set is lost; records conflict; commercial networks fail; the workload is redistributed; and the group must preserve household completeness.

The fourth stage adds the external service contracts. Route, district, power and water capacity arrive late, suffer losses and receive competing claims from another group. The allocation system must deny impossible promises and reveal the affected service.

The fifth stage completes 168 hours, regenerates the formation and begins a second mission before every person and asset has returned to the available pool.

The final stage repeats the design in a different metropolitan form and hazard with independent reconstruction and cost review. This remains aligned with the existing [NCAC full-mission test program](../../proving/test-enterprise/ncac-full-mission-test-program.md); it does not advance any of that program’s open gates.

## 14. Institutional consequence

The HCG implies a new professional domain inside the Department of Resilience: **civil terrain continuity**. It combines household service, occupied-building access, accessible mobility, rights, records, local command and technical interfaces. It should not be subordinated to a vehicle fleet or treated as a temporary emergency-management coordination function.

Civil Mobility and Continuity should generate the group, its company doctrine, collection fleet and origin-service professions. Community Stabilization should generate prepared district service. Land Engineering and Civil Road Mobility should generate heavy route release. Infrastructure Restoration should generate power and water support. Joint information and sustainment organizations should provide common services without displacing the group’s disconnected minimum.

The arrangement is intentionally cross-service. A coequal resilience institution should be able to create deep professions and then bind them into a public effect without pretending that one bureaucracy owns the entire causal chain.

The industrial consequence is similarly distributed. The HCG creates demand for purpose-built accessible collection cabins, building-access systems, powered vertical equipment, field networks, protected record systems, modular base equipment, decontamination systems and occupational training. Prepared districts create a national retrofit and inspection industry. Supporting formations create separate heavy equipment, utility and route product lines. The value lies in the interoperable system and repeated readiness economy, not one flagship platform.

## 15. Decision boundary and next pass

This pass establishes:

- `HCG-35B` as the preferred research unit of account for household collection;
- seven `HCC-5` companies inside one group;
- the organic and external mission boundaries;
- a shift-complete deployed workforce and role-specific readiness hypothesis;
- an item-level first shared-capacity demand screen;
- mass and capital conservation across the echelon split;
- a national lower and exclusive-support bracket without changing the baseline;
- five explicit mission failures; and
- eight technology programs derived from those failures.

It does not establish observed work rates, occupation supply, fixed-site availability, support travel times, full bills of material, equipment geometry, independent cost, shared inventory sufficiency, common-mode readiness or metropolitan replication.

The next force-generation pass should build the **canonical shared-capacity allocation ledger**. It should give common identities to prepared districts, route detachments, utility blocks, licensed crews, carriers, bases, information services and sustainment packages; assign them across all forty-eight families and ten campaigns; and make a second claim fail when the first has consumed the capacity. Only then should the canonical national workforce, capital and operating totals be revised.

The design remains deliberately more ambitious than preserving present institutions. It assumes that the United States can create a permanent civil-terrain profession, a maintained national building interface, purpose-built household-access and collection systems, governed offline civil records, and a joint allocation apparatus with the same seriousness applied to military force generation.

That ambition does not justify fictional capacity. The central discipline of the Department of Resilience is that every public promise must survive organizational boundaries. The HCG is smaller than the original complete cell because it is a more coherent formation. The system around it remains large because the catastrophe remains physically large.

No rendering is authorized. Twelve evidence gates and every shared-capacity admission remain open.
