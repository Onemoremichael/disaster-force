## Executive finding

`RBV-1` exposed a structural contradiction in the emerging Resilience Force. A dedicated national fire-control service that can sustain twenty-four independent public-service lines requires 256 system bodies, 84,480 prime movers, 79,200 governed frames, 180 arsenal bays, 48 launch stations and USD 3.6412016 trillion of initial capital. Repeating that vertically integrated ownership pattern for water, flood, medical, power, access and other effect portfolios would spend the national capital boundary several times while still allowing the same nominal capacity to be promised to several emergencies.

`RBW-1` rejects two easy answers.

The first is to preserve a separate miniature logistics, utility, communications and technical estate inside every operating service. That protects local control but turns common functions into serially duplicated institutions.

The second is to place all nominally similar assets in a central pool and subtract a sharing percentage from every service budget. That creates fictitious readiness. A prime mover, energy island, command node, treatment train or repair shop cannot satisfy two simultaneous service promises merely because both promises cite the same national pool.

The selected architecture is an **assured common force**. It distinguishes four separate facts:

1. who holds title to an article;
2. which technical family and configuration it belongs to;
3. which service obligation holds its exclusive reservation; and
4. where the complete article, crew, transport, spares and release authority are physically assigned.

The national fire-control guarantee remains unchanged. Every article needed for the `C24` readiness claim is reserved once, assigned to a launch or regeneration location, configuration-current and unavailable to a second readiness claim during the reservation interval. Common ownership does not weaken physical possession. It changes the institution that designs, buys, regenerates and allocates common capability.

The resulting capital decomposition is not a savings claim:

|---|---:|---:|---|
| fire-mission dedicated | 660.163867 | 18.13% | intervention hardware and its dedicated production share |
| hazard-control portfolio common | 1,146.008388 | 31.47% | water, conveyance, recovery and arsenal capacity shared within a governed effect portfolio |
| national common force | 1,649.267746 | 45.30% | energy, distribution, command, rescue, repair, sustainment, transport and national interfaces |

| sovereign technical estate | 165.256000 | 4.54% | destructive articles, training, evaluation, evidence and configuration custody |
| **national capital still required** | **3,641.201600** | **100.00%** | same physical `C24` boundary, now held in five explicit ledgers |

USD 1.649267746 trillion can therefore move from a fire-service ownership ledger to a National Common Force ledger, and USD 1.146008388 trillion can move to a Hazard Control Service ledger, without removing a single physical article from the fire-control guarantee. This is fiscal attribution, not avoided national expenditure.

Any actual reduction in the multi-portfolio national force can occur only after a later compiler proves that demand is nonconcurrent, configurations are interchangeable, transit and recall clocks close, crew and regeneration capacity exist, and one serialized article is never counted twice. Until that proof exists, the `C24` quantity and USD 3.6412016 trillion initial capital remain conserved.

This finding changes the institutional picture. The Department of Resilience should not be a federation of self-contained service fleets. It should operate a matrix of mission services, portfolio commons, a National Common Force, and a sovereign technical estate. Operating groups receive enforceable capacity, not informal help.

All physical findings, family equivalence findings, cross-portfolio demand distributions, service admissions, site selections, procurement releases, fielding releases and exterior renderings remain open or blocked.

The answer must preserve the central service rule of this project:

## 2. Why “common” is not one property

### 2.1 Four independent dimensions

Every article receives four classifications.

| Dimension | Governing question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| title | which institution owns and capitalizes the article? | National Common Force |
| technical family | which interface, root and configuration control it? | `ENG-5/7` Root 2, baseline 7.4 |
| reservation | which public-service obligation has exclusive claim during a stated interval? | Fire Group 07 onset reserve |
| assignment | where are article, crew, spares and release authority physically located? | Launch Station 07B |

None can substitute for another. National title does not establish availability. A compatible interface does not establish a qualified configuration. A reservation without physical assignment may miss the launch clock. An assigned article without configuration authority may be unsafe to release.

### 2.2 Revised meaning of self-contained

A formation is self-contained when it possesses **enforceable control of a complete service chain** for the interval and damage world named in its guarantee.

That control may be satisfied by articles titled to another Department component only when:

- each article is serialized and uniquely reserved;
- the reservation cannot be displaced by administrative preference;
- the article is physically co-present or can meet a tested assembly clock;
- configuration, crew, spares, transport and regeneration rights accompany it;
- no other readiness claim counts it during the same interval; and
- the formation can continue safely after loss of the central network.

Self-containment is therefore a property of the service obligation, not of the procurement account.

### 2.3 The common-pool fallacy

Suppose Fire Group 07, a flood brigade and a medical continuity command each list the same 1,000 prime movers in their plans. The Department owns 1,000 machines, but the ledgers contain 3,000 promises. A national inventory report may appear complete while two missions have no transport.

`NCFC-1` treats readiness claims as conserved liabilities. The sum of accepted reservations against a family, configuration, location and time window may never exceed the articles actually available after maintenance, quarantine, training and casualty exclusions.

## 3. Five ownership classes

### 3.1 Class `D` — mission dedicated

These articles exist to create the distinctive mission effect and are not credited to another portfolio without a new physical equivalence campaign.

For the present fire-control reference, `EFF-16` intervention cells are Class `D`.

Dedicated does not mean technologically isolated. Intervention cells may use common materials, compute, metrology, transport interfaces or production processes. The assembled mission article and its reservation remain dedicated.

### 3.2 Class `P` — portfolio common

These articles can serve several formations within a bounded effect portfolio, but their operating chemistry, contamination state, receiver interfaces or professional regime prevents immediate Department-wide interchange.

The fire-control reference places the following families in a Hazard Control Service portfolio:

- `WPT-500` treatment trains;
- `RAW-1000` intake paths;
- `RTL-100` trunkline sections;
- `RTR-12` route and repair robots;
- `BUF-100` ready-storage cells;
- `REC-185` recovery trains; and
- `SUR-100` surge-custody cells.

This is a jurisdictional hypothesis, not an equivalence result. A flood dewatering train and a fire-control recovery train do not become interchangeable because both handle water.

### 3.3 Class `N` — national common force

These families should be designed as Department-wide service utilities with mission variants governed under public interfaces:

- `ENG-5/7` energy islands;
- `PWR-20` field distribution units;
- `LCN-12` local command nodes;
- `SCN-2` set coordination nodes;
- `RES-6` rescue and medical cells;
- `REP-6` field repair shops;
- `SPR-24` serialized repair packs;
- `HAB-24` crew and sustainment units;
- `TIA-30` transport-interface adapters;
- `PM-30` prime movers; and
- `LF-30` governed load frames.

Class `N` does not imply one universal machine. It creates a family-of-families authority: shared interface law, configuration grammar, custody, allocation and industrial roots, with mission variants where physics requires them.

### 3.4 Class `M` — mobilizable civil capacity

Commercial and civil assets may augment operations only when their exact articles, crews, configuration, insurance, custody, access, maintenance and release conditions have been accepted in advance.

Unreserved market capacity receives no baseline readiness credit. A rental fleet, contractor labor list, hotel inventory, warehouse catalog or fuel contract can disappear under correlated demand. Class `M` may satisfy a declared surge increment; it may not silently replace Class `D`, `P` or `N` obligations.

### 3.5 Class `S` — sovereign technical estate

Public standards, reference articles, destructive systems, independent evaluation, metrology, evidence originals, software signing, configuration release and forensic capacity remain outside operational allocation.

They cannot be lent to an operating group to close a shortfall. Doing so would collapse the institution that determines whether the operational claim is true.

### 5.3 Conditional avoided-duplication envelope

For a later portfolio `p` and common family `f`, the maximum gross duplicate capital that may be avoided is:

\[
A_{p,f}=C_{p,f}^{dedicated}-C_{p,f}^{incremental\ common}
\]

subject to:

\[
I_f\geq\max_{w\in W}\left(\sum_p R_{p,f,w}\right)+O_{f,w}+Q_{f,w}+G_{f,w}
\]

where:

- `I` is installed accepted capacity;
- `R` is uniquely reserved mission demand;
- `O` is overhaul occupancy;
- `Q` is quarantine and casualty exclusion; and
- `G` is the protected regeneration and uncertainty margin.

The maximum is taken over compound demand worlds, not over each portfolio separately. Summing each portfolio maximum overbuys. Taking only the largest individual maximum underbuys. The correct answer depends on the joint worlds.

Because those joint worlds do not yet exist, the workbook presents coincidence cases only as sensitivities. They are not budget recommendations.

## 6. Availability contract

### 6.1 Contract object

Every common-force contribution to a service guarantee requires a machine-readable **Service Availability Warrant** (`SAW`). A warrant is a public liability, not a scheduling preference.

The minimum warrant contains:

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| warrant identity | immutable public identifier |
| service obligation | population, effect, time, duration and damage world |
| claimant | readiness-bearing formation |
| article serials | exact serialized articles or governed substitution set |
| family and configuration | accepted technical family, root and baseline |
| quantity and capacity | units plus service-rate measure |
| reservation interval | start, irrevocability point and lawful release |
| assignment location | launch station, arsenal or prepared receiver |
| launch and assembly ceiling | tested maximum to complete packet availability |
| crew warrant | named qualified watches and relief depth |
| transport warrant | mover, frame, route and custody entitlement |
| spare and repair warrant | field stock, arsenal bay and replacement priority |
| regeneration warrant | receiving site, slot and return ceiling |
| substitution law | accepted equivalence classes and prohibited substitutions |
| priority class | conflict rule before and after commitment |
| root requirement | unlike technical ancestry and surviving-root rule |
| offline authority | local authentication and safe-release method |
| evidence state | last accepted trial, maintenance and configuration records |
| financial charge | capital jurisdiction and annual readiness attribution |
| release signatures | service, common-force, configuration and independent release authorities |

A memorandum that says “transport support available as required” is not a warrant.

### 6.2 Warrant states

| State | Meaning | Readiness credit |
|---|---|---:|
| `W0` candidate | requested capacity; no serials committed | 0 |
| `W1` reserved | serials or accepted substitution set exclusively encumbered | conditional |
| `W2` packet assigned | articles, crew, transport and spares located to meet launch clock | yes |
| `W3` alert sealed | substitutions closed; evidence packet frozen | yes |
| `W4` committed transit | mission underway; no recall | active obligation only |
| `W5` operating | service effect underway | active obligation only |
| `W6` regenerating | unavailable until accepted return | 0 |
| `W7` quarantined or casualty | unavailable; discrepancy open | 0 |
| `W8` reference or destructive | permanently outside operational credit | 0 |

National readiness reporting counts only `W2` and `W3` against prospective obligations. `W4` and `W5` satisfy active obligations but cannot also support a second future claim.

### 6.3 Irrevocability

A common asset becomes nonrecallable at the earliest of:

- loading of mission-specific hazardous or sanitary material;
- departure from its warranted launch location;
- crew duty commencement under the mission clock;
- receiver configuration that disables a timely alternate use;
- issuance of public reliance instructions; or
- any action that makes recall increase expected loss of life.

No headquarters may solve a later shortage by recalling a committed article and continuing to count the first mission as served.

### 6.4 Substitution

Substitution is permitted only inside a preaccepted equivalence class. It must preserve:

- service rate and residual after named loss;
- interface geometry and transport limits;
- source, energy, thermal and waste accounts;
- crew qualification;
- maintenance and spares;
- software and command compatibility;
- evidence capture; and
- regeneration time.

Nominal megawatts, tonnes, beds, pumps or vehicles do not establish equivalence by themselves.

## 7. Anti-double-counting law

### 7.1 One article, one obligation, one interval

For article `a`, time interval `t`, and accepted prospective or active obligations `o`:

\[
\sum_o x_{a,o,t}\leq1
\]

where `x=1` only when the article is reserved or committed to that obligation.

For divisible service capacity, the same rule applies to independently isolable, metered and safe portions. A 20 MW distribution unit may not be divided on paper. It may support two obligations only if its architecture, protection, metering, crew and failure isolation physically create two accepted capacity partitions.

### 7.2 Five ledgers

The Department maintains separate ledgers for:

1. serialized inventory;
2. configuration and root state;
3. maintenance, quarantine and regeneration occupancy;
4. service warrants and time intervals; and
5. physical assignment and transit.

Readiness exists only at the intersection. No ledger may import a success flag from another ledger.

### 7.3 Conservation checks

For every family, configuration, root, region and time window:

\[
accepted\ reservations+committed+operating+regenerating+quarantined+reference\leqphysical\ inventory
\]

The terms are mutually exclusive states. If the inequality fails, the Department has created more promises than articles.

For every service warrant:

\[
complete=article\land crew\land transport\land spares\land command\land receiver\land regeneration
\]

A missing conjunct makes the warrant unavailable. Surplus in another family cannot compensate.

### 7.4 No fractional formation credit

The fire-control service remains group-based. A common-force ledger may report available movers or megawatts, but national fire-control readiness increases only when a complete eight-set group and its protected onset reserve close.

This prevents a large pool of disconnected assets from being described as several ready lines.

## 8. Conflict and priority constitution

### 8.1 Agency rank is not a priority rule

Flood, fire, medical, power and water missions all protect life. A standing rule that one service always outranks another would merely hide value judgments inside organizational hierarchy.

Allocation must compare the service consequence of denial.

### 8.2 Priority tiers

| Tier | Condition | Recall status |
|---|---|---|
| `T0` | active public service with dependent population | prohibited |
| `T1` | committed transit or sealed onset action | prohibited |
| `T2` | domestic protected onset reserve under an active warning world | only by civil authority after publishing the uncovered obligation |
| `T3` | compound domestic reserve not yet sealed | reallocable through national allocation board |
| `T4` | invited international warrant not yet committed | subject to treaty and host terms; cancellation recorded |
| `T5` | training, exercise, demonstration or industrial trial | first source of lawful reallocation |

The global mission is not inherently less valuable than a domestic mission. `T4` reflects a distinct uncommitted legal state, not the worth of non-US lives. Once committed, it becomes `T1` or `T0`.

### 8.3 Allocation decision record

When eligible claims exceed accepted capacity, the national civil authority must publish:

- the obligations in conflict;
- populations and service bundles affected;
- time-to-irreversible-harm ranges;
- substitution and local capacity;
- transport and regeneration consequences;
- uncertainty and dissent;
- the selected allocation;
- the obligation knowingly left uncovered; and
- the action required to restore the missing capacity.

The record cannot label the denied mission “deferred” if its service deadline will be missed.

### 8.4 Protected onset reserve

Each fire-control group retains one protected onset set among its eight operational sets. Common-force title does not allow this reserve to be absorbed into routine national scheduling. Its warrant may be released only when:

- the group’s current hazard world is below the registered release threshold;
- another accepted set can assume the onset obligation within the same clock;
- root balance remains intact;
- the group commander and national civil authority both sign; and
- the uncovered interval is zero.

## 9. Physical basing under common ownership

### 9.1 Common does not mean centralized

The National Common Force should be centrally governed and geographically distributed. Concentrating 84,480 movers, 79,200 frames, 1,824 energy islands or 6,080 distribution units in a few national depots would defeat the `C24` launch and station-loss logic.

The inherited forty-eight launch stations remain. Each group’s common articles are paired with its mission and portfolio modules under warrants. Title may be national; readiness assignment is local.

### 9.2 Three echelons

| Echelon | Function | Typical warrant state |
|---|---|---|
| assigned packet | complete set transport, utilities, command, rescue and repair at launch station | `W2` |
| protected regional reserve | unlike-root replacements, convoy and utility surge at arsenal cell | `W1` or `W2` |
| strategic common reserve | capacity not promised to a routine service claim; able to close compound gaps | `W1` only after allocation |

An article in the strategic reserve cannot also appear as a fire group’s protected onset capacity.

### 9.3 Root geometry

National common families retain at least two independent technical roots. Root balance applies to:

- design ancestry;
- material and critical process;
- software signer and control image;
- metrology and acceptance chain;
- spares and repair path;
- transport interface; and
- qualified workforce.

The common-force allocator cannot satisfy a two-root service warrant using two serials that share the same decisive dependency.

This resembles neither ad hoc interagency support nor private logistics contracting. It is a standing force-generation relationship.

## 11. Personnel and professions

### 11.1 Assignment is more important than payroll title

The 429,440-person `C24` public workforce remains conserved. A common-force decomposition cannot infer labor savings from equipment commonality. People must still operate 192 sets, maintain continuous watches, move packets, regenerate modules and hold protected reserves.

The workforce receives an assignment classification separate from asset ownership:

| Assignment | Parent positions before qualified reserve | Operational meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| fire formations | 283,200 | set crews and group command; dedicated while assigned |
| portfolio arsenals | 25,200 | hazard-control regeneration and custody |
| national common nodes | 16,800 | launch stations and national/global allocation |
| sovereign technical estate | 20,400 | schools, evaluation, liaison, configuration and rights |
| public industrial workforce | 32,000 | campuses, integration, transport and standards |
| qualified personnel reserve | 51,840 | fifteen percent of the 345,600 operating establishment |
| **total** | **429,440** | conserved parent workforce |

This table does not yet split the 276,480 set-crew positions by profession. That task requires a watch-by-watch work decomposition. Treating all set crews as fire specialists would miss water, energy, repair, rescue, logistics and command professions. Treating them all as common would miss the cohesion and local knowledge needed by a warranted formation.

### 11.2 New professions created by the common force

The model creates career fields beyond incident logistics:

- service-warrant design and allocation;
- common packet command;
- multimodal heavy-load custody;
- deployable power protection and black-start engineering;
- field utility architecture;
- public-interface and receiver commissioning;
- cross-family repair certification;
- offline configuration authentication;
- regeneration scheduling under compound demand;
- common-root industrial readiness; and
- liability and uncovered-obligation accounting.

### 11.3 Crew portability

An article is not common merely because several services can operate its controls. Crew portability requires accepted evidence for:

- task qualification;
- mission-specific safety;
- receiver and population interface;
- shift endurance;
- offline action;
- mixed-root maintenance;
- hazardous custody; and
- team integration under the receiving formation.

The technology program should pursue machine-assisted reconfiguration and training, but it may not presume that automation erases professional judgment.

## 12. Technology program

### 12.1 Technology objective

The common force should not standardize today’s machines. It should create an industrial demand for equipment designed from inception to be reconfigured, verified, transported, repaired and allocated across extreme civil missions.

### 12.2 Required advances

#### Public mission compiler

A machine-verifiable compiler should translate a service obligation and damage world into:

- required capability families;
- accepted configuration variants;
- unique article reservations;
- crew and watch structures;
- transport packets;
- receiver dependencies;
- spares and regeneration slots;
- root-diversity constraints; and
- an explicit uncovered-service report.

It must fail closed. It cannot invent availability when a required conjunct is missing.

#### Cryptographically verifiable offline custody

Disconnected formations need to authenticate article identity, configuration, maintenance state and warrant authority without a national network. This requires public signing infrastructure, tamper evidence, local revocation sets and later reconciliation rather than dependence on continuous cloud access.

#### Reconfigurable energy islands

Future energy islands should support several prime-energy paths, unlike control roots, modular conversion trains, high-voltage and medium-voltage outputs, thermal recovery, water production, and autonomous protection-zone formation. A mission module should be able to declare a service profile without rewriting safety logic in the field.

#### Self-handling load ecology

Prime movers, frames, adapters and modules should exchange loads without large prepared yards or a unique loader fleet. Development should include:

- robotic frame leveling;
- distributed traction and braking;
- autonomous low-speed packet formation;
- damaged-road and soft-ground modes;
- standardized lifting and restraint;
- embedded mass and center-of-gravity measurement; and
- human-supervised degraded operation.

#### Universal service-interface fabric

Water, electric, data, thermal, medical-gas and waste interfaces need public physical and information standards. The objective is not one connector for every use. It is a governed family that prevents an improvised transition from becoming the decisive failure.

#### Robotic inspection and regeneration

Arsenals should identify contamination, fatigue, software discrepancy and hidden damage at module scale; replace governed subassemblies; restore configuration evidence; and return articles through a complete acceptance cycle. Faster physical work receives no credit if evidence and custody lag behind it.

#### Distributed capacity sensing

Every common article should report service capacity rather than only mechanical status: delivered megawatts, safe water rate, pressure, supported people, isolation state, repair stock and predicted return time. The report must retain calibrated uncertainty.

#### Allocation under uncertainty

Decision systems should calculate the consequence of competing reservations, but final civil authority remains human and public. Algorithms may expose an uncovered population; they may not hide a value choice behind a single score.

### 12.3 Technology allocation

The inherited USD 250B technology authority can be reclassified without changing its total:

|---|---|---:|
| autonomous logistics and load exchange | `N` | 55 |
| arsenal process intensification and robotic regeneration | `P` | 45 |
| public configuration, evidence and hardware attestation | `S` | 40 |
| independent water, energy and thermal systems | `P` | 35 |
| advanced materials, joining and field repair | `P` | 30 |

## 13. Industrial economy

### 13.1 Lockheed-scale civil primes are insufficient by themselves

The user’s premise requires firms capable of delivering entire response systems at immense scale. `NCFC-1` adds a further requirement: those firms must compete inside a public technical system that prevents one integrator from owning every interface, repair right, software signer and source of truth.

The mature economy needs several kinds of enterprise:

- mission-system primes that integrate complete effect chains;
- common-force primes that build transport, energy, command and sustainment families;
- module specialists operating against public interfaces;
- arsenal and regeneration firms capable of multi-root work;
- public-interest software and configuration institutions;
- receiver-system builders serving cities, ports, utilities and hospitals;
- independent test and forensic organizations; and
- partner-nation production and service organizations.

### 13.2 Competition layer

Competition occurs across:

1. rival complete mission architectures;
2. unlike technical roots within selected families;
3. public-interface module suppliers;
4. regional production and regeneration campuses;
5. software and command implementations;
6. receiver and prepared-geography systems; and
7. outside reproduction from public technical data.

Commonality must not consolidate all demand into one irreplaceable supplier. The more missions depend on a family, the stronger its root-diversity and public-rights obligations become.

### 13.3 Export object

The common-force export object is a service architecture rather than a crate of equipment. It may include:

- a national warrant and allocation system;
- common utility and mobility families;
- mission-specific modules;
- receiver interfaces;
- arsenal and regeneration methods;
- schools and professional standards;
- public technical data and rights;
- partner production roots; and
- multinational reserve compacts.

No export credit is admitted. Partner demand, domestic replacement priority, host authority, data rights, configuration continuity and withdrawal obligations remain open.

## 14. Rival architectures

### 14.1 `A` — vertically integrated services

Every mission service owns its own transport, power, command, repair, sustainment, bases, schools and technical estate.

**Advantage:** clear service control.  
**Failure:** duplicates common functions, fragments professions and makes the USD 15.179T national force-capital boundary impossible to conserve across forty-eight families.

### 14.2 `B` — administrative common pool

A central organization holds generic equipment. Services request support during incidents.

**Advantage:** superficially low direct service budgets.  
**Failure:** no exclusive reservation, no co-present packet, no guaranteed launch time, no root law, no complete crew and regeneration entitlement, and pervasive double counting.

### 14.3 `C` — market mobilization

Government buys distinctive mission equipment and rents logistics, power, communications, housing and construction after an event.

**Advantage:** low peacetime public inventory.  
**Failure:** correlated demand, uncertain crews and condition, incompatible interfaces, contested transport, no protected onset reserve and no public control of configuration or return.

### 14.4 `D` — assured common force — selected

Mission services own distinctive effects. Portfolio services govern chemistry- and custody-bound systems. A National Common Force owns Department-wide utilities and logistics. A sovereign technical estate protects standards, evidence and release. Complete capacity is assigned by enforceable warrants and conserved across time.

**Advantage:** preserves physical service while preventing institutional duplication and false accounting.  
**Cost:** requires a sophisticated allocation constitution, serialized inventory, continuous exercises and a public willingness to state which obligation is left uncovered.

Only Architecture `D` proceeds.

## 16. Financial sensitivity and interpretation

### 16.1 Coincidence factor is a diagnostic, not a forecast

To make the scale of the common-force question visible, the workbook applies a simple coincidence factor to the USD 1.649267746T Class `N` capital of a hypothetical second portfolio with an identical national-common requirement.

| Coincident share | Incremental common capacity | Conditional duplicate capital avoided |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 100% | USD 1.649267746T | USD 0 |
| 75% | USD 1.236950809T | USD 0.412316936T |
| 50% | USD 0.824633873T | USD 0.824633873T |
| 25% | USD 0.412316936T | USD 1.236950809T |
| 0% | USD 0 | USD 1.649267746T |

These figures deliberately omit portfolio-common and sovereign capacity. They assume perfect technical equivalence, geography, timing, crews, maintenance and regeneration. Those assumptions are not true by default. The table is a sensitivity around an unproved boundary, not an estimate.

### 16.2 Why full concurrency can still justify a common force

When two portfolios peak together, common ownership may save no hardware. It can still create value through:

- one public interface regime;
- fewer incompatible technical families;
- shared schools and professional ladders;
- cross-mission production learning;
- common receiver preparation;
- consolidated spares with unique reservations;
- more credible root investment; and
- one auditable national allocation account.

Those benefits must be measured later. They are not monetized here.

### 16.3 Annual and lifecycle costs

`RBW-1` preserves the parent USD 290.465648B annual support and USD 12.19164112T twenty-five-year public cost. Capital jurisdiction does not eliminate operating labor, maintenance, training, exercises, industrial readiness or replacement.

The workbook reallocates costs where the parent basis permits exact tracing and leaves ambiguous workforce functions visible rather than forcing an artificial split.

## 17. Governance and constitutional safeguards

### 17.1 Authorities

| Authority | Owns | Cannot own |
|---|---|---|
| National Civil Resilience Authority | public obligation and conflict decision | technical acceptance |
| Mission Service | effect doctrine and formation readiness | common inventory truth |
| Portfolio Service | chemistry, custody and portfolio modules | independent evidence |
| National Common Force | common families, warrants, assignment and regeneration | final civil priority judgment |
| Family Technical Authority | interface, configuration and substitution law | allocation among populations |
| Independent Evidence Authority | observed claim and uncertainty | operational command |
| Cost and Capacity Authority | cost basis, capacity reconciliation and opportunity cost | service acceptance |
| Public Configuration Authority | baseline, signer, rights and release state | mission priority |

No one body may write the requirement, build the system, certify the result, allocate the asset and erase the failed obligation.

### 17.2 Public obligation ledger

The Department reports three values for every service guarantee:

1. **promised** — the legal and policy obligation;
2. **warranted** — the subset backed by unique accepted capacity; and
3. **uncovered** — the remainder without a complete capacity chain.

The uncovered value may not be rounded to zero, relabeled as a coordination task or hidden inside aggregate inventory.

### 17.3 Emergency authority

Emergency authority may reallocate `W1` and `W2` capacity under the priority constitution. It may not:

- count one article twice;
- recall `W4` or `W5` articles;
- waive a safety or configuration gate;
- convert a Class `S` article into readiness capacity;
- substitute an unaccepted technical family; or
- erase the newly uncovered obligation.

- serial and configuration records are complete;

- the receiving common-force command demonstrates offline operation; and
- the public obligation ledger shows no new uncovered interval.

## 21. Stock of research progress

### 21.1 What has become coherent

The project now contains a continuous derivation from public service to national force structure:

1. the Department’s obligation is defined as population service under damage;
2. mission kernels translate that obligation into physical rates and residuals;
3. complete sets carry water, energy, control, rescue, repair, transport and crews;
4. groups make rotation, reserve and regeneration explicit;
5. production programs create unlike roots, public rights and destructive evidence;
6. the national fire-control service converts one group into a twenty-four-line guarantee; and
7. `NCFC-1` prevents that service from becoming a template for forty-eight duplicate establishments.

This is a meaningful maturation. The concept is no longer mainly a list of platforms or agencies. It is becoming a constitutional system of public obligations, physical service chains, force generation, industrial roots, evidence and conserved allocation.

### 21.2 What remains structurally weak

The largest unresolved weakness is cross-portfolio integration. Fire control is now unusually detailed relative to flood, medical, power, thermal, food, communications and civil continuity. The common-force finding cannot be converted into a national order of battle until those portfolios expose comparable service kernels and concurrent demand worlds.

Other major gaps are:

- no watch-by-watch occupation bill for the national workforce;
- no common receiver architecture at national scale;
- no geographic network analysis for launch, arsenal and production sites;
- no multi-portfolio maintenance and regeneration queue;
- no probabilistic reliability basis for most platform counts;
- no complete Department-wide fiscal bridge from the USD 15.179T force-capital boundary;
- no export-demand or partner-production model; and
- no exterior form eligible for rendering.

### 21.3 Quality correction introduced by this pass

Earlier work sometimes used “common,” “shared,” “assigned” and “available” too loosely. `RBW-1` replaces them with separate title, technical-family, warrant and physical-assignment fields. It also blocks a tempting but unsupported reduction in the fire-control capital account.

This is a methodological gain: the project now has a way to be ambitious about common systems without creating imaginary capacity.
