# RBU-1 — Root A first fire-control group production-line, transport and operational-acceptance constitution

## Decision abstract

`RBU-1` converts the production-equivalent hypothesis in [RBT-1](f36-cisa40-sa6-root-a-production-equivalent-fire-control-group-set-crew-industrial-and-regeneration-constitution.md) into a representative industrial lot and an operational-acceptance campaign. It asks whether one architecture selected by the preceding evidence chain can be made through two independent production roots, assembled into eight operational sets, supported by a qualified transport pool, crewed by a new public formation, regenerated through three arsenals and kept in continuous service while its protected reserve is forced into a second incident.

The program does not assume that a favorable prototype becomes a fleet. Every operational unit is newly produced. The twelve-system lot contains eight group sets, one training system, one independent-evaluation system, one cold reference and reserve system, and one terminal destructive system. Two system builders each complete six systems. The eight operational sets alternate builders so the group does not inherit one production root as its hidden common cause.

The first major correction is industrial. `RBT-1` counted seventeen manufactured hardware families and thirty-four module-root cells, but treated prime movers and load frames as purchased transport. That is inconsistent with the claim that deployment is part of the system. `RBU-1` places the prime mover and governed load frame under the same two-root law. The representative industrial system therefore contains nineteen production families and thirty-eight root cells.

The second correction is financial. Multiplying the USD 8.7357 billion complete-set cost by all twelve system bodies would buy 4,200 prime movers and 3,744 frames even though only eight operational sets can be deployed and the four specialist systems use the common transport estate at different times. `RBU-1` instead finances twelve equipment-and-interface systems at USD 7.6111 billion each, then finances the accepted group transport pool once. This preserves twelve complete testable systems without hiding a second four-set vehicle fleet inside a unit-cost convention.

The production lot creates 4,858 nonconsumable mission modules after including the central group reserve. Each root supplies exactly 2,429. Ten module campuses divide those families into water, fluid distribution and recovery, energy and electric service, intervention and rescue, and command and sustainment clusters. Two separate transport campuses produce the mover and frame roots. Two system-integration campuses, one public standards and challenge plant, and three regional arsenals bring the representative industrial geography to eighteen major sites.

The two system lines run at a sixteen-week completion interval after their first accepted article. Root 1 completes systems in weeks 40, 56, 72, 88, 104 and 120. Root 2 is offset by eight weeks and completes systems in weeks 48, 64, 80, 96, 112 and 128. The eight operational sets are complete by week 96. The training, destructive, evaluation and cold-reference systems finish by week 128. Three consecutive systems from one root demonstrate repeatable execution of a released process. They do not establish mature yield, lifetime reliability or national surge rate.

The accepted transport pool contains 3,200 service prime movers and 3,000 governed load frames. Root 1 and Root 2 each supply half. Sixty-four additional movers and 120 additional frames are terminal and abuse articles. Every service mover completes a loaded 500-kilometre acceptance route; 800 movers also complete an additional 1,500-kilometre route. The resulting program controls 2.8 million loaded vehicle-kilometres before operational group deployment. The eight sets also execute all 112 registered twenty-five-vehicle packet trials.

The group requires 14,220 operational positions. A fifteen-percent qualification-seat reserve adds 2,133 initial seats, producing a 16,353-seat formation pipeline. Qualification remains tied to role, system version, module root, water mode, route class and arsenal release function. A roster does not become a crew because the required number of names exists.

Operational acceptance turns the eight-set rotation into an observed schedule. The primary line issues one order every seventy-two hours. Each order receives eighteen hours for regional transit and establishment, seventy-two hours of service, twenty-four hours for relief and withdrawal, and 336 hours for regeneration. Sets 1 through 7 take the first seven orders. Set 1 returns at hour 450 and can take the eighth primary order at hour 504.

The protected Set 8 receives an unannounced second-incident order at hour 435, when all seven line sets are still occupied. It establishes service at hour 453, operates through hour 525 and returns ready at hour 885. The test proves that the reserve is physically distinct and can start a second response. It also reveals the boundary of the eight-set construct. There is no ready set from hour 435 through 450, and after Set 1 accepts the next primary order at hour 504 there is no ready set until Set 2 returns at hour 522. One eight-set group can absorb the onset of a second incident. It cannot sustain two indefinite seventy-two-hour lines.

The full operational campaign contains twelve commissioning trials, twelve loaded set trials, 112 packet trials, nine seventy-two-hour missions, twenty-four set-casualty trials, nine regeneration releases, three arsenal-denial exercises, twelve outside reconstructions and one terminal full-system destruction. This is 194 registered trials and 648 hours of full mission service. All valid adverse outcomes remain in the record.

The representative capital program is USD 162 billion. It contains USD 91.3332 billion for twelve equipment-and-interface systems, USD 10.4 billion for the 3,200-mover and 3,000-frame service pool, USD 0.256 billion for destructive transport articles, USD 13.706 billion for central module reserve and USD 46.3048 billion for receiver corridors, crew formation, arsenal-denial work, outside reconstruction and operational integration. Peak annual support is USD 12 billion. The mission-led technology portfolio is USD 15 billion.

Work-package reconciliation produces 240 primary weeks and 260 maximum weeks. Cumulative use through `RBU-1` becomes 1,810/1,914 weeks, USD 395.68 billion capital and USD 58.235 billion technology. Concurrent public formation may reach 35,850 positions and annual support USD 22.97 billion. Those figures exceed `RAS-3R10`, so this pass issues `RAS-3R11`: 2,200 weeks, 45,000 public positions, USD 450 billion capital, USD 27 billion per year and USD 80 billion technology.

Acceptance may authorize `RBV-1`, the national quantity, regional basing and readiness-allocation constitution. It does not authorize operational fielding, export, national fleet quantity or an exterior rendering. The formation has now become specific enough to ask how many one-line and two-line groups the nation requires, but not specific enough to freeze vehicle form.

## 2. Decision object

The decision object is one **first fire-control group**, not a procurement quantity. It contains:

- eight serial operational sets;
- a 3,200-prime-mover service pool;
- a 3,000-load-frame service pool;
- the group module reserve;
- three regional arsenals;
- one group headquarters and operations element;
- one formation school and standards unit;
- an outside-evaluation element;
- industrial-readiness and configuration authorities; and
- access to four specialist system bodies that are not counted as line sets.

The group is accepted only if these pieces operate as one public-service formation. Passing module inspections without closing group chronology does not qualify the group. Completing the chronology without production-root evidence does not qualify industry. A factory and a formation are separate objects joined by configuration and return authority.

### 2.1 One architecture per group

One eight-set group uses one system architecture. If `RBS-1` admits two architectures, the second may form a later group under its own campaign. Mixing architectures inside the first group is prohibited unless an additional interchangeability program demonstrates common:

1. mission interfaces;
2. packet composition;
3. crew qualifications;
4. spare and repair state;
5. energy and water connections;
6. command and safeing grammar;
7. arsenal tooling;
8. evidence schema; and
9. release authority.

Architectural diversity can reduce a fleet-wide defect. It can also double training, spares, software, arsenal and coordination burdens. The first group therefore isolates the production-root question from the architecture-mix question.

### 2.2 What acceptance can establish

The campaign may establish that:

- two roots can build the released architecture;
- the first operational lot conforms to the registered bill;
- transport is adequate for the governed packets and routes;
- crews can establish and operate the service chain;
- arsenals can return used sets within the registered interval;
- a reserve can launch while seven line sets are occupied;
- outside authorities can reconstruct the relevant claims; and
- the public configuration system can reject an unsafe substitution.

It cannot establish national demand, mature reliability, twenty-five-year cost, export suitability, climate-loss reduction, or superiority to a future architecture.

## 4. Production-family correction

### 4.1 Nineteen controlled families

`RBT-1` required two roots for seventeen nonconsumable mission-module families. `RBU-1` adds:

- `PM-30`, the governed 30-tonne-payload prime mover; and
- `LF-30`, the multimode governed load frame and restraint system.

This yields:

\[
19\ \text{families}\times2\ \text{roots}=38\ \text{production-root cells}.
\]

The correction matters because a common mover powertrain, braking controller, tire class, restraint casting or frame weld process can immobilize every otherwise diverse module. Transport cannot remain a commercial afterthought inside a claim about national projection.

### 4.2 Consumables and non-root items

Mission energy, dry-entry water and transit energy have independent source, quality and custody programs. They are not counted as nonconsumable production families. Their containers, interfaces, meters and safety devices remain inside the relevant hardware roots.

Software is configuration-controlled across every family and system root. It does not become a twentieth factory family because the decisive issue is unlike implementation and recoverability within each physical root. A future software-supply study may split it into its own roots if observed common-cause evidence requires that change.

### 5.1 Serial allocation rule

Operational systems contain complete serial manifests. A Root 1 system builder does not have to use only Root 1 modules. That would make system and module roots perfectly correlated. The allocation authority instead balances module roots across serials while preserving:

1. complete traceability;
2. at least one unlike module-root path for each essential service in every operational pair;
3. no untested hybrid interface;
4. no undocumented root substitution; and
5. enough root-pure configurations to localize factory effects.

The detailed serial allocation remains a pre-build artifact. The present bill sets populations, not a hidden randomization.

### 5.2 Production reserve is not installed excess

The 610 reserve modules sit in three arsenals under serial control. They are not added to the twelve system bodies and not counted twice as installed redundancy. A reserve module earns availability only when:

- its storage state is current;
- the receiving system accepts its root and software state;
- a qualified crew can install and test it;
- the relevant arsenal or field authority can release it; and
- replenishment does not violate the protected-root allocation.

### 6.3 Site-loss meaning

Losing one clustered module campus may remove several families from one root. It does not erase the opposite roots. The consequence is slower replenishment and a narrower correction path, not immediate loss of the whole formation.

- the opposite root’s unique raw material;
- its only inspector or calibration standard;
- the common build-traveler service;
- the public signing authority;
- the only repair instruction;
- both transport roots; or
- the ability to reproduce a serviceable unit from the public package.

### 6.4 Public standards plant

The public standards and challenge plant does not build ordinary fleet units. It holds reference artifacts, blind-defect capability, material witnesses, public interface masters, controlled software states and transfer fixtures. It can inspect both roots but cannot release its own disputed finding without outside review.

The plant is deliberately separate from the integration campuses. A builder must encounter an independent receiver rather than certify its own outgoing inspection as public acceptance.

The other root must reproduce or independently reject the causal mechanism. Administrative similarity cannot substitute for a physical result.

### 8.3 Loaded vehicle acceptance

\[

\[

No service frame gains credit from a terminal article’s survival. The terminal result defines mechanism and inspection requirements.

### 11.4 Operational inference

The experiment supports a narrow claim if every gate closes:

> One eight-set group can maintain one seventy-two-hour primary start line and absorb the onset of one second incident while all seven line sets are occupied.

> One eight-set group can maintain two continuous lines.

Two indefinite lines would require at least fourteen line sets before protected reserve, because each line independently needs seven sets at the inherited 450-hour occupied interval. Shared arsenals, transport, crew and regional access would then need their own casualty derivation. `RBV-1` must decide whether the national force buys separate one-line groups, larger two-line formations or a layered combination.

## 12. Regeneration and three-arsenal exercise

### 12.1 Bay demand

The primary line creates 336 regeneration hours every seventy-two hours:

\[
336/72=4.667\ \text{average occupied bays}.
\]

Nine bays across three arsenals operate at 51.9 percent average utilization. One-arsenal loss leaves six bays at 77.8 percent. The reserve mission adds one unscheduled regeneration demand and tests whether the surviving system can absorb a temporary queue without changing the release standard.

### 12.2 Exercise phases

Each arsenal-denial exercise has five phases:

1. **normal receipt** — all three arsenals receive serial sets and modules;
2. **no-notice denial** — one arsenal, its inventory and its personnel become unavailable;
3. **custody reconciliation** — the group identifies usable, quarantined and inaccessible state;
4. **residual return** — the two surviving arsenals execute complete return work; and
5. **independent release** — neither surviving arsenal depends on the denied site for its decisive measurement or signature.

The program runs three exercises so every arsenal becomes the denied site once.

### 12.3 Reserve distribution

The module reserve is split across all three arsenals and across root identity. No arsenal receives all units of one family or one root. Exact integer allocations may be unequal, but loss of any site must leave:

- at least one usable replacement path for every essential family;
- no single-root monopoly created by the loss;
- enough trunkline and liquid-custody reserve for one full mission correction;
- an energy and control recovery path;
- repair instructions and tooling outside the denied site; and
- a lawful public release authority.

### 12.4 Regeneration is not maintenance completion

A set returns ready only when it has:

- a complete serial inventory;
- closed contamination and liquid accounts;
- restored energy and water interfaces;
- completed structural, electrical and software inspection;
- replaced or dispositioned every rejected unit;
- reconciled crew exposure and qualification;
- preserved adverse evidence;
- loaded the next mission configuration; and
- received independent release.

A closed work order does not by itself satisfy these conditions.

A weighted score cannot compensate for a failed gate. Schedule, cost or aesthetic appeal cannot buy acceptance of an unsafe or unreproducible root. The board may narrow a claim, repeat a registered trial under its repeat law, redesign the system or deny the program.

### 17.1 Technology admission

Technology funding buys rival paths, instruments and failure discovery. It does not lower the release standard. A new autonomous feature enters operational use only when:

- its authority boundary is explicit;
- local safe states work without remote service;
- a person can identify and interrupt unsafe action;
- cyber and corrupted-data cases are tested;
- logs survive power and network loss;

The relevant economic output is not patents alone. It is an industrial ecology able to reproduce complete civilian service under disrupted infrastructure.

## 20. Stop and branch law

The program stops, narrows or branches if:

- no architecture exits `RBT-1`;
- one group mixes architectures without interchangeability evidence;
- a development system is relabeled as a production serial;
- transport remains outside the two-root production law;
- a specialist system is counted as an operational line set;
- one root supplies only cosmetic diversity;
- the 4,858-module lot does not reconcile;
- system-line completion depends on hidden design-team labor;
- the service transport pool is double-counted through the twelve-set unit convention;
- a packet loses all of an essential service;
- vehicle-kilometre exposure is represented as a reliability bound;
- crew seats are counted before task qualification;
- `FG-08` borrows parts, crew or vehicles from an occupied line set;
- arsenal denial changes the release standard;
- an adverse record is removed after a repeat;
- outside reconstruction depends on the builder’s only copy;
- the terminal system is spared or replaced;
- a weighted score offsets a failed safety or public-service gate;
- the parent capacity is exceeded without amendment; or
- exterior form is selected before observed production and operational acceptance.

If one root fails while the other closes, the board may authorize a bounded single-root continuation for learning. It may not label that state a root-diverse operational group. If the eight-set chronology fails but individual systems work, the board may preserve systems and redesign force quantity. Failure should locate the claim that broke rather than erase useful evidence.

## 21. Research stocktake after Pass 157

### 21.1 What became authoritative

This pass adds a coherent first-group hypothesis:

1. one architecture per initial eight-set group;
2. twelve new system bodies with four specialist roles outside line readiness;
3. two system roots, six serials each and an eight-week offset;
4. nineteen dual-root production families after transport correction;
5. 4,858 mission modules, divided equally across roots;
6. eighteen major production, standards, integration and arsenal sites;
7. 3,200 service movers and 3,000 service frames;
8. 2.8 million loaded vehicle-kilometres;
9. 112 complete packet trials;
10. a 16,353-seat initial formation pipeline;
11. an eight-order primary chronology and forced reserve mission;
12. 194 registered operational-acceptance trials;
13. USD 162 billion capital, USD 12 billion annual support and USD 15 billion technology; and
14. a 240/260-week release-aware program inside `RAS-3R11`.

### 21.2 What the pass corrected

The research changed in four important ways.

First, prime movers and load frames became sovereign production families. A force that cannot reproduce its transport is not independent merely because its mission modules have two suppliers.

Second, the cost model stopped multiplying permanently paired transport across specialist systems that use the common pool at different times. The correction reduces hidden vehicle duplication while making scheduling conflict visible.

Third, the rotation schedule now distinguishes order, effect, service end and return. This resolves the earlier ambiguous use of “start” and makes the eighteen-hour establishment interval explicit.

Fourth, the protected reserve is no longer an unused eighth icon. The hour-435 second incident forces it into service and exposes two intervals with no ready reserve. The formation’s limit is now as important as its capability.

### 21.3 What remains unresolved

The research still lacks observed values for:

- architecture selection;
- serial mass, volume and center of gravity;
- factory yield and rework distribution;
- production-root independence;
- vehicle energy use and route availability;
- packet delay and failure distributions;
- crew learning, attrition and exposure;
- regeneration duration and bay service distribution;
- module replacement rate;
- two-incident duration and geographic correlation;
- public receiver construction demand;
- national quantity and placement;
- lifetime replacement and operating cost; and
- exterior geometry.

All corresponding evidence gates remain `OPEN`.

### 21.4 Quality assessment

The program has advanced beyond a list of imagined equipment. The fire-control thread now connects a reference phenomenon, operational mission, complete set, crew, transport, factories, arsenals, operating chronology, evidence authority, cost and parent capacity. Each stage can falsify the next.

The remaining weakness is scale allocation. One eight-set group is now physically and institutionally legible, but it says nothing about how many simultaneous domestic or invited missions the Department should guarantee. National force design cannot be obtained by multiplying one group by an arbitrary regional count.

The next pass should therefore begin with service guarantees and correlated-hazard demand, then derive force quantities backward through transit time, one-line and two-line formations, geographic basing, maintenance, training, national reserve, industrial replenishment and strategic transport. It should preserve uncertainty rather than hide it inside one fleet number.
