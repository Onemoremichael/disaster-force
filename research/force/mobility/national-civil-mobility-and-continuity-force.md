# The National Civil Mobility and Continuity Force

## Turning evacuation from an improvised transaction into an instrument of national power

> **Pass 33 decision:** The Department of Resilience should not own an evacuation program or a transport pool. It should generate a standing civil-mobility service whose unit of performance is a person entering complete service—with access, health, household, custody and return continuity intact—through a damaged national network.

> **Pass 34 supersession:** The force concept remains, but the restorable mesh no longer stands as the national reference. [Civil Mobility Assurance Under Deep Uncertainty](mobility-assurance-under-deep-uncertainty.md) reduces `A2` from 98.8-percent success in this fixed distribution to 7.6 percent of 1,000 broad parameter worlds. Protected-local continuity becomes the load-bearing architecture; `A2` remains its rescue, selective-relocation and return subsystem. The first `CTN-10` configuration also supersedes the 220-person crew hypothesis with 2,289 deployed staff.

> **Current assurance boundary:** the New York replication has now registered the complete empty result surface and its first hierarchical stress-world generator. That generator is a design-space instrument, not a probability model, and neither architecture has been executed. Civil-mobility architecture remains an open research question until the remaining `L0` data, custody, alternate-dependence and independent-reproduction gates close.

The [National Receiving Geography](../reception/national-receiving-geography.md) established that nationally owned capacity is not national service. Under its concurrent domestic stress, only 2,355.80 certified district-equivalents (`CDE`) survived against 2,500 demanded. A provisional road, rail, air and water corridor plan raised closure from 61.0 to 82.7 percent, but the corridor enterprise itself remained a coefficient: no actual network gates, population needs, vehicle classes, transfer nodes, crews, restoration formations or joint route-receiver failures.

This study replaces that coefficient with a first force design. It uses federal transportation layers to decide where a mode can enter the screen, 2024 American Community Survey estimates to distinguish movement requirements, and four rival architectures to expose the balance among local reception, accessible fleet, terminal capacity and route restoration. It then subjects each architecture to 500 fixed-seed trials in which receiver loss, route and terminal damage, campaign severity and mode-family impairment vary together.

The result changes the program again.

- A 2.5-million-person campaign creates **2,652.63 CDE of movement-place demand** after devices, caregivers, clinical teams and custody burdens are recognized.
- The purpose-built reference fleet contains **12,000 road cells, 600 trainsets, 400 civil airlifters and 180 waterborne bridge vessels**, surrounded by 160 transfer nodes, 160 route-assurance groups, sixteen commands and thirty-two distributed bases.
- Those elements have **$927 billion** of line-item capital before reserve, **$1.159 trillion** after a twenty-five-percent planning reserve and **443,080 direct crew-and-maintenance billets** before relief, training and affiliate multipliers.
- A universal-access architecture averages **97.8-percent national closure** but passes the joint distributional rule in only **13.8 percent** of trials.
- A restorable multimodal mesh is the first architecture to cross the provisional ninety-five-percent admission threshold, passing **98.8 percent** of trials. It carries roughly **$2.351 trillion in combined mobility-and-receiver capital**, **$166.7 billion in mature annual operations** and **$4.987 trillion over twenty-five years** under the common planning rules.
- That numerical admission is conditional on unproved hardening, terminal survival, route restoration, accessible loading and destination intake. It authorizes testing, not acquisition or rendering.

The governing finding is not that the United States needs more buses. It is that **civil mobility is a complete public-service system and an independent operating service**.

## 1. The mission is continuity, not departure

Evacuation metrics usually stop too early. A warning was issued, a bus departed or a boundary was crossed. The Department's service chain cannot.

> accessible notice and lawful authority → door-to-node collection → identity and household continuity → clinical or custodial regulation → protected transfer → line-haul → destination intake → complete service → return or durable continuity

Every arrow is an operational dependency and a rights boundary. If a person cannot leave a fourth-floor apartment after elevator loss, an available aircraft is irrelevant. If a mobility device cannot cross the rail-air interface, the second vehicle contributes zero end-to-end capacity. If a patient has no matched receiving bed, movement transfers clinical risk. If a family cannot discover where its members were sent, aggregate throughput has concealed a public harm. If temporary protection has no funded return or durable settlement path, response has become unmanaged displacement.

This mission has three distinct physical layers:

1. **Collection** brings people, devices, caregivers, service animals, medicines, records and essential property from doors and institutions to a protected node.
2. **Line-haul** moves compatible groups across regions by road, rail, air and water while sustaining health, sanitation, communication and custody.
3. **Transfer and reception** preserve continuity across mode changes and admit arrivals into a receiver that can actually supply complete service.

It also has two sovereign layers: a dispatch-and-rights authority before movement, and a recovery-and-return authority after it. Neither can be delegated to a vehicle operator.

## 2. What the evidence can and cannot say

The study separates observed footprint from designed performance.

| Evidence layer | What enters the model | What it can support | What it cannot support |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Population | Census Vintage 2025 denominator; 2024 ACS disability, vehicle and group-quarters estimates | geographic prevalence and screening cohorts | individual need, willingness or incident demand |
| Roads | NTAD National Highway Planning Network county coverage | presence of a major-road footprint along a catchment path | current condition, bridge survival, traffic or passenger throughput |
| Rail | 2026 North American Rail Network links and shared FRA nodes | a physical rail-topology screen | trackage rights, inspected track, loading, signaling or emergency passenger capacity |
| Air | 390 primary-airport records joined to aviation-facility attributes | primary-airport endpoints | usable pavement, fuel, airspace, terminal, crew or sortie rate |
| Water | 6,255 waterway nodes, 529 port nodes, 150 principal ports and 845 ferry terminals | navigable footprint and terminal presence | draft, berth, sea state, channel, passenger safety or weather availability |
| Passenger interface | 15,488 IPCD facilities, including 8,539 records with at least two modes | existing intermodal presence | accessible transfer rate or current emergency readiness |
| Performance | declared asset cycles, survival parameters and fixed-seed allocation | a falsifiable architecture comparison | forecast, validated probability or acquisition baseline |

The federal layers produce 3,968 ordered origin-destination-mode rows across thirty-two Service Catchment Areas. Of 992 possible ordered pairs per mode, 476 road, 596 rail, 992 air and 142 water paths enter the seventy-two-hour screen. Air's apparent completeness is a warning, not a triumph: great-circle time between primary airports omits runway damage, slot competition, ground access, aircraft position, fuel and destination queues.

The National Highway Planning Network service describes a 2014-vintage network despite a later service edit. It is retained only as a major-road coverage gate. A later pass must replace it with a current routable road graph and bridge-level fragility. Similarly, nearest-catchment assignment locates point facilities for a national screen; it is not a parcel intersection or siting claim.

## 3. Population is not homogeneous cargo

The model builds five mutually exclusive planning cohorts through an explicit precedence rule. This is a capacity-accounting device, not a classification of people.

| Cohort | National people | Share | Movement places per person | Governing burden |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Clinical continuity | 3.49M | 1.0% | 2.00 | monitored berth, clinical team, equipment, records and matched receiving care |
| Accessible support | 44.33M | 12.9% | 1.35 | lift, restraint, accessible toilet and communication, device, service animal and caregiver continuity |
| Institutional/custodial | 2.08M | 0.6% | 1.20 | origin authority, escort staff, identity, safeguarding and destination custody |
| No-household-vehicle | 16.41M | 4.8% | 1.10 | door-to-node collection and household grouping after modeled disability overlap |
| General household | 278.66M | 80.8% | 1.00 | family unity, accessible information, essential property and return still apply |

The clinical cohort combines state-distributed nursing-facility prevalence with an assumed four percent of the civilian noninstitutionalized disability cohort. That four-percent coefficient is deliberately visible and unvalidated. The accessible cohort receives the remaining disability prevalence. The no-vehicle count converts one-, two-, three- and four-or-more-person households into people; the last category uses a 4.5-person coefficient and removes a modeled disability overlap. Institutional rates are state-level because the selected ACS table is not available at county level in the table-based file used here.

These compromises matter. ACS disability status is not an evacuation-needs assessment. No-vehicle status does not imply immobility, and vehicle ownership does not imply a safe vehicle, fuel, driver or route. “General” does not mean self-evacuating. The purpose of the cohorts is to prevent a national seat count from silently excluding needs that consume space, time, personnel or a specialized chain.

Applied to the 2,500-CDE campaign, the place factors add 152.63 CDE of movement burden. Clinical continuity alone consumes twice its person count. The correction is modest nationally but decisive at constrained interfaces, because clinical and accessible shares of each mode bind before total fleet capacity does.

## 4. The force is a system of systems

The reference order of battle is sized as an architecture hypothesis. Each element has a complete operational role and a kill criterion; no exterior geometry has been selected.

| Element | Inventory | Gross 72-hour throughput | Line capital | Operational role |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| `ACM-60` accessible road cell | 12,000 | 1,440 CDE-place | $42B | door-to-node collection and regional line-haul in interchangeable seated, lift and litter configurations |
| `MCT-1200` continuity trainset | 600 | 1,440 CDE-place | $90B | high-density accessible movement across freight- and passenger-compatible track |
| `ACA-250` civil airlifter | 400 | 350 CDE-place | $180B | purpose-built austere passenger and clinical bridge; not a converted cargo fleet |
| `WCB-1800` waterborne bridge vessel | 180 | 324 CDE-place | $63B | shallow-draft movement without dependence on an intact deepwater passenger berth |
| `CTN-10` transfer node | 128 | 3,840 CDE intake | $96B | self-sufficient collection, triage, identity, sanitation and onward assignment |
| `CTN-50` theater transfer node | 32 | 4,800 CDE intake | $96B | metropolitan and territorial interchange with clinical regulation and reception control |
| Network restoration and route-assurance group | 160 | enabling capacity | $160B | inspect, clear, bridge, signal, energize and certify roads, track and terminals |
| Mobility, continuity and custody command | 16 | command capacity | $60B | dispatch, consent, patient regulation, rights, records, family unity and return |
| Distributed mobility base and depot | 32 | generation capacity | $140B | energy, heavy maintenance, training, crew regeneration, stores and dispersal |

The four moving fleets total 3,554 CDE-place of gross seventy-two-hour throughput. That number is intentionally larger than the campaign demand and intentionally not reported as service capacity. Readiness, mode-family impairment, accessible and clinical module shares, surviving paths, collection, destination intake and receiving capacity all reduce it.

### 4.1 Accessible Civil Mobility Road Cell (`ACM-60`)

The road element is not a bus specification. It is a sixty-person equivalent cell with interchangeable seated, mobility-device and litter positions; independent boarding power; accessible sanitation for long movements; local obstacle sensing; disconnected navigation; resilient communications; and operator-supervised automation. It must perform collection and line-haul without forcing a choice between vehicle density and the person's device or caregiver.

Its two long-haul missions in seventy-two hours are an open requirement. Driver rest, energy or fuel, cleaning, clinical turnaround, road delay and maintenance could break it.

### 4.2 Modular Continuity Trainset (`MCT-1200`)

The trainset treats the continental rail footprint as a strategic civil reserve without pretending existing passenger stations are sufficient. It needs deployable low- and high-platform interfaces, universal modules, accessible sanitation, onboard clinical zones, independent hotel power, temporary signaling integration and railroad-compatible braking and clearance.

The Department would require pre-negotiated access and compensation, not unilateral seizure. A physical line without lawful trackage rights and a certified route is unavailable.

### 4.3 Accessible Civil Airlifter (`ACA-250`)

The airlifter is purpose-built around mixed-needs passenger movement from an austere field. Its notional 250-person mission may exchange seats for monitored berths without changing the surrounding aircraft, loading or custody system. The force objective is not maximum payload. It is safe turnaround from shorter, degraded runways with independent loading, low ground-equipment dependence, high crosswind tolerance, rapid cleaning, medical power and a maintainable national fleet.

The current line assumes 3.5 missions per aircraft in seventy-two hours. Until mass, range, runway bearing, fuel, crew duty, maintenance and accessible boarding close together, `ACA-250` is a research program rather than an aircraft.

### 4.4 Waterborne Civil Bridge (`WCB-1800`)

The waterborne element serves islands, coasts, rivers and divided estuaries when land routes or deepwater berths fail. It requires shallow draft, redundant propulsion, independent embarkation, protected passenger spaces, clinical zones, sanitation, device storage at point of use and ship-to-shore transfer across damaged waterfronts.

One 1,800-person sortie in seventy-two hours is the screen. Sea state, channel condition, berth geometry, evacuation time, stability under mixed loading and waste custody remain open.

### 4.5 Continuity Transfer Nodes (`CTN-10` and `CTN-50`)

Transfer nodes are the architecture's decisive fixed-mobile interface. They must operate without grid, cloud, water or sewer; maintain accessible queuing; register and triage arrivals; preserve household, device and clinical identity; supply food, sanitation and medicines; regulate destinations; and release people into the next mode.

The threshold classes are 10,000 and 50,000 people per day. Those rates are not terminal-footprint estimates. They are targets for occupied proving events. If a transfer node cannot sustain its rate for seven days under disconnected conditions, the fleet must be reduced to the node's measured capacity.

## 5. A new operating service inside the Department

Pass 32 proposed a National Civil Mobility and Continuity Command. This pass raises the function to a **Civil Mobility and Continuity Service** within the Department of Resilience, with the national command as its joint operational headquarters.

The service requires six permanent communities:

1. **Ground collection and mobility**, including accessible vehicle operations, neighborhood collection, route reconnaissance and terminal approach.
2. **Rail continuity**, including train crews, railroad integration, deployable platforms, temporary signaling and network-rights cadres.
3. **Civil air mobility**, including flight, clinical cabin, austere ground operations, airspace coordination and independent sustainment.
4. **Water mobility**, including vessel crews, littoral and river pilots, embarkation teams, hydrographic reconnaissance and waterfront repair.
5. **Transfer, regulation and rights**, including patient regulation, accessibility, interpreters, identity, safeguarding, destination matching, appeals, family unity and return casework.
6. **Network assurance and regeneration**, including inspection, bridging, track and signal repair, depots, energy, schoolhouses, test and reserve generation.

The direct fleet, node, restoration, command and depot screen contains 443,080 positions. Relief and training factors increase the active establishment to about 1.026 million; reserve and affiliate factors produce about 3.444 million billet-equivalents. These are not headcount recommendations. They reveal that a force expected to operate continuously across several catastrophes cannot be staffed as a small headquarters supervising contractors.

The service also needs independent checks. A civil-rights and accessibility authority can stop an unsafe movement plan. A clinical authority controls patient regulation. A network-safety authority certifies restored routes. A privacy and data authority governs registries and matching. Host governments and sovereign partners retain their lawful roles. Operational urgency does not merge these authorities into the dispatch chain.

## 6. The stochastic architecture competition

Four rivals are tested against the same 2,500-CDE campaign.

| Architecture | Receiver scale | Mobility scale | Main proposition | Mean closure | Bottom-decile mean | Joint success | 25-year burden |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `A0` Borrowed-fleet baseline | 1.00× | 0.32× | Preserve the Pass 32 order-of-magnitude corridor budget and use existing-compatible assets | 67.1% | 33.9% | 0.0% | $0.595T |
| `A1` Universal-access force | 1.50× | 1.00× | Build the full accessible fleet and transfer-node chain | 97.8% | 81.7% | 13.8% | $4.240T |
| `A2` Restorable multimodal mesh | 1.60× | 1.25× | Add receiver hardening, larger fleet, duplicate terminals and route restoration | 100.0% rounded | 99.8% | 98.8% | $4.987T |
| `A3` Protected-local continuity | 4.20× | 0.60× | Buy dense local reception and retain a smaller rescue-and-return force | 100.0% | 100.0% | 100.0% | $6.299T |

Each architecture receives 500 trials beginning with seed 330905. Campaign severity is shared across affected catchments. Receiver survival, terminal and route availability and mode-family impairment respond to that common stress plus local variation. Protected cohorts are allocated first; within each cohort, the lowest local closure is served first. One person consumes the cohort's movement-place factor. Destinations cannot accept more than surviving receiver spare and intake capacity.

A trial succeeds only when three conditions hold simultaneously:

- total national closure is at least ninety-nine percent;
- every protected cohort closes at least ninety-seven percent nationally; and
- the population-weighted bottom demand decile across affected SCAs closes at least ninety-five percent.

This is an architecture screen, not a statistical estimate. The 500 trials quantify only pseudo-random variation under the declared parameters. They do not include uncertainty about those parameters, model form or data error.

### 6.1 Why `A1` fails despite a high average

`A1` fully serves the protected cohorts in the national aggregate and has a 97.8-percent mean closure. It still fails admission in 86.2 percent of trials. Its mean bottom-decile closure is 81.7 percent and its fifth-percentile bottom-decile result is only 66.1 percent.

The failure is geographic concentration. A fleet can use spare capacity somewhere and still repeatedly lose the terminal or route needed by the same origin. Adding accessible modules repairs exclusion by vehicle configuration; it does not repair network covariance.

This is the most important methodological result of the pass:

> National closure is a necessary accounting identity and an inadequate public promise. A force must prove who remains unserved, where, and under which shared failure.

### 6.2 Why `A2` advances—and why that is not validation

`A2` raises the receiver lattice to 1.60 times baseline, the mobility fleet to 1.25 times reference, the receiver-hardening factor to thirty percent, route survival to eighty-eight percent, terminal survival to ninety-two percent and failed-route restoration to sixty percent. Under those assumptions it passes 494 of 500 trials.

The result is useful because it identifies the buying logic: more vehicles alone are not the difference. The architecture advances only when the Department also buys surviving destinations, deployable terminals, route repair, disconnected dispatch and common-mode control.

But the apparent precision is conditional. A six-trial failure count does not validate 98.8-percent real-world assurance. Parameter uncertainty, tract-scale access, queueing, behavioral response, weather and rights refusal could reverse the ranking. `A2` advances to an adversarial research program, not a procurement milestone.

### 6.3 Why retain `A3`

The protected-local architecture passes every declared trial because its receiver density overwhelms the modeled geography. Its $6.299-trillion twenty-five-year burden is about $1.312 trillion greater than `A2`, while reducing reliance on mass relocation.

That difference is not waste by definition. If future evidence shows that route restoration is slower, coercion risks are higher, people cannot be moved safely at modeled rates or anti-correlated local sites are feasible, `A3` may dominate. Architecture competition must therefore preserve local protection rather than treating mobility as the inevitable answer.

## 7. Economics and industrial mobilization

The purpose-built mobility force has $927 billion of itemized capital before reserve:

- $375 billion for the four moving fleets;
- $192 billion for transfer nodes;
- $160 billion for route-restoration groups;
- $60 billion for commands and civil data/custody systems; and
- $140 billion for distributed bases and depots.

A twenty-five-percent planning reserve yields $1.159 trillion. Mature mobility operations are provisionally set at 7.5 percent of reserved capital, or $86.9 billion per year. These values are not independent estimates. They intentionally include enough institutional mass to prevent the vehicle price from masquerading as system cost.

`A2` combines 1.25 mobility forces with 1.60 receiving lattices. It reaches $2.351 trillion of capital and $166.7 billion of mature annual operations. Applying the Pass 31 operating ramp produces $4.987 trillion over twenty-five years.

That scale creates a new industrial economy:

- accessible vehicle and common-module manufacturing;
- railcar, signaling and deployable-platform production;
- a civil transport-aircraft prime and propulsion/systems supplier base;
- shallow-draft shipyards and passenger-safety systems;
- modular terminal utilities, sanitation and information systems;
- bridge, track, pavement and terminal sensing and rapid repair;
- national depots, training ranges and certified maintenance networks; and
- privacy-preserving civil identity, dispatch and capacity software.

The export product is not surplus hardware. It is a licensed service architecture: modules, fleets, nodes, standards, training, spares, software, safety cases and sovereign-operating agreements that partner nations can own. Domestic industrial diversity must be designed into the common technical architecture so that universal interfaces do not create a single vendor, software stack or component bottleneck.

## 8. Technology programs pulled by the failure chain

The Department should run competing programs against operational metrics rather than choose forms prematurely.

### Universal continuity module

A common protected-person module should cross road, rail, air and water interfaces without separating the occupant from essential equipment or requiring a new restraint and identity process. The objective is an eighty-percent reduction in transfer dwell. Any concept that gains density by separating family, device, caregiver or clinical support fails.

### Autonomous collection mesh

The program combines supervised autonomous vehicles, human operators, building egress teams, local maps, obstacle clearing and disconnected dispatch. The threshold is 10,000 complete pickups per day per cell when GNSS, cloud and traffic control are degraded. Autonomy that excludes unusual bodies, wheelchairs, service animals or chaotic curb conditions is not operational autonomy.

### Damage-tolerant civil rail bridge

The unit of merit is restored 1,200-person train paths per day. The program joins rapid structure and track inspection, temporary signaling, deployable passenger loading, common modules and pre-negotiated railroad access. A trainset without a certified path and destination slot is inventory, not capacity.

### Austere accessible air bridge

The aircraft, boarding equipment, cabin modules, medical power, turnaround system, crews, airspace and destination release must be tested as one chain. The threshold is a 250-person mixed-needs sortie from a 1,500-metre-class runway; the objective is four safe cycles per day. Aircraft design begins only after the mission configuration closes.

### Shallow-draft civil bridge

The threshold is 1,800 people per sortie without an intact deepwater berth. The experiment must include sea state, damaged waterfront, sanitation, medical custody, evacuation, stability and ship-to-shore transfer. A ferry-like hull picture is not progress.

### Self-proving route restoration

Machines should inspect structures, propose repairs and produce machine-readable evidence, while an independent authority retains the power to certify safety. The threshold is inspection and certified temporary repair inside twelve hours. Automation that can declare its own route safe fails the governance requirement.

### Continuity digital twin

Every receiver slot, route, vehicle, person need and custody transfer needs provenance and local validation under disconnection. The system must degrade safely when data are stale or contradictory. A centralized optimizer that becomes authoritative when the network is least trustworthy is a common-mode hazard.

### Reunification and return system

Destination assignment should create a return or durable-continuity case at the same moment. Persistent household records, claims, transportation reserve and host-community capacity must survive for months. Throughput purchased by making people administratively disappear is a system failure.

## 9. Rights and legitimacy set the usable capacity

The model gives protected cohorts priority but does not yet reduce flows for consent refusal, missing compacts, privacy limits or household separation. Those omissions are evidence gaps, not permission to ignore them.

The Department should adopt four capacity rules:

1. **No door-to-node closure, no passenger credit.** Accessible vehicles at a distant assembly point do not serve people who cannot reach it.
2. **No compatible end-to-end chain, no mode credit.** The least accessible interface bounds the journey.
3. **No lawful destination match, no dispatch credit.** Patient regulation, institutional custody, Tribal and territorial sovereignty, interstate authority and receiver admission precede movement.
4. **No funded return or durable option, no completion credit.** Departure is an intermediate event, not the public effect.

The Justice Department's ADA emergency guidance requires accessible emergency programs and attention to transportation, shelter, medication, equipment, services and communication. DOT guidance explicitly identifies older adults, people in group settings, people with disabilities and people without personal transportation, and calls for door-to-door service where required. HHS patient-movement doctrine joins transport to patient regulation, matched aircraft and destination reception. This force scales those chains; it does not replace their rights or clinical logic with a throughput target.

## 11. Evidence gates and next frontier

Twenty-two gates remain open. The most consequential are:

- a linked, disability-led and operator-reviewed model of who needs which chain;
- a current routable road graph with bridge, terrain and traffic failure;
- railroad rights, clearances, loading, signaling and damaged-route trials;
- airport pavement, fuel, airspace, terminal and austere-operation fragility;
- hydrographic, berth, sea-state and passenger-safety evidence;
- seven-day occupied tests of 10,000- and 50,000-person transfer nodes;
- complete mass, energy, structure, stability, crew and maintenance closure for all four fleet classes;
- fitted joint distributions for receiver, route, utility, communications and labor damage;
- parameter-uncertainty and model-form analysis around the apparent 98.8-percent `A2` result;
- tract- and person-scale equity under consent, household and clinical constraints;
- labor-market, schoolhouse, fatigue and industrial regeneration models; and
- independent cost, schedule, environmental and community-burden estimates.

The next pass should not draw the aircraft or vessel. It should attack `A2` in three ways:

1. replace fixed probabilities with broad parameter distributions and find the combinations that reverse the architecture ranking;
2. move from SCA averages to tract-scale collection and door-to-berth journeys, including time-dependent queues; and
3. configure one transfer node and one common passenger module deeply enough to establish mass, energy, staffing, sanitation, information and safety budgets.

Only if that work preserves the architecture should vehicle reference configuration begin. Rendering remains prohibited.
