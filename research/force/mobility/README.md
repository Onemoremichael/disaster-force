# Civil mobility and continuity

This subsystem owns the movement of people into complete service when remaining in place is unsafe or impossible.

It is deliberately separate from logistics. Moving a service pod toward a damaged region and moving a person away from one are not inverse versions of the same problem. Population movement carries consent, accessibility, clinical regulation, family unity, custody, destination admission and return obligations that cargo movement does not.

[The National Civil Mobility and Continuity Force](national-civil-mobility-and-continuity-force.md) is the current reference. It replaces Pass 32's abstract corridor multiplier with observed federal network gates, five mutually exclusive planning cohorts, purpose-built fleet and transfer-node classes, a workforce and fiscal account, and 500 joint route-receiver trials for each of four rival architectures.

The central result is distributional. A universal-access fleet can produce a high national average while leaving the same geographic tail repeatedly unserved. The reference architecture therefore combines accessible vehicles with self-sufficient transfer nodes, route-restoration groups, hardened receivers, disconnected dispatch and a return system.

Current maturity is `R1/R2` with `R2+` arithmetic. The model admits one architecture to testing; it does not validate its probabilities, fleet quantities, designs, sites or authorities. All twenty-two evidence gates remain open and renderings remain prohibited.
