# Civil Mobility Assurance Under Deep Uncertainty

## Why the apparent winner does not survive its next test

> **Pass 34 decision:** Do not treat the Pass 33 restorable multimodal mesh (`A2`) as the national architecture. Preserve its accessible fleets, transfer nodes, route-restoration formations and rights chain inside a protected-local-continuity architecture (`A3`), then determine how much relocation capacity remains necessary after local protection is made robust.

Pass 33 made a decisive conceptual advance: it replaced an abstract corridor multiplier with a standing Civil Mobility and Continuity Service. Its `A2` architecture passed 98.8 percent of 500 trials while moving distinct clinical, accessible, custodial, no-vehicle and general cohorts through jointly impaired routes and receivers. That was the correct result for the declared probability distribution. It was not yet a robust national decision.

This pass asks what happens when the distribution itself is not trusted.

One thousand paired outer-loop worlds vary twelve uncertain conditions across broad declared ranges: demand, common receiver availability, hardening effectiveness, route and terminal survival, restoration effectiveness, fleet availability, collection reach, destination acceptance, queue performance, mode-family common failure and accessible-module realization. Each world is applied to the same three purpose-built architectures. The ranges are exploratory, not fitted probabilities; the output therefore measures robustness to ignorance rather than forecast likelihood.

The result reverses the architecture ranking.

| Architecture | Pass 33 success | Broad-world success | Mean closure | P05 closure | Mean bottom-decile closure | Decision |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| `A1` Universal-access mobility force | 13.8% | 0.3% | 84.6% | 72.7% | 56.3% | reject as national architecture |
| `A2` Restorable multimodal mesh | 98.8% | 7.6% | 91.8% | 79.5% | 66.7% | retain as subsystem; redesign |
| `A3` Protected-local continuity | 100.0% | 99.8% | 100.0% | 100.0% | 100.0% | robust reference for the next proof |

`A3` is the best-performing architecture in 969 of 1,000 paired worlds. `A2` wins twenty-nine and `A1` wins two under the lexicographic rule of joint success, bottom-decile closure and national closure. The result does not establish that `A3` is economically optimal or empirically reliable. It establishes that `A2`'s earlier admission depended too heavily on a single favorable probability model.

The second finding is equally important. The national model still made collection a catchment-level coefficient. Four tract-scale pilots now show that even an undamaged centroid-distance screen leaves only 9.0 to 20.4 percent of weighted accessible collection burden within sixty minutes of the provisional node lattice. A national line-haul fleet cannot recover time already lost between a door and the first safe interface.

The third finding changes force composition. A first configuration of the `CTN-10` transfer node requires roughly 29,263 square metres of campus area, 4,513 tonnes of deployed system mass and 2,289 deployed staff to process 10,000 people per day under the declared queue, accessibility, utility and relief assumptions. Pass 33 carried 220 crew plus eighty maintainers. Replacing only the crew assumption across 128 nodes adds 264,832 direct positions before schoolhouses, reserve components and the industrial base.

The transfer node is not a terminal accessory. It is a population-continuity formation.

## 1. Decision standard: robust service, not optimized relocation

A mature Department of Resilience should not choose between “shelter in place” and “evacuation” as slogans. It should allocate protected-person-hours across three coupled strategies:

1. **protect locally** by hardening occupied systems, distributing reception and sustaining neighborhoods;
2. **move selectively** when local service cannot be restored safely or quickly enough; and
3. **return or continue durably** without converting emergency movement into unmanaged displacement.

The governing objective is not minimum transport cost or maximum passenger throughput. It is the least-regret architecture that preserves life, agency and complete service across plausible but poorly known futures.

For this pass, one world succeeds only when all three conditions hold simultaneously:

- national service closure is at least 99 percent;
- the minimum closure across clinical, accessible, custodial and no-vehicle cohorts is at least 97 percent; and
- the population-weighted bottom demand decile across the thirty-two Service Catchment Areas is at least 95 percent.

These are design rules, not observed outcome thresholds. They make the moral structure of the decision explicit: a high national mean cannot purchase permission to strand the same population or geography repeatedly.

## 2. The uncertainty experiment

### 2.1 Why a second stochastic layer is necessary

Ordinary Monte Carlo analysis assumes that the analyst knows the relevant distributions and their dependence well enough to sample them. Disaster-force design rarely meets that condition. A future national system has no historical record. Climate, settlement, infrastructure condition, behavior, institutional legitimacy and technical performance are nonstationary. Several of the most important quantities—route restoration after compound damage, destination acceptance under migration pressure, or accessible transfer throughput—do not yet have defensible national distributions.

Under those conditions, precision is dangerous. The appropriate sequence is:

> fixed case → stochastic events within fixed assumptions → broad parameter worlds → fitted joint distributions → adversarial operational trials

Pass 33 completed the second step. This pass performs the third.

### 2.2 Outer-loop parameters

| Parameter | Range | Operational meaning | Why it can reverse the result |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Demand multiplier | 0.90–1.35 | campaign movement and protection demand | consumes receiver, collection and fleet margin together |
| Common receiver availability | 0.72–1.00 | national or multi-region receiver impairment | reduces both local service and destination spare |
| Hardening realization | 0.45–1.10 | fraction of planned hardening benefit achieved | tests whether paper hardening survives installation and operations |
| Route-survival shift | −0.20–+0.04 | deviation from architecture route assumption | removes nominal multimodal diversity |
| Terminal-survival shift | −0.20–+0.03 | deviation from terminal assumption | squares through origin and destination interfaces |
| Restoration realization | 0.30–1.10 | realized share of modeled repair effectiveness | attacks `A2`'s defining advantage |
| Fleet availability | 0.60–1.02 | mature mission-capable fraction | joins maintenance, crew and positioning loss |
| Collection reach | 0.40–1.00 | door-to-node capacity available | prevents line-haul from claiming inaccessible demand |
| Destination acceptance | 0.55–1.00 | lawful and operational intake fraction | represents staffing, consent, host capacity and release friction |
| Queue factor | 0.50–1.00 | realized transfer-node service rate | converts terminal presence into time-dependent throughput |
| Common-mode addition | 0.00–0.18 | added mode-family impairment probability | tests fuel, software, labor, authority and weather covariance |
| Accessible realization | 0.65–1.00 | usable specialized module share | tests whether named accessible capacity functions in service |

Latin-hypercube sampling stratifies each range into 1,000 intervals and pairs the resulting worlds across architectures. Event-level campaign severity, local receiver noise and route-opening draws remain pseudo-random within each world. This preserves the Pass 33 allocator and its thirty-two catchments while attacking the uncertain coefficients around it.

The experiment intentionally does not assign probabilities to the worlds. “7.6 percent success” therefore means 76 of the 1,000 sampled combinations pass. It does not mean the nation has a 7.6 percent chance of success.

### 2.3 Why `A2` breaks

`A2` was constructed around three margins: receiver capacity above local demand, surviving or restored paths to that spare capacity, and enough compatible fleet and intake to move the shortfall. Deep uncertainty attacks those margins jointly.

Demand and common receiver availability dominate the closure correlations. Destination acceptance, queue performance and collection reach matter because they act as serial gates after receiver spare is created. Route and terminal parameters matter most in the geographic tail, but their marginal rank correlations are smaller once the system is already receiver- and demand-constrained. That is not evidence that routes are unimportant; it is evidence that a serial system is governed by its tightest uncertain gates.

`A3` carries roughly 4.20 times the baseline receiver lattice and only 0.60 of the mobility force. Its local surplus absorbs most of the sampled uncertainty before a person must enter the relocation chain. This is why it remains robust even when fleet, queue, route and terminal performance deteriorate.

The policy meaning is not “never move people.” It is:

> Relocation should be a protected exception, not the load-bearing mechanism of national resilience.

Civil mobility remains necessary for no-notice rescue, medically indicated movement, island and coastal isolation, custodial continuity, temporary concentration of scarce clinical capacity, voluntary relocation and return. It should no longer be asked to compensate for a structurally sparse receiving geography under broad uncertainty.

### 2.4 Cost and regret

The existing common-rule screen assigns `A2` about $2.351 trillion in capital and $4.987 trillion over twenty-five years. `A3` carries about $3.063 trillion in capital and $6.299 trillion over twenty-five years. The $1.313 trillion lifecycle difference is large, but it purchases a structural change: complete service is much less dependent on the synchronized survival of origin collection, terminal processing, line haul, destination intake and receiving capacity.

No benefit-cost conclusion is yet valid. The model does not monetize mortality, morbidity, involuntary displacement, household separation, economic interruption, land use or co-benefits from everyday local infrastructure. Nor does it include the workforce correction produced by the `CTN-10` configuration. The immediate decision is therefore to preserve both rivals while shifting the reference:

- `A3` sets the national protection architecture;
- `A2` becomes the civil-mobility subsystem nested inside it; and
- a later optimization determines the minimum mobility force that preserves rescue, selective relocation and return across the residual cases.

## 3. Collection is a separate network

### 3.1 The national model's hidden first mile

An origin catchment may have rail, air, road and water paths and still fail every resident who cannot reach the interface. The collection problem begins inside buildings and institutions, not on a national route graph.

Its service chain is:

> accessible warning → request or lawful movement authority → household binding → vertical extraction → curb or landing point → neighborhood pickup → collection hub → transfer node

The chain depends on elevators, stairs, electrical medical equipment, caregivers, accessible vehicles, service animals, language, trust, traffic control, fuel, dispatch, debris clearance and safe waiting. It is different from line haul in vehicle form, tempo, information, labor and public authority.

### 3.2 Four tract pilots

The model uses 2024 ACS five-year tract estimates for population, disability and zero-vehicle households, joined to 2024 Census Gazetteer internal points. A weighted farthest-first algorithm selects analytical candidate nodes. The demand case assumes ten percent of the jurisdiction's population enters a seven-day movement campaign, and one `CTN-10` processes 70,000 people in that period.

| Pilot | Tracts | Population | 10% campaign | `CTN-10` nodes | Weighted accessible share ≤60 min | Share ≤90 min | P90 time |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Harris County, Texas | 1,111 | 4.810M | 481,050 | 7 | 9.0% | 48.1% | 111.6 min |
| Miami-Dade County, Florida | 698 | 2.714M | 271,415 | 4 | 20.4% | 79.9% | 96.4 min |
| King County, Washington | 494 | 2.274M | 227,375 | 4 | 11.5% | 64.5% | 105.1 min |
| San Juan Municipio, Puerto Rico | 127 | 333,456 | 33,346 | 1 | 16.4% | 97.5% | 84.9 min |

Travel distance is straight-line distance multiplied by 1.35. General collection assumes forty kilometres per hour plus thirty minutes of dispatch and boarding; accessible collection assumes twenty-eight kilometres per hour plus forty-five minutes. Those values are deliberately favorable because they omit damaged roads, bridge outages, congestion, pickup sequencing and building extraction.

The pilot nodes are not proposed sites. Tract internal points are not addresses or parcel centroids. The selection does not consider floodplains, seismic soil, ownership, environmental justice, utility access, road topology, host consent or co-location hazards.

Even so, the result is enough to reject one assumption: **a few large transfer nodes do not create a door-to-node service.**

### 3.3 The required collection force

The Civil Mobility and Continuity Service needs a formation below the `CTN-10`:

| Element | Function | Governing metric | Distinctive capabilities |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Household access team | locate, communicate and bind household movement | verified households reached per shift | multilingual communication, consent, records, welfare checks |
| Vertical extraction team | move people and devices through failed buildings | protected persons extracted per hour by building state | stair devices, compact lifts, medical power, firefighter interface |
| Accessible collection cell | door-to-hub movement | completed compatible journeys per vehicle-hour | lift/litter/seated reconfiguration, device and caregiver continuity |
| Neighborhood mobility control | route, stage and sequence pickups | ninety-fifth-percentile request-to-pickup time | disconnected dispatch, curb management, traffic and safety |
| Collection support platoon | energy, maintenance, sanitation and crew relief | sustained twenty-four-hour availability | mobile charging/fuel, field repair, rest areas, spare devices |
| Collection assurance cell | independent rights and service audit | unresolved exception age and appeal time | accessibility authority, privacy, safeguarding and complaint handling |

This is not a subordinate “last mile” task. In many disasters it will be the decisive mobility effort, with more touch labor and more heterogeneous equipment than the line-haul fleet.

## 4. `CTN-10`: from label to formation

### 4.1 Mission contract

`CTN-10` must receive, protect, regulate and release 10,000 people per day for seven days while disconnected from ordinary utilities and communications. It must accept mixed arrival modes, preserve family and device continuity, regulate clinical and custodial movements, assign lawful destinations, provide sanitation and thermal safety, and release compatible groups to road, rail, air or water movement.

The node does not earn throughput credit when people cross a gate. Credit occurs when a complete record, compatible movement configuration and verified destination are released together.

The provisional daily operating window is eighteen hours. A 1.80 peak factor produces a 1,000-person-per-hour design peak. Every principal process stage is sized below seventy-percent utilization before stochastic arrival variability, outages and rework. That low target is intentional: emergency queues are not stationary, service times have long tails, and some exceptions require several professionals at once.

### 4.2 Process architecture

| Stage | Flow share | Mean service | Servers | Staff on shift |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Arrival and accessible wayfinding | 100% | 3 min | 72 | 87 |
| Identity, consent and household binding | 100% | 5 min | 120 | 144 |
| Clinical regulation | 15% | 10 min | 36 | 72 |
| Access and functional-needs fitting | 20% | 8 min | 39 | 71 |
| Custody and safeguarding | 3% | 12 min | 9 | 23 |
| Mode and destination assignment | 100% | 4 min | 96 | 116 |
| Boarding release | 100% | 3 min | 72 | 101 |

These stages require 614 process staff on shift. A thirty-five-percent support allowance adds 215 for utilities, sanitation, food, material handling, communications, maintenance and staff care. The node therefore carries 829 people on shift. A 2.4 shift multiplier and fifteen-percent command/relief allowance produce a 2,289-person deployed formation.

This is not a validated table of organization. It exposes the order of magnitude and a crucial design choice: automate paperwork and material movement aggressively, but do not automate consent, clinical judgment, safeguarding, appeal or destination legitimacy.

### 4.3 Holding and occupied area

At six hours of average dwell, the node holds 2,500 people on average. A 1.50 peak factor creates 3,750 peak occupants. Sixty square feet (5.574 square metres) per person yields 20,903 square metres of occupied area. Adding forty percent for processing, utilities, storage, circulation and staff produces a 29,263-square-metre campus.

The sixty-square-foot coefficient is source-informed but not a design standard for this node. It sits above minimum cot-space guidance because a transfer population includes wheelchairs, litters, devices, baggage, service animals, family groups, queues and active processing. A future physical mock-up must allocate the area rather than multiply one coefficient.

### 4.4 Utility budget

| Utility | Provisional requirement | Basis | Failure consequence |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Water | 550 m³/day | 55 litres per processed person-day | drinking, hygiene and sanitation failure |
| Toilets | 188 | one per twenty peak occupants | public-health failure and inaccessible queues |
| Showers | 75 | one per fifty peak occupants | hygiene and decontamination shortfall |
| Average electric load | 2.8 MW | thermal, clinical, process, information and support hypothesis | occupied environment and processing fail |
| Peak electric load | 4.5 MW | sectionalized plant and transient hypothesis | peak queue and thermal service fail |
| Daily energy | 67.2 MWh | average load over twenty-four hours | autonomy claim fails |
| Battery | 22.4 MWh usable | eight hours at average load | islanding transition and silent operations fail |

The node requires water quality, fire water, wastewater, solid waste, food, drainage, infection control, heating, cooling and clean-air configurations that are not closed here. It also needs independent black-start sections so one electrical fault cannot collapse clinical, identity, sanitation and thermal service together.

The mass is a first-order deployment ledger, not a designed manifest. At twenty-five tonnes per equivalent move it requires 181 movements. That makes a deeper architectural distinction unavoidable:

- a **prepared CTN site** should own hardstand, drainage, buried utility trunks, structural anchors, communications pathways and some occupied buildings;
- a **mobile CTN formation** should bring process modules, redundant utilities, clinical/access equipment, information systems, staffing and replacement stocks; and
- an **expeditionary CTN** for places without prepared sites will trade throughput and deployment time for greater mobile completeness.

One nominal `CTN-10` class should therefore become a family with common interfaces rather than a single transportable object.

### 4.6 Information, safety and rights budget

The node's information system must operate when national networks are unavailable and when personal data collection itself could create harm. The minimum disconnected ledger binds:

- a person-controlled identity token or locally issued surrogate;
- household, caregiver, service-animal and essential-device relationships;
- consent, lawful authority and any contested status;
- clinical movement constraints and destination acceptance;
- current location, next movement, responsible operator and exception state;
- data minimization, access log, retention clock and appeal path; and
- return eligibility, contact preference and durable-continuity plan.

No optimization engine may silently trade family unity, accessibility, custody safeguards or clinical acceptance for throughput. The system must expose rejected assignments and stale receiver claims to an independent rights and safety cell. In disconnected mode, local decisions must synchronize later without allowing the central record to erase a lawfully documented exception.

## 5. `UCM-8`: a continuity interface, not universal cargo

Pass 33 proposed a universal continuity module that could cross road, rail, air and water modes without separating a person from a device, caregiver or clinical support. The first configuration narrows its role.

The `UCM-8` uses a nominal twenty-foot intermodal envelope: 6.06 metres long, 2.44 metres wide and 2.59 metres high. Eight occupants receive 14.79 square metres of gross floor area, or 1.85 square metres each before structure, equipment, clearances and an accessible toilet. The provisional mass account is 3,200 kilograms tare, 1,600 kilograms for people and devices and 500 kilograms of consumables, for 5,300 kilograms gross.

Average power is 5 kW and peak power 20 kW. A 60 kWh usable battery provides twelve hours at the average load before reserve. Eighty litres each of fresh-water and wastewater capacity support a short transfer, not independent habitation.

Every value is a target. Crashworthiness, fire, smoke, restraint, egress, structural attachment, electromagnetic compatibility, infection control, thermal extremes, vibration, noise, motion sickness, aircraft loading, rail buff loads, maritime stability and five-point mass properties remain open.

The most important result is operational, not geometric. If `UCM-8` carries the twenty percent of `CTN-10` flow provisionally assigned to accessible or clinical continuity, the node must handle 250 module departures per day. If every traveler uses a module, it must handle 1,250 per day. Universal use likely moves the bottleneck from passenger handling to module storage, cleaning, inspection, return and cross-modal lift.

The module should therefore be **universal across modes but selective across people**. Its purpose is to prevent a transfer from breaking continuity where reconfiguration is costly or unsafe. General household groups should use purpose-built accessible vehicles and boarding systems without being containerized.

## 6. Revised force architecture

The force spine now contains five coupled layers:

> local protection → collection → transfer → line haul → reception and return

No layer is subordinate in the engineering sense. Each can set the end-to-end rate to zero.

### 6.1 Protected Local Continuity Command

The `A3` result implies a standing command and investment account for occupied-system protection: distributed thermal refuges, water and sanitation continuity, community power, clean-air spaces, neighborhood medical continuity, accessible shelter, food channels and local civil communications. This is not merely the receiving lattice enlarged. It is the ability to keep people in service near their homes when relocation would add risk.

### 6.2 Collection Command

Collection Command owns door-level access, vertical extraction, accessible pickup, dispatch, neighborhood staging and delivery into transfer nodes. It trains against damaged buildings and streets, not only transport terminals.

### 6.3 Transfer and Continuity Command

Transfer and Continuity Command generates `CTN` families, continuity modules, clinical and custodial regulation teams, disconnected identity systems, sanitation, material handling and independent rights cells. It is closer to a temporary city operator than an airport terminal operator.

### 6.4 Mode commands and Network Assurance Command

Road, rail, air and water commands generate line-haul fleets and crews. Network Assurance Command inspects, clears, repairs, signals, energizes and certifies routes and terminals. Restoration credit is issued only after an independent safety authority accepts a machine-readable case.

### 6.5 Return and Durable Continuity Command

Return is planned with the first outward movement. The command maintains household location, destination obligations, property and device continuity, transport back, staged re-entry and a lawful durable alternative when return is impossible. Without this command, mobility power can become displacement power.

## 7. Technology programs now required

The pass does not select vehicles. It identifies invention programs pulled by failed mission threads.

### 7.1 Continuous collection graph

Develop a privacy-preserving, rapidly updated graph that joins requests, building access state, elevators, debris, curb capacity, transit, accessible vehicles, pickup dwell and transfer queues without requiring an intact cloud. Success is measured by the ninety-fifth-percentile protected journey from door to node, not routing speed.

### 7.2 Robotic vertical access

Competing systems should move people and critical devices through dark, hot, smoky, wet or structurally uncertain buildings. Candidate technologies include stair-climbing platforms, compact temporary hoists, cooperative load-sharing robots and exterior access systems. The kill criterion is any design that transfers unacceptable fall, crush, abandonment or caregiver-separation risk to the occupant.

### 7.3 Self-balancing transfer campus

A `CTN` should sense queues, thermal load, water, waste, power, staff fatigue, destination state and transport arrival, then reconfigure lanes and utilities without central connectivity. Automation may recommend resource shifts; accountable human authorities retain consent, clinical, custody and safety decisions.

### 7.4 Universal cross-modal interface

Develop common mechanical, electrical, data, restraint, lifting and environmental interfaces for selective continuity modules. Multiple vendors and mode-specific carriers should compete behind the interface. A module fails if it gains compatibility by imposing one proprietary handling ecosystem.

### 7.5 High-rate cleaning and reverse logistics

The UCM concept lives or dies on inspection, decontamination, recharge, consumable replacement, fault isolation and return. The technology program must prove a full cycle, not a one-way demonstration.

### 7.6 Verified restoration clock

Route-restoration systems need embedded inspection, temporary structure, signaling, power and certification tools whose output can be independently challenged. The objective is a distribution of time to lawful reopening by damage state. “Cleared” and “open” are not sufficient states.

### 7.7 Local-protection substitution science

The largest economic question is how one dollar of protected-local capacity substitutes for relocation demand by hazard, duration and population. The Department should run paired district experiments comparing distributed refuge, receiver hardening, selective movement and mass movement under identical service deficits.

## 8. Evidence program

Fourteen gates block `R3` and rendering.

### Deep uncertainty

1. Calibrate the twelve outer-loop ranges from historical, experimental or expert-elicited evidence.
2. Fit joint dependence among demand, receiver loss, route loss, terminal loss, labor, communications and restoration.
3. Demonstrate time-to-certification distributions for route restoration by mode and damage state.

### Collection

4. Replace tract internal-point distance with a routable, damaged street/bridge/transit graph.
5. Measure demand, refusal, spontaneous movement and household grouping under different warning and trust conditions.
6. Demonstrate accessible door-to-node performance with elevator loss, devices, caregivers, service animals and medical power.

### `CTN-10`

7. Operate a representative intake line under peaked, multilingual, exception-heavy and disconnected conditions.
8. Close power, HVAC, water, wastewater, solid waste, setup mass, redundancy and degraded modes.
9. Validate staffing, fatigue, credentialing, supervision and relief across a seven-day mission.
10. Prove consent, data minimization, family unity, privacy, appeal, safeguarding and audit in degraded mode.

### `UCM-8`

11. Test occupied geometry, restraint, transfer, toilet, thermal, noise, vibration and infection-control cases.
12. Prove road, rail, air and water restraint, power, data and handling interfaces.
13. Close structural, crash, fire, egress and five-point mass properties.
14. Demonstrate cleaning, recharge, inspection, repair, return and total pool requirements.

## 9. What is established, what is not

This pass establishes five design conclusions:

1. The fixed-distribution `A2` admission is not robust to broad parameter uncertainty.
2. Protected-local capacity should be the load-bearing national architecture, with civil mobility nested inside it.
3. Door-to-node collection is an independent force layer and cannot remain a catchment coefficient.
4. A 10,000-person/day transfer node is a campus-scale formation whose workforce and deployment mass materially change the national force.
5. A cross-modal continuity module should be selective for high-burden transfers, not universal for every traveler.

It does not establish:

- probabilities of architecture success;
- an optimal balance between local protection and mobility;
- any transfer-node site;
- a validated queue, utility, mass, staffing or cost account;
- a viable `UCM-8` geometry or fleet quantity;
- individual movement need from ACS categories;
- lawful authority for any particular movement; or
- readiness of any vehicle, node or formation.

The model is `R2+` exploratory arithmetic around `R1/R2` architectures. Every configuration and uncertainty gate remains open.

## 10. Continuation decision

The next pass should stop enlarging the national seat count and solve the local-protection/mobility boundary.

Build one coupled metropolitan campaign with a damaged routable collection graph, time-dependent requests and queues, building-access states, protected-local service options, `CTN` activation, selective `UCM` use, line-haul paths, destination acceptance and return. Optimize for protected-person-hours, distributional closure, coercion risk, workforce and lifecycle burden—not passenger throughput. Carry `A2` as a nested mobility package and `A3` as the national reference.

In parallel, split `CTN-10` into prepared, mobile and expeditionary variants; construct a spatial process layout and discrete-event queue model; and reopen the national workforce and cost account using the 2,289-person provisional formation.

No vehicle or node rendering is authorized. The next useful visual is an analytical process-and-interface drawing after the layout, utility, safety and rights budgets close—not an exterior concept image.
