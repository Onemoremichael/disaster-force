# Configuring the public-service pod

> **Supersession note — Pass 29:** the bridge/reuse and full-substitution comparisons remain useful, but the 11,738.09-tonne independent-essential case below was an allowance screen rather than a closed human-sustainment configuration. The [`PCC-50` Population Continuity Complex](../capabilities/population-continuity/configuration-model.md) replaces shelter, food, sanitation, energy and workforce allowances with a cohort-specific configuration. It screens the independent pod at 15,152.86 tonnes and moves worst-case ten-pod closure from hour 71 to approximately hour 84.

## Executive judgment

Pass 27 defined a six-cell Service Entry Force whose upland, air and offshore formations could lose any two cells and still deliver the first Cascadia public-service package by hour 72. It did so by treating the mass entering the theater as a uniformly mixed service kernel. That abstraction was useful for sizing the movement architecture, but it was not yet a force design. Real teams are indivisible. Food, shelter, clinical care and electrical service do not scale identically with tonnes. Batteries, fuel, oxygen, medicine and food impose different custody rules. A machine can arrive before its crew, a field hospital before its oxygen system, or a generator before anyone has authority to energize the line.

Pass 28 replaces the continuously divisible kernel with a discrete **Public Service Pod**, designated `PSP-10`. Every 29,611.68-tonne Service Entry Cell contains three pods. Each pod carries approximately ten percent of the Pass 25 reference effect, one-third of the cell's independence support, and one indivisible service-island command node. The national six-cell force therefore contains eighteen pods.

Ten complete pods are required to close the hour-72 contract. Food is binding: 2.4 million people require 9.23 pod-equivalents at the declared 260,000-person/day capacity, so the accounting standard rounds upward. A formation receives no fractional pod credit merely because some cargo has arrived. Its effect contract, configuration manifest, dependency passport, interface/load record and authority release must all close.

That discrete rule changes the timing result. The Pass 27 bridge/reuse configuration closes its worst two-cell-loss case at hour 63 rather than hour 60. A new **independent-essential** envelope adds climate-managed shelter and 72 hours of liquid-equivalent energy, raises pod mass from 9,870.56 to 11,738.09 tonnes, and closes at hour 71. A deliberately severe **full-substitution** envelope imports a more complete shelter system, three ready-to-eat meals per person-day, 72 hours of energy and a current-practice analogue workforce. It raises pod mass to 16,818.71 tonnes. Only eight pods arrive by hour 72 after both upland cells are lost. The existing force therefore fails.

This is the principal finding: **independence is not a property of a vehicle or unit. It is a time-bounded claim about a public service inside a declared dependency envelope.** A force can bridge a damaged local system, deliver minimum life support without local utilities, or substitute for the larger system. Those are different missions with different mass, workforce, movement and technology.

The accompanying [service-entry pod configuration model](../../models/service-entry-pod-configuration-model.xlsx) contains twenty-seven sheets, 106 component lines, three independence cases, all fifteen two-cell-loss combinations, package classes, a current-analogue workforce screen, interface loads, technology programs and twelve evidence gates.

## From cargo to a public-service object

### Why tonnes were an inadequate unit

Mass is indispensable for movement planning, but it is not the output of a resilience force. A tonne of bridge structure, potable water, pharmaceuticals, switchgear, field kitchens or responder shelter produces different effects at different times. Some cargo becomes useful only after several complements arrive. Some resources are continuously divisible; others are lumpy:

- a command authority can govern a service island or it cannot;
- a clinical team must contain the right specialties and operate across shifts;
- an electrical island needs source, protection, transformation, conductor, grounding and release;
- a kitchen needs food, fuel, potable water, hygiene, waste handling and distribution;
- a shelter needs safe space, thermal protection, accessibility, registration, security, health support and sanitation;
- a water train produces no public effect if intake, power, quality, residuals or distribution fails.

The military analogue is useful precisely because it is stricter than a cargo list. Department of the Air Force guidance defines a standard unit type code as a complete mission capability linked to its manpower and logistics detail; it directs cross-functional, self-sustaining teams to be packaged as complete capabilities. It also requires accurate weights, dimensions, hazardous-material codes and shift-complete manpower. The resilience pod adopts that discipline but changes the output from a military mission statement to a time-bounded public-service contract. See [DAFMAN 10-406](https://static.e-publishing.af.mil/production/1/af_a3/publication/dafman10-406/dafman10-406.pdf).

### The five records that make a pod real

A `PSP-10` is not one box. It is a configuration-controlled association of loads, people, data, rights and release evidence. Five records travel together:

1. **Effect contract.** States the water, power, care, shelter, food, sanitation, communications, access, hazard-control and command effect that the pod must produce, by time and for how long.
2. **Configuration manifest.** Identifies every team, machine, stock, adapter, spare, software baseline and package position required to create those effects.
3. **Dependency passport.** Declares what the pod expects from the origin, host, other cells, providers and civil authorities—and what happens when each dependency is absent.
4. **Interface and load record.** Captures mass, dimensions, center of gravity, restraint, hazardous and cold-chain condition, energy and data connections, sequence, custody, destination and receiving burden.
5. **Authority and safety release.** Names the official who can permit movement, energization, potable use, clinical operation, structural access, environmental discharge and civil transfer.

The pod receives effect credit only when all five agree. This prevents a paperwork fiction in which the personnel record assumes equipment that the load record omitted, the equipment record assumes power that the dependency passport assigns to the host, or the operational dashboard claims service before the technical authority releases it.

## The `PSP-10` service contract

The pod is sized against the smallest complete Pass 25 public-service layer rather than an arbitrary shipping unit.

| Effect | One pod | Hour-72 contract | Whole pods required |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Service-island command | 1 node | 9 nodes | 9 |
| Access throughput | 3,000 t/day | 25,000 t/day | 9 |
| Responder basing | 3,000 people | 25,000 people | 9 |
| Water | 2,000 m³/day | 18,000 m³/day | 9 |
| Food | 260,000 people/day | 2.4 million people/day | **10** |
| Shelter | 50,000 people | 450,000 people | 9 |
| Sanitation | 50,000 users | 450,000 users | 9 |
| Critical power | 65 MW | 500 MW | 8 |
| Communications | 280,000 users | 2.4 million users | 9 |
| Health | 60 staffed beds | 500 staffed beds | 9 |
| Hazard control | 12 route-equivalent km/day | 100 km/day | 9 |

The uneven margins are intentional. “Ten percent” describes the configuration lineage, not a promise that unlike public effects share one exact scalar. The contract uses the maximum whole-pod requirement, which is ten.

This creates two important rules. First, substitution between pods is permitted only at the complete-effect boundary. A water-heavy pod cannot compensate for a missing food-and-sanitation pod unless the campaign authority explicitly relaxes the contract. Second, the Department should periodically rebalance the pod as empirical service demand improves. If heat, smoke, disability, age, season or public-health conditions make shelter or power binding, the composition must change visibly rather than hiding the new burden in “support.”

## The baseline configuration

### Three pods per cell

The Pass 25 first-wave reference mass is 88,130 tonnes. A Service Entry Cell carries 30 percent of each of its sixteen effect modules, or 26,439 tonnes. A 12-percent independence allowance adds 3,172.68 tonnes. The complete cell is therefore 29,611.68 tonnes; dividing it into three identical pods produces a 9,870.56-tonne baseline.

The workbook decomposes this allowance into 106 configuration lines. It is intentionally finer than a force-family list and coarser than a bill of materials. Each line has a module, mass share, unit count divisible across three pods, primary package class, arrival sequence, cold-chain flag, hazardous separation rule and maturity state. The sixteen effect modules are:

- civil command, degraded communications and reconnaissance;
- medical stabilization and search and rescue;
- airfield, port, littoral and route opening;
- responder basing;
- water, critical power, sanitation, food, shelter and energy.

Seven more lines decompose the support allowance into staff sustainment; repair, spares and test; handling and autonomy; data redundancy; energy reserve; safety and decontamination; and packaging and adapters. Their shares and masses reconcile, but they remain allocations. The next engineering cycle must replace each allowance with weighed designs and validated crew schedules.

### Interface families, not a magic container

The configuration uses eight provisional Kernel Interface Families: an air-pallet position, 20-foot and 40-foot ISO envelopes, flatrack, roll-on/roll-off vehicle, tank, powered cold-chain module and segregated battery module. These are planning boundaries, not selected products.

Current standards show why the pod cannot be a monolith. Air Force guidance caps a 463L palletized increment at 10,000 pounds, while large ISO and rolling systems have their own gross-weight, dimensional, road, floor-loading, restraint and handling limits. Every 9,000–17,000-tonne pod must therefore be compiled into hundreds of mode-compatible positions without losing effect completeness. The stable object is the manifest and interface contract; the loads underneath it may differ by mode.

The Department should standardize external lifting, restraint, identity, power, fluid, data, release and degraded-mode interfaces while maintaining rival implementations behind them. A common boundary enables substitution. A common supplier, controller or internal architecture creates covariance.

## Three meanings of independence

The phrase “self-sufficient for 72 hours” is dangerously incomplete. Self-sufficient in relation to what? The model therefore distinguishes three operational envelopes.

### Bridge/reuse

The pod bridges damaged systems and substitutes only where required. It may reuse inspected buildings, surviving distribution networks, bulk food stocks, external energy or local labor. These dependencies are permissible only when the passport names them, reconnaissance verifies them, and a fallback exists.

This envelope preserves the Pass 27 mass of 9,870.56 tonnes per pod and 177,670.08 tonnes for six cells. It is the correct force for a theater with large but discontinuous surviving capacity. It is not a universal definition of independence.

### Independent essential

The pod produces the minimum declared life-support service for 72 hours without host power, water, shelter, food preparation, communications backhaul or heavy repair. Civil priority setting and legal legitimacy remain irreducibly shared; no technical system should attempt to replace them.

Two first-order increments are modeled:

- shelter rises from a 300-tonne baseline allowance to 1,250 tonnes per pod, equivalent to 25 kilograms per protected person; and
- 65 MW of continuous power receives a 72-hour liquid-energy-equivalent reserve, adding approximately 917.53 tonnes above the 223-tonne baseline energy module.

The resulting pod is 11,738.09 tonnes, an 18.9-percent increase. A cell is 35,214.27 tonnes and the six-cell force is 211,285.62 tonnes. These are mass screens, not designs. They deliberately expose the technology objective: achieve climate-safe shelter and service energy at or below the envelope through textiles, panels, storage, local-energy harvest, compact generation, microgrids or other rival pathways.

The current military and civil practice analogues support the complete-system framing. USACE describes emergency power as site assessment, generator sizing and connection, installation, fueling, operation, maintenance, inspection and eventual deinstallation—not as generator delivery. EPA similarly treats mobile water response as transportation, deployment, operation, contaminant effectiveness, residual management and safe discharge. See [USACE Emergency Power](https://www.usace.army.mil/Media/Fact-Sheets/Fact-Sheets-View/Article/475473/emergency-power/) and [EPA emergency water-treatment research](https://www.epa.gov/emergency-response-research/water-treatment-and-waste-management).

### Full substitution

The full-substitution case asks what happens if the force imports almost the entire early service system. The screen assumes:

- 75 kilograms of shelter system per protected person;
- three ready-to-eat meals per person-day for 72 hours;
- the same 65 MW, 72-hour liquid-energy-equivalent reserve; and
- 6,643 deployed people per pod, derived from current resource analogues rather than the 2,500-person technology target.

These increments raise a pod to 16,818.71 tonnes, a cell to 50,456.13 tonnes and the six-cell architecture to 302,736.78 tonnes—70.4 percent above the Pass 27 mass.

The food comparison illustrates why service definition matters. The baseline 520-tonne food allocation is compatible with three days of a compact 0.54-kilogram/person-day survival ration, but not with three conventional ready-to-eat meals per day. The latter screens near 2,007.72 tonnes for 260,000 people over three days, adding 1,487.72 tonnes. Both could be called “food for 260,000 people.” They are different service contracts, labor systems, cultural experiences, waste streams and public-health risks.

## Labor is a physical design variable

Pass 27 attributed 2,500 deployed people to each pod. Pass 28 tests that target against current resource analogues. The purpose is not to reproduce present institutions; it is to measure how far a new force must move beyond them.

The screening stack includes one incident-management element, thirty-nine 20,000-meal/day field kitchens, two-shift medical coverage for 50,000 shelter residents, ten urban-search-and-rescue teams, three disaster medical teams and a residual allocation for utilities, access, mobility, base operations, data and safety. It totals 6,643 people per pod. The kitchen, shelter-health, rescue and medical figures use typed-team analogues; the team counts and residual category remain design assumptions.

FEMA explains resource typing as a common language for the minimum capabilities of equipment, teams and units. That makes current typed resources valuable empirical anchors, but not the terminal architecture. The Department's task is to preserve or improve the public effect while reducing labor, exposure and support burden through autonomous handling, machine inspection, modular utilities, remote specialist cells, better shift design and local credentialing. See [FEMA's resource-typing framework](https://preptoolkit.fema.gov/web/build-sustain-capability/national-resource-hub/resource-typing).

The gap is large. Reaching 2,500 from the 6,643-person screen requires a 62.4-percent reduction or a narrower contract. Calling the difference “automation” would be another hidden allowance. Each occupation and shift must be tested against service quality, safety, fatigue, repair, handoff and the additional technicians required to maintain the automation itself.

## Discrete delivery and the hour-72 contest

### Crediting whole pods

Each cell delivers three pods sequentially. Under the bridge/reuse configuration:

- an upland cell activates at hour 6 and credits pods at hours 15, 23 and 31;
- an air cell activates at hour 18 and credits pods at hours 33, 48 and 63; and
- an offshore cell activates at hour 30 and credits pods at hours 42, 54 and 66.

If both upland cells are lost, the tenth surviving pod arrives at hour 63. This supersedes Pass 27's hour-60 result, which credited continuously proportional mass on a six-hour decision grid.

The independent-essential case credits its tenth pod at hour 71 and twelfth at hour 72. It technically closes every two-cell-loss combination but has almost no deterministic margin. Any queue, release delay, damaged package, effect imbalance or overrun can break the promise. It should be treated as a threshold technology objective, not a comfortable reference posture.

Full substitution credits only eight pods by hour 72 after both upland cells are lost. The tenth pod arrives at hour 90. The force fails before stochastic loss, weather or public-effect uncertainty is added.

### The throughput required to recover the full-substitution case

There are two clean architectural responses if the cell count and activation times remain fixed. Either both surviving air cells or both surviving offshore cells must each deliver a third full-substitution pod by hour 72:

| Recovery path | Required net rate per cell | Current net rate | Increase | Required gross rate |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Air | 934.37 t/h | 669.38 t/h | 39.6% | 1,465.68 t/h |
| Offshore | 1,201.34 t/h | 841.50 t/h | 42.8% | 1,570.37 t/h |

For the Pass 27 `SAC-120` planning case, the air gross rate implies approximately 147 aircraft attributable to each air cell rather than 105, assuming a 120-tonne mission payload and twelve-hour cycle. This does not prove that buying more aircraft is the right solution. It sets a complete-mission discriminator among larger fleets, larger aircraft, shorter cycles, improved availability, earlier activation, distributed basing, faster loading, lighter pods and other entry modes.

The same logic governs offshore response. A nominally faster ship is irrelevant if hydrography, transfer, surf transit, temporary terminals, hazardous separation or onward distribution constrains released-service throughput. The measured output must be complete pods released at service islands.

## Technology portfolio created by the configuration

The failure cases define a more coherent innovation agenda than a platform wish list.

### Kernel compiler and autonomous cross-dock

The `PSP-01` kernel compiler maintains effect completeness while aircraft, loads, nodes and destinations change. It must expose shortages rather than optimize them out of view. `PSP-02` supplies high-rate physical handling with multiple control stacks and a credible manual degraded mode. Together they should survive seeded loss of at least 20 percent of planned loads and recompose the remaining force within minutes.

### Compact life-support systems

`PSP-03` targets climate-managed, accessible shelter at or below 25 kilograms per person for the essential envelope. `PSP-04` seeks culturally and nutritionally acceptable, low-mass food systems that minimize water, kitchen labor, cold chain, packaging and waste. These programs must be tested with representative populations and environmental extremes, not only in warehouses.

### Portable service energy

`PSP-05` treats 65 MW for 72 hours as a service-island problem rather than a generator catalog. Rival pathways include dense liquid systems, storage, local renewable harvest, grid reuse, fuel-flexible sources and compact reactors where their licensing, heat rejection and security systems can close. All connect through a source-neutral electrical interface. The objective is to reduce recurring mass and correlated fuel dependence while preserving grid formation, protection and black start.

### Low-labor public-service production

`PSP-06` targets the 62.4-percent gap between the current-analogue workforce screen and the 2,500-person pod goal. The program must compare automated and human processes on service quality, safety, fatigue, maintainability and total workforce—including remote operators, software assurance, repair and training.

### Higher-throughput entry modes

`PSP-07` and `PSP-08` are no longer aircraft and no-pier equipment programs in isolation. Their test is whether two cells of one family can deliver and release six full-substitution pods by hour 72 after both upland cells disappear. This is the correct scale for clean-sheet heavy airlift, ground-effect transport, autonomous lighters, high-rate causeways, shallow-draft carriers and novel cargo-handling systems to compete.

### Machine-readable release

`PSP-09` addresses a less visible bottleneck. A bridge, airfield, water system, clinical module or electrical island produces no lawful effect until a named authority accepts evidence. Robotics and digital nondestructive evaluation should shorten that path, but accountable officials remain in the loop. The output is a configuration-stamped safety case, not an algorithmic recommendation.

These nine programs are allocated within the existing $60 billion Pass 27 research, development, test and evaluation line. Pass 28 does not add another budget layer. Its program decomposition prevents the entry-force technology account from collapsing into an aircraft program while shelter, food, labor, interface and release problems remain unfunded.

## Organizational consequences

### Public-service pods become the common force language

The Department should maintain a national library of service pods, just as sophisticated force-planning institutions maintain deployable capability records. Each pod record combines effect, manpower, logistics, movement, dependency, readiness and authority. It is versioned, weighed, exercised and tied to a specific service contract.

This changes planning at every echelon:

- services generate and certify pod components;
- the Joint Deployment and Distribution Enterprise compiles mode-specific loads;
- geographic commands request service effects and dependency envelopes;
- the Campaign Assurance Authority grants independence credit;
- technical authorities issue bounded release;
- the Civil Resilience Authority governs priorities, rights and transfer; and
- the independent Research, Test and Evaluation Authority removes cells, corrupts manifests and tests the complete thread.

### Cells remain the assurance unit

The pod does not replace the cell. The pod is the smallest effect-complete deployable increment; the cell is the smallest independently supported campaign path. Three pods in one cell still share origin, movement family, command, repair, technical authority and other risks. They may improve graceful degradation, but they do not count as three independent paths.

That distinction prevents false redundancy. Eighteen pods riding through six cells provide six origin-to-effect correlation groups, not eighteen. Similarly, two suppliers using the same battery chemistry, control software, labor pool or port may represent one effective dependency.

### There is no purely technical full substitution

Even the full-substitution envelope retains civil dependencies. Public priorities, consent, property access, sovereign and tribal rights, clinical legitimacy, environmental limits and transfer cannot be imported as equipment. The Department can carry communication, staff and procedures that keep governance functioning. It cannot replace the affected public.

The force therefore maximizes technical independence while preserving civil dependence where legitimacy requires it. This is a constitutional design principle as much as an operational one.

## What is proved, what is not

Pass 28 proves a limited set of arithmetic propositions under declared assumptions:

- the 88,130-tonne reference wave reconciles into a 29,611.68-tonne cell and three 9,870.56-tonne baseline pods;
- the eleven public-service contracts require ten whole pods;
- 106 manifest lines reconcile to their module allowances;
- all fifteen two-cell-loss cases close by hour 63 in the bridge/reuse case and hour 71 in the independent-essential case;
- the full-substitution case delivers only eight pods by hour 72 after both upland cells are lost; and
- the air and offshore throughput thresholds above would restore deterministic ten-pod closure if all other assumptions remained true.

It does not prove that one pod can actually produce the declared effects, that its mass is complete, that package positions fit aircraft and ships, that 2,500 people can operate it, that any movement rate is attainable, or that the cells are independent. Twelve gates remain open: effect divisibility, manifest completeness, package compatibility, shelter density, food effect, energy endurance, workforce, air throughput, offshore throughput, independence, civil transfer and the complete two-cell-loss mission.

The `PSP-10` and the Service Entry Cell remain `R2+`: first-order configuration arithmetic with a bounded operating concept. Rendering remains blocked. The next valid image is not a beauty view of a ship or aircraft; it is a verified operating diagram of a complete pod moving through its origin, loads, entry node, service island, dependencies and release authorities.

## Research agenda created by this pass

The immediate program should proceed in this order:

1. Replace the food, shelter, energy and support allowances with rival component configurations and measured mass.
2. Build a shift-complete workforce model by occupation, qualification, fatigue, automation and replacement echelon.
3. Compile the 106 lines into actual air, sea and surface load plans with dimensions, center of gravity, restraint, hazardous separation, cold-chain power and receiving equipment.
4. Run a digital and physical kernel-compilation exercise in which loads, destinations and entry nodes fail after dispatch.
5. Test one integrated essential pod at representative population scale for 72 hours without host utilities.
6. Test two independent cells through separate origins and modes while a third party injects shared-provider, data, authority, repair and labor failures.
7. Only then freeze candidate vehicle payload bays, connectors, handling geometry and surrounding formation diagrams for rendering.

The conceptual advance is therefore larger than a new module. The Department of Resilience now has a candidate equivalent to a deployable force package: a repeatable public-service object whose outcome, people, equipment, movement, dependencies and authority can be audited as one thing. That is the level at which a coequal national instrument can be built, bought, exercised and held to account.
