# Pass 197 — Sovereign receiver commons and forward-partnership topology

**Research object:** `SPC-2`  
**Decision level:** mature Department strategy  
**Verdict:** `CLOSE`

## Branch and resolution

The deepest active branch is `SPC-2`, the second of five permitted strategic-power passes after force generation returned to Department architecture.

**Resolution test:** Reversing the forward topology would change whether 675 invited formations depend on permanent U.S. bases, how much capacity must exist abroad, whether a host can refuse or continue service independently and whether the international promise is physically credible, so it changes the mature architecture and central thesis.

## Decision

The two international reach networks will be **federated sovereign receiver commons**, not U.S. resilience bases. Host-owned civil infrastructure, public authority, workforce, production and evidence systems remain continuously useful. U.S. rights are narrow, service-specific and activated by invitation; U.S. formations do not garrison the network.

Four alternatives fail. An expeditionary hub concentrates refusal and infrastructure failure. A cache stores objects without creating receivers, labor or repair. A standing U.S. garrison turns constructive power into foreign presence. A contractor franchise makes proprietary continuity and purchasing power govern public service. The selected commons has seven layers:

| Layer | National topology | Function |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| network compact | 2 | invitation, reciprocity, release and public accounting |
| sovereign receiver district | 12 | host authority, population service and distributed admission |
| unlike access precinct | 36 | separately caused arrival, utility, handling, control and safeing paths |
| family-specific reception slot | 842 | receiver, interface, crew, stock, residual and reset capacity |
| partner production root | 192 | two separated paths per family–network obligation |
| public assurance cell | 96 | metrology, configuration, adverse evidence and outside replay |
| training and transfer academy | 12 | receiver professions and independent operation |

This is an abstract topology. It selects no country, installation or exterior form.

## Family-complete reception

The invited force is heterogeneous: 334 formations in the Indo-Pacific design campaign and 341 in the Atlantic/Americas campaign, each spanning forty-eight non-substitutable families.[^1] Counting aggregate berths would conceal the decisive shortage. For every family in each network, `SPC-2` finds the smallest installed integer capacity satisfying:

> installed slots − largest of six district allocations ≥ campaign family demand

The result is 415 slots for the 334-formation campaign and 427 for the 341-formation campaign: 842 total, including 167 family-specific `N−1` slots. All ninety-six family–network tests survive refusal or loss of their largest district allocation. The margin is sometimes zero by design; unrelated spare capacity cannot cure a missing flood, clinical, port-opening or grid receiver.

Each receiver district then carries three unlike precinct lineages, each sized to 50 percent of district reception. Any two retain the full installed district capacity. “Unlike” means separate access, utility, handling, control, safety and operating causes—not three gates sharing one power plant or authority.

## Presence without a base

The zero-notice Indo-Pacific campaign has fourteen days of inherited transit; the Atlantic/Americas case has ten.[^1] Useful service must therefore begin locally before the main force arrives. Four custody tiers separate that bridge:

1. host civil plant, workforce and partner stocks remain continuously host-controlled;
2. U.S. prepositioned modules remain under bilateral dual-key custody and earn no force credit before acceptance;
3. accepted U.S. serials operate under U.S. technical and host civil authority and become non-recallable without bilateral release; and
4. transferred systems move to host operation, repair, evidence and reproduction without a mandatory U.S. key, cloud, supplier or operator.[^2]

Twenty-five percent of invited early-entry mass is about 612,000 tonnes. Fourteen days of the inherited international sustainment signal is about 683,000 tonnes. Because early mass may contain some initial sustainment, these are separate ledgers: a fully nested lower bound is 0.683 Mt; an additive handling upper bound is 1.30 Mt. Item-level configuration must resolve overlap before procurement, but cannot erase either obligation.

## Productive sovereignty

Every family in each network receives two partner production roots in different districts and one separately placed public assurance cell. Their initial scope is receiver interfaces, consumables, field repair, replacement parts and transferable service modules—not a fictional claim that every partner immediately reproduces a heavy U.S. carrier. Root pairs may not share decisive ownership, tools, suppliers, control, data custody or qualification authority.

This creates a technology program aimed at sovereignty: self-erecting civil receiver precincts; autonomous multimodal discharge; reversible utility transformation; offline host control; machine-verifiable dual-key custody; partner microfactories and metrology; autonomous stock rotation; rapid shorefall-to-inland utility corridors; mobile residual metabolism; rights-preserving service twins; adversarial partner ranges; and exit-state compilers. Innovation is successful when the host can safely continue, refuse, repair and reproduce—not when the U.S. system remains indispensable.

## Economic boundary

The base screen values family receiver plant at 25 percent of the USD 1.56T installed-family reference, then adds thirty-six access precincts, 192 production roots, ninety-six assurance cells, twelve academies, forward mission stock, bridge stock, protective works and 20 percent program reserve. The result is USD 1.28T, with a declared USD 0.976–1.81T sensitivity. Mature operations screen at USD 102B/year; the ten-year build plus ramped twenty-five-year operation exposes about USD 3.38T.

These are design comparisons, not appropriations. Receiver capacity already appears inside the inherited common-capital and operating screens; `SPC-2` cross-classifies that boundary and does not add it automatically to the USD 17.4T Department total.[^3] Partner commercial revenue also remains outside readiness and treaty credit.[^4]

The nineteen-sheet workbook contains the complete family and district allocation, precinct capacity, material bridge, partner roots, custody ladder, failure worlds, cost sensitivity and twelve technology programs. Fourteen saved-file audits pass and four adversarial mutations restore. All partner identities, production evidence and treaty authority remain source-empty; released authority is zero and rendering remains blocked.
