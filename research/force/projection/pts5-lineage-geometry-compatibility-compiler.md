# `PTS-5` lineage–geometry compatibility compiler

## From attractive subsystems to a force that can still arrive, operate and repeat

**Research status:** first-principles configuration compiler and access-assurance lower bound; no pair or group admitted

**Decision date:** 7 September 2026

**Companion model:** [`pts5-lineage-geometry-compatibility-compiler.xlsx`](../../../models/force/projection/pts5-lineage-geometry-compatibility-compiler.xlsx)

## Executive judgment

The Department of Resilience should not buy an energy source, a carrier or even a nominally complete `PTS-5` service cell. It should buy governed combinations of source, heat sink, carrier, site, workforce, receiver and reset capacity whose public effect survives the failures the Department promises to absorb.

That is a different acquisition object.

Pass 73 defined six rival energy and heat-rejection lineages. Pass 74 defined five rival deployment geometries. Each pass deliberately isolated one design coordinate. This pass crosses them, producing thirty lineage–geometry pairs and testing each pair in thirty-two integrated worlds. The 960 resulting records expose interactions that neither source selection nor host selection can reveal alone.

No universal pair exists. None passes all thirty-two declared worlds, none is admitted, all forty-two representative evidence gates remain open and rendering remains blocked.

The result is nevertheless much more specific than “more research is needed.” It separates the force into operating roles:

- six pairs are plausible `T1 MOBILE` rapid-entry competitors;
- eight are `T1 PREPARED` competitors whose speed is purchased through installed geography;
- six are `T2 TRANSITION` competitors that can contribute endurance but miss the rapid-service role in some hosts;
- five strategic firm-source pairs remain `T3 FIRM`; and
- all five transported-storage pairs require a **new host family** before their worlds can even be credited.

The storage result is decisive. A 2,784-tonne dry storage-only lineage, approximately 11,384 m³ before carrier structure, does not become a mobile response system by dividing it into modules. It exceeds the present road, rail, shallow-water, prepared-anchor and tri-modal envelopes. The program either needs a new heavy-storage carrier family, a step change in delivered storage density, a distributed prepared-stock doctrine, or a different source logic. Treating those alternatives as interchangeable would hide the central force-design choice.

The more consequential result occurs above the individual pair. A `PTS-50` group owes ten simultaneous `PTS-5` service cells and presently carries twelve physical cells. If road, rail and navigable water are three independent mobile access modes and the force claims it can lose any two of them, a fractional split across the modes is not redundancy. The surviving mode must retain complete cell-equivalents.

Let `p` be the number of independently available fixed cells. Each mobile mode must then carry `10 − p` complete cells. The physical inventory lower bound is:

```text
total packages = p + 3(10 − p) = 30 − 2p
```

Keeping total inventory at or below twelve requires `p ≥ 9`. Nine fixed cells plus one complete road cell, one complete rail cell and one complete water cell preserve ten cells after any two mobile modes fail—but only while the nine fixed cells remain independently available.

At an unprepared receiver, `p = 0`. The same guarantee requires thirty complete mobile cell-equivalents: ten independently available through each of the three modes. A three-way division of twelve unique cells does not close the guarantee. It produces three mutually exclusive fragments of one force.

This theorem changes the policy question. The Department cannot simultaneously promise an unprepared receiver, ten complete service cells, survival of any two access-mode losses and a twelve-cell inventory. It must relax a guarantee, store readiness in geography, multiply the mobile force, or invent a new form of access independence.

The corresponding twenty-five-year national program boundaries are approximately:

| Architecture boundary | Physical packages per `PTS-50` group | National program, constant 2026 dollars | What it does not prove |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Pass 75 research comparator | 12 | $4.753T | current twelve-cell force does not close two-mode denial |
| unprepared two-mode guarantee | 30 | $8.322T | receiver, route and simultaneous full-mission evidence remain open |
| six fixed cells plus four copies per mode | 18 | $5.705T | loses the ten-cell guarantee after anchor plus one mode fail |
| nine fixed cells plus one copy per mode | 12 | $4.397T | closes transport denial only while fixed service survives |
| ten fixed cells | 10 | $3.961T | ceases to be a general mobile projection force |

These are architecture boundaries, not procurement recommendations. The cost declines as readiness is stored forward because the declared fixed-cell comparator is cheaper than three separately carried complete mobile copies. That is a result of the present hypotheses, not a universal economic law. Land, hardening, local workforce, political agreements, maintenance access, common hazards and fixed-site loss are not free.

The decision now belongs to doctrine before it belongs to vehicle design: **where does the Department store readiness, and which simultaneous denials does it promise to survive?**

## 1. Why the compiler is the acquisition object

The earlier competitions were necessary but incomplete. An energy lineage answers where power comes from, how it starts, how long it lasts, what heat it creates and what happens when its source is denied. A deployment geometry answers how modules move, what routes and terminals they require, how much carrier tare accompanies them, where their heat field can operate and how much work is required before public service is released.

Neither coordinate has an independent operational meaning.

A source with excellent endurance can fail because its heat sink cannot operate at an occupied site. A road-compatible module can fail because the complete lineage becomes a 3.5-km road train. A barge can carry the mass but cannot serve a landlocked receiver. A prepared anchor can release service in hours but ceases to exist when the fixed site is lost. A tri-modal lattice can distribute pieces across three modes and still deliver no complete service when two routes fail.

The compiler therefore treats a pair as the smallest design object eligible for further competition:

```text
PAIR = SOURCE LINEAGE × DEPLOYMENT GEOMETRY × RECEIVER ENVELOPE
       × SETUP WORK × HEAT DESTINATION × CONTROL STATE × RESET STATE
```

It then asks a second question the pair cannot answer:

```text
GROUP = COMPLETE PAIRS × INDEPENDENT ACCESS × FORWARD STOCK
        × RESERVE × SIMULTANEOUS-DENIAL GUARANTEE
```

This two-level structure prevents three recurring errors.

First, it prevents an attractive component from inheriting the readiness of a complete system. Second, it prevents pass counts from becoming a weighted ranking whose assumptions are hidden. Third, it prevents diversity of names or transport modes from being credited as independence before complete service survives through the whole chain.

### 1.1 The constant public contract

Every pair inherits the same base obligation:

| Obligation | Declared value |
|---|---:|
| accountable population | 5,000 people |
| mission duration | 168 h |
| protected-service floor | 40% |
| protected-service deadline | 24 h |
| complete-service deadline | 48 h |
| `PTS-50` group requirement | 10 simultaneous cells |
| present organic group inventory | 12 cells |

The service includes source, conversion, electricity, useful cooling, heat rejection, protection, control, building interface and the wet or dry inventories required for the declared mission. A carrier arriving by hour twenty-four does not pass if safe and useful building service remains unreleased at hour forty-nine.

### 1.2 What the model compiles

For every source–host pair the workbook calculates:

- fixed and mobile dry mass;
- mobile wet mass;
- governed module positions and volume;
- road, rail and water carrier positions;
- support vehicles, rail support, barges and assist craft;
- carrier tare, transported-system mass and installed-system mass;
- road-train length, rail-consist length and shallow-water draft;
- protected, full and degraded-crew setup time;
- crew requirement;
- source heat duty, installed rejection and compounded hot-smoke `N-1` ratio;
- no-water rejection ratio;
- occupied-site area, utility reach, noise and exhaust compatibility;
- current host-envelope fit;
- temporal role;
- pair-world failure identities;
- configured capital, annual operations and twenty-five-year lifecycle cost; and
- evidence and admission state.

These values are engineering hypotheses used to expose contradictions. They are not measurements, current commercial specifications or claims about an existing fleet.

## 2. The thirty-pair force map

The compiler does not declare a winner. It assigns each pair to the role its coupled configuration can plausibly perform before representative testing.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `L1` organic multi-liquid bridge | `T1 MOBILE` · 23/32 | `T1 MOBILE` · 19/32 | `T1 MOBILE` · 24/32 | `T1 PREPARED` · 27/32 | `T1 MOBILE` · 22/32 |
| `L2` conditioned gas + liquid bridge | `T1 MOBILE` · 23/32 | `T2 TRANSITION` · 11/32 | `T1 MOBILE` · 25/32 | `T1 PREPARED` · 28/32 | `T2 TRANSITION` · 15/32 |
| `L3` transported storage-only | new host · 0/32 | new host · 0/32 | new host · 0/32 | new host · 0/32 | new host · 0/32 |
| `L4` local-resource hybrid | `T2 TRANSITION` · 15/32 | `T2 TRANSITION` · 11/32 | `T2 TRANSITION` · 15/32 | `T1 PREPARED` · 28/32 | `T2 TRANSITION` · 16/32 |
| `L5` prepared metropolitan anchor | `T1 PREPARED` · 20/32 | `T1 PREPARED` · 18/32 | `T1 PREPARED` · 21/32 | `T1 PREPARED` · 26/32 | `T1 PREPARED` · 22/32 |
| `L6` strategic firm microreactor | `T3 FIRM` · 14/32 | `T3 FIRM` · 11/32 | `T3 FIRM` · 14/32 | `T3 FIRM` · 28/32 | `T3 FIRM` · 15/32 |

The numerator is not a score. It is an index into named failure identities. A pair that passes twenty-eight worlds can still be ineligible for the mission if the four failures include unprepared access, anchor loss or the complete-service deadline. A pair passing fewer worlds may be indispensable in the one geography where its host retains access.

### 2.1 `T1 MOBILE`: rapid entry remains a portfolio

The six rapid mobile competitors are four `L1` pairings and two `L2` pairings.

`L1` remains the broadest rapid mobile bridge because its seven-day energy inventory moves with the cell. It pays for that independence in wet logistics, combustion separation, noise and reset. The shallow-water pairing reaches twenty-four worlds, the best mobile `L1` result, but is not available to a landlocked receiver. The road pairing reaches twenty-three and preserves partial service after a largest-packet loss, but total road denial removes it. The rail and tri-modal versions introduce terminal and mode-correlation failures.

`L2` becomes `T1 MOBILE` only in road and shallow-water form. Rail and tri-modal integration work moves it into `T2 TRANSITION`. Its conditioned-gas dependence also means that mobility of the carrier is not mobility of the energy source; the liquid bridge is bounded.

The implication is not to choose road or barge. It is to retain rival mobile pairs whose geography and source dependencies are deliberately different, then test whether those differences remain independent through suppliers, bases, crews, control systems and receivers.

### 2.2 `T1 PREPARED`: speed is stored before the event

All five `L5` pairings are prepared by definition. `L1`, `L2` and `L4` also become `T1 PREPARED` in `G4` because fixed service and civil works absorb much of the arrival burden.

Prepared capability should not be described as less mobile equipment. It is a separate weapon system distributed across geography:

- hardened electrical and thermal interfaces;
- independently tested switchgear and protection;
- fixed rejection fields or qualified heat sinks;
- accessible staging and utility corridors;
- local stocks and black-start capacity;
- trained public and private operators;
- legal authority and building-access agreements;
- inspected connection points; and
- an exercised handoff to arriving mobile plant.

The prepared anchor is fast because a large part of the mission has already been performed. Its 26–28-world results do not make it universal. Loss of the fixed anchor, an unprepared destination or a receiver outside the prepared lattice removes the premise that created the speed.

### 2.3 `T2 TRANSITION`: strategically useful is not first-arrival capable

Six pairings remain useful but do not belong in the first 48-hour complete-service echelon: `L2-G2`, `L2-G5` and four fully mobile `L4` pairings.

The distinction matters institutionally. If every asset is called rapid response, planners will consume the same deployment window several times. Transition formations should be scheduled to replace liquid-intensive `T1` bridges, connect qualified local resources, extend endurance and release first-wave crews and carriers. Their readiness metrics should measure takeover time and continuity through handoff, not arrival theater.

### 2.4 `T3 FIRM`: firm source is a campaign asset

Every `L6` pair remains `T3 FIRM`, including `L6-G4`, which passes twenty-eight worlds under the declared configuration. The role assignment is not punishment for mass or regulation. It recognizes the larger system required for firm-source service: licensing, surveyed sites, security, residual-heat management, specialized operators, long-duration custody and a separate arrival chain.

A strategic firm source can transform a long campaign, a repeated event or a region whose liquid and gas logistics are exhausted. It should not receive hour-48 arrival credit from an assumed route or an undeclared prepared site.

### 2.5 `L3`: modularity does not repeal mass and volume

`L3` contains 2,784 dry tonnes, of which 2,334 tonnes are transported storage. Its modeled base cube is about 11,384 m³ and its governed position count reaches approximately 115 before host duplication. Across the five geometries, the compiled mobile system requires roughly 117–122 positions.

The resulting envelopes include:

- approximately 111 road cargo positions in the road lattice;
- approximately 47 rail cargo cars plus road support in the rail case;
- four shallow-water hosts in the barge case;
- approximately forty-four arriving road cargo positions even after the prepared anchor fixes 62% of dry plant; and
- forty-six road cargo, sixteen rail cargo and two shallow-water hosts in the tri-modal case.

The existing hosts were never designed for that burden. The compiler therefore assigns zero world credit rather than allowing an impossible carrier assumption to flow through the rest of the model.

This creates a clean-sheet research competition among four fundamentally different answers:

1. raise delivered storage density and fire isolation enough to leave the break;
2. design a purpose-built heavy road/rail/water storage carrier family;
3. pre-position most storage as geographically distributed resilience stock; or
4. use storage for black start and transient support rather than seven-day primary energy.

The answer should be earned by measured system articles, not chosen from nominal cell chemistry.

## 3. Integrated worlds and failure identity

The thirty-two worlds combine rather than sequence the principal boundaries:

- base seven-day and deadline service;
- liquid, gas, local-resource and prepared-anchor denial;
- road, rail, water and terminal loss;
- landlocked, rail-free and unprepared receivers;
- site area and utility stand-off;
- wet ground and 0.6-m flooding;
- 50°C ambient, smoke and largest rejection-train loss;
- heat-rejection water denial;
- public-boundary noise and exhaust separation;
- largest mobile-packet loss;
- 25% setup-crew loss;
- commercial network and cloud loss;
- three-day reset and second mission;
- severe compound receiver cases; and
- three pairwise losses among road, rail and water.

The model separates **available service** from **physical admission**. Energy, route, ground, thermal, packet and offline-control factors determine the fraction of service that remains. Site, reach, noise, exhaust, setup time, reset and host-envelope tests determine whether that service can lawfully and physically be released.

This avoids a common category error. A source may retain one unit of theoretical energy while the pair still fails because its heat field does not fit, its carrier cannot reach the site or its crew cannot complete setup before the deadline.

### 3.1 Why no pair should pass every world

Several worlds are intentionally mutually discriminating. A water-dependent host cannot also be credited as landlocked. A prepared anchor should not silently become an unprepared mobile system. A combustion-heavy rapid bridge should not receive occupied-site exhaust credit without a sufficient separation geometry. A firm source should not inherit the setup time of a liquid generator.

The absence of a universal pair is therefore an expected architectural result. The Department should seek a portfolio whose failure boundaries are complementary, not a fictional universal machine.

### 3.2 Pair diversity is not yet portfolio independence

Two pairs with different codes may still share:

- the same energy lot or upstream component;
- one module controller or protection firmware;
- one specialized crew school;
- one mobilization base or reset depot;
- one rail terminal, waterway segment or bridge corridor;
- one rejection-train supplier;
- one receiver interface; or
- one civil authority needed for release.

The present workbook exposes pair behavior but does not award group independence for different names. The next group-level compiler must carry serialized ancestry and route/site custody from the earlier `WRG-50` work into the `PTS-50` formation.

## 4. The access-assurance theorem

### 4.1 Statement

Suppose a `PTS-50` group must preserve ten complete service-cell equivalents. Suppose road, rail and water are the three mobile access modes and the guarantee covers loss of any two. Let `p` complete cells be fixed at the receiver and independently available. Then each mobile mode must contain at least `10 − p` complete cell-equivalents.

The minimum package count is therefore:

```text
N(p) = p + 3(10 − p) = 30 − 2p
```

For a twelve-package inventory:

```text
30 − 2p ≤ 12
p ≥ 9
```

The lower bound is independent of whether the mobile packages use road vehicles, railcars, barges, aircraft or another carrier. It follows from the guarantee and the meaning of complete service.

### 4.2 Why fractional splitting fails

If twelve unique cells are divided four each across road, rail and water, loss of any two modes leaves four, not ten. If every cell is divided into three mode-specific fractions, loss of two modes leaves one-third of each cell. Ten partial sources without complete heat, control, distribution and receiver chains are not ten service cells.

Independence requires an independently releasable public effect, not a distinct shipment label.

### 4.3 The forward-stock curve

| Fixed cells `p` | Mobile cells per mode | Total packages | Retained after any two transport modes fail | Retained after fixed anchor + road fail |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 10 | 30 | 10 · pass | 20 · pass |
| 3 | 7 | 24 | 10 · pass | 14 · pass |
| 6 | 4 | 18 | 10 · pass | 8 · fail |
| 9 | 1 | 12 | 10 · pass | 2 · fail |
| 10 | 0 | 10 | 10 · pass | 0 · fail |

The last column exposes the unresolved boundary. Forward stock substitutes geography for mobile replication, but concentration in the fixed anchor creates a different common mode. The `p = 9` solution closes the declared two-transport-mode theorem and fails a fixed-anchor-plus-road challenge.

No option in this table closes every combination of fixed-site and route loss. That would require more stock, another independent mobile mode, a lower simultaneous service guarantee, faster restoration between losses, or a geographically federated set of fixed cells that does not share one anchor failure.

### 4.4 Doctrine choices made explicit

The Department now has four coherent doctrines to compare:

**Expeditionary replication.** Carry ten complete copies through each independent mode. This preserves the strongest unprepared-receiver guarantee and creates the largest fleet, workforce, base and lifecycle burden.

**Prepared federation.** Install at least nine independently releasable cells across protected receiving geography and retain one complete cell on each mobile mode. This preserves a twelve-package inventory against two transport-mode losses but shifts the hard problem into fixed-site independence.

**Hybrid risk acceptance.** Hold three to six fixed cells and seven to four copies per mode. This uses 18–24 packages and accepts some anchor-plus-mode combinations as service failures.

**Selective guarantee.** Do not promise ten simultaneous cells after any two mode losses everywhere. Mission-compile the guarantee by geography, population risk, warning time and adversarial or environmental likelihood.

The model does not select among them. Selection requires an explicit public guarantee and a national receiving-geography design.

## 5. Program economics

The Pass 74 national comparator was $4.693T over twenty-five years in constant 2026 dollars. The current lineage-and-host cell layer accounts for $2.564T. Removing that layer leaves $2.129T in common national systems: non-cell formations, shared equipment, pools, operations, nodes, schools and bases.

Pass 75 expands the geometry technology and proving portfolio from $180B to $240B. The resulting $4.753T research comparator is:

```text
$4.693T Pass 74 force + $0.060T incremental coupling research
= $4.753T
```

It is not an assured-force total.

The access alternatives replace the current $2.564T cell layer with a different number of representative fixed and mobile lifecycle packages, retain the $2.129T common program and add the $60B coupling increment. This yields the $3.961T–$8.322T range shown above.

### 5.1 What the range means

The upper end is not “wasteful redundancy.” It is the literal price of making three mobile modes independently capable of the same ten-cell effect at an unprepared receiver under the present cost hypotheses.

The lower end is not “cheap resilience.” It stores nearly all operational capacity in prepared geography and gives up general force projection. Its fixed-cell cost does not yet include a mature national land, hardening, local staffing, recertification, environmental permitting or replacement program.

The meaningful comparison is not mobile versus fixed cost in isolation. It is the cost of a public guarantee across the complete chain:

```text
cost of readiness = equipment + carriers + prepared geography + workforce
                    + bases + proving + operations + replacement + reset
                    + the residual risk of the failures not covered
```

### 5.2 No false precision

All monetary values are deterministic constant-dollar comparators. They do not include a probabilistic benefit-cost claim, avoided-loss estimate or social discounting decision. The next fiscal pass should introduce learning curves, replacement schedules, geographic site classes, utilization, workforce pipelines, correlated cost risk and explicit uncertainty bands only after the Department chooses the guarantee being costed.

## 6. The $240B coupling-technology program

The compiler converts contradictions into sixteen measurable research and industrialization programs:

| Program family | 25-year envelope | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| compiler, module envelope and energy/rejection exchange | $38B | make invalid pairs rejectable before dispatch and permit governed cross-host substitution |
| heavy storage, firm anchor and civil-utility host families | $86B | create carriers and prepared systems where present envelopes fail |
| replicated access and stock positioning | $34B | make a surviving mode carry complete service rather than a fraction |
| fixed/mobile custody and mission allocation | $16B | preserve authority, configuration and source selection across prepared and arriving systems |
| rapid acceptance, hot-smoke sites and cross-prime safety | $30B | turn net power, final heat destination and common interfaces into field evidence |
| reverse logistics and dual-denial demonstration | $36B | prove reset, quarantine and complete-group service after simultaneous losses |
| **total** | **$240B** | |

The most important programs are not necessarily the largest vehicles.

**The lineage–geometry compiler** should become a government-owned configuration service with offline operation, signed interface records and a safe failure state. It should reject combinations whose mass, cube, heat, setup, receiver, route or evidence state does not close.

**The measured module envelope** should govern mass, center of gravity, restraint, fold-out clearances, lifting, electrical and fluid boundaries, maintenance space and damaged-state recovery. A nominal container standard is insufficient.

**Energy and rejection exchange** should allow a host to substitute sources or heat sinks without losing protection, controls or public-service accounting. Cross-host exchange is a novel capability and a potential common mode; both must be tested.

**The heavy-storage carrier family** exists because all five `L3` pairings break current envelopes. The research target is not “carry more batteries.” It is at least one complete storage lineage that leaves the host-family break while preserving thermal safety, damaged-module custody, route access and setup time.

**Replicated tri-modal service** must demonstrate one complete, independently releasable cell on a surviving route. The trial should deny the other two modes physically and remove their crews, nodes, control paths and spares from the test.

**Dual-denial group demonstration** is the culminating acquisition trial: ten complete service cells and a credible second-event state after two transport-mode losses and one correlated supplier hold.

## 7. Force and institutional implications

### 7.1 A new service profession: resilience configuration engineering

The compiler implies a permanent profession responsible for the validity of the service configuration from origin through reset. Its practitioners combine mission engineering, safety, logistics, power and thermal systems, site integration, software assurance and public-service accounting.

They do not replace operators or acquisition engineers. They own the cross-boundary proof that no one contractor or service can see completely.

### 7.2 Receiving geography is part of the force

The nine-forward-cell lower bound makes prepared geography a force element rather than an external assumption. A receiving site that contributes fixed-cell credit needs:

- independent energy, rejection, control and building-release paths;
- a defined hazard and access envelope;
- sovereign or contractually durable custody;
- continuously trained operators;
- stocks and maintenance depth;
- periodic full-load testing;
- a safe isolation boundary from arriving mobile plant; and
- separate failure ancestry from the other fixed cells credited in the group.

Nine cells inside one floodplain, utility protection zone, cyber authority or labor pool are not nine independent cells.

### 7.3 Bases become readiness reservoirs

An unprepared guarantee creates extraordinary mobile replication. A prepared guarantee creates extraordinary fixed-site obligations. In both cases, bases, depots and regional nodes hold operational value only when they preserve complete deployable configurations, not piles of modules.

The Department therefore needs configuration-controlled storage, live utility test stands, hot and smoke proving, rail and water interfaces, damaged-module quarantine, reverse logistics and enough crew depth to launch and reset independent paths without borrowing the same specialists.

### 7.4 Industry competes complete effects

Prime contractors should compete complete source–host pairs and later complete group architectures. The government owns interfaces, evidence schemas and the mission compiler. Industrial plurality is measured through serialized supplier ancestry and demonstrated substitution, not the number of logos on common components.

This is the civil analogue of a major force-development enterprise: distinct primes for road, rail, shallow-water, prepared and firm-source systems; government arsenals and proving grounds; long-term production reservations; operator and maintainer schools; and recurring full-mission exercises.

## 8. Evidence and admission

The workbook registers forty-two open gates.

The first thirty require one representative integrated article for every source–host pair. Each article must measure complete service, carrier burden, site, heat, setup and reset with the source and host configuration locked together. Evidence from `L1-G1` does not silently migrate to `L2-G1`, and evidence from `L1-G1` does not prove `L1-G3`.

The final twelve gates test the boundaries that pair articles cannot close alone:

1. common service-interface exchange across two lineages and two hosts;
2. hot-smoke `N-1` operation at complete-pair scale;
3. no-water heat rejection at an occupied site;
4. exhaust and acoustic public-boundary compatibility;
5. an hour-48 complete-service deployment campaign;
6. a heavy-storage host competition;
7. a licensed firm-source arrival chain;
8. a complete `PTS-50` group after two transport modes are denied;
9. a prepared-forward group after fixed-anchor plus route loss;
10. a correlated supplier hold using serialized ancestry;
11. a three-day reset and second mission with the same articles and crew; and
12. a blinded seven-day district mission reporting protected people-hours and second-event state.

Strict admission remains zero. A pair's role is a research disposition, not permission to procure, deploy or illustrate a final vehicle.

## 9. What this pass changes

Pass 73's energy lineages remain valid subsystem competitors. Pass 74's deployment geometries remain valid host competitors. Their isolated world counts no longer serve as force-selection evidence.

The current design authority is the coupled pair and the complete group guarantee:

```text
ENERGY LINEAGE alone                  → insufficient
DEPLOYMENT GEOMETRY alone             → insufficient
LINEAGE × GEOMETRY pair               → minimum article for competition
PAIR PORTFOLIO × independent access   → minimum formation for assurance
```

The pass also overturns two convenient intuitions.

First, tri-modal carriage does not imply tri-modal resilience. Independence exists only when a surviving mode retains complete service.

Second, a twelve-cell reserve structure cannot close a ten-cell unprepared-receiver guarantee after any two of three modes fail. That is a mathematical contradiction, not a scheduling problem.

## 10. The next decision

The next pass should not add more platforms. It should compile rival **national readiness topologies** around the newly explicit guarantee.

At minimum it should compare:

- thirty-copy expeditionary replication;
- nine-cell prepared federations with three mobile complements;
- eighteen- and twenty-four-package hybrid forces;
- selective guarantees by receiver class; and
- a novel fourth access path or non-transport substitution.

Each topology must allocate the seventy-two provisional `PTS-50` groups to actual receiving geographies, state which fixed cells are independently available, serialize mobile and fixed failure ancestry, test anchor-plus-mode and supplier-plus-mode compounds, and carry lifecycle cost with uncertainty.

Only after one topology closes should the project derive external vehicle dimensions and render a representative family. The rendering gate is therefore unchanged.

## Method and source policy

This pass uses only the internal Pass 73 and Pass 74 models plus declared Pass 75 design assumptions. It adds no external factual claim and does not use external websites. Its purpose is deductive force design: preserve inherited balances, expose cross-coordinate contradictions and convert them into falsifiable research requirements.

The companion workbook contains thirteen sheets, thirty compiled pairs, thirty-two integrated worlds, 960 pair-world records, sixteen technology programs, forty-two open evidence gates and seventy-nine independent arithmetic and integrity checks. All seventy-nine checks pass and the formula-error scan returns zero matches.

No pair or complete group has reached representative evidence maturity. Rendering remains blocked.
