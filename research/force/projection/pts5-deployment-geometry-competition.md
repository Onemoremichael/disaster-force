# `PTS-5` deployment-geometry competition

## From an energy lineage to a road, rail, shallow-water and prepared civil utility force

**Research status:** first-principles host and site competition; no geometry admitted

**Decision date:** 7 September 2026

**Companion model:** [`pts5-deployment-geometry-competition.xlsx`](../../../models/force/projection/pts5-deployment-geometry-competition.xlsx)

## Executive judgment

The form of a civil utility force cannot be selected by placing a generator, chiller and fuel tank on a vehicle. The public mission has to be decomposed into transportable modules, carrier positions, working clearances, heat-exchanger face, air-intake area, hazardous separation, setup labor, utility reach, route dependencies, safe stop and reset before a vehicle silhouette becomes meaningful.

The [`PTS-5` energy-lineage competition](pts5-energy-and-heat-rejection-lineage-competition.md) establishes that one 5,000-person occupied-building cell needs 1.422 MWe continuously, 1.920 MWe at peak, 1.965 MWth of useful cooling and 238.923 MWh over seven days. Its rapid organic liquid lineage carries 580 dry tonnes of equipment, 54.790 tonnes of liquid inventory and 4.765 MWth of occupied-site heat rejection after source-conversion waste is counted.

This pass holds that service payload constant and asks what happens when it is made physically deployable in five rival geometries:

- `G1`, a road-distributed lattice;
- `G2`, a rail utility consist;
- `G3`, a shallow-draft utility barge;
- `G4`, a prepared metropolitan anchor; and
- `G5`, a tri-modal split lattice.

The comparison does not choose the best carrier. It reveals five different force logics.

The 580 dry tonnes become thirty governed dry-module positions. Two segregated liquid positions add 54.790 tonnes and bring the service stack to thirty-two positions and 2,630 m³. Geometry-specific interface duplication then raises mobile payload to 659.790–784.790 tonnes for the fully mobile competitors. The prepared anchor reduces arriving payload to 291.358 tonnes only by installing 359.600 tonnes of dry plant before the event.

Carrier tare is a first-order force burden. Once tractors, support vehicles, railcars, locomotives, barge structure and assist craft are counted, the complete transported system spans 471.358 tonnes for the prepared-anchor mobile element to 1,504.790 tonnes for the tri-modal lattice. Every wholly mobile competitor exceeds 1,000 tonnes when its carriers are included even though every service payload remains below the prior 1,000-tonne cell screen.

Heat rejection defines much of the visible form. The baseline road geometry installs 9 MWth in four isolable trains. At the declared 50°C, smoke and largest-train-loss factors, it retains 5.346 MWth, a ratio of 1.122 against the 4.765-MWth duty. That plant requires approximately 105.9 m² of exchanger face, 707 m³/s of design airflow and 404 m² of usable intake area before source, cable, fuel, egress, maintenance or public separation is added. Across the five geometries, free intake alone spans 382–449 m².

The vehicle counts are therefore substantial:

| Geometry | Mobile payload | Carrier tare | Complete transported system | Representative host count |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `G1` road-distributed lattice | 674.790 t | 450 t | 1,124.790 t | 27 road cargo positions + 6 support positions |
| `G2` rail utility consist | 659.790 t | 540 t | 1,199.790 t | 11 rail cargo cars + 3 support cars + 8 road positions |
| `G3` shallow-draft utility barge | 669.790 t | 620 t | 1,289.790 t | 1 barge + 2 assist craft + 3 road support positions |
| `G4` prepared metropolitan anchor | 291.358 t arriving | 180 t mobile carrier | 471.358 t arriving; 830.958 t including fixed plant | 12 road cargo positions + 4 support positions |
| `G5` tri-modal split lattice | 784.790 t | 720 t | 1,504.790 t | 13 road cargo + 8 support, 5 rail cargo + 3 support, 1 barge + 2 assist craft |

Those counts are design hypotheses, not present-fleet descriptions. They expose the scale of the object that must be tested.

All five geometries meet the declared base, 24-hour protected-service and 48-hour full-service cases. They pass 17, 14, 18, 20 and 17 of twenty-three deterministic worlds. The counts are not rankings. `G4` passes the most because it stores readiness in geography; it fails the unprepared-receiver and long-stand-off worlds that define the need for a national mobile force. `G3` performs well because water access eliminates many ground constraints; it fails when the receiver is landlocked or the water route is denied. `G1` is the strongest unprepared compound response but fails total road denial, stand-off, acoustic, exhaust, degraded-crew and reset boundaries.

No geometry is universal. The correct acquisition object is a governed service-module grammar and a portfolio of host families, not one universal resilience vehicle.

The model assigns 864 organic host positions across seventy-two provisional `PTS-50` groups and adds ninety-six pooled shallow-water hosts that can re-host compatible modules where geography permits. The geometry layer carries $441.600 billion of incremental host capital and $535.200 billion of twenty-five-year incremental operating cost. After removing Pass 73's provisional $216 billion metropolitan-works allowance and inserting geometry-specific hosts, shared route nodes, technology, proving, workforce and bases, the national comparator becomes approximately **$4.693 trillion in constant 2026 dollars**.

That is not a procurement recommendation. It is the fiscal consequence of admitting carrier structure, prepared civil works and setup labor into the service force rather than treating them as free support.

Thirty-four representative evidence gates remain open. No host, carrier, module envelope or site plan is admitted. Rendering remains blocked.

## 1. The decision boundary

The previous pass competed complete energy and heat-rejection lineages. It intentionally stopped before choosing transport form. That stop matters because an energy source and a carrier answer different questions.

An energy lineage determines:

- where primary energy comes from;
- how long it remains available;
- how it is converted;
- what heat and residuals it creates;
- which supplier and infrastructure dependencies remain; and
- how it is replenished and reset.

A deployment geometry determines:

- how many physical articles exist;
- which routes and terminals can accept them;
- how they are restrained, lifted and transferred;
- how much carrier tare moves with the payload;
- how the source and heat sink are separated from residents;
- how modules become one electrical and thermal island;
- how many qualified person-hours are needed before release;
- what survives a route or packet loss; and
- how the system leaves without damaging the receiver.

The present competition holds one source lineage constant—the Pass 73 organic multi-liquid rapid bridge—so that geometry effects remain visible. It does not claim that every final host will carry that source. The next synthesis must cross all six energy lineages with the geometry results before any host can advance.

The public contract also remains constant. Every base configuration must provide complete `PTS-5` service rather than a fraction of nameplate equipment:

| Public-service obligation | Value |
|---|---:|
| accountable population | 5,000 people |
| continuous net electricity | 1.42216 MWe |
| peak net electricity | 1.91992 MWe |
| useful cooling | 1.965 MWth |
| occupied-site rejection duty | 4.765105 MWth |
| mission duration | 168 h |
| protected-service deadline | 24 h |
| complete-service deadline | 48 h |
| degraded protected-service floor | 40% |

A carrier arriving at hour twenty is not a passed service mission if its modules remain on transport frames, the heat field cannot breathe, the grounding plan is incomplete or building connections are unreleased at hour forty-nine.

## 2. The governed service stack

The first geometry requirement is a common module grammar. The model divides the 580-tonne dry service payload into six functional families and treats the liquid inventory as a seventh.

| Module family | Positions | Dry mass | Wet mass | Cube | Setup work |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| grid-forming source submodules | 8 | 170 t | — | 680 m³ | 256 h |
| T0 storage and inverter submodules | 4 | 70 t | — | 280 m³ | 96 h |
| four rejection trains in paired modules | 8 | 150 t | — | 640 m³ | 400 h |
| switching, transformation and protection | 3 | 60 t | — | 240 m³ | 108 h |
| thermal production, pumps and storage interface | 4 | 70 t | — | 300 m³ | 160 h |
| distribution, control and building interface | 3 | 60 t | — | 420 m³ | 180 h |
| segregated liquid inventory | 2 | — | 54.790 t | 70 m³ | 100 h |
| **total** | **32** | **580 t** | **54.790 t** | **2,630 m³** | **1,300 h** |

This is a configuration grammar, not a bill of material. It answers enough physical questions to prevent the service payload from disappearing inside an aggregate tonne total.

The largest declared article is one liquid position at 27.395 tonnes. The dry modules remain at or below 21.25 tonnes. A thirty-tonne road position can therefore accept every article in the baseline. A twenty-four-tonne road sensitivity cannot accept the liquid position even if aggregate convoy capacity is large enough. That distinction is the same complete-object rule developed in the [canonical cargo compiler](canonical-cargo-and-transport-closure.md): spare tonnes on another carrier do not make an indivisible incompatible article move.

### 2.1 The module is an interface, not a box

A governed module needs more than outer dimensions. Its release record must include:

- dry, wet and operating mass;
- center of gravity in transport and operating states;
- external dimensions and fold-out envelope;
- lifting, jacking, restraint and recovery points;
- electrical voltage, frequency, fault and grounding behavior;
- fluid pressure, temperature, quality and isolation behavior;
- data, time, identity and disconnected-control behavior;
- heat, intake, exhaust, noise and hazardous boundaries;
- setup sequence and required human qualifications;
- allowed carrier and node positions;
- damage state, safe stop and reverse-flow configuration; and
- supplier, software and maintenance ancestry.

The government should own this boundary. Contractors should compete implementations behind it.

### 2.2 Commonity needs a failure budget

The module grammar may standardize connectors and evidence without standardizing every critical implementation. A common high-voltage connector can lower setup time. One national protection controller or one firmware authority can turn the same interface into a fleet-wide common mode.

Each standard should therefore declare what it may make common and what must remain plural. Mechanical fit, electrical protection, fault isolation, data provenance and manual-safe behavior require separate review.

## 3. Heat rejection determines site form

The most important dimensional result is not a vehicle length. It is the field needed to move heat safely away from occupied buildings.

The baseline dry-dominant design installs 9 MWth across four equal trains. It must retain the 4.765-MWth duty after three simultaneous capacity reductions:

```text
installed rejection × 50°C factor × smoke factor × N-1 fraction

9.0 MWth × 0.88 × 0.90 × 0.75 = 5.346 MWth

5.346 ÷ 4.765 = 1.122
```

The ratio is adequate under the declared coefficients but not generous. The shallow-water geometry's corresponding ratio is 1.048. A modest loss of air-side performance can remove service even when source power is available.

At a declared nominal face flux of 0.085 MWth/m², a 9-MWth field needs approximately 105.9 m² of exchanger face. The complete air movement is larger:

```text
airflow = installed heat ÷ (air density × specific heat × temperature rise)

9,000 kW ÷ (1.05 kg/m³ × 1.01 kJ/kg-K × 12 K)
= 707.2 m³/s
```

At 2.5 m/s free-area velocity and a 70% usable-area factor, the baseline needs approximately 404.1 m² of intake area. The five competitors require 381.7–449.0 m².

Those figures are not radiator footprints. They are flow boundaries. Recirculation clearance, discharge separation, filters, acoustic treatment, access aisles, fire lanes, source spacing and building interfaces add more land.

### 3.1 The site accounts

| Geometry | Compound heat ratio | Exchanger face | Free intake | Total occupied-site account | Utility reach | Noise | Exhaust separation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `G1` road lattice | 1.122 | 105.9 m² | 404.1 m² | 4,754.1 m² | 400 m | 62 dBA | 35 m |
| `G2` rail consist | 1.109 | 108.2 m² | 413.1 m² | 6,113.1 m² | 900 m | 54 dBA | 600 m |
| `G3` shallow barge | 1.048 | 103.5 m² | 395.1 m² | 4,145.1 m² | 800 m | 52 dBA | 300 m |
| `G4` prepared anchor | 1.108 | 100.0 m² | 381.7 m² | 2,631.7 m² | 300 m | 50 dBA | 75 m |
| `G5` tri-modal lattice | 1.330 | 117.6 m² | 449.0 m² | 4,549.0 m² | 750 m | 56 dBA | 80 m |

Only `G2` exceeds the 5,000-m² occupied-site screen. That is not necessarily a defect. Its 900-m utility reach allows the consist and heat field to sit on a remote linear site. The relevant test becomes corridor loss, voltage and thermal-fluid performance across that separation.

`G1` fits the declared area but fails the present acoustic and exhaust-separation screens. A diagram that placed all road modules neatly beside the occupied building would therefore depict an unsafe arrangement. The design needs remote source pods, much stronger acoustic treatment, a longer utility corridor or a different energy lineage.

### 3.2 Smoke performance is a technology program

Reducing the common smoke capacity factor from 90% to 75% makes the compound heat ratios 0.935, 0.920, 0.870, 0.927 and 1.108. Only the five-train `G5` field remains above one. `G1` loses both the dedicated hot-smoke world and the severe compound world, reducing its pass count from seventeen to fifteen.

This sensitivity establishes a clean-sheet technology target. Filter area, self-cleaning, fan reserve, bypass modes, intake placement and maintenance exposure must be designed as part of the host rather than purchased as an environmental accessory.

## 4. Carrier tare is force mass

Earlier cell comparisons used transported payload because no carrier form had been selected. This pass separates three masses:

1. **service payload**, which performs the public mission;
2. **carrier tare**, which moves and supports that payload; and
3. **fixed installed plant**, which stores readiness at the receiver.

For `G1`, 674.790 tonnes of mobile payload require a declared 450-tonne carrier and support tail. The complete transported system is therefore 1,124.790 tonnes. For `G5`, the extra interfaces and three carrier modes push the system to 1,504.790 tonnes.

Carrier tare cannot be omitted because it consumes:

- production capacity;
- staging area;
- road, rail and waterway capacity;
- operating energy;
- maintenance and spares;
- drivers, crews and control effort;
- transfer-node time; and
- reverse-flow capacity after the mission.

It also determines whether a supposedly modular service package is actually available. Thirty-two modules without tractors, rail paths, a barge, handling systems and qualified crews are warehouse inventory, not force projection.

## 5. `G1`: road-distributed lattice

`G1` divides the cell among three separately routed road columns. It adds forty dry tonnes and 180 m³ of interface duplication so source, storage, protection and rejection functions can remain partially independent.

The resulting mobile payload is 674.790 tonnes and 2,810 m³. At thirty tonnes and 110 m³ per governed road position, it needs twenty-seven cargo positions including a segregated hazardous position. Six support positions raise the representative movement to thirty-three vehicles. With a 15-m operating interval between 13.6-m articles, the modeled road train is approximately 0.94 km long.

The length does not mean the formation should travel as one parade. The geometry is explicitly a three-column lattice. Separate origin, staging and route histories are necessary for the claimed 66.7% service retention after the largest column is lost.

`G1` requires 1,680 qualified setup hours. A 140-person cell with 75% setup qualification and sixteen productive setup hours provides exactly 1,680 hours. That ratio of 1.000 is a boundary, not comfortable resilience. A 25% setup-capacity loss reduces the ratio to 0.750.

The geometry establishes protected service at hour twelve and full service at hour thirty-six under the declared base. It passes seventeen of twenty-three worlds.

Its failures are:

- complete road-access denial;
- a 600-m source stand-off because its current utility reach is 400 m;
- the 55-dBA night boundary;
- the 50-m exhaust-separation boundary;
- the 25% setup-crew loss; and
- the three-day second-mission reset.

`G1` is the only geometry to pass the severe `W22` unprepared, landlocked, terminal-denied, one-road-corridor-loss and hot-smoke compound. It does so because three self-deploying columns preserve 66.7% of service and require no fixed anchor or transfer terminal. This makes it the correct severe mobile bridge for further research, not an admitted road vehicle.

### 5.1 The road program is not a truck purchase

The acquisition problem includes:

- a governed powered-position or tractor–trailer family;
- bridge- and pavement-aware route release;
- dispersed convoy control without commercial networks;
- automated staging and configuration verification;
- wet-ground leveling and grounding;
- public-traffic deconfliction;
- driver and maintenance cycles;
- fuel, charging and recovery for the carriers themselves; and
- rapid reverse flow after use.

A purpose-built road host should be judged by complete service released per route-hour and qualified person-hour, not maximum payload on a test track.

## 6. `G2`: rail utility consist

`G2` places ninety percent of the mobile service payload on rail and uses a ten-percent road shuttle. It adds twenty-five dry tonnes and 120 m³ of interface work.

The service payload is 659.790 tonnes and 2,750 m³. Eleven cargo cars and three support cars form the utility consist. With locomotive and clearance allowances, the modeled length is approximately 324 m. Four road cargo and four road support positions serve the bounded last-mile function.

The rail host can move source, storage, rejection and control modules as a persistent linear utility plant. Its 900-m utility reach permits source and heat equipment to remain farther from residents than the road lattice. It passes the 55-dBA and exhaust-separation worlds in the declared configuration.

The cell requires 1,730 setup hours. Its 150-person crew provides 1,800 hours, a ratio of 1.040. Full service arrives at hour forty-four, leaving only four hours of declared schedule margin.

`G2` passes fourteen worlds. It fails:

- rail-access denial;
- terminal or siding denial;
- a rail-free full-service receiver;
- the 5,000-m² adjacent-site screen;
- wet or soft ground at the declared protected-service floor;
- 0.6 m of receiver flooding;
- the 25% setup-crew loss;
- the three-day reset; and
- the severe terminal-denied compound.

The result does not reject rail. It defines two different rail doctrines.

The first is **direct service from a prepared or surviving siding**. The consist remains assembled and exports power and thermal service through a corridor.

The second is **rail delivery to an expeditionary transload node**. In that doctrine the railcars are strategic carriers, not the operating site. The model does not yet credit that case because whole-module transfer rate, cranes, autonomous handling, road shuttle count and receiver release remain unproved.

## 7. `G3`: shallow-draft utility barge

`G3` hosts the complete cell on one 72 m × 18 m shallow-water platform supported by two assist craft and three road-interface positions. It adds thirty-five dry tonnes and 160 m³ of interface structure.

The service payload is 669.790 tonnes. With a 480-tonne host lightship and the declared 0.62 block coefficient, the hydrostatic screen is:

```text
draft = displacement ÷ (length × beam × block coefficient)

(480 t + 669.790 t) ÷ (72 m × 18 m × 0.62)
= 1.431 m
```

The result sits 0.369 m below the declared 1.8-m draft boundary. It does not establish stability, trim, freeboard, current, maneuverability, bridge clearance or lock compatibility. Those remain separate gates.

The barge establishes protected service at hour fourteen and complete service at hour thirty under a no-pier bank-interface hypothesis. It needs 1,670 setup hours; the 140-person crew supplies 1,680, a ratio of 1.006.

`G3` passes eighteen worlds. It handles wet ground and receiver flooding because the primary plant remains afloat. The source can sit 300 m from occupied buildings, and the utility corridor reaches 800 m.

Its five failures are decisive:

- water-access denial;
- a landlocked full-service receiver;
- 25% setup-crew loss;
- the three-day reset; and
- the severe landlocked compound.

The 1.4-m draft sensitivity also fails the waterborne boundary by 0.031 m. That small difference is operationally large. A channel, lock or seasonal river stage does not award partial credit because a design nearly fits.

### 7.1 The barge is a host, not extra service capacity

The program carries ninety-six pooled barge hosts. Those hulls re-host governed service modules. They do not create ninety-six additional cells unless source, rejection, protection, distribution and crew capacity are also procured and assigned.

This accounting prevents the fleet from counting the same modules once in an organic group and again on a national waterway host.

### 7.2 Water is an access route, not the heat sink by default

The barge retains dry-dominant heat rejection in the no-water world. Surface water can be an additional heat sink only after temperature, contamination, ecology, intake, discharge, rights and handback boundaries are proved. The hull may not assume unlimited cooling merely because it floats.

## 8. `G4`: prepared metropolitan anchor

`G4` stores sixty percent of service capacity and 62% of dry equipment at a prepared receiver. The fixed account is 359.600 dry tonnes. A twenty-percent liquid bridge and mobile interface element bring the arriving payload to 291.358 tonnes.

Twelve road cargo positions and four support positions carry the mobile element. The representative transported system is 471.358 tonnes. Adding the fixed plant produces an 830.958-tonne installed system.

The anchor establishes protected service in approximately two hours while the fixed plant remains available and complete service in twelve. If the anchor is lost, the mobile fallback requires the full twelve-hour setup and retains a 40% protected service floor.

The fixed rejection field gives `G4` the smallest site account, 2,631.7 m², and the lowest modeled public-boundary sound, 50 dBA. The current utility reach is only 300 m because the anchor is expected to be located inside the prepared service district.

The setup account is 794 hours. Ninety deployed people provide 1,080 hours in the baseline and 810 hours after a 25% capacity loss. The degraded ratio remains 1.020, making `G4` the only competitor to pass that world.

It passes twenty of twenty-three worlds. Its failures are:

- full service at an unprepared receiver;
- the 600-m damaged-building stand-off; and
- the severe unprepared compound.

That is why the pass count is not a ranking. `G4` is excellent where a configured anchor exists and irrelevant where it does not.

### 8.1 Prepared infrastructure belongs in force readiness

The anchor must be governed like deployed equipment. Its state includes:

- structural and flood condition;
- switchgear, protection and islanding configuration;
- thermal-loop integrity;
- stored energy and fuel custody;
- rejection-field condition;
- access and exclusion routes;
- owner, utility and civil authority;
- exercised crew assignments;
- module compatibility;
- inspection history; and
- the exact fallback mode after anchor loss.

A receptacle or empty concrete pad is not a prepared anchor.

## 9. `G5`: tri-modal split lattice

`G5` divides mobile service among road, rail and shallow water in 40%, 30% and 30% packets. Each packet has separately releasable controls and interfaces. That independence adds 150 dry tonnes and 420 m³, the largest duplication account in the competition.

The mobile payload reaches 784.790 tonnes and 3,050 m³. It uses thirteen road cargo and eight road support positions, five rail cargo and three rail support cars, and one shallow host with two assist craft. The representative road element is 0.60 km; the rail element is approximately 204 m; the water packet draws 0.890 m under the common 72 m host assumption.

`G5` installs 10 MWth across five rejection trains. It retains 6.336 MWth in the hot-smoke N-1 case, a ratio of 1.330. The additional rejection and split interfaces expand its mobile system to 1,504.790 tonnes including carrier tare.

The cell needs 2,020 setup hours. Its 170-person crew provides 2,040, a ratio of 1.010. It establishes protected service at hour ten and full service at hour forty-six.

`G5` passes seventeen worlds. It retains at least 60% service after total loss of any one transport mode in a receiver where the other two modes can arrive. It fails:

- a landlocked full-service receiver because the water packet is absent;
- a rail-free full-service receiver;
- the 55-dBA night boundary at its declared 56 dBA;
- the 25% setup-crew loss;
- the three-day reset; and
- the severe compound, where landlocked and terminal-denied conditions reduce the surviving road packet below the protected-service floor.

Tri-modal distribution is therefore not universal route independence. It works only where the three routes converge and their terminals, authorities and setup crews are genuinely separate.

## 11. Geometry across the four energy echelons

Pass 73 establishes four temporal energy echelons. Geometry changes what each echelon looks like.

### `T0`: local black start and no-break continuity

`T0` belongs at the service point. Every geometry needs isolated storage, protection, critical micro-power, thermal buffering and local control. A railcar or barge still cannot make an occupied building wait for a source start sequence.

The prepared anchor can hold much of `T0` fixed. A mobile lattice carries it in multiple modules. The interface and evidence must remain common even when the physical host differs.

### `T1`: rapid utility bridge

`T1` is the focus of this pass. It is the complete source, rejection and distribution geometry that reaches protected service by hour twenty-four and full service by hour forty-eight.

Road, rail, shallow water and fixed anchors each solve a different entry problem. The force-generation system must assign them from route and receiver evidence rather than from regional habit.

### `T2`: seven-day endurance

The 54.790-tonne liquid inventory is part of the service stack. Its two positions preserve local segregation but do not prove independent supplier ancestry. Replenishment, substitution with gas or local resources, and movement to prepared energy must remain controlled transitions.

Geometry also affects resupply. A barge can receive a bulk liquid transfer without placing tankers beside residents. A rail consist can carry additional energy in the same path. A road lattice can disperse lots. A prepared anchor may hold the inventory before the event. Those differences require separate custody and failure models.

### `T3`: strategic firm service

Strategic firm power should attach through the same electrical, thermal and evidence boundaries but use a separate geometry. A microreactor, coastal energy carrier, rail firm-source unit or prepared fixed plant cannot inherit the first-arrival performance of `G1` or `G4` without its own route, site, cooling, authority and setup proof.

The [Nuclear Water–Energy System](../../systems/water/nuclear-water-energy-system.md) reaches the same conclusion at larger scale: the strategic source, public process, corridor and receiving system should remain segmented.

## 12. Force composition

The model creates a provisional geometry-host layer without claiming a final `PTS-50` composition.

Across seventy-two groups, it assigns:

| `G1` road-distributed | 432 | — |
| `G2` rail utility | 144 | — |

The organic allocation is six road, two rail, two prepared and two tri-modal host positions per provisional group. It is an industrial and readiness comparator, not a table of organization.

The shallow-water hosts are pooled because navigable geography is not evenly distributed. They are re-hosting capacity, not ninety-six extra public-service cells.

### 12.1 Why this pass does not publish a new group mass

Pass 73's `PTS-50` contains a mixed energy-lineage allocation. This pass holds the `L1` rapid-bridge payload constant to isolate geometry. Adding a `G1`, `G2` or `G5` mass directly to the Pass 73 group would imply that every gas, local-resource and prepared-anchor package has the same module and carrier burden as `L1`.

That is not established.

The previous 12,200-tonne group remains the service-package comparator. The present host masses are a geometry overlay awaiting a lineage–geometry compatibility compiler. The program can price provisional host capacity without pretending that the final coupled formation mass is known.

## 14. Clean-sheet technology portfolio

The failures define fourteen major development programs.

### `DG-01`: governed heavy intermodal module

Develop a module that can move on a thirty-tonne road position and an eighty-tonne rail position without repacking. Measure complete loaded mass, cube, restraint, connector access, transfer time and damaged-state recovery.

### `DG-02`: self-leveling wet-ground support

Develop deployable bearing, leveling, grounding and access systems that establish released electrical and thermal plant on saturated or damaged surfaces. Ground pressure and installed service time matter more than unloaded vehicle flotation.

### `DG-03`: optionally crewed civil road column

Create a road family that can distribute one cell across three genuinely independent routes, operate without commercial networks and preserve human authority in public traffic. Autonomy reduces exposure and repetitive work; it does not erase route release or civil responsibility.

### `DG-04`: disaster rail utility consist

Develop a persistent rail plant with source, rejection, controls, living support and direct utility export. Compete direct-siding operation with whole-module transload rather than blending them into one optimistic rail case.

### `DG-05`: autonomous heavy transloader

Create a field node able to move complete modules between rail, road and prepared pads without a surviving commercial crane. Measure throughput under damaged ground, misalignment, power loss, rain, smoke and reduced crew.

### `DG-06`: shallow-draft utility host

Develop an 800-tonne governed payload host that remains below the declared 1.8-m draft boundary with complete stability, trim, freeboard, recovery and module-service access. Compete modular barges, self-propelled hosts and coupled shallow craft.

### `DG-07`: debris-current mooring and recovery

Develop mooring, station keeping, fendering, debris interception, intake protection and assist craft that preserve service through one craft loss and rapidly abandon an unsafe bank.

### `DG-08`: prepared civil utility anchor

Build an islandable fixed `T0/T1` plant with module exchange, dry rejection, flood protection, local records and tested loss behavior. Treat its configuration state as national readiness.

### `DG-09`: 600-m utility corridor

Develop rapid power, thermal-fluid, sensing and return corridors that preserve fault behavior, pressure, insulation, traffic crossings and safe disconnection over at least 600 m.

### `DG-10`: fold-out high-ambient rejection field

Create compact transport modules that unfold into a 4.765-MWth safe field after 50°C, smoke and one train loss. Measure net capacity after fan power, filtration, recirculation, acoustic control and maintenance access.

### `DG-11`: occupied-site acoustic and exhaust boundary

Develop low-noise source enclosures, remote exhaust, plume controls, clean-air intake placement and local sensing that meet an explicit public boundary rather than equipment-only ratings.

### `DG-12`: robotic site composer

Build machines that place modules, supports, ducts, barriers, cables and fluid corridors, then assist inspection and test. The measure is released service per qualified person-hour with the robotic system both available and denied.

### `DG-13`: offline geometry compiler

Compile route, axle, clearance, waterway, bank, ground, heat, sound, exhaust, setup and receiver evidence into one deployable configuration without requiring cloud service.

### `DG-14`: multi-prime host interchange

Require source, rejection and service-spine modules from at least two primes to re-host on at least two carrier families. Government owns the interchange and evidence contract.

## 15. Workforce is part of geometry

The setup accounts span 794–2,020 qualified person-hours.

| Geometry | Setup work | Deployed crew | Normal margin | Margin after 25% capacity loss |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `G1` | 1,680 h | 140 | 1.000 | 0.750 |
| `G2` | 1,730 h | 150 | 1.040 | 0.780 |
| `G3` | 1,670 h | 140 | 1.006 | 0.754 |
| `G4` | 794 h | 90 | 1.360 | 1.020 |
| `G5` | 2,020 h | 170 | 1.010 | 0.757 |

These values expose three problems.

First, almost every mobile geometry is crew-limited. The declared base margins are so narrow that a qualification mismatch, heat exposure, delayed shift or unavailable handler can defeat the service deadline.

Second, headcount does not equal useful setup capacity. The model counts only 75% of deployed people as setup-qualified and gives each sixteen productive hours inside the forty-eight-hour window. A command, medical or security billet cannot be silently converted into an electrical jointer or thermal technician.

Third, robotic handling is not a headcount deletion. It changes task duration and exposure, but manual-safe setup and recovery remain evidence gates. A failed robot may not leave an energized module in an unrecoverable state.

The force therefore needs recognized professions in heavy electrical work, thermal systems, intermodal handling, rail operations, shallow-water utility service, civil site engineering, acoustic and air-side control, configuration management and public handback.

## 16. Command and mission compilation

The geometry should be compiled for a receiver before dispatch.

The configuration path is:

```text
public-service requirement
        ↓
energy and heat-rejection lineage
        ↓
governed module stack
        ↓
route and host geometry
        ↓
site, corridor and setup plan
        ↓
released building service
        ↓
reverse flow, handback and reset
```

The geographic civil authority owns public priorities, access and lawful service release. The generating formation owns the complete module configuration. Mobility commands own route and host release. Receiving and utility authorities own site admission and handback. Independent test authorities reproduce the result.

No single actor should be able to turn a route forecast into service credit. The chain must show which module, carrier, crew, site and building interface produced each protected people-hour.

### 16.1 Partial service must be explicit

The twenty-three worlds use a 40% protected-service floor after certain route or packet losses. That is not permission to divide every service by forty percent.

The cell must publish what remains:

- which buildings and refuges are connected;
- which medical and vertical loads remain protected;
- what cooling and water output is available;
- which people are outside the safe service envelope;
- what source and heat-sink constraints remain; and
- when the next transition or safe stop occurs.

Useful degraded service is a configured public product, not a ratio applied after failure.

## 17. Industrial organization

The host program creates a civil heavy-industry market that does not exist at defense scale today.

Its supplier structure includes:

- heavy road chassis, powered trailers and recovery systems;
- railcars, independent-power consists and field transload;
- shallow-draft hulls, assist craft, mooring and bank interfaces;
- prepared substations, thermal anchors and flood-protected civil works;
- switchgear, conductors, transformers and protection;
- pumps, compressors, thermal stores and heat exchangers;
- high-volume fans, filters, acoustic systems and exhaust treatment;
- modular supports, jacks, mats and rapid foundations;
- robotics, sensing and disconnected control;
- fuel custody and hazardous transfer;
- technical training, depots and regional proving sites; and
- configuration and ancestry systems.

The Department should maintain several host primes and several module primes. It should fund government-owned reference modules, interface test rigs, destructive route and site trials, and periodic no-notice cross-prime substitution.

Competition should occur at more than award. Production lines, technical data, depot access, tooling, software authority and qualified suppliers must remain plural through the fleet life.

## 18. Evidence and admission

Thirty-four representative gates remain open. They cover:

- module packing and cross-prime exchange;
- road article, route and three-column behavior;
- rail clearance, direct service and expeditionary transload;
- barge hydrostatics, current, debris, locks, banks and assist-craft loss;
- prepared-anchor structure, endurance and loss transition;
- tri-modal packet service and disconnected coordination;
- 24-hour protected and 48-hour complete service;
- measured setup work and robotic denial;
- 50°C smoke N-1 heat rejection;
- intake, acoustic, exhaust, plume, wet-ground and flood behavior;
- 600-m utility-corridor performance;
- demobilization and three-day reset;
- mixed-host group behavior;
- carrier and component ancestry holds; and
- a blinded occupied-district mission.

Strict admission remains zero. Passing a spreadsheet world does not close an evidence gate.

### 18.1 Required proving sequence

The proving enterprise should proceed in seven levels:

1. **component truth:** mass, cube, heat, sound, fault and flow measurement;
2. **module truth:** complete transport and operating envelopes;
3. **host truth:** loaded road, rail and shallow-water articles;
4. **site truth:** setup, rejection, grounding, corridor and public boundaries;
5. **degraded truth:** route, crew, train, terminal and anchor denial;
6. **formation truth:** twelve-cell coordination with independent hosts; and
7. **public truth:** protected people-hours, exceptions, handback and second-mission state.

A host may not skip from a component bench to an illustrated concept fleet.

## 20. Limitations

This is an `R2+` first-principles configuration model, not a design release.

The module masses, cube, setup hours, carrier tare, site areas, acoustic levels, route shares, host costs and operating costs are declared design hypotheses. They are not measurements of current products.

The model does not yet close:

- module external dimensions and center of gravity;
- actual axle groups, dynamic road loads or bridge weak links;
- rail gauge, curvature, axle, braking, signaling or clearance routes;
- barge stability, trim, freeboard, propulsion, air draft, locks or seasonal access;
- complete air-side pressure drop, fan power, recirculation or acoustic treatment;
- cable voltage, thermal-loop diameter, pumping power or 600-m losses;
- source emissions, plume or public exposure;
- building connection geometry;
- ground-bearing and temporary-foundation design;
- exact setup task precedence and shift fatigue;
- carrier operating energy and maintenance;
- reverse-flow contamination and damaged-module custody;
- lineage–geometry compatibility;
- origin-to-receiver travel distributions;
- production learning, schedule, inflation or independent cost estimates; or
- representative measured district performance.

The $4.693-trillion comparator remains useful because it prevents host capacity, fixed readiness and labor from being treated as free. It is not precise enough for procurement.

## 21. Next research decision

The next pass should not select `G1`, `G3` or `G4` from their pass counts. It should build a **lineage–geometry configuration compiler** across the six Pass 73 energy lineages and five Pass 74 host geometries.

That compiler should evaluate all thirty pairings for:

- module mass and cube;
- source and rejection compatibility;
- route and terminal requirements;
- fixed versus mobile shares;
- hazardous separation;
- water, exhaust and sound boundaries;
- setup and operating workforce;
- energy replenishment;
- independent failure ancestry;
- host exchange and reset; and
- complete `PTS-50` portfolio closure under two simultaneous geometry denials.

It should reject impossible pairings explicitly. It should also identify where a new technology program changes feasibility rather than merely improving a score.

Only after at least one lineage–geometry pair has a bounded external envelope, host count, site plan, task network, degraded behavior and admission path should the project produce a preliminary rendering. Even then, the rendering should depict a measured configuration with visible heat, access, separation and human-work zones rather than a single heroic vehicle.
