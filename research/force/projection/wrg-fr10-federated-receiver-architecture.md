# A receiver is not a place; it is a set of failure boundaries

## Federated first-reach water architecture for the Department of Resilience

> **Successor correction — Pass 67.** The [unknown-agent water-assurance architecture](wrg-fr10-unknown-agent-water-assurance.md) resolves this paper's final institutional common mode without weakening the stop rule. It separates five release states and four evidence classes, prohibits a negative field screen from qualifying a held primary source, and competes independent provenance and sealed reserve as physical service paths. A qualified half-demand source plus twenty-four-hour reserve safely maintains the floor in ten of twelve worlds; a second continuous provenance reaches eleven. The twelfth remains a universal safe stop. Every architecture remains unadmitted and rendering remains blocked.

Pass 65 found the limit of local redundancy. Its strongest first-reach water topology—two route-separated half-flow trunks feeding four storage sectors—maintained at least fifty percent of declared service in six of seven registered failure worlds. It still collapsed when the receiver as a whole was placed on contamination hold. That failure could not be repaired by adding another pipe to the same terminal, another tank behind the same quality authority, or another distribution point on the same public network. The architecture had diversified conveyance while preserving a common receiver.

Pass 66 moves the system boundary outward. It asks:

> What minimum combination of independently releasable water-service islands, accessible catchments, direct branch paths, local power and control, clean storage, quality authority and source provenance can maintain at least fifty percent of accessible public service for twenty-four hours through a registered receiver failure?

The answer is not “more sites.” Several sites can remain one receiver if a terminal gallery, distribution main, power feeder, controller, laboratory, source, release order or access plan can stop all of them together. A federation exists only when those boundaries can fail separately and the surviving capacity can still be reached by the public.

The companion [federated receiver model](../../../models/force/projection/wrg-fr10-federated-receiver-architecture.xlsx) competes four architectures across ten deterministic failure worlds. Under the declared assumptions, a central receiver passes one world, a physically paired receiver passes three, an independent three-island receiver passes eight, and a dual-provenance four-island mesh passes nine. The tenth world—a national unknown-contaminant no-issue order—correctly stops all four. Service arithmetic cannot authorize unsafe issue.

None of the architectures is admitted. Every scenario result is untested, all fourteen technical and mission gates remain open, and the rendering gate remains blocked. `R4` is the test leader, not a selected design. This pass defines what a receiver federation would have to be and what evidence would be required to claim it.

## Executive judgment

Seven findings should govern the next water-force design cycle.

First, **site count is not independence count**. `R2` divides service between two physical islands, yet both share the terminal manifold, power and central control, quality-release network and primary source. It therefore survives a local island hold while failing four receiver-wide losses. Readiness must publish each independence dimension rather than report a single number of sites.

Second, **federation converts a receiver-wide failure into a bounded loss only when every necessary service path is partitioned**. `R3` combines three half-demand hydraulic islands with three distribution zones, three direct branches, three black-start domains, three local quality-release domains and six storage sectors. It maintains the declared floor in eight worlds. Its protection does not come from distance alone; it comes from a complete chain that remains operable after a common terminal, grid, controller, distribution or quality network disappears.

Third, **installed capacity and accessible service are separate ceilings**. Three half-demand islands retain exactly fifty percent hydraulic capacity after two islands are lost. That is not enough if the surviving island cannot lawfully serve the displaced catchments. `R3` passes the two-island screen only because the model declares that half of the affected catchments can redirect to the survivor. Setting redirectability to zero turns the pass into a failure. This percentage is a test variable, not evidence that people can travel, queue or receive water.

Fourth, **clean storage is what bridges compound loss**. `R2` has 8,500 cubic metres of usable inventory, but after one of two islands is held only 4,250 cubic metres remain credited. That sustains 42.5 percent of a 10,000-cubic-metre daily requirement and fails the one-island-plus-treatment-outage world. `R3` and `R4` each preserve 8,500 cubic metres of usable inventory after the same proportional loss and pass the twenty-four-hour screen. Raising usable storage from eighty-five to one hundred percent makes `R2` pass, proving that sanitation and operational drawdown—not gross tank volume—bind the result.

Fifth, **receiver diversity does not diversify the water entering it**. `R3` retains one primary source and fails when that source is placed on quality hold. `R4` adds an independently provenanced source, treatment, power and residual chain capable of half normal demand. It maintains exactly fifty percent service in that world. Removing the alternate source turns the pass into a failure. The alternate-source chain is therefore a distinct acquisition object rather than a percentage allowance inside “water production.”

Sixth, **the final universal stop is institutional and epistemic**. In the national unknown-agent world, primary source quality and civil issue authority are both zero. Every architecture stops. This is the safe result. The next program must reduce uncertainty through faster detection, independent provenance and a bounded public-health decision ladder; it must not design around the authority by allowing operators to issue water merely to preserve a service metric.

Seventh, **receiver independence has material, geographic and fiscal mass**. The declared systems range from about 4,324 to 5,579 dry tonnes per set, from about 4,496 to 5,914 chargeable tonnes, and from 113 to 148 forty-tonne surface positions. Replacing the Pass 65 fielding allowances with the four receiver concepts produces provisional twenty-five-year program comparators from $127.2 billion to $156.0 billion. The values are not estimates. They expose the capital consequence of buying branches, autonomy cells, storage, prepared area and alternate provenance instead of treating “coordination” as free.

## 1. The correction: receiving capacity is an architecture

Emergency planning often treats the receiver as a destination field in a movement plan: equipment leaves an origin, crosses a node and arrives at a site. The [National Receiving Lattice](../reception/national-receiving-lattice.md) corrected that abstraction at national scale by defining receiving capacity as land, buildings, interfaces, stocks, inspectors, records and operating teams. [National Receiving Geography](../reception/national-receiving-geography.md) then separated aggregate capacity from geographic accessibility. Pass 66 applies those principles to one complete water service.

A water receiver is not admitted because it has acreage, a pipe stub and space for tanks. It must accept produced water; preserve sanitary custody; control pressure; isolate damaged or contaminated sections; power pumps and controls; test and release water; provide accessible public and institutional issue; and maintain accountable operating authority. If any element is missing, delivered bulk water has not become public service.

The receiver also has time-dependent state. An island may be physically present but uncommissioned, commissioned but empty, charged but isolated, hydraulically live but under quality hold, or released but inaccessible to its assigned population. The operational object is therefore a vector:

```text
receiver state =
  hydraulic isolation
  × clean connected inventory
  × distribution availability
  × branch availability
  × power and control
  × quality release
  × source provenance
  × public access
  × civil issue authority
```

The multiplication is conceptual: a zero in any binding dimension can reduce delivered service to zero even when every other subsystem is healthy. A single readiness label conceals that fact. The Department of Resilience would require every prepared receiver to publish the vector, its common-mode register and the evidence date behind each claim.

This changes the force itself. Receiving formations cannot be an afterthought staffed when equipment arrives. They must be generated, trained, inspected and exercised as part of the standing force. Prepared geography is not local assistance to a national deployment; it is the fixed half of a national mission system.

## 2. What counts as a service island

Pass 66 defines a **water-service island** as a bounded receiver unit that can accept or withdraw clean water, preserve isolation, black start, make a lawful local quality determination and issue water through an accessible interface after the common systems outside its boundary have failed.

That definition creates nine controlled boundaries.

| boundary | credit condition | event that revokes credit |
|---|---|---|
| hydraulic island | bidirectional isolation prevents pressure or contaminant transfer | contamination or pressure crosses a closed boundary |
| storage island | clean measured inventory has independent withdrawal | shared manifold, cleaning path or pump removes all inventory |
| public distribution | safe issue remains outside a held network | one main, pressure zone or access closure stops all users |
| branch path | supply reaches the island without a common terminal gallery | one local event severs every branch |
| power and control | local black start and offline control preserve issue | grid or central controller loss stops the island |
| quality release | local sampling supports a bounded lawful release class | central data or laboratory loss stops all issue |
| source provenance | intake, treatment, power and residual custody are independent | one contaminant or process hold removes every source |
| catchment access | the assigned population can reach a surviving interface | distance, disability, heat, queue or civil restriction defeats access |
| civil issue authority | an accountable release remains valid | an unknown hazard lawfully requires a universal stop |

No island receives independence credit merely because it is separated by a fence or distance. Conversely, two islands need not reproduce every staff function if a shared function cannot remove their protected service. The design task is to register the common mode, bound its effect and demonstrate the boundary under seeded loss.

The definition also rejects a false choice between fixed and mobile infrastructure. Some boundaries are most economically prepared: easements, public access, sanitary pads, network isolation and local legal authority. Others can be projected: branch modules, storage, autonomous power, treatment, sensing and communications. The complete island is assembled from both. The national force owns the requirement and the mobile complement; the receiving command owns the prepared state; the independent test enterprise decides whether the combination earns credit.

## 3. Service calculation

The model uses the Pass 65 `A4` route-separated supply system as the common upstream baseline. Normal product-water demand is 10,000 cubic metres per day for a planning population of 200,000 at fifty litres per person-day. The required degraded-service floor is fifty percent for twenty-four hours. Storage receives an eighty-five-percent usable share to account for sanitary and operational drawdown.

For architecture `a` in failure world `w`, surviving island count is:

```text
N_surviving(a,w) = max(N_islands(a) - N_lost(w), 0)
```

Installed capacity surviving the loss is:

```text
C(a,w) = N_surviving(a,w) × capacity_per_island(a)
```

The accessible catchment share is kept separate:

```text
A(a,w) = min(
  N_surviving / N_islands
  + redirectable_share × min(N_lost / N_islands, 1),
  1
)
```

This formulation credits the unaffected catchments plus only the declared redirectable portion of users assigned to lost islands. It does not claim that redirection is feasible. That requires an occupied travel, accessibility and queue trial.

When primary treatment is unavailable, storage-supported service is:

```text
S(a,w) = min(
  usable_storage(a) × N_surviving / N_islands
  ÷ [normal_daily_demand × evaluation_hours / 24],
  1
)
```

When the primary source is healthy, source supply is the product of surviving trunk share and treatment availability. An independently provenanced alternate source can provide its declared share even when the primary source is held. Water available is the greater of surviving source supply and credited storage during a treatment outage.

Achieved service is the minimum of surviving capacity, accessible catchment, distribution path, branch path, power and control, quality release, water available and civil release. The architecture passes only when that minimum is at least fifty percent. This weakest-link rule prevents excess capacity in one dimension from averaging away a catastrophic loss in another.

No probabilities are assigned. The ten worlds are a requirements register, not a forecast distribution. A design that passes nine worlds is not described as having ninety-percent reliability. It has removed nine named common modes on declared assumptions and remains exposed to every unregistered or untested mechanism.

## 4. The competing architectures

### `R1`: central receiver

`R1` is the control. One full-demand island receives the Pass 65 `A4` supply. It has four storage sectors and 10,000 cubic metres of gross storage, but no credited network-isolated distribution zone, direct branch, black-start domain or local quality-release domain. Its alternate-source share and catchment redirectability are zero.

The design passes only `W4`, loss of one strategic trunk corridor, because the surviving route preserves exactly fifty percent supply while the central receiver remains healthy. It fails a one-island hold because the single island is gone. It fails loss of the primary distribution network, terminal manifold, external grid and central control, or central quality network because each is universal. It also fails the compound island-and-treatment world, the primary-source hold and the national no-issue order.

`R1` matters because it makes the receiver common mode visible without adding design complexity. It is also the reference against which additional capital should be judged. An alternative earns its cost only by removing a named loss while preserving the complete service chain.

### `R2`: physically paired service islands

`R2` creates two half-demand islands and two network-isolated public-distribution zones. It splits the same 10,000 cubic metres of gross storage across four sectors and declares that twenty-five percent of a lost island’s catchment can redirect. The islands are at least two kilometres apart in the planning case.

The architecture passes a one-island hold: one half-demand island survives and the accessible share is 62.5 percent. It also passes a primary distribution hold because its two public-service zones are isolated from that network, and it passes one strategic trunk loss. It fails a two-island hold and the compound storage world. More importantly, it fails common-terminal, central-power and central-quality losses because physical separation did not extend through those dependencies.

This is the pass’s most important negative control. `R2` looks federated on a map and is not a federation in operation. It demonstrates why the Department must prohibit the use of site count as a readiness metric.

### `R3`: independent `N+1` receiver triad

`R3` creates three half-demand islands for 150 percent installed capacity. Each has its own distribution zone, direct branch path, black-start and offline-control domain, and local quality-release domain. Six storage sectors provide 15,000 cubic metres gross and 12,750 usable. Half of the catchment assigned to a lost island is declared redirectable.

After one island loss, two islands preserve full-demand capacity and 83.3 percent accessible catchment. After two losses, the survivor preserves exactly fifty-percent capacity and 66.7 percent accessible catchment. When one island and primary treatment are lost together, the two surviving islands retain 8,500 cubic metres of usable storage; catchment access binds at 83.3 percent and the architecture passes the full day.

The triad passes eight worlds. Direct branching defeats terminal loss. Local autonomy defeats grid and central-control loss. Local release defeats central quality-network loss. Network-isolated zones defeat primary-distribution loss. It fails the primary-source hold because all three islands still receive water of one provenance. It also obeys the national no-issue order.

`R3` is the minimum architecture in this pass that turns the Pass 65 receiver-wide local hold into bounded island loss. It is deliberately overinstalled hydraulically to preserve half demand after two islands disappear. Whether three fifty-percent islands are superior to four one-third islands is not resolved; the comparison shows the different protection mechanisms.

### `R4`: dual-provenance four-island mesh

`R4` uses four one-third-demand islands for 133.3 percent installed capacity. Each has an independent distribution zone, direct branch, local power and control, and local quality release. Eight storage sectors provide 13,333 cubic metres gross and about 11,333 usable. The planning case uses five kilometres of minimum separation, sixteen branch route-kilometres and fifty-percent lost-catchment redirectability.

The additional mechanism is a second provenance capable of half normal demand. “Provenance” means more than a second hose from the same plant. The protected chain requires an intake or sealed source not defeated by the primary contaminant, separate treatment, separate critical power, independent quality custody and a residual path whose failure cannot close both sources. The model assigns 900 dry tonnes and 1,050 chargeable tonnes to this chain as a declared configuration allowance. No technology is selected.

After one island loss, three islands preserve full-demand capacity and 87.5 percent accessible catchment. After two losses, two islands retain 66.7 percent capacity and 75 percent access. In the compound island-and-treatment world, the surviving storage supports 85 percent service for twenty-four hours. In the primary-source hold, alternate provenance provides exactly fifty percent.

`R4` passes nine worlds. It still fails the national unknown-agent order because civil issue authority is zero. That failure is not a defect that can be solved by adding another ordinary source; it identifies the need for a bounded emergency-quality doctrine, forensic detection and an external provenance whose safety case is valid under the unknown-agent condition.

| `W3` | primary public-distribution network hold | fail | pass | pass | pass |
| `W4` | one strategic trunk corridor lost | pass | pass | pass | pass |
| `W5` | common terminal manifold unavailable | fail | fail | pass | pass |
| `W6` | external grid and central control unavailable | fail | fail | pass | pass |
| `W7` | central quality data and release network unavailable | fail | fail | pass | pass |
| `W8` | one island held and primary treatment unavailable | fail | fail | pass | pass |
| `W9` | primary source placed on quality hold | fail | fail | fail | pass |
| `W10` | national unknown-contaminant no-issue order | fail | fail | fail | fail |
| **declared passes** | **of ten** | **1** | **3** | **8** | **9** |

Three aspects of the table require care.

First, `PASS` means only that the deterministic calculation meets the declared fifty-percent threshold. The evidence column in every scenario remains `UNTESTED`. Strict admissions are zero for all four architectures.

Second, exact-threshold passes are fragile. `R3` after two island losses, all architectures in the strategic trunk world, and `R4` under primary-source hold sit at exactly fifty percent in at least one binding dimension. A small unmodeled loss would reverse them. Representative trials should therefore report margin, duration and population distribution rather than a binary result alone.

Third, the table does not reward an unsafe continuation. `W10` closes service because the competent public-health authority lacks sufficient evidence to issue. A safe stop can be a mission failure in the service ledger and the correct operating decision at the same time. The Department’s doctrine must preserve that distinction.

## 6. The force–receiver contract

A mature Department of Resilience would divide responsibility without dividing the outcome.

The **generating water service** would own deployable treatment, route-separated trunk systems, branch modules, storage modules, autonomy cells, quality workcells, repair and sanitation complements, source-provenance packages, crews and configuration records. It would certify that a complete complement can be generated and transported.

The **receiving command** would own prepared pads, easements, isolated network interfaces, accessible public-service areas, local utility boundaries, traffic and queue plans, local records, operating relationships and the ability to accept command of the deployed system. It would certify the fixed state before deployment and maintain it between events.

The **geographic employment command** would allocate supply paths and islands, establish operating priorities, manage catchment transfer and decide when the assembled system can enter public service. It could not waive a quality or civil-rights gate merely to improve throughput.

The **independent research, test and evaluation authority** would seed losses, observe the complete chain and admit or deny each protection claim. Generating and receiving commands could propose thresholds but could not validate their own readiness.

The **public-health and quality authorities** would retain release and no-issue decisions. The architecture would give them more independent evidence and more safe alternatives, not subordinate their judgment to operational tempo.

This structure is more ambitious than interagency coordination. The receiver federation would be a standing, capitalized force element with registered configurations, inspection cycles, readiness states and career professionals. Its fixed and mobile parts would share doctrine and train together. A receiver whose staff, network isolation or access plan has lapsed would lose readiness credit before a disaster, just as an unserviceable platform would.

## 7. Technology programs created by the requirement

The architecture is intended to drive new capability, not merely arrange present equipment differently.

### Self-proving sanitary boundaries

Today’s large temporary water systems require laborious connection, pressure testing, flushing, disinfection and sampling. A service island needs couplings, valves, liners and manifolds that can establish and attest a sanitary boundary quickly under debris, poor access and degraded communications. Embedded pressure, flow and quality sensing should produce a signed local release record without relying on one national network. The research target is not “faster pipe”; it is an independently auditable transition from packed equipment to lawful public issue.

### Autonomous branch construction

Four-island federation creates route and branch work that cannot be closed by a single terminal gallery. Robotic survey, trenchless crossing, autonomous conduit handling, rapid anchoring and machine-assisted connection could make genuinely separate paths deployable at useful speed. Route planning must optimize correlated-loss exposure, not shortest distance. Its output should expose shared bridges, floodplains, substations, control networks, access roads and labor dependencies so a human authority can challenge the result.

### Island-scale black-start utilities

Each island needs enough power to operate pumps, controls, communications, lighting, sampling, accessible issue and local thermal protection when the grid and central controller disappear. Compact grid-forming power, DC distribution, fault-isolated motor drives, resilient controls and standardized utility interfaces should be acquired as an autonomy cell. It must operate offline, synchronize when permitted and fail safe without converting every island into another common cyber dependency.

### Distributed quality workcells

The quality problem is partly analytical throughput and partly authority. Each island requires automated sampling, chain of custody, bounded detection, local data interpretation and a preauthorized release class. Novel sensing should target time under uncertainty: broad-spectrum screening, rapid concentration, confirmatory methods and secure comparison across islands. Unknown-agent conditions must escalate rather than silently produce a green status.

### Alternate-provenance source chains

`R4` creates a major clean-sheet program. Candidate mechanisms could include a second surface or groundwater intake, sealed bulk transfer, atmospheric or recovered-water routes for nonpotable substitution, or coastal and inland desalination where source conditions permit. Each candidate must close intake, pretreatment, primary treatment, product stabilization, critical power, residual management, quality custody and public distribution. Sharing a feed, chemical stock, membrane train, laboratory or brine route may invalidate the provenance claim.

### Accessible demand routing

Catchment redirectability is presently the softest assumption in the model. A real system needs household communication under network loss, accessible travel options, heat-safe queues, institutional delivery, language access, medical-priority routing and verifiable wait time. Local-first scheduling and privacy-preserving population state could help, but the metric is observed service among affected people—not app adoption or vehicle count.

### Regeneration after contamination

A federation accumulates more boundaries that must be restored. Automated tank cleaning, replaceable sanitary liners, decontaminable branch modules, consumable replenishment, evidence preservation and configuration reconciliation should support a second full mission. A system that survives one event by creating weeks of bespoke recovery is not a mature force design.

These programs should remain competitions among mechanisms. The model does not establish that four islands, eight sectors, a five-kilometre separation or one half-demand alternate source is optimal. It creates measurable coefficients and failure gates against which different technologies can compete.

## 8. Cost, mass and industrial implication

Pass 65’s strongest local topology carried a declared twenty-five-year program comparator of $127.2 billion, including twenty-four initial operational sets and twelve prepared receiver nodes. Pass 66 holds the common research, proving, production and support structure constant, removes the predecessor’s $1.65 billion equipment and $1.10 billion receiver allowances, and inserts architecture-specific values.

| architecture | dry tonnes per set | chargeable tonnes per set | 40 t surface positions | equipment per set | receiver per node | revised 25-year program |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `R1` | 4,324 | 4,496 | 113 | $1.65B | $1.10B | $127.2B |
| `R2` | 4,369 | 4,541 | 114 | $1.75B | $1.35B | $132.6B |
| `R3` | 4,669 | 4,861 | 122 | $1.90B | $1.80B | $141.6B |
| `R4` | 5,579 | 5,914 | 148 | $2.30B | $2.20B | $156.0B |

The mass model adds forty-five dry tonnes for each branch beyond the first, twenty-five tonnes for each local autonomy and quality domain, thirty-six tonnes per additional thousand cubic metres of gross storage, and 900 dry tonnes for the alternate-source set. Chargeable mass applies packed-cube density floors and a 1,050-tonne alternate-source burden. These are declared coefficients intended to prevent independence from remaining weightless. They are not released bills of material.

Prepared area rises from eighteen hectares in `R1` to thirty-four in `R4`. Branch route length rises from one to sixteen kilometres. Those variables imply a substantial receiver industrial program: standardized sanitary civil works, buried and surface interfaces, protected power, quality laboratories, public-service areas, route access, storage foundations and maintainable isolation. The mobile industrial base would add conduits, robotics, treatment, power electronics, sensors, pumps, autonomous controls and regeneration equipment.

At maturity, such a program would support production lines and professions analogous in continuity to defense acquisition. But its output would be measured in surviving public service, not platform count. Contractors would compete on installation time, boundary integrity, safe release, accessible throughput, recovery and common-mode removal. Long-term contracts would preserve suppliers for sanitary materials, power systems, treatment consumables and repair components between disasters.

The program range should not be read as a budget recommendation. Land, released site designs, actual construction, operating workforce, source-specific treatment, public-distribution work, rights acquisition and validated production learning curves remain outside the comparator. The useful conclusion is narrower: the difference between a central receiver and a dual-provenance federation is large enough to be an explicit force-design decision and too large to hide inside contingency.

## 9. Evidence contract

Fifteen gates control progression.

1. Prove hydraulic isolation with no cross-boundary contaminant or pressure transfer.
2. Maintain at least fifty percent accessible service for twenty-four hours during a one-island hold.
3. Repeat the requirement during a correlated two-island hold for `R3` and `R4`.
4. Issue safe water outside a held primary public-distribution network.
5. Maintain service after loss of the common terminal manifold.
6. Black start and operate locally after external grid and central-control loss.
7. Sample, decide and release lawfully after central quality-network loss.
8. Sustain the floor from clean storage during a simultaneous island hold and primary-treatment outage.
9. Maintain half service from independently provenanced water during primary-source quality hold.
10. Obey a national no-issue order and demonstrate a safe alternate-provenance decision ladder.
11. Demonstrate catchment transfer, accessibility and a maximum sixty-minute queue.
12. Move from detection through isolation and certified public issue within two hours.
13. Show that receiver separation removes shared hazard, route, power, control and labor causes rather than relocating them.
14. Sanitize, recharge, reconcile records and pass a second representative mission.
15. Release rendering only after the selected architecture has representative physics and federation evidence.

Gates one through fourteen are open. Gate fifteen is blocked. The proving campaign should begin with `R2` because it is the most plausible false federation: seed terminal, power and quality losses across two apparently separate sites and observe whether service actually remains. It should then build an `R3` proving receiver, vary island and catchment assignment, and measure the exact-threshold two-island case. `R4` should enter only with a configured alternate-source chain whose source, treatment, power, laboratory and residual dependencies are enumerated before test.

The trial must measure public effect at short intervals: safe volume issued, people and institutions served, unavailable catchments, queue distribution, outage duration, water-quality state, operator interventions and unauthorized cross-boundary transfer. Aggregate daily volume can conceal a six-hour collapse, an inaccessible interface or unequal service.

## 10. Acquisition decision and next proof

No architecture should be selected for production. `R1` remains the central-receiver control. `R2` should be retained as the false-federation comparator. `R3` is the minimum local federation and should become the first representative proving object. `R4` is the source-diverse test leader and should advance as an upper comparator after its alternate provenance is physically configured.

The immediate acquisition action is to create three linked competitions:

- a **receiver-island boundary program** for sanitary isolation, branches, black-start autonomy, distributed quality release and accessible public issue;
- an **alternate-provenance program** for complete half-demand source chains under primary-source hold; and
- a **federation proving program** that seeds receiver-wide failures, measures population service and denies independence claims not supported by observation.

The next analytical pass should resolve the remaining institutional common mode: the national unknown-agent no-issue order. It should not seek a way around the stop. It should design a **safe alternate-provenance decision architecture**: contaminant classes, detection limits, quarantine, sampling authority, independent laboratories, sealed or independently sourced inventory, bounded emergency release, transparent public communication and the evidentiary threshold for restoring service. The output should be an authority-and-technology competition, not a presumption that more sensors eliminate uncertainty.

In parallel, object-level work should serialize one `R3` island and one `R4` alternate-source chain into transportable modules, fixed works, crews, consumables, spares and commissioning tasks. That is the point at which the current mass allowances can be replaced by controlled objects and the architecture can be crossed with carrier, node and receiver admission.

## Conclusion

The Pass 65 failure was not that the water force lacked another pipe. It was that every pipe, tank and distribution point still terminated inside one receiver-wide quality and authority boundary. Pass 66 shows what it takes to remove that common mode.

A real federation requires independently survivable hydraulic islands, clean storage, public interfaces, branch paths, power and control, quality release and accessible catchments. It also requires a clear civil authority that can stop unsafe issue. Three half-demand islands with complete local independence survive eight of ten registered worlds; a four-island mesh with a second half-demand source provenance survives nine. Several physical sites without those boundaries survive only three.

The difference is a force-design principle. The Department of Resilience should generate receivers as mission systems, not addresses; acquire independence as physical and institutional capital; and test it by deliberately removing the shared systems on which it claims not to depend. The mature object is neither a mobile plant nor a prepared site. It is a federation that can lose one of its own service worlds and still keep safe water reachable.

That ambition is appropriately defense-scale. It demands purpose-built equipment, prepared geography, standing receiving formations, independent testing, long-horizon technology competitions and an industrial base organized around public-service continuity. It also preserves the discipline that makes the ambition credible: no arithmetic pass becomes a field claim, no site count becomes an independence claim, and no service target overrules a lawful safety stop.
