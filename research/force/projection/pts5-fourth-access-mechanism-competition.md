# `PTS-5` fourth-access mechanism competition

## A new access domain must deliver public service, not merely an unusual vehicle

**Research status:** first-principles mechanism and portfolio competition; eight rivals and twenty-eight pairs tested; no mechanism admitted

**Decision date:** 9 September 2026

**Companion model:** [`pts5-fourth-access-mechanism-competition.xlsx`](../../../models/force/projection/pts5-fourth-access-mechanism-competition.xlsx)

## Executive judgment

The Department of Resilience should not define a “fourth access path” as one spectacular carrier. It should create a new **access ecosystem** able to move, bridge or locally reproduce a complete public-service effect when road, rail and navigable water all fail. The relevant acquisition article is one complete `PTS-5` rapid-bridge cell at the receiver, operating by hour 48—not an aircraft in flight, a crawler at the perimeter or a cable laid toward the city.

This pass holds eight clean-sheet mechanisms against fifteen hard-access behavior worlds and one evidence-withheld stop. Six physically transfer a 635-tonne launch article: a 320-tonne-payload heavy buoyant liftship operated in pairs; a 650-tonne wing-in-ground amphibious utility carrier; a four-vehicle autonomous terrain train; an eight-aircraft, 80-tonne distributed heavy-VTOL lattice; a seven-vehicle ballistic cargo-glider salvo; and a paired amphibious ground-effect crawler. Two substitute for bulk carriage: a robotic utility-corridor projector that bridges power, water and data from a surviving source, and a 150-tonne local-conversion seed force that turns assured local water, feedstock, energy and salvage into the required service bundle.

No mechanism is universal. The heavy buoyant liftship and distributed heavy-VTOL lattice each close eight of fifteen declared worlds. The autonomous terrain train closes seven; the amphibious crawler five. Water-dependent, source-dependent, feedstock-dependent and large-landing-footprint mechanisms close narrower but still strategically distinct missions. Every current-evidence score remains zero.

The best two-mechanism portfolio in the declared screen combines heavy buoyant lift with autonomous terrain trains. It closes thirteen of fifteen worlds: long-range island and partner missions through the atmosphere, and damaged inland, steep, smoky and high-wind missions through distributed ground mobility. It still fails the 80-kilometre utility-gap case, where moving an entire cell is inferior to projecting service from a surviving source, and the second simultaneous hard-access region, where both selected sets are single-campaign resources.

That result is more important than selecting the pair. It derives a mature doctrine of **three access actions**:

1. **Carriage** moves the complete cell across the denied boundary.
2. **Bridging** reaches across the boundary with power, water, thermal exchange and communications while the source remains outside it.
3. **Conversion** moves a compact seed and produces service from qualified matter and energy already inside the boundary.

A mature Fourth Access Force therefore needs several physics families, independent bases, receiver-survey teams, weather intelligence, open payload interfaces, recovery and reset. The carrier is only the most visible part.

The provisional national screen uses the twenty-two hard-access groups inherited from Pass 76, a 1.5 concurrent-campaign factor and a 1.3 readiness-and-attrition factor, producing forty-three mission sets. Single-mechanism programs add approximately $267–701 billion after research and shared enterprise. The behavior-leading `M1+M3` research pair adds $848 billion and recompiles the Pass 77 national comparator from $6.099 trillion to approximately **$6.848 trillion** after removing the earlier $99 billion placeholder once. This is not a recommendation. The pair retains two declared mission gaps and zero current evidence.

Pass 78 adds a $280 billion technology increment, bringing the coupled technology portfolio to $735 billion. It creates programs in ultra-large buoyant structures, autonomous mooring, amphibious ground-effect flight, terrain robotics, high-specific-power vertical lift, synchronized lift autonomy, robotic utility corridors, self-isolating utility tissue, local-conversion microfactories, assured feedstock characterization, controllable cargo-glider descent, receiver acceptance, offline command, compound-weather prediction, interchangeable `PTS-5` packaging, full-mission proving and industrial-rate capacity.

Rendering remains blocked. The competition has now produced the requirements that later renderings must express, but none of the declared payload, propulsion, landing, weather, safety or reset envelopes has representative evidence.

## 1. Pass 77 stocktake

Pass 77 established the fixed service island as a complete civic-metabolism boundary and corrected the thirteen-package national topology. Ten fixed cells and three mobile cells preserve the ten-cell service floor only if decisive fixed dependencies are separated into ten ancestry partitions. An eleven-island, fourteen-package alternative can tolerate six balanced partitions. This created a direct economic contest among another island, another partition and a novel access mechanism.

The pass left twenty-two fourth-path cells in the geography-assigned force as placeholders. It also named candidate mechanism families without allowing their novelty to decide the architecture. Pass 78 therefore holds one complete cell and receiver deadline constant, opens each mechanism into its bases and nodes, and denies current credit until representative evidence exists.

## 2. The acquisition article

### 2.1 Complete launch burden

The common article contains 580 tonnes of dry service equipment and 55 tonnes of wet and launch consumables, for 635 tonnes at dispatch. It occupies 2,630 cubic metres and thirty-two governed module positions. The mechanism must establish at least 90 percent of the complete service bundle by hour 48.

The mass is intentionally unforgiving. A 500-tonne carrier does not deliver 79 percent of a valid cell if the omitted positions contain black start, heat rejection, water quality, building interfaces or control. It delivers no complete cell. A physical mechanism receives credit only when its vehicles jointly deliver all thirty-two positions, preserve configuration and leave enough time to unload, connect, test and release service.

### 2.2 Service substitution

The mass rule does not force every solution to move 635 tonnes. A robotic corridor can deliver electrical, water, thermal and communications service across a denied area from a surviving source. A conversion seed can manufacture or process the effect from material already inside the receiver. These mechanisms receive credit only when their **complete-service potential**, dependencies and entry time meet the same public contract.

This distinction prevents an error in both directions. It does not let a small drone shipment masquerade as a complete response. It also does not require the Department to transport a desalination plant when a protected source can send water through a new corridor, or carry seven days of fuel when a qualified local conversion system can produce it safely.

### 2.3 Access closure

The pass uses the following minimum condition:

```text
complete service fraction ≥ 0.90
arrival + receiver setup ≤ 48 hours
range ≥ standoff-to-receiver distance
environmental envelope closes
receiver footprint and authority close
offline command remains available
concurrent sets are not double-counted
```

Every term is conjunctive. Excess range cannot compensate for an unusable landing area. A safe landing cannot compensate for missing payload modules. A functioning corridor cannot compensate for the loss of its standoff source.

## 3. Fifteen behavior worlds

The mechanism library faces island denial, dispersed archipelago, debris-filled coastal water, inland bridge collapse, mountain landslide, desert heat and drought, flood current and debris, frozen northern range, wildfire smoke and turbulence, earthquake liquefaction, cyclone winds, long-range partner support under cyber denial, an 80-kilometre utility gap, a receiver with usable local feedstock but no corridors, and a second simultaneous hard-access region.

Each world specifies range, runway, water-surface and road availability, slope, wind, visibility, smoke, local-feed and standoff-source state, cyber condition, receiver footprint and concurrent campaign demand. The worlds are not probabilities. They preserve failure identity and expose which mechanisms depend on the same environmental or institutional fact.

A sixteenth world withholds representative evidence. It guarantees that a calculated pass cannot become strict admission.

## 4. Eight mechanism families

### 4.1 `M1` — `HBL-320` heavy buoyant liftship

Two variable-buoyancy liftships each carry 320 tonnes. The pair delivers 640 tonnes from a 4,000-kilometre standoff, needs neither runway nor water surface and enters service in a declared forty-two hours. The requirement is far beyond present civil heavy lift: large damage-tolerant envelopes, propulsion and control in turbulence, variable buoyancy, autonomous mooring, precision low-speed load placement and safe operation around occupied neighborhoods must become one system.

`M1` closes eight worlds. It is valuable for islands, archipelagos, coastal and inland cities, floodplains, Arctic distance and remote partner support. It fails steep terrain, wildfire smoke and turbulence, restricted landing footprints, cyclone wind and a second simultaneous mission. Its nominal continental reach does not remove atmospheric and receiver dependence.

The liftship should not be imagined as a cargo zeppelin enlarged by scale. Its useful military analogue is the strategic airlift system: vehicle, global bases, weather service, route authority, load engineering, mobile mooring, recovery, depots and trained crews. The Department would need several hull and propulsion lineages so one materials defect did not erase the fleet.

### 4.2 `M2` — `WIG-650` amphibious utility carrier

`M2` carries a complete cell in one 650-tonne load over 2,500 kilometres. It uses ground effect over water and transitions to a prepared or surveyed littoral receiver. Its promise is high mass without a port or conventional runway.

The mechanism closes only the open-water archipelago world in the current register. That narrow score is not rejection. It identifies the mission for which the system is uniquely attractive and prevents the Department from claiming it can penetrate debris-choked, frozen or high-wind water merely because it is amphibious. A real program must solve wave-state operations, floating debris, beach transition, corrosion, spray ingestion, emergency alighting, unloading and ecological constraints.

### 4.3 `M3` — `ATUT-160` autonomous terrain train

Four articulated vehicles each carry 160 tonnes. Distributed electric or hybrid traction, active suspension, low ground pressure, robotic route survey and carried bridging allow the set to cross 800 kilometres of damaged land without requiring an intact road. The declared cell reaches service in forty-five hours.

`M3` closes seven worlds and supplies the best complement to `M1`. It handles steep terrain, mud, smoke, high wind and constrained footprints better than large atmospheric systems. It does not solve transoceanic or Arctic distance and cannot serve a second region with one set.

The underlying innovation program should treat the terrain train as a moving utility formation. Vehicles exchange power, distribute load, deploy short bridges, stabilize slopes, clear obstacles and leave a usable service corridor behind them. Autonomy reduces exposure and coordinates many axles; it does not eliminate human route authority, recovery crews or the need to understand occupied terrain.

### 4.4 `M4` — `VHL-80` distributed heavy-VTOL lattice

Eight aircraft each carry one or more governed module positions and collectively deliver 640 tonnes. No single airframe carries the complete cell, so configuration control, synchronized routing and diversion recovery are part of the platform. Two campaign sets are available inside one force package in the declared design.

`M4` closes eight worlds, including the simultaneous-region case. It is constrained by smoke, winds above 25 metres per second, the longest ranges and steep receiver terrain. The program requires high-specific-power propulsion beyond current civil practice, brownout-resistant landing, quiet approach, autonomous load coordination, hot-and-high performance, modular energy and safe failure of one aircraft without silently losing a critical service position.

The lattice is more industrially significant than a single super-heavy helicopter. It creates production scale across propulsion, rotors, power electronics, flight controls, load modules and depot systems, while preserving the option to dispatch subsets for smaller missions.

### 4.5 `M5` — `RUC-100` robotic utility-corridor projector

`M5` carries only 400 tonnes, but its purpose is not cargo delivery. It lays up to 100 kilometres of high-capacity power cable, potable and process-water hose, thermal loop, communications repeaters and protected control nodes from a surviving source to the receiver. It closes the 80-kilometre utility-gap world that defeats the leading carrier pair.

The mechanism is a strategic access system because a corridor can cross flooded, burned, mined, unstable or privately fragmented terrain without restoring an entire network first. Robotic survey, aerial placement, surface crawling, directional boring and autonomous isolation would be mixed by geography. The decisive research is self-isolating “utility tissue”: standardized segments able to detect rupture, contamination, overload and intrusion, isolate locally and reroute service.

Its dependency is explicit. If the standoff source fails, the corridor delivers nothing.

### 4.6 `M6` — `LCS-150` local-conversion seed force

`M6` moves 150 tonnes of assay, robotics, machine tools, process equipment, controls, recipes and initial catalysts. It creates the remaining service mass from qualified local water, biomass, fuels, aggregates, metals, polymers and salvage. The declared system closes the local-feedstock world with a forty-eight-hour service-entry boundary.

This is the most conceptually ambitious mechanism. It replaces logistics mass with knowledge, adaptable machines and material assurance. It also has the most dangerous hidden assumption: “local material exists” is not the same as safe feedstock, legal custody, useful composition or timely conversion. The program must treat rapid chemical, biological and structural characterization, recipe provenance, contamination control and output certification as core machinery.

At maturity, conversion forces could produce pipe, cable, fittings, membranes, structural members, simple motors, pumps, fuels and water products. They should not receive credit for highly specialized components that remain nationally supplied.

### 4.7 `M7` — `BCG-100` ballistic cargo-glider salvo

Seven reusable or recoverable 100-tonne gliders provide global range without relying on a receiver runway. The system separates launch from terminal descent and trades base dependence for speed and reach.

The current design closes only the long-range cyber-denied partner world because its four-hectare landing footprint, wind limit and terrain requirements are severe. Its value is as an extreme comparator: if controllable atmospheric entry, precision energy management and safe high-mass landing could shrink the receiver footprint, the mechanism would create a truly distinct ancestry from surface and conventional aviation.

The program must not externalize damaged gliders, abort zones or recovery risk onto the receiving population.

### 4.8 `M8` — `AGC-320` amphibious ground-effect crawler

Two 320-tonne vehicles combine low-altitude ground-effect movement over open terrain or water with powered crawling through the final approach. The system aims to cross the seam that defeats pure marine and pure land systems.

It closes five worlds, especially damaged coastal and inland receivers at regional range. Slope, wind, range and concurrency remain limiting. Its strongest research contribution may be transition mechanics: debris-tolerant lift, beach and mud entry, distributed traction and the ability to unload without a terminal.

## 5. Portfolio logic

Twenty-eight two-mechanism combinations are evaluated by world. A pair receives credit when either member closes the complete mission. No averaging is permitted.

The behavior-leading pair is `P02`, `M1+M3`, with thirteen worlds. It combines atmospheric reach with terrain tolerance and has a declared forty-three-set national program of $848.3 billion, including $130 billion of mechanism-specific research and a $120 billion shared enterprise. It misses `W13`, where the utility corridor is the appropriate mechanism, and `W15`, where both members lack concurrent depth.

The result suggests a force with four layers rather than one winner:

- a long-range atmospheric layer for island, Arctic and partner access;
- an autonomous terrain layer for damaged inland and severe-weather approach;
- a service-bridge layer where intact sources can reach across damage; and
- a conversion layer where qualified local material can replace transported mass.

Amphibious, VTOL and cargo-glider programs remain competitors within and across those layers. The mature portfolio may buy narrow mechanisms in small numbers because their missions are strategically irreplaceable.

## 6. The base and node enterprise

A new access mechanism is not independent because its propulsion differs. It must separate source, base, crew, control, weather, supplier and receiver ancestry.

The provisional enterprise contains eighteen heavy-lift launch and mooring bases, twenty-four amphibious and ground-effect bases, thirty-six terrain-train depots, thirty distributed heavy-VTOL bases, forty-eight utility-corridor depots, thirty-two local-conversion arsenals, seventy-two receiver survey teams, twelve joint weather and access observatories, and eight independent destructive-test ranges.

These quantities are requirements hypotheses. They expose the mature institution hidden by a vehicle drawing. Each base must stock energy, payload interfaces, recovery equipment, crews and spares. Each must exercise loading, dispatch, abort, receiver acceptance, damaged-vehicle recovery and reset. Weather observatories must remain useful when national communications or one prediction model fails. Receiver teams must establish geotechnical safety, air and surface obstacles, utility interfaces, contamination state, civil authority and public exclusion zones before arrival.

The Department should create a **Fourth Access Systems Command** to generate and allocate this force; a **National Access Observatory** for independent weather, terrain and receiver intelligence; and an **Access Configuration Authority** that owns the thirty-two-position payload standard across every mechanism. Mode-specific services would operate the vehicles, while Regional Federation Commands would own the complete public-service result.

## 7. Economics and industrial policy

The single-set lifecycle comparators range from $2.481 billion for local conversion to $11.524 billion for the heavy-VTOL lattice. These values combine configured set capital with 15.81 ramp-weighted years of mature operation. They omit no research from the national comparison: mechanism-specific research is added once, as is the $120 billion common base, node, command and proving enterprise.

For forty-three selective sets, the single-family national programs range from $266.7 billion to $700.5 billion. After removing the $99 billion Pass 76 placeholder, the resulting whole-force boundaries span approximately $6.266–6.700 trillion. The leading behavior pair reaches $848.3 billion and a $6.848 trillion national boundary. A simple low screen produces $5.615 trillion.

These figures demonstrate scale, not accuracy. A coequal resilience instrument would sustain multiple new aerospace, marine, land-robotics, utility and distributed-manufacturing sectors for decades. It would require strategic materials, machine tools, certification, technical schools, depots and dormant production capacity. The industrial policy should fund at least two qualified lineages in every decisive subsystem and preserve government access to interface, safety and test data.

The export opportunity is substantial but should follow the service architecture. Partner nations may buy regional terrain trains, utility projectors, conversion arsenals, amphibious carriers or shared liftship access rather than a replica of the U.S. force. Common payload and service interfaces allow multinational pooling without surrendering national operation or local authority.

## 8. Technology program

The $280 billion Pass 78 increment is an integrated mission portfolio, not eight disconnected vehicle demonstrations. Its seventeen programs are tied to coefficients in the mission worlds.

Ultra-large buoyant structures and autonomous mooring seek to raise payload, wind tolerance and receiver flexibility. Amphibious ground-effect research attacks wave, debris and land transition. Terrain autonomy targets slope, gap and road denial. High-specific-power vertical lift and synchronized autonomy target payload, range and multi-aircraft completion. Robotic corridor laying and self-isolating utility tissue target service reach and damage survival. Microfactories and feedstock characterization target local substitution without unsafe improvisation. Precision cargo-glider descent targets global reach and landing footprint.

Common programs build receiver survey, offline command, compound-weather prediction, interchangeable `PTS-5` packaging, independent proving and industrial rate capacity. These common programs are where a Department-scale technology mission differs from a collection of prototypes. They make rival mechanisms composable and allow failure evidence to alter procurement before fleets are locked in.

## 9. Proving sequence

**Stage 1: common payload and receiver standard.** Freeze the thirty-two module positions, structural and restraint envelope, energy and fluid connections, data and authority interfaces, loss behavior and service-entry test.

**Stage 2: mechanism physics articles.** Build full-scale propulsion, structure, terrain, landing, corridor and conversion demonstrators. Do not infer complete-cell performance from reduced-scale demonstrations where scaling controls the result.

**Stage 3: complete set trials.** Each mechanism must move or substitute the entire service cell, establish it at a blind receiver and operate the seven-day public contract. Teams that designed the mechanism cannot own final evidence custody.

**Stage 4: compound-world trials.** Insert wind, smoke, debris, slope, cyber denial, source loss, base loss, crew loss, module diversion and damaged receiver information. Force safe abort and recovery.

**Stage 5: portfolio and second-event trial.** Dispatch different mechanisms to two regions while a third remains on alert. Trace every common base, supplier, crew school, weather model, control root and recovery asset.

**Stage 6: limited operational force.** Buy multiple mechanism families only after their distinct world coverage is demonstrated. Keep narrow but irreplaceable capabilities even when their aggregate pass counts are low.

## 10. Evidence and falsification

Thirty-four gates remain open. They cover complete payload, source and base independence, launch cycle, range, wind, visibility, smoke, receiver survey, runway-free receipt, water and debris, terrain and slope, offline command, civil authority, interfaces, unloading, workforce, reset, concurrency, supplier ancestry, energy, environmental and public safety, community rights, cross-border custody, casualty and abort, damaged-vehicle recovery, full service, seven-day endurance, repeat mission, cost and independent reconstruction.

The fourth-access concept should be revised or rejected if:

- complete-cell loading or service entry cannot close by hour 48;
- weather windows make availability correlated with the disasters that create demand;
- receivers require infrastructure comparable to the road, rail or port being bypassed;
- one launch base, energy source, crew school or control stack disables nominally different mechanisms;
- service bridges fail when the standoff source is inside the same hazard domain;
- conversion systems cannot establish feedstock safety and product certification in time;
- vehicle recovery consumes the second-event reserve;
- community exclusion, noise, downwash, crash or environmental burdens make receiver operation unsafe; or
- an additional fixed island, ancestry partition or legacy mobile cell produces greater service assurance per lifecycle dollar in the assigned geography.

## 11. Pass 78 stocktake

### 11.1 What is now authoritative

At `R2`, this pass makes the 635-tonne complete access article, three access actions, eight mechanism families, fifteen behavior worlds, twenty-eight pair competition, forty-three-set selective planning basis and $280 billion technology increment current research references. It also makes the base-and-node enterprise part of the mechanism boundary.

### 11.2 What the pass falsifies

It falsifies a universal fourth carrier. No single rival closes more than eight behavior worlds. It also falsifies physical carriage as the only form of access: service bridging and local conversion uniquely close missions that the leading carrier pair misses or handles inefficiently. Finally, it rejects vehicle-level novelty as evidence of an independent access domain.

### 11.3 What remains provisional

Every mechanism coefficient, world boundary, cost, quantity, technology outcome and industrial program remains a hypothesis. The leading pair is not selected. All current-evidence results and strict admissions are zero. No mechanism has demonstrated complete payload, receiver entry, seven-day service, compound denial or reset.

### 11.4 Where the burden moved

The burden moved from a placeholder fourth-path cell into launch bases, depots, weather intelligence, receiver survey, payload interchange, energy, recovery, authority, industrial lineages and second-event readiness. It also moved from transporting mass into assuring sources and feedstock whenever bridging or conversion substitutes for carriage.

### 11.5 Highest-leverage next pass

Pass 79 should configure the `M1+M3+M5` **tri-vector access group** as one formation. It should assign every `PTS-5` module to liftship, terrain train or utility projector; derive vehicles, crews, bases, energy, loading, receiver equipment, abort, recovery and reset; and test whether the three mechanisms truly have independent ancestry. The `M4` heavy-VTOL lattice should remain the concurrent-region challenger.

Only after that configuration should the project begin detailed renderings. The first images should depict requirements-controlled articles with visible payload interfaces, receiver footprints, heat and propulsion systems, recovery equipment and operating formation—not unconstrained concept art.

## Method and source boundary

This pass uses only prior repository requirements and declared clean-sheet design hypotheses. No external-site research is introduced. Values are research requirements and architecture comparators, not observed performance, forecasts or procurement estimates. The companion workbook preserves formulas, world-by-world failure identity, pair coverage, cost reconciliation, evidence gates and sensitivity so later evidence can supersede the result without erasing its logic.
