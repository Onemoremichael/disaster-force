# `ESK-40V`: physical tile and carrier-interface closure

## A clean-sheet service architecture for compound civilian force projection

## Executive judgment

Pass 88 established the correct strategic requirement and the wrong physical simplification. Its `DCP-2R` formation distributed a complete 635-tonne public-service effect across three atmospheric carriers or five terrain carriers so that the loss of one carrier and one further payload element would not remove an irreplaceable function. That was a real advance over merely buying spare vehicles. But the assumed forty identical 15.875-tonne tiles were an accounting device, not a first-principles payload architecture.

This pass opens that device.

The decisive finding is that **equal family count does not require equal tile mass**. The inherited 635-tonne service cell naturally separates into five physical families:

| Family | Public function | Cell burden | Eight-tile certified mass |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| `E` | firm energy, black start, wet energy inventory and protection | 179 t | 22.375 t/tile |
| `W` | water production, wastewater, storage and process stocks | 116 t | 14.500 t/tile |
| `T` | useful cooling and final heat rejection | 110 t | 13.750 t/tile |
| `D` | medium-voltage, fluid and building distribution | 112 t | 14.000 t/tile |
| `C` | civil control, survey, medical care, life safety, repair and assay | 118 t | 14.750 t/tile |
| **Complete cell** | **one source-to-public-service effect** | **635 t** | **79.375 t per five-family set** |

Four five-family sets still equal the atmospheric carrier's 317.5-tonne payload. Two sets still equal the terrain carrier's 158.75-tonne payload. Three air carriers with four sets each therefore preserve eight tiles per family after any one carrier is lost. Five terrain carriers with two sets each do the same after any one terrain carrier is lost. Rejecting one additional tile leaves seven in the affected family. The redundancy algebra survives while the physical burden becomes more credible.

The correction exposes a new contradiction. The inherited `GCH-340R` has eighteen payload positions—sixteen dry positions and two wet saddles—but the distributed service kernel requires twenty independently restrained and quarantinable tiles. The inherited `SAT-170R` has nine positions but requires ten. Payload cube, deck area and assigned mass can fit; the certified interfaces cannot. The selected `ESK-40V/CUIC-1` reference therefore adds two compact tile saddles to each air carrier and one to each terrain carrier.

That apparently small change propagates. A provisional six-tonne fixed-mass addition raises the air carrier's modeled gross state from 1,461.415 to **1,468.322 tonnes** and onboard energy from 2,185.075 to **2,194.831 megawatt-hours**. A three-tonne terrain correction, after bridge and energy feedback, raises gross from 717.185 to **720.957 tonnes** and modeled damaged-soil pressure from 34.07 to **34.249 kilopascals**, leaving only 0.751 kilopascals below the declared limit. An additional half-hour interface state moves stressed public service to hour **52.917** for air and **53.569** for terrain. The terrain route retains only 0.431 hours—approximately twenty-six minutes—inside the hour-54 requirement. Reset moves to hours 163 and 167; terrain retains one hour.

The architecture is therefore not admitted. `ESK-40V` becomes the **behavior and technology-development reference**, while full exact-cell duplication remains the conservative control. The detailed modular program is no longer cheaper at first acquisition. Replacing the generic $240 billion `ESK` allowance with a $1.320 trillion dual-lineage tile program, $85 billion of air-carrier integration, $60 billion of terrain integration and $95 billion of receiver adapters raises the `DCP-2R` boundary to **$6.26079719 trillion** over twenty-five years. That is **$256.92095 billion more** than the full-duplication control. It still avoids 207 carrier articles, creates a modular civil-systems industrial base and establishes an architecture that can learn across missions, hazards, allies and generations. Those are the reasons to investigate it. Savings are not.

The reference contains 5,916 tile articles: 4,730 loaded into forty-three ready formations, 946 in the rotating reserve and 240 development or destructive articles. It requires a provisional 970 deployed people, 2,175 affiliated positions and a minimum watch of 304. A $1.050 trillion mission-driven technology portfolio is nested inside the tile program. Twenty-five campaigns each face eight gates, producing 200 evidence obligations. Every gate begins open and earns zero maturity credit.

Exterior renderings remain blocked. This pass is a requirements architecture, not representative hardware geometry. The first visual work should be internal burden, hazard, interface and maintenance diagrams made from measured family articles. Carrier form should follow those results.

## 1. The strategic requirement

The Resilience Force is not moving cargo for its own sake. It is projecting a civilian state:

> a defined population receives a defined bundle of safe service by a stated time, for a stated duration, under stated damage.

For the present `PTS-5` lineage, that state is a 5,000-person service island with continuous and peak electric power, seven-day energy, dead-bus recovery, potable and process water, wastewater acceptance, clean-water storage, useful cooling, final heat rejection, medium-voltage and fluid distribution, building interfaces, offline control, communications, medical stabilization, survey, access, repair and independent public release.

The two-mode `DCP-2` lower bound gives the state two physically distinct access routes: atmospheric carriage and damaged-terrain carriage. Pass 88 added a compound casualty promise:

```text
loss of one access mode
+ loss of one carrier in the surviving mode
+ rejection of one further service tile
=> complete public service by absolute hour 54
```

This is deliberately more demanding than ordinary redundancy. Spare transport mass is not enough. The surviving payload must still contain every causal function between source and public effect. Nor may air and terrain merely use different vehicles around the same fragile power electronics, membrane lot, control kernel, certification office or receiver machine. The force must preserve both **functional completeness** and **causal separation**.

## 2. What Pass 88 got right—and what it left artificial

The `ESK-40` predecessor represented one cell as eight tiles in each of five service families. Its allocation made every two-air-carrier subset and every four-terrain-carrier subset contain eight of each family. The extra tile-loss allowance reduced the active family to seven contributors while preserving its normalized service floor.

That structure solved a combinatorial problem. It did not solve the engineering problem. Giving every tile the arithmetic mean mass of 15.875 tonnes forced unlike burdens into identical packages:

- the energy family was too light relative to its generation, storage, fuel and protection burden;
- water, residuals and thermal rejection carried custody and volume characteristics unlike civil-control equipment;
- formation-wide wet packs risked recreating a common decisive casualty;
- identical mass encouraged identical packaging even when maintenance, hazards and center-of-gravity behavior differed; and
- the carrier studies had closed around eighteen and nine payload positions, not twenty and ten independently controlled tiles.

The correction is not to abandon functional coding. It is to make the code physical: repeat family counts, allow family masses and forms to differ, preserve public-service semantics, and require each physical lineage to close its own hazards and interfaces.

## 5. Two independent physical lineages

Common service meaning is essential for civil interoperability. Common decisive implementation is dangerous. The reference therefore specifies one atmospheric lineage and one terrain lineage for each family.

### 5.1 Atmospheric lineage

The air-carried tiles favor high value per unit volume, rapid self-deployment after unload and limited receiver earthwork.

- **`EA`** uses distributed hydrogen electrochemical-turbine conversion, solid-state protection and isolated energy custody. Hydrogen is not selected as a universal national fuel; it is a lineage hypothesis that aligns with the `GCH-340R` conversion environment and must still prove storage, fire, embrittlement, ventilation and cold-weather behavior.
- **`WA`** combines electro-adsorptive separation, membrane distillation and electro-oxidative residual control. It aims to reduce large rotating equipment and tolerate variable feeds while retaining product and residual assays.
- **`TA`** uses transcritical carbon-dioxide machines with folding dry microchannel rejection wings. The clean-sheet objective is high-temperature lift without relying on receiver water, not novelty for its own sake.
- **`DA`** uses aluminum-composite reeled medium-voltage conductors, aramid-reinforced fluid lines and gantry robots that deploy from the tile saddle.
- **`CA`** uses an optical local mesh, aerial survey, compact clinical/access equipment and precision repair. Its control root remains offline and physically local.

The provisional air geometry for one five-family set is 304 cubic metres and 78 square metres of deck. Four sets occupy 1,216 cubic metres and 312 square metres, inside the inherited 1,400-cubic-metre and 352-square-metre payload boundaries. The design mass is 304.6 tonnes against the 317.5-tonne certified assignment.

### 5.2 Terrain lineage

The terrain-carried tiles favor impact tolerance, maintainability, contaminated-ground work and direct compatibility with route-opening machinery.

- **`ET`** uses a multi-liquid pressure-gain generator, flywheel/battery dark start and rotating protection. It deliberately avoids the air lineage's main conversion and protection stack.
- **`WT`** uses ceramic ultrafiltration/reverse osmosis, ultraviolet oxidation and ceramic/biological residual processing.
- **`TT`** uses low-charge hydrocarbon compression and a segmented hybrid dry/wet rejection field that may be placed around terrain and plume constraints.
- **`DT`** uses copper-alloy sectional medium-voltage equipment, reinforced hose systems and tracked adapter robots.
- **`CT`** uses wired fieldbus control, ground survey, heavy access and repair tools, and a separately supplied clinical suite.

One terrain set occupies a provisional 222 cubic metres and 73 square metres. Two sets require 444 cubic metres and 146 square metres, inside the inherited 800-cubic-metre and 184-square-metre boundaries. Design mass is 152.3 tonnes against the 158.75-tonne certified assignment.

Neither lineage is selected technology. The purpose of naming them is to make the innovation burden and the independence claim falsifiable. A future competition may replace every implementation while preserving the public-service contract.

## 6. `CUIC-1`: a civil utility interface constitution

Interoperability and independence must be separated deliberately. `CUIC-1` standardizes the observable public boundary while allowing different machines behind it.

It fixes:

- certified mass, center of gravity, restraint, lift and quarantine states;
- 13.8-kilovolt AC service and a 1.5-kilovolt DC dark-start boundary;
- potable product, raw feed, return/residual and thermal supply/return custody states;
- offline status, configuration, assay and public-handoff records;
- fire, drainage, pressure, hot-surface and chemical isolation declarations; and
- the authority transition from vehicle, to family service, to public release.

It does **not** require a universal saddle, controller, protection device, pump, membrane, compressor, firmware root, sensor set or certifying office. Air and terrain use separate physical receiver adapters. No shared decisive item earns compound-assurance credit. Common messages and units exist so separately released effects can be federated after release.

This is analogous to a constitutional separation between language and mechanism. The Department wants every service authority to understand what “assayed potable flow available” means. It does not want the same hidden device to decide that state in both supposedly independent paths.

## 7. Carrier packing and physical interfaces

### 7.1 Atmospheric carrier

Each `GCH-340R` carries four tiles from every family: twenty articles, 317.5 certified tonnes. The selected arrangement places twenty compact saddles across two longitudinal cargo keels, ten per keel. Family sets are mirrored about the vehicle's longitudinal center near `x ≈ 150 m` and lateral centerline `y = 0`.

The saddles must do more than hold boxes. Each one must:

- restrain the certified family mass through gust, arrest, ground coupling and offload states;
- provide a measured center-of-gravity and custody state;
- isolate electrical, fluid, chemical, fire and drainage boundaries;
- permit an individual tile to remain aboard or move to quarantine without disturbing a family-wide store;
- transfer the tile through a mode-specific receiver adapter; and
- expose no pre-release utility bridge between selected and held carriers.

The six-tonne correction is a provisional mass allowance for the two added saddles, local structure, transfer hardware and controls. Applying the inherited mass-energy feedback gives:

```text
revised air gross
  = 1,461.4148 t + 6.0 t × 1.1512
  = 1,468.3220 t

revised onboard energy
  = 2,185.0746813 MWh + 6.0 t × 1.626 MWh/t
  = 2,194.8306813 MWh
```

This is a coupled requirement, not a claim that the existing structure can accept it. Structural modes, floor reactions, contact-foot loading, cold-dense support conversion, evacuation and recovery must be rerun on a representative test carrier.

### 7.2 Terrain carrier

Each `SAT-170R` carries two tiles from every family: ten articles, 158.75 certified tonnes. Ten saddles are distributed through the two 11.5-metre cargo bodies, with paired families straddling the center joint and a vehicle reference center near `x ≈ 23 m`, `y = 0`.

The additional saddle and local transfer hardware are assigned three fixed tonnes. The inherited bridge-and-energy feedback fraction is 0.2047368:

```text
revised terrain gross
  = 717.185 t + 3.0 t / (1 - 0.2047368)
  = 720.957336 t

revised damaged-soil pressure
  = 34.07 kPa × 720.957336 / 717.185
  = 34.249205 kPa
```

The result remains under 35 kilopascals on paper. A 0.751-kilopascal margin is too small for confidence without measured contact, dynamic loading, soil remolding, debris entrapment, articulation and center-joint behavior. Any deterioration reopens contact area, axle/pod count, route doctrine or carrier number.

## 8. The casualty compiler

The physical code is simple enough to audit manually.

### Air

```text
3 carriers × 4 tiles/family = 12 tiles/family dispatched
loss of any carrier          =  8 tiles/family survive
loss of one further tile     =  7 tiles in affected family
```

### Terrain

```text
5 carriers × 2 tiles/family = 10 tiles/family dispatched
loss of any carrier         =  8 tiles/family survive
loss of one further tile    =  7 tiles in affected family
```

The compiler receives signed carrier, tile, crew, receiver and custody states. It enumerates legal survivor quorums, excludes quarantined articles, verifies that every family reaches its physical service minimum and sends the proposed configuration to an independently developed checker. It does not turn a failed assay into capacity, borrow from the other mode before release or substitute aggregate mass for a missing function.

The held carrier remains a controlled reserve. If a selected survivor later fails, the receiver may recompile around the held vehicle. Until that decision, no crew, energy, fluid, part or tile from the held carrier counts toward public service.

## 9. Mission clock and receiver consequences

The tile architecture adds a new interface state: saddle identification, isolation confirmation, family custody transfer and receiver-side assay routing. The model assigns 0.5 hours to each path.

| Mode | Inherited absolute service | Interface state | Revised service | Design stress | Stressed service | Margin to hour 54 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Air | 47.917 h | 0.500 h | 48.417 h | 4.500 h | 52.917 h | 1.083 h |
| Terrain | 49.569 h | 0.500 h | 50.069 h | 3.500 h | 53.569 h | 0.431 h |

The margin is deterministic. It is not a percentile, reliability claim or probability of success. Receiver queues, parallel-work interference, weather, contamination, family assay throughput and crew performance remain unmeasured. The narrow terrain margin means the first full mission trial may overturn the hour-54 requirement, the carrier count or both.

Air and terrain receiver complexes remain five kilometres apart before release. They share civil service semantics but not pre-release energy, fluid, cranes, saddle adapters, control roots or assay authority. Each receiver must isolate a failed carrier, unload a complete survivor quorum, stage quarantined tiles, assemble all five families, assay the actual public outputs and release service through civil authority. Cross-tie opens only after both effects are independently safe.

Reset receives one additional hour for saddle, connector, custody and structural inspection. Air closes at hour 163. Terrain closes at hour 167 against a 168-hour ceiling. This is another stop condition, not comfortable reserve.

## 15. Representative proof sequence

The next work should proceed in this order:

### Article 1: ten representative family tiles

Build one tile from every family in both lineages. Measure dry and wet mass, center of gravity, startup, continuous and peak output, energy, thermal rejection, water quality, residual custody, noise, plume, fire, drainage, isolation, maintenance access and controlled shutdown. A tile that meets output only in a laboratory utility environment fails.

### Article 2: two five-family sets

Operate each lineage's five families simultaneously under network and GNSS denial. Measure cross-coupling, harmonics, thermal interference, consumable competition, control recovery and public-service assay. No family may borrow a prohibited resource.

### Article 3: structural carrier installations

Install twenty compact air saddles and ten terrain saddles in representative carrier structures. Test restraint, transfer, crash and gust loads, local modes, fire, drainage, CG movement, bridge and ground-pressure feedback, evacuation and recovery.

### Article 4: loaded mode formations

Assemble one twenty-tile air load and one ten-tile terrain load, then a complete survivor quorum for each mode. Run the full origin-to-release mission and measure the interface clock rather than assuming half an hour.

### Article 5: destructive receiver trial

Remove an entire access mode, reject one carrier from the survivor formation and destroy or quarantine one additional tile. Require the compiler, crews and authorities to reconstruct and independently assay the public effect at an unprepared receiver by hour 54.

### Article 6: reset and repeat

Restore certified loads by hour 168, change lots and crews, insert a hidden ancestry defect, and repeat. A one-time demonstration without regeneration and detection is not force capability.

## 16. Stop rules

The reference reopens if any of the following occurs:

- a certified family mass exceeds its carrier allocation;
- seven tiles cannot deliver the required service simultaneously;
- wet custody or another decisive resource becomes formation-wide;
- the compact saddles violate structure, restraint, evacuation, fire, drainage or transfer requirements;
- damaged-soil pressure exceeds 35 kilopascals in the admitted terrain condition;
- the stressed terrain mission exceeds hour 54;
- either reset exceeds hour 168;
- air and terrain share a decisive implementation, supplier, firmware root, test authority or receiver dependency;
- an unprepared receiver cannot assemble and assay the service effect; or
- the modular premium persists without verified lifecycle, adaptation, production, export or carrier-avoidance advantage.

Failure does not imply abandoning the Department's mission. It may imply more carriers, different family partitions, prepared receiver investment, different service duration, a changed deadline, or selection of the full-duplication control. The public obligation governs the platform—not the other way around.

## 17. What the model proves and does not prove

The companion workbook reconciles the declared burden, family masses, carrier loads, service coefficients, survivor counts, position corrections, feedback calculations, mission clock, reset, fleet, workforce, program boundary, technology portfolio and evidence-state register. Ninety deterministic model controls and 200 evidence-state controls pass with zero formula errors.

It does not prove:

- that either technology lineage can meet its mass and output target;
- that the carriers can accept the saddle modifications;
- that the half-hour interface allowance is real;
- that the terrain soil margin survives dynamic trials;
- that the suppliers and authorities are causally independent;
- that the cost envelope predicts appropriations or realized procurement cost;
- that the organization can recruit and retain the required professions; or
- that the complete public effect can be delivered in a real damaged geography.

The correct evidence posture is therefore:

## 18. Consequence for the mature Department

`ESK-40V` changes how the Department should think about power projection.

First, its strategic inventory is not merely ships, aircraft and vehicles. It is **certified public-service quanta** whose function survives loss and whose interfaces are legible to civil authorities.

Second, modularity is an institutional commitment. It requires standards, configuration control, independent certification, stored spares, production continuity, receiver adapters, training and a compiler able to recompose damaged formations. A modular box without those institutions is cargo.

Third, technological independence must be purchased and governed. Two lineages cost more than one optimized design. That premium is analogous to paying for arsenals, shipyards, ranges and competing suppliers in other strategic sectors. The benefit is not elegance. It is the ability to keep learning and operating when a technology, supplier, hazard or political relationship fails.

Fourth, the first generation can rationally cost more than a conservative alternative. A national technology program may still be justified if it creates a reusable industrial grammar that drives down future adaptation time, replaces carrier mass with verified functional density, enables allied production and makes entirely new response missions possible. Those benefits must eventually be measured rather than asserted.

Finally, the payload now begins to shape the carriers. That is healthy. The `GCH-340R` and `SAT-170R` are not heroic objects around which missions must bend. They are provisional carriage systems that must change when public-service physics demands another interface, mass state, crew action or failure boundary.

## 19. Rendering boundary and next research move

The project now knows more about what must be made, but not enough to draw authoritative exteriors. The permissible next visuals are requirements diagrams derived from representative articles:

- family mass and hazard cross-sections;
- maintenance and removal envelopes;
- saddle reaction and custody diagrams;
- air and terrain load maps;
- receiver quarantine and assembly flows; and
- public interface and authority-state diagrams.

Exterior carrier renderings remain deferred until those objects establish real geometry.

The next research move is the **representative tile article and coupled carrier proof specification**: select measurable design envelopes for one tile per family-lineage, define the structural and receiver test articles, allocate the 200 evidence gates to named facilities and independent authorities, and determine whether the twenty-six-minute terrain clock margin is credible enough to retain hour 54. That pass should be allowed to invalidate `ESK-40V`, change the carrier formation or select the full-duplication control.
