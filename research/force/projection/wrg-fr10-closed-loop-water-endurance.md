# The water force cannot throw mass away

## Closed-loop endurance under residual and consumable denial

> **Successor correction — Pass 70.** The [end-to-end independence study](wrg-fr10-end-to-end-independence.md) connects the endurance module back into the whole service chain. It shows that two source cores can still share one-cut carrier, receiver, power, quality, material, crew, depot, control and base dependencies. A three-cell upper comparator closes the declared dependency worlds, but every representative gate remains open. This paper continues to own residual and consumable closure; the successor owns end-to-end independence and force depth.

Pass 68 established that there is no universal water machine. A nuclear coastal carrier, a protected-groundwater group, a variable-feed inland treatment group and a sealed-reference transfer lattice each close different source and access conditions. The combined portfolio serves ten of twelve declared theaters, but every family fails two cases: a fourteen-day inland mission with no lawful residual outlet and a thirty-day inland mission without external process consumables.

Those failures are not logistics details. They expose the central physical problem in a disaster water force. A treatment plant does not destroy contaminants, salts, suspended matter or process chemicals. It separates, transforms, concentrates, immobilizes or transfers them. A force that claims endurance because its product tank remains full while its residual tank, media store, chemical cabinet, clean-in-place system or heat-rejection path is exhausted has counted only the desirable side of the process.

This paper asks what a purpose-built Department of Resilience would acquire if it treated that problem as a first-order national technology program:

> What rival architectures could deliver 5,000 cubic metres of safe water service per day, retain every residual for fourteen days, operate for thirty days without external treatment consumables, stop safely when a source leaves its registered envelope and remain repeatable rather than becoming a one-mission disposal system?

Four architectures compete:

- `E1`, **carry and contain**, which accepts the baseline process and closes endurance by carrying thirty days of chemistry and fourteen days of liquid-residual custody;
- `E2`, **regenerate and dewater**, which makes process reagents, restores media and converts most liquid residual into a smaller liquid stream plus contained solids;
- `E3`, **quality-segmented service**, which reserves high-grade treatment for ingestion and medical demand while supplying a separately controlled use-specific product for other functions; and
- `E4`, a **circular source cell**, which pursues endogenous chemistry, regenerable media, high internal recovery and near-zero liquid discharge while retaining the separated solid mass.

The companion [closed-loop water endurance model](../../../models/force/projection/wrg-fr10-closed-loop-water-endurance.xlsx) keeps high-grade product, use-specific product, liquid residual, solid residual, consumables, power, dry equipment, wet custody and cost in separate accounts. All four concepts close the original fourteen-day and thirty-day cases by construction. Only `E2` and `E4` close a combined thirty-day world with neither a residual outlet nor consumable resupply. Both then fail an energy world in which the inherited 2 MW power block delivers only 75 percent of its allocation. `E4` alone begins a second fourteen-day no-outlet mission without depot reset. No architecture passes all ten behavior worlds. All seventeen evidence gates remain open, all strict admissions remain zero and rendering remains blocked.

This is the intended result. The pass produces a technology portfolio and test contract, not a preferred vehicle.

## Executive judgment

Ten conclusions should govern the water-endurance program.

First, **closed loop is a balance-sheet claim, not a product label**. The correct boundary includes raw feed, safe product, use-specific product, rejected water, vapor, separated liquid, solids, spent or regenerated media, make-up chemicals, cleaning water, sampling loss and unaccounted mass. The force may lawfully release, reuse, stabilize, package or transfer a stream. It may not omit it.

Second, **storage is the reference competitor for every advanced process**. `E1` adds 8,750 cubic metres of liquid-residual capacity to the inherited seven-day buffer. At the declared 36 tonnes of hardware per 1,000 cubic metres, that incremental volume requires 315 dry tonnes before 85 tonnes of hazardous handling, isolation and inspection equipment. The resulting 400-tonne overlay is not elegant, but it is understandable, testable and technologically conservative. Regeneration must outperform that complete system, not an imaginary zero-mass tank.

Third, **regeneration converts the logistics problem; it does not end it**. `E2` cuts the declared liquid residual from 1,250 to 187.5 cubic metres per day and process consumables from 2.0 to 0.4 tonnes per day. In exchange it adds 960 dry tonnes of dewatering, endogenous-chemistry, media-regeneration, power and thermal equipment, consumes another 2.4 kilowatt-hours per cubic metre and creates five tonnes of contained solids per day. A thirty-day campaign still ends with 150 tonnes of solids requiring verified custody.

Fourth, **water quality is a service architecture**. `E3` supplies 3,000 cubic metres per day of high-grade water and 2,000 of use-specific water. It reduces liquid residual to 810 cubic metres per day and keeps added equipment to 450 tonnes. It also creates two product boundaries, two connection doctrines and a cross-connection hazard. It fails the declared cross-connection world. Lower treatment intensity is legitimate only where the use, source, product network, labeling, sampling and public authority are separately qualified.

Fifth, **quality segmentation cannot evade unknown-agent safety**. If the source lies outside a registered envelope, neither the high-grade nor use-specific network receives credit. `E3` does not turn uncertain water into acceptable toilet, cooling or cleaning supply by changing its name. It applies only after the source and use-specific process are affirmatively bounded. The [unknown-agent assurance rule](wrg-fr10-unknown-agent-water-assurance.md) remains controlling.

Sixth, **deep circularity shifts the limiting resource toward power, heat rejection and reliability**. `E4` carries only 0.2 tonnes per day of make-up consumables and produces 50 cubic metres per day of retained liquid. It closes the combined thirty-day denial and has enough solid custody for 450 tonnes. Its total declared critical load is 1.75 MW against a 2 MW power block. At 75-percent available power, only 1.5 MW remains and the mission fails. The model therefore rejects the notion that efficiency inside the water balance automatically improves force resilience.

Seventh, **repeatability is different from endurance**. A machine can finish one campaign with full residual stores, fouled media and exhausted cleaning capacity. That is not a repeatable formation. `E1`, `E2` and `E3` fail the immediate second-mission world under their present assumptions. `E4` passes only because its declared solid and liquid stores, consumables and regeneration configuration retain margin. That remains an untested hypothesis.

Eighth, **a safe stop is a successful behavior but not a service outcome**. All four architectures pass the out-of-envelope source world by holding product. The model counts that as correct behavior and zero service. This distinction prevents a safety architecture from being penalized for refusing unsafe water while also preventing a safe hold from being reported as delivered relief.

Ninth, **the industrial program is a process-materials and waste-custody enterprise, not a membrane purchase**. Its critical suppliers include separation media, electrochemical reagent systems, solids handling, sanitary tanks, hazardous packaging, heat exchangers, power conversion, mass-balance instrumentation, field laboratories, connection-control systems, cold qualification, cleaning systems and depot regeneration. The highest-value prime may integrate flows, authority and configuration rather than manufacture the core membrane.

Tenth, **none of the four should be selected**. The declared twenty-four-chain, twenty-five-year incremental comparators range from $46.88 billion for carry and contain to $145.0 billion for the circular source cell. The figures are controlled hypotheses, not estimates. The appropriate next commitment is a competitive sequence of mass-balance rigs, full-scale treatment articles, receiver networks and repeat missions. Procurement quantities follow demonstrated mission coefficients.

## 1. The forcing cases

The [assurance-source competition](wrg-fr10-assurance-source-family-competition.md) created two clean failure statements.

`W7` places the variable-feed inland group beside a valid river for fourteen days. The transfer route is denied and there is no lawful residual outlet. At 5,000 cubic metres of product per day and a declared liquid-residual factor of 0.25 cubic metres per cubic metre of product, the train creates:

```text
daily liquid residual = 5,000 × 0.25 = 1,250 m³/day
fourteen-day residual = 1,250 × 14 = 17,500 m³
inherited seven-day buffer = 1,250 × 7 = 8,750 m³
unclosed volume = 8,750 m³
```

`W11` permits both prepared groundwater and valid surface water but removes external consumable resupply for thirty days. The inland group carries fourteen days of consumables. This pass declares a complete C3 process-consumable intensity of 0.40 kilograms per cubic metre: 0.25 for pretreatment, 0.05 for membrane or adsorption support, 0.08 for stabilization and 0.02 for cleaning and laboratory use. The thirty-day requirement is therefore:

```text
daily consumables = 5,000 × 0.40 ÷ 1,000 = 2.0 t/day
thirty-day requirement = 60 t
inherited fourteen-day stock = 28 t
unclosed inventory = 32 t
```

The individual categories are declared bookkeeping values. They must be replaced by measured recipes over a feed-envelope matrix. Their present purpose is to make the force-design implication visible: residual volume and process materials scale with service, time and feed, not with the number of vehicles shown in a concept drawing.

The forcing cases are also incomplete in a useful way. They test one denial at a time. A mature formation may face both simultaneously, lose a process train, operate below installed power, freeze, encounter a connection error or finish the first mission with no depot route. The new stress set therefore includes ten worlds:

1. fourteen days without a residual outlet;
2. thirty days without consumable resupply;
3. thirty days without either;
4. a source outside the treatment envelope;
5. loss of one regeneration train;
6. 75-percent available power;
7. hazardous classification of every retained residual;
8. a thirty-day freezing campaign;
9. a cross-connection challenge; and
10. an immediate second fourteen-day no-outlet mission.

These worlds are deterministic. They assign no likelihood and imply no national ranking. Their role is to prevent one attractive improvement from hiding its new dependency.

## 2. What “closed loop” must mean

A closed-loop requirement should be written around custody and repeatability rather than literal molecular closure. A field system is closed for a named mission when every material entering the system is either:

- present in released product inside an authorized service class;
- retained in a measured internal inventory;
- transformed into a characterized and contained output;
- reused through a qualified process path;
- lawfully discharged through a verified receiving boundary; or
- accounted for inside a declared measurement tolerance.

This definition permits practical systems. It does not require a perpetual-motion water plant. It does require a complete mass ledger.

For a mission interval from time zero to time `T`, the accounting identity is:

```text
feed water + process materials + cleaning inputs + initial inventories
= high-grade product + use-specific product + liquid residual
 + solid residual + vapor and measured loss + ending inventories
 + unaccounted mass
```

The unaccounted term is not a convenient remainder. It is a measured error that must remain inside a test tolerance. A system with a small liquid-residual stream and a large unexplained mass loss has not demonstrated circularity.

The force also needs an energy and availability ledger:

```text
required service power
= base treatment power
 + regeneration or dewatering power
 + pumping and product separation power
 + heat rejection and freeze protection
 + laboratory, control and hotel load
```

Nameplate generation does not establish closure. The available quantity must reflect maintenance, environmental derating, starting current, protection, distribution loss, reserve and the loss of a power module. Pass 69 uses a simple 75-percent availability world to locate the dependency. A later design must replace that scalar with a protected one-line, load sequence and failure matrix.

Finally, the formation needs a **reset ledger**. It records which tanks are full, which media have reached loading limits, which reagents require precursor refill, which filters need replacement, how much solid package capacity remains, what cleaning interval is due and whether the quality authority can release a second campaign. Without the reset ledger, “thirty-day endurance” may mean “thirty days until depot overhaul.”

## 3. `E1`: carry and contain

`E1` treats the existing variable-feed process as fixed. It carries the missing consumables and adds enough liquid-residual custody to complete fourteen days without discharge. The architecture is intentionally blunt.

The inherited seven-day buffer holds 8,750 cubic metres. The additional 8,750 cubic metres requires 315 tonnes of tank, liner and manifold hardware under the inherited planning coefficient. Another 85 tonnes provide hazardous-work separation, sampling, inspection, transfer equipment and secondary containment. Total added dry mass is 400 tonnes. With the parent C3 chain, the declared complete configuration is 2,150 dry tonnes, 2,450 land-chargeable tonnes and 102 crew.

The consumable stock grows to sixty tonnes. This is wet mission inventory, not dry equipment. It requires lot identity, compatible storage, freeze and heat control, fire protection, spill containment, controlled issue and reverse logistics. A thirty-day chemical stock cannot be represented by an unlabeled pallet allowance.

`E1` passes eight of ten behavior worlds and seven service-bearing worlds. It passes the original two forcing cases, the power-derated world and the freezing world. It fails the simultaneous thirty-day denial because its 17,500-cubic-metre residual capacity is 20,000 cubic metres short. It also fails the immediate second mission because the first no-outlet campaign fills the custody system.

The important strategic role for `E1` is not necessarily fleet dominance. It is the **control architecture** for the technology competition. Every regenerative proposal should be compared against the cost, mass, crew, deployment burden, hazard and reliability of simply carrying the material. A process that removes 300 tonnes of storage hardware while adding 500 tonnes of fragile regeneration equipment has not improved deployability unless it also improves repeatability, service, energy or custody.

`E1` also defines a valuable asset family in its own right. A Department of Resilience could acquire standardized residual farms, collapsible or panelized custody cells, sanitary and hazardous variants, automated level and leak surveillance, rapid secondary containment, transfer skids, protected sampling points and serialized end-state records. These are not generic commercial tanks. They are expeditionary environmental-custody systems designed to deploy, survive, prove condition, accept multiple process outputs and leave no abandoned waste.

The primary research questions are structural and operational rather than exotic:

- What storage geometry minimizes chargeable mass, setup time, footprint and ground load?
- How do liners, joints and seals behave across heat, freezing, ash, hydrocarbons, disinfectants and concentrated brine?
- Can a damaged cell be isolated without losing the whole farm?
- How is accumulated liquid safely moved when roads, sewers and treatment works remain unavailable?
- What is the maximum mission duration before storage mass and site area defeat deployment?
- Can the system begin a second mission after partial transfer rather than full depot reset?

The decisive `E1` failure is not the first fourteen days. It is what happens on day fifteen and at the second incident.

## 4. `E2`: regenerate and dewater

`E2` attacks both forcing cases inside the process boundary. Its target is an 85-percent reduction in liquid residual and an 80-percent reduction in external consumable intensity. At the common service level, it produces 187.5 cubic metres of retained liquid and five tonnes of contained solids per day. Its thirty-day liquid requirement is 5,625 cubic metres, within the inherited 8,750-cubic-metre buffer. Its thirty-day make-up consumable requirement is twelve tonnes.

The configuration adds four systems:

1. a 320-tonne residual-dewatering train that recovers reusable liquid and creates a controlled solid stream;
2. a 280-tonne endogenous-chemistry plant that produces selected reagents from compact precursors, recovered streams, electricity or locally qualified inputs;
3. a 200-tonne media-regeneration plant that cleans, restores, tests and returns media to service; and
4. 160 tonnes of power, conversion, thermal rejection and control equipment.

The total overlay is 960 dry tonnes and twenty added crew. The complete chain reaches 2,710 dry tonnes and 3,010 land-chargeable tonnes. Its total critical average is 1.575 MW, leaving 0.425 MW against the inherited 2 MW installed allocation in the baseline world.

`E2` passes the original forcing cases and the combined thirty-day denial. It fails four new worlds. One regeneration-train loss leaves only 60 percent of required capacity. A 25-percent power derating leaves 1.5 MW, less than the 1.575 MW average requirement. The architecture is not cold-qualified, and the declared article cannot begin the immediate second mission before inspection and reset.

Those failures define the actual research program. “Regenerable” is not a binary property. Media may lose capacity, change selectivity, shed fines, develop biological growth, retain an unknown agent or require cleaning conditions that cannot be supplied in the field. Onsite reagent generation may produce a useful disinfectant while still depending on precursor purity, electrodes, membranes, calibration materials, ventilation or hazardous byproduct control. Dewatering may reduce volume while increasing worker exposure or creating a solid that is harder to transport and dispose of than the original liquid.

The program should compete complete regeneration cells against named feed families. Each test must report:

- initial and restored media capacity;
- number of regeneration cycles before retirement;
- contaminant breakthrough and product-side carryover;
- reagent yield and precursor mass;
- water consumed by cleaning and regeneration;
- liquid and solid residual composition;
- thermal and electrical demand;
- cycle time and service lost during regeneration;
- worker task, exposure and protective-equipment burden;
- sensitivity to freezing, dust, ash and unstable power; and
- configuration required to isolate one failed train.

The desired breakthrough is not one miraculous chemistry. It is a family of robust, inspectable processes whose material advantage survives the complete field system.

## 5. `E3`: quality-segmented service

`E3` changes the service requirement rather than only the machine. It asks whether every delivered litre must pass through the same high-grade treatment path.

The declared split provides 3,000 cubic metres per day of high-grade water for drinking, food preparation, medical use and other ingestion-sensitive functions. It provides 2,000 cubic metres per day through a separate registered use-specific process for flushing, selected cleaning, dust control, fire protection, cooling makeup or other authorized functions. The exact use list is not universal. Local public-health and utility authorities must release it for the source, process, network and incident.

The lower-intensity stream is assigned a residual factor of 0.03 cubic metres per cubic metre and a consumable intensity of 0.08 kilograms per cubic metre. Combined with the high-grade stream, the architecture produces 810 cubic metres of liquid residual and uses 1.36 tonnes of consumables per day. Fourteen-day liquid custody is 11,340 cubic metres. Thirty-day consumables are 40.8 tonnes.

The overlay adds 150 tonnes for process separation, 200 for dual distribution and backflow isolation, 93 for additional liquid custody and seven for quality and connection control. Total added dry mass is 450 tonnes. The complete formation is 2,200 dry tonnes, 2,500 land-chargeable tonnes and 108 crew. Its declared critical requirement is 1.272 MW, so it survives the 75-percent power world.

`E3` passes seven behavior worlds and six service-bearing worlds. It closes the two original failures. It does not close the combined thirty-day no-outlet world because 24,300 cubic metres of liquid residual exceed its 11,340-cubic-metre custody. It fails the cross-connection challenge and the immediate second mission.

The architecture is more institutionally difficult than its mass suggests. A dual-quality system needs:

- physical separation from plant outlet to point of use;
- incompatible or keyed connectors;
- visible and tactile identification that survives weather and darkness;
- backflow protection and pressure hierarchy;
- continuous or frequent quality surveillance appropriate to each class;
- building and facility plumbing surveys;
- operator and public instruction;
- authority to define permitted uses and change them;
- procedures for inadvertent connection or ingestion; and
- a return-to-normal plan that does not leave ambiguous temporary plumbing.

The cross-connection world is therefore a core mission, not a paperwork check. The proving network should deliberately introduce mislabeling, hose swaps, failed check valves, pressure reversals, unauthorized taps, damaged markers and fatigued crews. An architecture that works only when every connection is correct does not belong in a disrupted city.

Quality segmentation also forces the Department to own demand composition. A single litres-per-person figure hides hospitals, dialysis, food preparation, sanitation, cooling centers, shelters, residential towers, fire systems and industrial stabilization. The water force needs a service scheduler that allocates quality, quantity, pressure, time and access by function. This can reduce high-grade process demand without reducing human protection. It can also create inequity or confusion if implemented as a vague “nonpotable” category. The service classes must be few, legible and enforceable.

## 6. `E4`: circular source cell

`E4` is the clean-sheet technology target. It combines serial separation, internal water recovery, endogenous chemistry, regenerable media, independent mass-balance control and solid-residual conditioning. It delivers the full 5,000 cubic metres per day as high-grade product under the model assumption.

The declared liquid-residual factor is 0.01 cubic metres per cubic metre of product, producing fifty cubic metres per day and 1,500 cubic metres over thirty days. This is near-zero liquid discharge, not zero waste. The concept produces fifteen tonnes of solid residual per day and carries 450 tonnes of solid custody. Consumable make-up is 0.04 kilograms per cubic metre, or six tonnes for thirty days, against nine tonnes of stock.

The 1,400-tonne overlay contains:

- 520 tonnes of high-recovery separation equipment;
- 260 tonnes of endogenous chemistry and media regeneration;
- 250 tonnes of solid conditioning, stabilization, packaging and custody;
- 280 tonnes of additional power conversion and heat rejection; and
- 90 tonnes of control, mass-balance and independent quality equipment.

The complete chain is 3,150 dry tonnes, 3,450 land-chargeable tonnes and 128 crew. The incremental process requirement is 3.0 kilowatt-hours per cubic metre plus 0.15 MW of supporting load. Total critical average power is 1.75 MW. Baseline margin is only 0.25 MW.

`E4` passes eight behavior worlds and seven service-bearing worlds. It closes the combined denial, loses one of three regeneration trains without losing required service under the declared capacity arrangement and is the only rival ready for the immediate second mission. It fails the 75-percent power world and the cold campaign.

The model’s energy sensitivity is particularly important. With 2.0 rather than 3.0 additional kilowatt-hours per cubic metre, the total critical requirement is about 1.542 MW. Even that slightly exceeds the 1.5 MW available in the derated world. Increasing the incremental process to 5.0 kilowatt-hours per cubic metre raises total critical demand to about 2.167 MW and fails even against the full inherited power allocation. A circular cell cannot be specified independently of its power architecture.

This creates several rival research pathways rather than one program:

- **electrically dominant cells** that simplify process materials but require larger protected power;
- **thermally integrated cells** that use waste heat, mechanical vapor recompression or low-grade process heat while increasing heat-rejection and coupling complexity;
- **selective-recovery cells** that pursue deep closure only for the streams that dominate volume or hazard;
- **modular sacrificial cells** whose contaminated submodules are sealed and exchanged while the balance is regenerated; and
- **site-coupled cells** that use qualified local industrial, wastewater or energy interfaces without making them universal dependencies.

The clean-sheet objective should be a measured frontier among deployable mass, critical power, feed envelope, residual form, external consumables, crew workload, restart time and cycle life. “Zero liquid discharge” alone is an inadequate acquisition metric.

## 7. The force element

The four rivals should not initially become four unrelated vehicle fleets. The Department should establish a **Water Endurance Squadron** as a configurable formation attached to variable-feed and selected groundwater groups. Its initial structure would include five functional elements.

### Endurance command and mass authority

This cell owns the material, energy and reset ledgers. It cannot report mission endurance unless product, residual, consumables and second-mission state all close. It coordinates environmental, public-health, worker-safety and logistics authorities without collapsing their independent release powers.

### Residual custody company

This company operates liquid cells, solid packaging, secondary containment, sampling, transfer, spill response and final-disposition records. It provides the `E1` control capability and the custody back end for every advanced architecture. It should exist even if the Department eventually selects deep regeneration.

### Process-materials company

This company manages reagents, precursors, media, cleaning systems, calibration materials, sterile parts and regeneration. It treats every lot and cycle as a serialized configuration object. It also operates depot interfaces and determines whether a train is ready for another mission.

### Product-class and connection company

This element enables `E3`. It deploys keyed connectors, backflow protection, dedicated manifolds, tracer and pressure surveillance, use signage, accessible issue points and connection-control teams. Its quality officers remain separate from treatment operators.

### Power, thermal and reliability company

This element integrates additional generation or storage, conversion, heat rejection, cold protection, load sequencing, protection and black start. It also owns regeneration-train isolation and degraded-capacity plans. It prevents the process program from treating electricity as a limitless external utility.

The formation should be modular at employment but integrated in qualification. A commander may deploy only added storage for a short mission, but the storage module must still have a tested interface to the parent treatment, quality, power, custody and demobilization systems. Modularity does not excuse an incomplete chain.

## 8. A new industrial sector

At mature scale, water endurance could support the kind of industrial ecosystem the larger Department concept envisions. The prime contractors would not simply repackage municipal equipment. They would compete complete deployable service coefficients under hostile conditions.

One class of firm would build **expeditionary process plants**: rugged feed handling, separation, regeneration, clean-in-place, product stabilization, laboratories and control. Another would build **environmental custody systems**: liquid cells, solids stabilization, packaging, leak detection, protected transfer and digital disposition records. A third would build **temporary utility networks**: keyed multi-quality distribution, accessible issue nodes, sanitary landing, metering and backflow control. Power and thermal firms would integrate islandable generation, storage, protection and heat rejection. Specialized depots would restore media, inspect tanks, process hazardous returns, remanufacture modules and preserve configuration identity.

This industrial structure has civil export value because the products are not weapons and the problems are widespread: drought, flood, wildfire contamination, earthquake, industrial spill, island isolation, utility failure, displaced populations and infrastructure reconstruction. Exportability, however, depends on more than the machine. Receiving countries need training, consumables or precursor supply, environmental authority, quality laboratories, maintenance, spare modules and a lawful residual pathway. The Department should sell or support complete service systems rather than creating foreign stranded assets.

The common technical architecture should standardize only the boundaries that benefit from scale:

- product-quality classes and release records;
- hydraulic, electrical, data and mechanical interfaces;
- mass-balance and custody records;
- module identity and configuration history;
- safe-stop behavior;
- sampling and laboratory handoff;
- power-quality and load-shed interfaces;
- residual package classes; and
- depot acceptance and reset criteria.

It should not force every feed through the same process or every mission into the same vehicle. The [common technical architecture](../../systems/common-technical-architecture.md) is valuable precisely because it can preserve a common contract while rival physical solutions continue to compete.

## 9. Technology program

The Department should organize the research portfolio around six forcing functions.

### 9.1 Separation with bounded residual form

Research should measure net product and every output across challenging feed matrices. High recovery is valuable only when concentrate scaling, fouling, cleaning demand, solid form and energy remain manageable. Promising directions include selective membranes, electrically switched adsorption, capacitive and electrochemical separation, robust oxidation, high-solids clarification, mechanically assisted dewatering and hybrid trains that route different contaminants to different endpoints.

### 9.2 Endogenous process materials

The target is not chemical independence in the abstract. It is lower theater mass and fewer fragile lineages. Onsite generation should be evaluated by precursor mass, purity tolerance, shelf life, energy, hazardous intermediates, electrode or membrane life, calibration burden and byproduct custody. A locally generated reagent whose production cell depends on a unique imported membrane may not improve national resilience.

### 9.3 Regenerable media and clean-in-place

Media require cycle-life evidence. Cleaning systems must minimize water use, preserve product isolation, capture spent solution and verify restoration. The program should reward modular isolation and parallel regeneration so one media train can be serviced while another remains online.

### 9.4 Residual stabilization and packaging

The force needs compact, field-verifiable ways to dewater, bind, encapsulate, dry or otherwise stabilize separated solids. The output package must remain traceable, inspectable, transportable and acceptable to a final facility. Research must include worker exposure and fire, gas, leachate and rewetting behavior.

### 9.5 Mass-balance sensing and control

Meters and analyzers should reconcile feed, product, cleaning water, liquid residual, solids and inventory. Control logic must detect unexplained loss, isolate a train and place product on hold. This is an autonomy opportunity, but manual-safe operation remains mandatory. A network outage cannot erase custody.

### 9.6 Low-energy closure and heat integration

Every water-recovery target should state marginal kilowatt-hours per cubic metre, peak load, heat rejection, start sequence and degraded output. Research should compete process efficiency with larger power blocks, storage, waste-heat use and service segmentation. The system objective is mission closure, not laboratory energy performance at steady state.

The [national proving network](../../proving/test-enterprise/national-proving-network.md) should host the program. Different sites can provide salt, turbidity, ash, hydrocarbons, cold, damaged-grid, residual-denial and receiver-network conditions while using one evidence ledger.

## 10. Acquisition and program economics

The workbook presents four incremental twenty-four-chain comparators. They sit on top of the parent variable-feed formation and do not replace the Pass 68 source-family programs.

| architecture | added equipment per chain | added dry mass | twenty-five-year program comparator |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `E1` carry and contain | $0.32B | 400 t | $46.88B |
| `E2` regenerate and dewater | $0.65B | 960 t | $79.40B |
| `E3` quality-segmented service | $0.40B | 450 t | $60.00B |
| `E4` circular source cell | $1.10B | 1,400 t | $145.00B |

Each total includes declared equipment, research and development, proving, industrialization, twelve-node adaptation, common systems and twenty-five years of operations. The four-line sum of $331.28 billion is not a proposed portfolio. It shows the scale of pursuing all alternatives as full fleets, which the paper does not recommend.

The acquisition sequence should use competitive down-selection without premature platform commitment.

**Phase 1: balance rigs.** Multiple vendors demonstrate contaminant separation, reagent generation, media regeneration, residual conversion and mass closure at subsystem scale. Every test publishes the full balance and energy demand.

**Phase 2: integrated 500-cubic-metre-per-day articles.** Rival systems run long enough to expose fouling, cleaning, precursor, staffing and residual-package behavior. The Department owns the interface and evidence record.

**Phase 3: full-scale 5,000-cubic-metre-per-day mission articles.** Vendors integrate with parent intake, power, laboratory, receiver and custody systems. Tests include no-outlet, no-resupply, power derating, freezing and safe-stop challenges.

**Phase 4: multi-vendor formation exercises.** Storage, regeneration, segmented service and circular technologies combine in different proportions. The Department evaluates whether mixed architectures create additional training, spare, authority or depot common modes.

**Phase 5: repeat mission and export configuration.** Articles begin a second mission before complete depot reset and then transition to maintainable host operation or recover without abandoned residuals.

Contracts should pay for measured service and evidence rather than brochure capacity. Useful award metrics include safe high-grade cubic metres delivered, authorized use-specific service, retained residual per product unit, external consumable mass per service-day, critical power, chargeable mass, crew-hours, setup distribution, cycle life, safe-stop performance and second-mission readiness.

## 11. Test and admission contract

Seventeen gates govern the next step. They are summarized here and specified in the workbook.

1. A full-scale thirty-day mass balance must close.
2. Fourteen-day liquid custody must show no unaccounted release.
3. Regeneration must sustain thirty days without external process consumables.
4. Residual reduction must hold across the registered feed envelope.
5. A dual-quality network must survive faults and operator error without cross-connection.
6. Out-of-envelope feed must produce a verified safe stop.
7. Required service must close at declared derated power.
8. Loss of one regeneration train must match the claimed capacity state.
9. Cold-soak operation must preserve flow, sensors, containment and chemical condition.
10. A second mission must begin without a fictional depot reset.
11. Independent quality authority must detect false release and false hold cases.
12. Hazardous residual must remain traced to lawful destination.
13. Availability must survive representative maintenance faults.
14. Complete chargeable mass and setup must close in a theater deployment.
15. The crew must sustain continuous operation without hidden host labor.
16. Environmental withdrawal and every final output must be recorded.
17. Integration with a second source must preserve physical independence.

No modeling result substitutes for these gates. A scenario pass says only that the declared numbers satisfy the declared logic. A strict admission requires representative evidence, configuration identity and rendering eligibility. The [evidence ledger](../../proving/assurance/evidence-ledger.md) should retain the source data, article state, deviations, failures and corrective actions.

## 12. What has and has not been learned

This pass establishes several durable design facts.

- The Pass 68 residual and consumable failures are solvable in more than one way.
- Brute-force storage remains a credible reference architecture.
- Regeneration can close both denials but moves burden into equipment, solids, power and train reliability.
- Service segmentation can reduce high-grade treatment demand but creates a public-health distribution system.
- Deep circularity can make repeated missions plausible while making power the limiting resource.
- Safe-stop behavior and delivered service must be reported separately.
- Residual custody, process materials, product-class networks and reset state deserve permanent formations and industrial programs.

It does not establish that the declared reduction factors, masses, power levels, costs, crew sizes or cycle lives are feasible. It does not select membrane, adsorption, oxidation, crystallization, electrochemical, thermal or biological processes. It does not define a national fleet quantity. It does not prove the parent C3 feed envelope. It does not close groundwater-specific consumables, coastal-carrier residuals or sealed-transfer regeneration. It does not establish environmental permission to store, transform or move a residual. It does not authorize a vehicle rendering.

The immediate successor should connect this endurance module back into the whole assurance chain. It must preserve serialized source identity and independent provenance through carrier, prepared node, treatment, receiver island, product-class network, residual custody, depot regeneration and a second event. That model should quantify which components are common across the four source families, which are theater-specific and which create a hidden shared failure.

The deeper institutional conclusion is already clear. A Department of Resilience cannot buy water production and outsource everything undesirable to the disaster. Its force must own the whole transformation, including the material left behind and the ability to serve again.
