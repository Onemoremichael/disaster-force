# `DCP-2` terrain–air interface and `ATUT` subsystem competition

## Purpose and decision

This study asks whether the two provisional strategic carriers in `DCP-2` still form a credible two-mode force once their destination interface, energy ancestry, payload states, receiver geometry, maintenance, authority and mission clocks are made physical at equal depth.

The answer changes both the interface and the terrain carrier.

There should be no common pre-release transfer system. The selected behavior reference is `I3`, a **federated twin-receiver architecture** in which the atmospheric and terrain cells enter, unload, assay and reach civil public release through separate sites, energy systems, maintenance chains, custody records, technical authorities and civil-release officers. A post-release corridor may exchange water, power, people and spares, but its loss cannot change either service result. The interface is a common service grammar implemented twice, not a common machine.

The Pass 84 `SAT-170` reference also does not survive unchanged. Its sixty-tonne energy line and 155-square-metre footprint were deliberately left as leading risks. Closing energy mass and route energy as one feedback system produces a provisional 176.45-tonne liquid-fuel turbine-electric plant. The higher vehicle mass, a 1.15 dynamic load factor, 1.10 pod imbalance and loss of one of twelve contact pods then require at least 250.82 square metres of deployed contact. The revised `SAT-170R` reference therefore uses a 255-square-metre telescoping adaptive-pod system.

Those corrections raise the carrier from 332 tonnes tare and 502 tonnes rated gross to **478.45 tonnes tare and 648.45 tonnes rated gross**. They do not break the declared mission. Four isolated nine-megawatt generation sectors supply 36 megawatts installed and 27 megawatts after one sector loss, against a calculated 22.51-megawatt maximum demand. The terrain cell reaches public service at relative hour 41.82 and absolute hour 47.82. The unchanged atmospheric reference reaches public service at absolute hour 47.92. Complete dual service is therefore available at hour 47.92, with 6.08 hours to the hour-54 boundary.

At national scale, the corrected 219-vehicle terrain architecture is a $1.33874524 trillion research comparator. Receiver federation adds $170.78694 billion above the earlier generic receiver allowance. Revised `DCP-2` becomes $2.73651603 trillion and the associated national boundary becomes $10.11965758 trillion—$626.70524 billion above Pass 84.

The companion [executable model](../../../models/force/projection/dcp2-terrain-air-interface-and-atut-subsystem-competition.xlsx) contains sixteen sheets governing the interface competition, mission states, receiver land, energy fixed points, contact physics, revised vehicle, two-mode clock, failure worlds, workforce, reset, production, cost, technology portfolio and 120 evidence gates. All 120 calculation, reconciliation and register checks pass. The formula-error scan is empty. Every evidence gate remains open and strict readiness credit is zero.

No exterior rendering is authorized.

## 1. The force object is a public effect, not a pair of vehicles

`DCP-2` contains two independently governed 635-tonne service cells. The atmospheric cell divides across two `GCH-340` carriers; the terrain cell divides across four `ATUT-170` carriers. Either complete cell must be able to create the registered public effect after loss of the other access physics.

That requirement is easily weakened by apparently helpful integration. One common receiver, one common energy farm, one common heavy loader, one common software kernel or one release board may reduce visible duplication while turning two transport modes into one operational system. If that common system fails, both cells may arrive and neither may become public service.

The governing question is therefore not:

> How should the two carriers exchange cargo at a joint terminal?

It is:

> Can each mode move a complete governed cell from its own source through its own receiver to signed public service without borrowing payload, energy, maintenance, authority or decisive ancestry from the other, while still permitting useful interoperability after release?

This distinction changes the meaning of an interface. A military-style common pallet is useful because many logistics chains can move an object toward a functioning theater. `DCP-2` is designed for the case in which the theater itself is damaged. Its interface must preserve **failure independence before efficiency**.

## 2. The common interface is a semantic contract

The selected architecture uses the same service semantics on both paths:

- every payload position has a stable identity;
- every custody transfer is signed and can be reconciled offline;
- technical departure release is distinct from receiver entry;
- receiver entry is distinct from unload completion;
- unload completion is distinct from service assay;
- service assay is distinct from civil public release; and
- post-release exchange cannot retroactively become a release prerequisite.

The two modes may expose the same schema—position identity, hazard class, energy state, assay result, service capacity and release state—without sharing the mechanisms that implement it. The air cell may use one loading and control lineage; the terrain cell another. Both can be legible to a civil incident command without depending on one loader, one network, one certificate or one contractor.

This is a deeper form of interoperability than equipment commonality. It permits a common operating picture while preserving plural physical paths.

## 3. Mission-state grammar

The interface is governed through ten states.

| State | Meaning | Governing rule |
|---|---|---|
| `S0` | source-controlled serialized cell | each mode begins with a complete governed payload lot |
| `S1` | mode accepts payload | mass, hazard, custody and position close inside that mode |
| `S2` | technical departure release | vehicle, crew and route are signed by mode-specific authority |
| `S3` | independent transit | no cross-mode payload or energy transfer |
| `S4` | receiver zone controlled | bearing, exclusion, black start and civil control are established independently |
| `S5` | technical unload complete | all serialized positions reconcile inside the mode |
| `S6` | service assay complete | power, cooling, water, wastewater and medical functions pass their own tests |
| `S7` | civil public release | the cell becomes an independently lawful public service |
| `S8` | optional post-release federation | surplus power, water, people or spares may cross a fail-open corridor |
| `S9` | return and reset | vehicle, payload, crew, receiver and records return to certified load state |

Four prohibitions follow.

First, an incomplete cell may not become complete by taking positions from the other mode before `S7`. That would convert carrier loss in one mode into payload depletion in the surviving mode.

Second, cross-mode power may not be required for transit, unload, black start, assay or the first 72 hours of receiver service. Surplus exchange after release is permitted; planned dependency is not.

Third, one mode’s technical release, service assay or civil acceptance cannot release the other. Authority is an operating resource and a potential common mode.

Fourth, the downstream service yard cannot be on either critical path. Its denial may reduce combined capacity or convenience after release, but it cannot prevent either cell from reaching `S7`.

## 4. Four receiver architectures

The study competes four complete arrangements against thirteen disqualifying gates: independent entry, technical release, energy, maintenance, custody, public-release nodes, survival after denial of either receiver, survival after denial of the shared yard, network denial, absence of decisive common ancestry, hour-54 dual service and receiver separation.

### 4.1 `I1`: common service yard

`I1` brings both modes into one large receiver and shares energy, maintenance, public release and service assembly. It is compact and operationally legible in the nominal case. It passes only three of thirteen hard gates.

The architecture fails because the yard is not an interface; it is a third strategic system. A wreck, exclusion perimeter, fire, contaminated fuel lot, damaged switchgear, lost release board or blocked service aisle can deny both cells at once. Two access physics do not produce two public-service paths when both terminate in one indispensable machine.

### 4.2 `I2`: adjacent split zones with a shared spine

`I2` creates separate entry and technical-release zones inside one campus, then joins them through common energy and maintenance infrastructure. It passes six gates.

This is better than `I1` but still fails the guarantee. A common energy spine, common recovery system or campus-wide exclusion event can remove both. Physical labels on adjacent pads are not equivalent to independent operation.

### 4.3 `I3`: federated twin receivers

`I3` places the air and terrain receivers five kilometres apart under the current research hypothesis. Each reaches `S7` with separate:

- entry and exclusion control;
- black start and service energy;
- payload custody and unload equipment;
- technical release;
- service assay;
- civil public release;
- maintenance and recovery; and
- control and configuration records.

A six-kilometre, eighteen-metre service corridor connects the two nodes after release. It may carry medium-voltage circuits, treated-water hoses or pipe, vehicles, maintenance teams and communications. Every cross-tie is normally open or otherwise configured so that isolation does not revoke local service. `I3` is the only architecture that passes all thirteen gates.

The five-kilometre separation is not a universal safety distance. It is a declared common-mode hypothesis inherited from the configured formation. Fire, flood, plume, blast, earthquake, storm, landslide and hostile-action studies may require larger distance, protected colocation or a dynamic topology. What becomes authoritative here is not the number’s universal validity; it is that separation must be an explicit, hazard-conditioned design variable.

### 4.4 `I4`: rolling terrain–air rendezvous

`I4` avoids a fixed common yard by bringing the terrain convoy to a moving atmospheric rendezvous. It is attractive where a prepared receiver does not exist. It passes seven gates and keeps the arithmetic clock inside hour 54.

Its failure is logical rather than geographic. Payload transfer and common release occur before either cell is independently public. The rendezvous becomes a coupled timing, custody, geometry and authority event. This architecture may be valuable for conditional missions, but it cannot carry the universal two-mode guarantee.

## 6. Why the Pass 84 energy line fails

Pass 84 assigned sixty tonnes to a 600-megawatt-hour energy system. That implied ten kilowatt-hours per kilogram at the complete system boundary before tanks, conversion, thermal rejection, isolation and reserve. The value was intentionally marked as a technology target.

The deeper model closes energy as a feedback system. Let:

- (M_0) be non-energy vehicle mass plus rated payload;
- (M_e) be complete energy-system mass;
- (a) be route energy per tonne of gross mass;
- (E_w) be fixed route-work energy;
- (r) be the onboard reserve multiplier;
- (s) be usable consumable energy per tonne; and
- (H) be fixed plant, containment and thermal-system mass.

Mission energy is:

\[
E_m = a(M_0 + M_e) + E_w
\]

and complete energy-system mass is:

\[
M_e = H + \frac{rE_m}{s}.
\]

Solving gives:

\[
M_e = \frac{H + r(aM_0 + E_w)/s}{1-ra/s}.
\]

The denominator is decisive. If (ra/s \leq 0), adding energy mass creates at least as much new energy-system mass as it solves. The architecture has no finite fixed point under the registered mission coefficient.

For the revised terrain carrier:

```text
non-energy tare                         302.00 t
rated payload                           170.00 t
base mass M0                            472.00 t
route coefficient a                      0.68 MWh/t
route-work energy                        80.00 MWh
reserve multiplier                        1.175
```

This is a transparent first-order closure, not a claim that energy consumption is perfectly linear with mass or terrain. A representative program must replace the single coefficient with route-state distributions, thermal loads, starts, idling, recovery, degradation and diversions.

## 7. Energy-system competition

Five candidates are compared at complete-system level.

| Candidate | Complete energy mass | Rated gross | Onboard energy | Required one-pod-out contact | Result |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `E1` battery-electric | no finite fixed point; 9,999 t sentinel in model | 10,471 t sentinel | 8,460 MWh sentinel | 4,050 m² | diverges |

| `E4` regenerative metal-air | 295.83 t | 767.83 t | 707.50 MWh | 296.99 m² | mass and contact fail |
| `E5` compact nuclear-electric | 260.00 t declared plant | 732.00 t | 678.87 MWh required | 283.13 m² | mass, start, reset and casualty gates fail |

The candidate coefficients are design hypotheses, not market specifications. They exist to expose the mission thresholds an innovation program would have to move.

### 7.1 Why battery mass diverges

At 0.25 megawatt-hours of usable energy per tonne, the reserve-adjusted mass feedback coefficient is 3.196. Each tonne of battery adds enough route demand to require more than three additional tonnes of energy system. The failure is not that batteries are generally unsuitable for disaster response. Batteries remain excellent for local machines, quiet operation, regenerative capture, black start and transient power. They do not close this 648-tonne, 800-kilometre universal carrier as the sole energy source under the registered coefficient.

### 7.2 Why the lighter hydrogen candidate is not selected

`E3` is lighter in the declared arithmetic. It fails for two formation reasons.

First, the current `DCP-2` register assigns hydrogen-electric lineage `A` to the atmospheric path and liquid-electric lineage `B` to terrain. Selecting hydrogen for terrain would create a decisive common ancestry before the atmospheric plant itself has been resolved. The authoritative rule is the ancestry separation—not permanent loyalty to a particular chemistry. If later `GCH-340` work selects a liquid lineage, the terrain energy choice must be reopened.

Second, the candidate does not yet close the field-casualty boundary. Cryogenic storage, rapid transfer, boil-off, puncture, crash isolation and receiver exclusion are not contained in a favorable gravimetric coefficient.

This is a central finding: **vehicle optimization must not consume formation resilience**.

### 7.3 `E2` as the provisional terrain reference

`E2` assumes 5.04 kilowatt-hours of usable electrical energy per kilogram of consumable fuel, fifty-five tonnes of fixed conversion, containment and thermal hardware, and 4.2 megawatt-hours per cubic metre of delivered energy at the complete stored-fuel boundary. The fixed-point solution is:

```text
energy-system mass                      176.45 t
mission fuel mass                       121.45 t
fixed plant / containment / thermal      55.00 t
mission energy                           520.95 MWh
onboard deliverable energy               612.11 MWh
energy reserve                            17.50%
```

This is not a conventional generator bolted to a transporter. The behavior reference is four isolated nine-megawatt sectors with high-temperature conversion, crash-isolated tanks, fault-contained power electronics, field-replaceable thermal modules, local dispatch under network denial and one-sector-out operation. The plant is a clean-sheet civil propulsion and mobile-power program.

Liquid fuel is retained here because it closes the complete provisional formation boundary, not because the Department should stop pursuing hydrogen, metal-air, advanced electrochemistry or compact nuclear systems. Those alternatives remain funded rivals.

## 8. Static ground pressure was false comfort

Pass 84 calculated 31.77 kilopascals from 502 tonnes across 155 square metres:

\[
p = \frac{502\times9.81}{155}=31.77\ \text{kPa}.
\]

That arithmetic assumed all pods share static load perfectly. It did not include increased energy mass, moving loads, load-control error or a damaged pod.

The revised requirement uses:

- 648.45 tonnes rated gross;
- a 1.15 dynamic amplification;
- a 1.10 load-imbalance factor;
- eleven of twelve pods remaining; and
- a 35-kilopascal saturated-ground ceiling.

Required deployed area is:

\[
A \geq
\frac{648.45\times9.81\times1.15\times1.10}{35}
\times\frac{12}{11}
=250.82\ \text{m}^2.
\]

The selected target is 255 square metres. After loss of one pod, 233.75 square metres remain. Compounded pressure is:

\[
p_d =
\frac{648.45\times9.81\times1.15\times1.10}{233.75}
=34.43\ \text{kPa}.
\]

The nominal all-pod soft-ground value is only 24.95 kilopascals. The difference between 24.95 and 34.43 is exactly why nominal pressure cannot govern design.

Pressure is still only a proxy. Bearing failure, shear, sinkage, rut accumulation, lateral soil flow, repeated passes, steering disturbance and transitions between soil classes must be measured. The present calculation decides the minimum geometry worth testing; it does not prove mobility.

## 9. Contact-system competition

Five architectures face the same revised gross mass and compound case.

### 9.1 `C1`: fixed distributed tracks

Fixed tracks retain road speed and simple transition logic. At 155 square metres, however, the compounded pressure is 56.64 kilopascals. The architecture also misses the step screen. It fails the universal weak-soil case.

### 9.2 `C2`: hydraulic load-spreading outriggers

`C2` reaches 280 square metres and 32.85 kilopascals in the compound case. It fails because deployment takes 1.5 hours, movement on the spread pads is limited to five kilometres per hour and step capacity is one metre. It is a receiver stabilizer, not a complete contact system.

### 9.3 `C3`: walking shoe array

The 300-square-metre walking system has the best combination of low pressure, traction and obstacle height among solid-contact rivals. Its transition and six-kilometre-per-hour off-road speed break the strategic route. It remains valuable for local works or final-site construction.

### 9.4 `C4`: air-cushion assist

Air cushion produces 25.55 kilopascals and retains speed. It fails the one-pod-equivalent traction requirement, step, route width and recovery pull. Low reaction does not guarantee controlled motion.

### 9.5 `C5`: `TAP-255` telescoping adaptive pods

`C5` is the only candidate closing all declared screens:

```text
deployed contact                         255.00 m²
one-pod residual                         233.75 m²
compounded pressure                       34.43 kPa
contact transition                         0.42 h
off-road speed                            18.00 km/h
one-pod-out traction ratio                  1.90
step                                       1.80 m
transit / deployed width              9.50 / 11.80 m
recovery pull ratio                         1.40
```

The provisional mechanism uses twelve independently steerable track cassettes. Each extends longitudinal contact and limited lateral shoes while remaining within a 12-metre route envelope. A failed cassette can be isolated and unloaded from the active control law. The behavior is novel but not magical: it creates a specific full-gross pod-quarter article, soil lane, transition time, debris challenge and replacement clock for the test enterprise.

## 11. Failure behavior

Twenty-two worlds test the integrated formation. The behavior model preserves a public effect after denial of either receiver, either energy lineage, either maintenance chain, either release authority, either whole mode, the shared yard, the post-release corridor, national network and GNSS, one `GCH-340`, one `SAT-170R`, one terrain contact pod or one terrain power sector.

Three worlds clarify the boundary.

First, simultaneous denial of both access modes fails by design. The present guarantee is one complete independent cell after a single mode cut, not survival of all conceivable combinations.

Second, discovery of a decisive common ancestor fails assurance even if both vehicles continue to operate in the nominal calculation. Supplier genealogy, software kernels, fuel processing, certification and specialized maintenance can create a hidden joint cut.

Third, withholding representative evidence leaves behavior arithmetic unchanged and strict credit at zero. This prevents model completeness from becoming institutional readiness.

- civil authorities accept the service for public use.

No one signature substitutes for the others.

### Phase A: coefficient articles

Build full-gross pod quarters, nine-megawatt energy sectors, containment cells, thermal modules, offline control rigs and receiver black-start systems. Challenge the coefficients directly, including negative results and destructive limits.

### Phase B: coupled subsystem vehicles

Place all twelve contact pods, four energy sectors, structure, route kit, control and representative payload ballast into a complete terrain article. Test dynamic load sharing, pod loss, power-sector loss, soil transitions, route work, fuel casualty and maintenance removal without treating separate laboratory successes as an integrated pass.

### Phase C: complete terrain cell

Operate four vehicles carrying a serialized 635-tonne service cell over the registered route. Insert unannounced route, crew, vehicle, energy, communications and receiver faults. Measure time to safe public service, not arrival.

### Phase D: federated two-mode mission

Dispatch two `GCH-340` and four `SAT-170R` articles from separated origins to separated receivers. Deny one mode, one receiver, one release authority, the shared yard and the cross-tie in randomized trials. Require the surviving mode to reach `S7` without borrowed resources.

### Phase E: reset and production

Return vehicles, crews, payload systems, receivers and records to certified load state within 168 hours, then repeat. In parallel, prove supplier genealogy, replacement capacity, production rate, depot throughput and substitute lineages.

Every gate is open. A complete workbook, prototype or successful demonstration does not admit the system until representative evidence, independent authority and repeatability close the registered claim.

## 18. Implications for a mature Department of Resilience

### 18.1 Projection forces need sovereign endpoints

Strategic mobility is incomplete without receivers. The Department’s equivalent of ports, airfields, depots and theater-opening units must include lawful civil-service release under failed infrastructure. Receiver networks deserve the same planning, land, protection, exercises and capital seriousness as carriers.

### 18.2 Commonality is not resilience

Common interfaces can lower training and inventory costs, but common decisive mechanisms erase physics diversity. Mature acquisition must distinguish semantic commonality from ancestry commonality. The former lets institutions cooperate; the latter can make them fail together.

### 18.3 Large civil machines will be systems of systems

The revised `SAT-170R` is not a truck scaled to 170 tonnes of payload. It integrates strategic transit, route sensing, deformable-soil mobility, small-gap bridging, megawatt generation, offline control, payload custody, receiver entry, cooperative recovery and seven-day reset. Its clean-sheet industrial peers would look more like prime contractors and national laboratories than specialty-vehicle vendors.

### 18.4 Innovation should be mission-coupled

The Department should fund advanced energy, autonomy, robotics, materials and power electronics, but require them to close a public-effect chain. A breakthrough that removes a receiver dependency or cuts energy feedback may be more valuable than one that improves a component record while leaving the formation unchanged.

### 18.5 Redundancy must include institutions

Separate vehicles are insufficient when they share a fuel process, control kernel, technical school, depot, certificate or public authority. A coequal resilience force needs supplier genealogy, plural certification, independent test authority and standing professions designed as part of operational redundancy.

### 18.6 The apparent cost of seriousness will rise

Each physical pass has increased the modeled boundary because shallow placeholders were replaced with complete systems. That is not automatically evidence that the force is unaffordable. It is evidence that strategic assurance cannot be priced as equipment acquisition. Later competition, learning and technology may lower cost, but only after the requirement is honestly represented.
