# Pass 196 — Constructive-power projection and strategic-employment constitution

**Research object:** `SPC-1`  
**Decision level:** mature Department strategy  
**Verdict:** `CLOSE`

## Branch and resolution

The deepest active branch is `SPC-1`, the first of five permitted strategic-power passes after `DFG-5` returned force generation to Department architecture.

**Resolution test:** Reversing this result would change which missions receive standing force, whether invited capacity is recallable, what international service is allowed to accomplish and whether the Department becomes a coercive auxiliary, so it directly changes the central thesis and mature architecture.

## Decision

The Department will project **sovereign constructive power**: the organized ability to preserve or restore complete public service under hostile physical conditions and leave the recipient independently more capable. It is coequal with military power, not subordinate to it; material rather than symbolic; and bounded against coercion rather than merely described as benevolent.

Four alternatives fail. A humanitarian auxiliary inherits another institution’s lift, access and priority. A dual-use coercive arm destroys invitation and civil trust. A fee-for-service expeditionary contractor makes purchasing power determine protection. A universal global service promises more than a sovereign state can reserve and would dissolve domestic duty. The selected doctrine instead has four modes:

| Mode | Standing purpose | Force treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `P1` guaranteed domestic continuity | constitutional service across states, tribes and territories | eight simultaneous campaigns; 1,745 committed formations |
| `P2` invited stabilization | treaty service after sovereign invitation and U.S. acceptance | two simultaneous campaigns; 675 committed formations |
| `P3` forward resilience partnership | receivers, exercises, interfaces and transfer preparation | no formation credit |
| `P4` productive statecraft | useful civil output, reciprocal standards and sovereign production | market activity; no formation credit |

The first two modes are promises; the latter two make those promises faster, more reproducible and less dependent. Neither commerce nor partnership may be booked as ready force.[^1]

## The force is already strategically divided

The inherited stress constitution contains eight domestic and two invited campaigns. Reconstructing all forty-eight family claims assigns exactly 1,745 formations to domestic duty and 675 to invited stabilization—72.1 and 27.9 percent of the committed force.[^2] The invited share is therefore not surplus aid.

At the replacement value and staffing density of each owned family, domestic commitments expose about USD 2.93T of formation capital and 3.70M affiliated billets; invited commitments expose about USD 1.25T and 1.44M. These are proportional exposures of committed serials, not new appropriations. They deliberately exclude the 502 protected-reserve formations, 745 geographic-buffer formations, 1,584 regenerating formations, common capital and the annual operating account because none has yet been constitutionally assigned between strategic modes.[^2][^3]

An invited claim is optional before invitation and U.S. acceptance. Once accepted and admitted, its serials are not domestically recallable unless both sovereign authorities release them. A subsequent domestic catastrophe must draw protected reserve and publish any uncovered family deficit; it cannot manufacture homeland readiness by breaking an accepted international service contract.[^4]

## What constructive power produces

Strategic effect has nine non-fungible ledgers: catastrophe denial, economic continuity, alliance reliability, partner sovereignty, industrial mobilization, technology competition, standards power, democratic legitimacy and strategic restraint. The metric is not money spent, tonnes delivered, sorties flown, gross output or favorable attention. Every credited effect requires six conditions: complete service, rights acceptance, sovereign control, durable transfer, independent reproduction where contracted, and non-coercion.

This produces a distinctive form of deterrence by denial. Hazards and adversaries gain less leverage from collapsing water, care, energy, mobility or public administration when a separate national instrument can restore them. Alliance credibility follows from honoring a difficult service contract, not displaying a platform. Industrial and standards power follow when partners can reproduce complete capability without a permanent U.S. operator, key, cloud or supplier monopoly.

## Treaty-scale service, not expeditionary permission

Every invited campaign requires twelve seals: invitation; public-service vector; authority map; rights and access; receiver admission; data sovereignty; logistics and residuals; independent evidence custody; bilateral release; transfer; partner reproduction; and public accounting. Host authorities retain admission and stop power. U.S. authorities retain the right to decline a claim and to suspend unsafe service, but not to convert acceptance into indefinite operating license or unilateral recall.

Ten uses are prohibited: combat or blockade; regime change or political conditionality; migration interdiction or forced return; unrelated intelligence; extraction or tied reconstruction; prestige deployment without complete service; unilateral recall; permanent substitution for local government; dependence through remote control; and domestic militarization. Military or host forces may protect access under separate authority. That interface does not transfer combat, intelligence or political purpose to the Department.

## Technology consequence

Sovereignty must become a system property. The research program therefore needs treaty compilers, offline receiver meshes, rights-bounded bilateral evidence, non-coercion monitors, partner-reproducible production roots, portable public utilities, adversarial civil-service ranges and exit-by-design architectures. A machine that cannot transfer its safe operation, evidence, repair and decisive supply path is strategically incomplete, however impressive its output.

The companion workbook carries twenty-three sheets: architecture competition, four modes, forty-eight-family and ten-campaign posture, treaty and non-coercion law, transfer, partner sovereignty, economic exposure, scenarios and twelve technology directions. Fourteen terminal audits pass after saved-file recalculation; four mutation cases restore. All treaty identities and effect evidence remain source-empty, released strategic credit is zero and rendering remains blocked.
