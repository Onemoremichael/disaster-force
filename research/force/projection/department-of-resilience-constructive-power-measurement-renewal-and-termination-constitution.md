# Pass 199 — Constructive-power measurement, renewal and termination constitution

**Research object:** `SPC-4`  
**Decision level:** mature Department strategy  
**Verdict:** `CLOSE`

## Branch and resolution

The deepest active branch is `SPC-4`, the fourth of five permitted strategic-power passes.

**Resolution test:** Reversing the measurement law would change whether the Department is judged by public service and partner independence or by spending, activity and diplomatic preference, and would change which capabilities are renewed, redesigned or killed, so it directly changes the central thesis and mature architecture.

## Decision

Constructive power will be governed through a **noncompensatory public-service and sovereignty ledger**. Avoided-loss monetization, delivery dashboards, diplomatic-influence indices and composite resilience scores all fail because they permit success in one domain to conceal fatal failure in another. No single constructive-power index will exist.

Nine strategic effects remain separate:

| Effect | Governing denominator | Prohibited substitute |
| --- | --- | --- |
| catastrophe denial | population-service hours above irreversible-loss floors | aggregate avoided loss |
| economic continuity | essential-service deficit days closed | GDP or insured loss alone |
| alliance reliability | complete accepted contracts delivered without unilateral recall | deployments or statements |
| partner sovereignty | independently operated, repaired and reproduced capability | equipment transfer or training |
| industrial mobilization | complete configurations reproduced under denial and clock | jobs, factories or spending |
| technology competition | independent roots validly qualified, resized or killed | patents or prototypes |
| standards power | independent implementations without central runtime | citations or market share |
| democratic legitimacy | rights-complete service-floor parity and remedy | approval or visibility |
| strategic restraint | zero prohibited-use violations and complete scope closure | favorable diplomacy |

The ledgers share a constitutional order, not a common unit. Water, care, power, mobility, sovereignty and rights cannot be converted into one score without erasing the thing the force exists to protect.[^1]

## Evidence before claim

Every effect is examined through five independent planes: physical service; rights and distribution; sovereignty and transfer; causal counterfactual; and industrial and technical renewal. Across twelve forward receiver districts and nine effects, this creates 108 district–effect accounts and 540 evidence records. Two campaigns create eighteen effect ledgers. Five horizons—preregistration, bridge service, full campaign, day-90 transfer and day-365 sovereignty—create ninety persistence records.[^2][^3]

All remain `SOURCE EMPTY / HOLD`. Planned coverage is not evidence.

Causal attribution does not require withholding life-saving service. It requires triangulation: a preregistered physical service-flow balance; matched untreated or delayed geography where ethical; interrupted time series; a structural engineering twin; negative-control outcomes or exposures; spillover and displacement accounting; and comparison with a rival feasible intervention. An impressive observed restoration remains valuable service, but its strategic causal claim stays `HOLD` if the counterfactual fails.

Distribution is a hard boundary. Average service cannot hide a neighborhood, disability group, territory or displaced population below the declared floor. Externalities moved across populations, services, time, geography or the environment remain in the denominator rather than appearing as someone else’s problem.

## Lexicographic renewal

Seven layers govern disposition in order:

1. integrity and validity;
2. rights and non-coercion;
3. complete public service;
4. partner sovereignty;
5. causal attribution;
6. readiness and industrial renewal; and
7. cost and strategic choice.

Only an integrity-invalid attempt may be repeated without counting its outcome. A rights or coercion failure stops the application. A first valid service or sovereignty failure orders redesign; recurrence in the successor kills the branch. Invalid causality holds the strategic claim. Stale readiness orders resize or redesign. Cost can select `RENEW`, `RESIZE` or `EXPAND` only after every prior layer passes. Prestige, sunk cost and strategic desire cannot rescue a failed capability.[^4]

The workbook’s eleven hypothetical cases prove the action law for `REPEAT`, `HOLD`, `STOP`, `REDESIGN`, `RESIZE`, `RENEW`, `EXPAND` and `KILL`. They are tests of the constitution, not observations about the force.

## Evidence decays; history does not

Formation readiness renews every ninety days. Receiver districts and partner production roots renew annually through denied-access exercises and accepted cold lots. Network compacts renew every two years. Capital programs face a five-year disposition.

Historical mission evidence remains permanently visible, including adverse findings. It does not automatically confer current authority. When no real catastrophe occurs, representative campaigns and productive-service evidence may renew readiness; actual strategic effect remains `UNOBSERVED`, neither assumed successful nor treated as failed. This prevents both opportunistic triumph and punishment for disasters that did not happen.

Eight terminal rules make stopping a program constructive. Intentional coercion, concealed adverse evidence, engineered dependency, repeated valid service or rights failure, expired production, prestige retention or disappearance of actual need can kill an application or branch. Evidence, remedies, public rights, useful components and industrial conversion survive termination.
