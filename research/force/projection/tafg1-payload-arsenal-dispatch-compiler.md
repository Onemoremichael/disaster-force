# `TAFG-1` payload, arsenal and dispatch compiler

## Strategic access begins in the arsenal and ends at public-service release

**Research status:** first-principles payload-custody, dispatch and concurrency compiler; behavior only; no architecture or formation admitted

**Decision date:** 7 September 2026

**Companion model:** [`tafg1-payload-arsenal-dispatch-compiler.xlsx`](../../../models/force/projection/tafg1-payload-arsenal-dispatch-compiler.xlsx)

## Executive judgment

The Department of Resilience should treat a strategic payload arsenal as part of the force, not as warehouse support. Pass 79 configured `TAFG-1` around atmospheric carriage, autonomous terrain carriage and robotic utility bridging. This pass traces the payload through the institutions that make those vectors real: physical lots, suppliers, arsenals, loading equipment, energy, control, technical release, receiver entry and service acceptance.

The result changes the force design in two ways.

First, a common payload standard requires plural custody. One integrated hub, `C0`, appears efficient but closes only eight of thirteen registered one-region cut worlds. A lost loading bay, common technical release, base-energy failure, national-network denial or interface defect can remove all three nominal paths. The reference `C1` architecture separates atmospheric cell `A`, terrain cell `B` and corridor payload `C` into three arsenals with independent loaders, energy lineages, control partitions and technical release. It closes all thirteen one-region cut worlds in the declared screen. Forty-three ready groups imply 129 arsenal nodes, a 102-person deployed custody layer per group and a $211.7296 billion twenty-five-year custody program.

Second, the pass separates nominal concurrency from assured concurrency. `F1-C2` can split its two physical cells between two receiver echelons, but one region is then served by a single access vector. `F2-C2`, the Pass 79 eight-aircraft heavy-VTOL challenger, can nominally open a second region, but losing one aircraft leaves only 555.625 tonnes of an evenly distributed 635-tonne payload. That is below the 571.5-tonne 90-percent mass floor before accounting for missing functions. The earlier fifteen-world behavior count was therefore a nominal result, not an assured one.

A ten-aircraft revision, `F2R-C2`, loads 720 tonnes and retains 648 tonnes after one evenly loaded aircraft is lost. It can pass the vehicle-loss mass screen if functions are distributed with genuine `N+1` behavior. It still fails loss of the VHL mode or its site because region B has no second physics family. Only `F3-C2`, two complete tri-vector groups with two receiver echelons and dual-federated custody, preserves two regions after a vehicle loss and a mode-or-site loss in the declared comparison.

This does not select full duplication. It defines the burden that a more efficient rival must close. The current reference for one-region assurance is `F1-C1`: 590 deployed people including the custody layer, an estimated 1,312 affiliated positions, and a $7.38314155 trillion whole-force boundary. The assured two-region upper comparator is `F3-C2`: 1,136 deployed people including custody, an estimated 2,529 affiliated positions, and an $8.34663795 trillion boundary.

The deterministic dispatch chains enter service at hour 42 for `M1`, hour 45 for `M3`, hour 46 for `M5` and hour 47 for VHL. Those margins are too narrow to imply readiness. They exclude empirical delay distributions, weather holds, queue interference, casualty work, reloading and failed receiver acceptance. They are targets for a representative campaign, not proof.

All forty evidence gates remain open. Current and strict credit are zero. Rendering remains blocked until physical lot geometry, loading envelopes, cross-loading rules, receiver footprints and representative schedules establish what the systems actually need to look like.

## 1. The starting error: the vehicle is not the dispatch chain

Pass 79 found that three different propulsion families can still share a decisive failure. If one module pool, loading point or release authority controls every vector, a fire, contamination hold, software defect, labor action, grid loss or administrative stop can remove the entire formation.

That conclusion matters because modern force projection is often discussed through visible platforms. Aircraft, ships and terrain systems attract attention. Their readiness, however, depends on a less visible chain of magazines, handling equipment, energy, crews, maintenance, weather, command, authority and receiving infrastructure. A Department of Resilience built at military scale would need an equally serious arsenal state.

The arsenal is not merely a place where equipment waits. It preserves configuration; separates supplier and lot ancestry; keeps consumables within environmental limits; rehearses loading; controls software and interface versions; maintains lifting and connection equipment; certifies crews; holds energy; supports safe dispersal; and can release a complete public-service article while national communications are degraded. Its output is not inventory. Its output is a dispatchable, reconstructable service chain.

This pass therefore begins at the sixteen-package, thirty-two-position `PTS-5` manifest and asks a harder question: after a named cut, does at least one complete set of positions, people, machines, energy, authority and receiver access still reach public-service release by hour 48?

## 2. Formal service-chain condition

For access vector `v`, receiving region `r` and failure world `w`, a complete effect exists only if all of the following survive together:

```text
complete governed payload lot
× compatible loading system
× vehicle capacity and environmental release
× qualified crew and control partition
× independent energy path
× viable receiver zone
× technical release
× civil acceptance
× service connection by hour 48
= one usable public-service effect
```

The multiplication is logical rather than monetary. One missing term makes the effect unavailable. Excess vehicle capacity cannot replace a missing potable-water boundary. A second loader does not help if both require the same unavailable grid. An air and terrain path are not alternatives if their position records point to the same quarantined payload lot.

The pass uses three nested claims.

**Nominal closure** means that one complete chain exists with no inserted failure. This is the weakest result.

**One-region cut closure** means that one complete chain remains after each registered one-region loss. This is the `C1` reference requirement.

**Assured concurrent closure** means that two regions each retain a complete chain after a vehicle loss and after loss of an access mode or site. This is the strategic two-region requirement. Two nominal deliveries do not satisfy it.

No behavior claim becomes current evidence without representative measurement. The evidence-withheld world remains a universal stop.

## 3. The governed payload system

### 3.1 Sixteen packages and thirty-two positions

The physical service cell retains the Pass 79 boundary: 580 dry tonnes, 55 tonnes of wet launch load and 635 tonnes at dispatch. Sixteen functional packages occupy thirty-two governed positions.

The largest dry allocations are 82 tonnes for firm power conversion, 62 for final heat rejection, 48 for useful cooling, 48 for dry process stocks and assay, 43 for water treatment and quality, and 42 for distributed black start. Water storage, wastewater and residual custody, medium-voltage distribution, water and thermal distribution, building interfaces, communications, survey robotics, medical and vertical service, crew safety and repair complete the article.

The 400-tonne corridor payload is separately allocated across the same functions. It does not reproduce every machine. It projects equivalent end service from a qualified source through conductors, hoses, thermal paths, repeaters, isolation devices, terminators and route robotics. Its position record must show which carried function is replaced, which source-side function remains outside the damaged area and what evidence supports equivalence.

### 3.2 Four lot families

The compiler assigns a distinct identity to each package in four families:

- `A-P01` through `A-P16` belong to the atmospheric-carriage cell;
- `B-P01` through `B-P16` belong to the terrain-carriage cell;
- `C-P01` through `C-P16` are utility-bridge records and physical segments; and
- `D-P01` through `D-P16` define the VHL concurrency payload.

These identifiers are not serial numbers. They are placeholders for a future hierarchy linking every position to configuration, producer, material and component lots, software, inspection, environmental history, current location, loader compatibility, technical authority and public-service release.

Cell `A` and cell `B` use the same external interface specification but cannot be routine draws from one physical pool. Bridge `C` has a different physical form and must prove service equivalence package by package. VHL `D` needs its own allocation because distributing positions across aircraft creates a different loss problem than dividing one cell across a pair of liftships or four terrain vehicles.

### 3.3 Common standard, plural implementation

The Department needs interchangeability without monoculture. The configuration authority should standardize lifting and restraint boundaries, power and fluid interfaces, communications, identity, environmental limits, test points and release records. It should qualify more than one internal implementation and supplier lineage for decisive functions.

The `C1` hypothesis uses two interface implementations. A defect in one implementation leaves at least one compatible path. That is a declared design rule, not evidence. A real program would need destructive mismatch tests, contaminated connectors, wrong-version modules, missing records, cyber denial, hurried cross-loading and independent reconstruction from signed local records.

## 4. Three custody architectures

### 4.1 `C0` common integrated hub

`C0` stores cells `A` and `B`, bridge `C` and VHL lot `D` at one integrated payload hub. One shared bay, utility, network and joint release simplify peacetime operations. Forty-three ready groups require forty-three hubs under the accounting convention.

The architecture passes nominal dispatch, loss of one mode-specific arsenal function, loss of an air or land receiver zone, loss of the standoff utility source and loss of two access modes when the remaining vector and common hub survive. It fails common loading, technical-release, energy, network and interface worlds. It closes eight of thirteen one-region cut worlds.

The failure is structural. Centralization turns flexible payloads into a single strategic target. The architecture remains useful as a cost and failure comparator, not as a reference.

Its declared custody program is $83.4915 billion: $21.5 billion of node capital, $33.9915 billion of ramp-weighted operations, $20 billion of research and development and $8 billion of shared systems. Replacing the embedded dispatch allowance produces a $7.25490345 trillion `F1` national boundary, but the apparent saving purchases a known common mode.

### 4.2 `C1` tri-partition custody

`C1` establishes one atmospheric arsenal, one terrain arsenal and one corridor arsenal per ready group. The three hold different payload lots, use different loading equipment, draw from different energy lineages, operate different control partitions and retain separate technical release.

The national force therefore contains 129 primary arsenal nodes. The atmospheric node uses paired lift towers, a hydrogen-electric lineage, an air-control partition and air technical release. The terrain node uses four platform loaders, a liquid-electric lineage, terrain control and land release. The corridor node uses four spool cells, its own depot energy, utility control and energization release.

One receiver service echelon remains common at formation level. That is acceptable for the one-region reference because losing one vector still leaves another vector serving the same civil mission. It is not enough for two regions.

`C1` closes all thirteen registered one-region cut worlds. Its custody layer adds 102 deployed and 256 affiliated positions to the Pass 79 formation, producing 590 deployed and an estimated 1,312 affiliated positions for `F1-C1`.

The declared program is $211.7296 billion: $45.15 billion of node capital, $81.5796 billion of ramp-weighted operations, $60 billion of R&D and $25 billion of shared systems. The whole-force boundary reaches $7.38314155 trillion after replacing the $60 billion embedded dispatch allowance once.

### 4.3 `C2` dual-federated custody

`C2` adds alternate atmospheric, terrain and corridor arsenals, separate loading systems, energy, controls and technical releases, and a second receiver echelon. Forty-three groups imply 258 nodes.

This architecture permits the formation's two physical cells to be dispatched to two regions. It also creates alternate custody when a primary node fails. It closes the thirteen one-region worlds and the nominal second-region world. It does not create another vehicle physics family for each region. When the second region is combined with a mode or site loss, one region loses its only remaining complete path.

The custody layer adds 204 deployed and 512 affiliated positions per group. The program reaches $363.526 billion: $82.56 billion in node capital, $135.966 billion in operations, $95 billion in R&D and $50 billion in shared systems. Applied to `F1`, the national boundary is $7.53493795 trillion.

`C2` is therefore a concurrency substrate, not an assured concurrent force by itself.

## 5. Loading systems are strategic equipment

### 5.1 Atmospheric cell

Two `HBL-320` liftships provide 640 tonnes of gross payload capacity for the 635-tonne article. The declared eight-hour load requires two independent lift towers and a 48-person loading crew. Each position must remain traceable through lifting, restraint, balance, environmental protection and transfer.

The five-tonne gross margin is not a vehicle-loss reserve. Loss of one evenly loaded liftship leaves 317.5 tonnes. `M1` survives at formation level only because `M3` or `M5` can produce the service effect.

### 5.2 Terrain cell

Four `ATUT-160` vehicles also provide 640 tonnes for the 635-tonne article. Four platform loaders and a 44-person loading crew complete the declared load in eight hours. Loss of one evenly loaded vehicle leaves 476.25 tonnes, below the service floor. The formation again depends on a different vector, not internal carrier redundancy.

The loading system must account for axle and track loads, center of mass, articulation, bridge compatibility, mud and slope recovery, access to maintenance points and unloading without a prepared terminal. A simple cargo manifest cannot establish these properties.

### 5.3 Utility bridge

Four `RUC-100` projectors carry the complete 400-tonne corridor system. Four spool and machine cells with a 38-person loading crew complete the declared load in six hours. Gross utilization is 100 percent. Loss of one projector leaves 300 tonnes and therefore no complete bridge.

`M5` is resilient at formation level because physical carriage remains available, not because the corridor set is `N+1`. A future design may trade added projectors and distributed spares against cost, road burden and deployment time.

### 5.4 VHL lattice

The eight-aircraft lattice has 640 tonnes of gross capacity and loads 635 tonnes. If mass is distributed evenly, each aircraft carries 79.375 tonnes. Losing one leaves 555.625 tonnes, or 87.5 percent of launch mass. That fails even the 90-percent mass screen.

The functional problem is stronger. The service cell contains indivisible functions. A lost aircraft may take all or a decisive part of black start, heat rejection, water quality, protection or receiver interface. Eight aircraft with five tonnes of total spare gross capacity cannot provide functional `N+1`.

The ten-aircraft revision loads 720 tonnes, or 72 tonnes per aircraft. Losing one leaves 648 tonnes. It passes the mass screen with 76.5 tonnes above the 571.5-tonne floor. It receives only a **hypothesis** of functional closure. The additional 85 tonnes above the 635-tonne launch article must be allocated across replicated or fungible functions so every permitted aircraft-loss case retains a complete service effect. Representative loading and loss trials must prove that property.

## 6. Deterministic hour-48 dispatch

The compiler schedules each vector from incident declaration to service release.

### 6.1 `M1` at hour 42

Air technical release takes two hours; crew muster four; payload loading eight; flight preparation two; transit and placement eighteen; and receiver connection eight. The chain reaches service at hour 42, leaving six hours of nominal margin.

### 6.2 `M3` at hour 45

Land release takes two hours; crew muster four; loading eight; route confirmation three; traverse and unload twenty; and receiver connection eight. The chain reaches service at hour 45, leaving three hours.

### 6.3 `M5` at hour 46

Utility release takes two hours; crew muster four; loading six; source assay four; route survey four; deployment eighteen; and test and energization eight. The chain reaches service at hour 46, leaving two hours.

### 6.4 VHL at hour 47

Air release takes two hours; crew muster four; distributed loading six; flight preparation three; transit and unloading twenty; and receiver connection twelve. The chain reaches service at hour 47, leaving one hour.

These schedules intentionally expose fragility. They contain no queue for a delayed crew, bad record, loader fault, deicing, contaminated module, weather hold, route obstruction, rejected source, rights dispute, casualty, damaged receiver or repeated test. A mature requirement should specify a probability distribution of service-entry time and a protected deadline percentile, not a single deterministic sum.

Preparation can create margin. Standing receiver surveys, prequalified sources, rehearsed crews, preassembled load trains, automated record reconciliation, parallel release and mobile test systems can reduce critical-path time. The Department's innovation goal should be safe parallelization and rapid exception handling, not pressure on personnel to waive controls.

## 7. Fifteen operational loss worlds

The compiler holds fifteen operational worlds and one evidence stop.

The first thirteen are one-region cuts: nominal dispatch; loss of the atmospheric, terrain or corridor arsenal; loss of the primary loading bay; loss of one vector's technical release; loss of one base-energy lineage; national-network denial; loss of an air or land receiver zone; loss of the standoff utility source; loss of two access modes; and one interface-implementation defect.

`C0` passes eight. `C1` and `C2` pass all thirteen.

The fourteenth world activates a second region at hour 6. `C2` passes because it has two receiver echelons and can split physical cells. `C0` and `C1` fail.

The fifteenth world combines the second region with one mode or site loss. Custody architecture alone cannot pass. Only the formation-level `F3-C2` comparison preserves two complete effects.

The sixteenth world withholds representative evidence. Every calculated architecture may remain physically plausible, but current and strict admission fail.

## 8. Concurrency correction

### 8.1 `F1-C1`: assured one-region reference

`F1-C1` keeps the complete tri-vector group under tri-partition custody and one receiver echelon. It maintains one region after one vehicle loss and one mode-or-site loss. It cannot serve two regions. This is the least declared architecture that closes every one-region cut.

### 8.2 `F1-C2`: nominal split

`F1-C2` adds dual custody and a second receiver echelon. Region A receives `M1` plus the `M5` bridge; region B receives `M3`. Both can nominally reach service. Loss of the sole region-B vector returns the force to one region. The design demonstrates that two complete cells and two receiver teams are necessary but not sufficient for assured concurrency.

### 8.3 `F2-C2`: eight-aircraft nominal concurrency

`F2-C2` keeps a full `TAFG-1` in region A and sends the eight-aircraft VHL lattice with cell `D` to region B. Nominally, both regions receive service. One VHL aircraft loss breaks both mass and functional closure, so assured service falls to one region.

Pass 79 described this formation as closing the concurrent-region behavior. Pass 80 narrows that finding: it closes nominal concurrency only. This is a proper supersession, not a contradiction hidden by terminology.

### 8.4 `F2R-C2`: vehicle-loss challenger

`F2R-C2` replaces eight VHL aircraft with ten and increases the distributed payload to 720 tonnes. It can retain the 90-percent mass floor after one aircraft loss, and the compiler grants a provisional functional hypothesis. It therefore retains two regions after one vehicle loss.

Loss of the VHL mode, its arsenal or its operating site still removes region B. The formation retains only one region under the stronger mode-or-site test. It is a vehicle-loss challenger, not an assured multi-physics solution.

### 8.5 `F3-C2`: assured upper comparator

`F3-C2` sends one complete tri-vector group and receiver echelon to each region. Each region retains atmospheric, terrain and corridor options under separate custody. The design preserves two regions after one vehicle loss and one mode-or-site loss in the declared model.

It is deliberately expensive and organizationally heavy. Its purpose is to prevent a weaker design from receiving credit because no complete alternative was modeled. A later competition should attempt to close the same compound requirement with a smaller second-region package.

## 9. Workforce consequence

The arsenal system creates six standing role groups: arsenal command, payload custody, loading operations, energy and base utilities, release and configuration, and recovery and site safety.

`C0` requires 50 deployed and 124 affiliated positions per group. `C1` requires 102 deployed and 256 affiliated. `C2` requires 204 deployed and 512 affiliated. These people are additional to the Pass 79 formation because the earlier model counted carrier, corridor, service, receiver and reset roles but did not fully staff plural arsenals and loading chains.

`F1-C1` therefore rises from 488 to 590 deployed people and from 1,056 to an estimated 1,312 affiliates. `F3-C2` rises from 932 to 1,136 deployed and to an estimated 2,529 affiliates using the Pass 79 affiliation ratio plus the custody layer.

The affiliation values are planning estimates. They expose training, relief, depot, security, inspection and school depth. They are not workforce authorizations. A later duty-cycle model must replace the inherited ratio with shift calendars, qualifications, leave, attrition, surge and instructor requirements by occupation.

The institutional point is not provisional arithmetic. Payload custody and loading require professional formation. The Department needs configuration officers, lot-control technicians, strategic riggers, rapid-utility load planners, energy and cryogenic operators, air and ground release officers, receiver-acceptance specialists, hazardous-material teams, offline record custodians and independent reconstructors. These are careers, not vendor callouts after an incident.

## 10. Financial and industrial consequences

The cost model removes the $60 billion dispatch allowance embedded in Pass 79 and adds each detailed custody program once. This prevents a more complete successor from being stacked on top of its placeholder.

The one-region reference `F1-C1` combines $726.7 billion of formation lifecycle, $165 billion of article-family R&D, $160 billion of shared formation enterprise, $180 billion of cross-cutting technology and $211.7296 billion of custody program. After removing the embedded allowance and adding the $5.99971195 trillion other-force boundary, the national comparison reaches $7.38314155 trillion.

The nominal split `F1-C2` reaches $7.53493795 trillion. The eight-aircraft `F2-C2` reaches $8.11546995 trillion. The ten-aircraft `F2R-C2` reaches $8.20856995 trillion. The assured two-group `F3-C2` reaches $8.34663795 trillion.

Changing assigned hard-access groups from twenty-two to thirty raises ready groups from forty-three to fifty-nine. The `F1-C1` boundary rises to $7.70069675 trillion and `F3-C2` to $8.96874995 trillion, then returns exactly when the input is restored. This sensitivity shows that formation lifecycle and custody nodes scale with force demand while national common accounts remain fixed.

The industrial program is broader than warehouses. It creates dispersed arsenal construction; large precision loading systems; protected energy and control islands; standardized but competitive module production; environmental storage; high-rate inspection; autonomous inventory movement; deployable test equipment; local signed data infrastructure; training simulators; and independent reconstruction laboratories.

The most valuable export may be the arsenal and interface system rather than the largest vehicle. Partner countries can field locally appropriate carriers while sharing tested service modules, custody rules, loaders, receiver interfaces and joint exercises. The design should support sovereign records, local release authority, multiple suppliers and maintenance without permanent dependence on an American cloud or prime contractor.

## 11. Technology campaigns

### 11.1 Robotic strategic arsenals

Arsenals should compile, inspect and marshal complete service cells continuously. Mobile robots can move modules, read configuration identity, check seals, perform non-destructive inspection, manage environmental storage and assemble mode-specific load trains. Human authorities retain exception judgment and release.

The research problem is trustworthy automation under degraded records. A robot should not declare a cell complete because thirty-two boxes are present. It must establish that each box has the correct function, version, lot ancestry, software, consumables, inspection and interface state.

### 11.2 Adaptive load compilation

The same external service cell must be divided differently across two liftships, four terrain vehicles or a VHL lattice. A load compiler should optimize mass, balance, thermal and environmental constraints while preserving function under permitted loss. It must produce an auditable position-to-vehicle map and identify configurations that cannot satisfy the mission.

This is a natural field for formal methods and machine-assisted design. The tool must be able to prove when no safe loading solution exists and preserve human-readable reasons. It should not optimize around a missing function or assume a delayed aircraft will arrive after the service deadline.

### 11.3 Offline authorization and configuration identity

The force needs signed local records that survive national-network denial. Each position, loader, vehicle, source, crew and release should carry verifiable identity and current state. Authorization should be federated so one network or credential service cannot halt the force, but revocation, safety stops and civil accountability must remain effective.

The goal is not an immutable ledger for its own sake. It is reconstructable custody: independent evaluators can determine what was loaded, why it was released, what changed in transit and which evidence supported public service.

### 11.4 High-rate physical interfaces

The hour-48 margin requires connectors, restraints, lifting points, fluid paths, power protection and test equipment designed for rapid handling at large scale. The Department should sponsor competing interface families and adapters, robotic connection, automatic contamination barriers, mistake-proof alignment and rapid isolation after damage.

The interface must fail safely around occupied communities. Fast connection cannot mean exposing residents to energization, contamination, uncontrolled pressure, cryogenic hazards or falling loads.

### 11.5 Representative dispatch ranges

The proving network needs full-size arsenal-to-receiver ranges where complete payloads can be stored, loaded, dispatched, diverted, unloaded, connected, operated, recovered and reloaded. Trials should insert wrong modules, missing records, loader faults, energy loss, crew unavailability, network denial, contaminated positions, receiver rejection, aircraft or vehicle loss and a second-region activation.

The test article is the full chain. A platform demonstration that starts with a preloaded payload and ends at touchdown cannot validate dispatch readiness.

## 12. Departmental organization

The Fourth Access Systems Command should own formation readiness, but three other authorities remain separate.

An **Access Configuration Authority** owns service-package definitions, interface versions, lot identity and compatibility. It does not manufacture every module or release every mission.

A **Strategic Resilience Arsenal Command** operates dispersed payload arsenals, loading systems, storage, energy, security, inspection and cross-loading. Mode-specific arsenal regiments retain the professions and equipment needed for atmospheric, terrain, corridor and VHL chains.

An independent **Access Test Directorate** owns representative trials, failure insertion and reconstruction. It must be able to reject the formation desired by operators and acquisition leaders.

Regional Federation Commands own receiver preparation and public priorities. Technical release remains with aviation, land and utility authorities; civil acceptance remains with lawful incident and receiving authorities. No technical office may convert equipment readiness into a unilateral right to operate in an occupied community.

## 13. Evidence and rendering boundary

Forty gates cover the four payload families, supplier ancestry, primary and alternate arsenals, loading systems, energy, control, technical release, civil authority, receiver echelons and zones, the four dispatch timelines, offline operation, interface defects, cross-loading, vehicle and mode loss, second-region activation, compound loss, casualty response, reset, independent reconstruction and rendering authorization.

Every gate is open. The model reports calculated behavior but zero current and strict credit. In particular:

- the 42-, 45-, 46- and 47-hour schedules have not been measured;
- the ten-aircraft functional `N+1` claim has not been demonstrated;
- supplier and energy lineages have not been traced to primary dependencies;
- dual arsenals have not been shown to avoid geographic or utility coupling;
- receiver echelons have not completed representative acceptance; and
- no formation has repeated the full dispatch-to-reset cycle under loss.

Rendering remains blocked. The payload register now supplies identities and functional allocations, but drawings would still invent dimensions, centers of mass, loader geometry, module arrangement, corridor reels, access clearances and human work zones. Those choices should emerge from the next physical configuration studies.

## 14. Pass 80 stocktake

### What became authoritative at `R2`

The unit of strategic access is now a complete source-to-release chain. The payload standard is instantiated as separately identified atmospheric, terrain, corridor and VHL lot families. `C1` tri-partition custody is the reference one-region architecture because it separates three arsenals, loaders, energy lineages, control partitions and technical releases and closes all thirteen declared one-region cut worlds.

Two-region capacity is divided into nominal, vehicle-loss and mode-or-site-loss claims. The eight-aircraft VHL formation is nominal only. The ten-aircraft revision is a vehicle-loss challenger. Two complete tri-vector formations are the current assured upper comparator.

### What the pass falsified

It falsifies the common integrated payload hub as a resilience architecture. It falsifies the Pass 79 inference that eight VHL aircraft provide assured concurrency. It also falsifies physical payload count as sufficient concurrency: two cells without two receiver echelons and independent fallback paths do not create two assured regions.

### What remains provisional

Every lot allocation, phase duration, loading rate, crew count, node cost, interface implementation, supplier separation and loss result remains a hypothesis. The model does not establish geographic node locations, spatial blast or flood separation, actual handling envelopes, shift sufficiency, weather distributions or receiver acceptance. `F3-C2` is not selected.

### Where the burden moved

The next burden is to find a second-region package smaller than full duplication that still retains two dissimilar complete effects after a mode or site loss. That package needs its own receiver echelon, payload custody, loading, energy, control, technical release and reset. A larger VHL fleet alone cannot close the requirement.

### Next highest-leverage pass

Pass 81 should conduct a **multi-mode second-region formation competition**. It should hold the compound concurrency requirement fixed and compare full `TAFG` duplication against leaner pairs such as VHL plus utility bridge, VHL plus local conversion, terrain carriage plus corridor projection, and atmospheric carriage plus prepositioned receiver capacity. Each rival must carry separately governed payload lots, have two receiver and release echelons, meet hour 48, survive a vehicle loss and retain both regions after loss of one mode or site.

The competition should include discrete vehicles, payloads, people, bases, energy, industrial lineages, reset and twenty-five-year cost. It should also identify the first physical configurations whose dimensions are stable enough to authorize renderings. Until then, no external-site research or rendering is introduced.
