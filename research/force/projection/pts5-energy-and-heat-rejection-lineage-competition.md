# `PTS-5` energy and heat-rejection lineage competition

## From a generic service cell to a four-echelon civil energy force

**Research status:** first-principles configuration competition; no lineage admitted

**Decision date:** 7 September 2026

**Companion model:** [`pts5-energy-and-heat-rejection-lineage-competition.xlsx`](../../../models/force/projection/pts5-energy-and-heat-rejection-lineage-competition.xlsx)

## Executive judgment

The energy system for an occupied-building heat response cannot be selected by comparing generators. It must be selected by comparing complete paths from stored or available energy through conversion, electrical and thermal distribution, heat rejection, source custody, safe stop, replenishment and reset.

The [heat-blackout service study](heat-blackout-power-and-thermal-service-cell.md) derives a 50,000-person district requirement of 14.22 MWe of continuous electricity, 19.65 MWth of useful cooling and 2,389 MWh over seven days. Its `PTS-50` upper comparator divides that responsibility among ten primary and two reserve `PTS-5` cells. This pass holds the public-service contract constant and asks what one nominal 5,000-person cell would require from six rival energy lineages.

The cell must provide 1.422 MWe continuously, carry a 1.920 MWe peak, sustain 1.965 MWth of useful cooling and dispose of at least 2.801 MWth from the cooling cycle. It consumes 238.923 MWh in 168 hours before an energy-inventory reserve. Those values are inherited design calculations, not measured urban loads.

The first correction is that 2.801 MWth is not the complete occupied-site heat burden. Any source converting energy at the receiver also rejects conversion losses. Under the declared coefficients:

| Lineage | Cooling-cycle rejection | Source-conversion waste | Total occupied-site rejection |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `L1` organic multi-liquid bridge | 2.801 MWth | 1.964 MWth | 4.765 MWth |
| `L2` conditioned gas with liquid bridge | 2.801 MWth | 1.313 MWth | 4.114 MWth |
| `L3` transported storage-only | 2.801 MWth | 0.158 MWth | 2.959 MWth |
| `L4` local-resource hybrid | 2.801 MWth | 0.786 MWth | 3.587 MWth |
| `L5` prepared metropolitan anchor | 2.801 MWth | 0.393 MWth | 3.194 MWth |
| `L6` strategic firm microreactor | 2.801 MWth | 2.887 MWth | 5.689 MWth |

This is not a secondary engineering adjustment. It changes equipment mass, occupied-site area, air movement, water demand, sound, plume, worker exposure, setup and the number of independent trains. A nuclear source reduces recurring fuel logistics but creates the highest modeled rejection burden. A battery eliminates combustion exhaust but does not eliminate the building cooling load and becomes a mass problem when asked to carry a week of energy. A prepared anchor minimizes mobile source work only by storing capacity in geography before the event.

No lineage is universal. The six rivals pass 16, 15, 15, 11, 13 and 13 of eighteen deterministic service worlds. Those counts are not scores. `L1` passes the most worlds but fails when its governing liquid-energy lineage is held and when combustion exhaust cannot be accepted at the occupied receiver. `L3` survives every source-denial world but requires 2,333 tonnes of energy storage and 2,784 tonnes for the complete cell. `L6` provides long endurance but misses the 48-hour service deadline and the declared mass, footprint, reset and compound boundaries.

The correct force object is therefore not one universal `PTS-5` machine. It is a **common civil utility service spine** that accepts several qualified energy and heat-rejection packages. The operating concept needs four temporal echelons:

1. cell-local black start and no-break continuity during the first six hours;
2. a rapid utility bridge from approximately six to seventy-two hours;
3. a transition to verified seven-day energy and heat-sink lineages; and
4. strategic firm service for long campaigns and repeat events.

The model uses a provisional mixed `PTS-50` account of six `L1`, two `L2`, two `L4` and two `L5` organic packages, with storage-only and strategic firm systems held in national pools. That is a budget and force-generation comparator, not a selected table of organization. It revises the group to 12,200 dry tonnes, 2,852 deployed people, $11.768 billion in direct equipment and $14.122 billion in configured capital. The counted seven-day liquid inventory is 407.8 tonnes per group; pipeline gas, makeup water, filters, refrigerants, residuals and other process materials remain open.

A seventy-two-group national portfolio, twenty-four prepared metropolitan programs, thirty-six storage-only experimental packages, forty-eight strategic firm-source attachments, twenty-five years of operations, and a defense-scale technology and workforce program totals approximately **$3.543 trillion in constant 2026 dollars**. This is not a procurement recommendation. It is the first fiscal expression of treating occupied thermal survival as a major national mission instead of generator support.

Twenty-eight representative gates remain open. No source, rejection architecture, carrier geometry or prime contractor has been selected. Rendering remains blocked.

## 1. The decision is larger than fuel choice

Conventional temporary-power studies often ask whether diesel, natural gas, batteries, renewables or nuclear power is cheaper or cleaner per unit of electricity. That comparison is incomplete for a resilience force because the mission does not buy electricity at a bus. It buys protected people-hours in damaged civil terrain.

The energy decision changes at least nine coupled systems:

- what must travel from origin;
- what must already exist at the receiver;
- how quickly the cell can establish service;
- how it forms and protects an islanded electrical system;
- how it starts compressors, pumps, fans, elevators and medical loads;
- how much heat, exhaust, sound, water and exclusion it imposes on occupied space;
- what upstream supplier, pipeline, grid, depot, component and credential dependencies remain;
- how the cell is refueled, maintained, stopped and handed back; and
- whether it can reset for a second event without borrowing from another formation.

A source can be excellent in one dimension and unusable as a response system. A microreactor may have months or years of core endurance yet arrive after the thermal emergency has peaked. A battery may start instantly yet require several thousand tonnes when treated as seven-day primary energy. A gas source may be efficient and compact but only while pipeline pressure, composition and upstream compression survive. A prepared district-energy anchor may be the fastest and lightest option where it exists and irrelevant elsewhere.

The Department should therefore compare **energy lineages**, not fuel nouns. A lineage includes the source, upstream ancestry, conversion equipment, starting path, storage, controls, heat rejection, site, workforce, consumables, safety case, replenishment and reset.

## 2. The service contract

The present competition inherits one-tenth of the Pass 72 district requirement. It does not use protected New York campaign outcomes and does not infer a representative American building stock.

| Cell service obligation | Value |
|---|---:|
| accountable population | 5,000 people |
| mission duration | 168 h |
| service-establishment deadline | 48 h |
| continuous net electricity | 1.42216 MWe |
| peak net electricity | 1.91992 MWe |
| useful cooling | 1.965 MWth |
| cooling-cycle heat rejection | 2.80117 MWth |
| electrical energy before reserve | 238.92288 MWh |
| source-delivered energy with 15% reserve | 274.761312 MWh |

The continuous electrical requirement already includes the power needed for cooling equipment, temporary-distribution loss, medical-device continuity, vertical service, communications, refrigeration, life safety and water pumping. The thermal output is useful cooling delivered to occupied rooms and refuges. It is not the heat rejected outdoors.

The 48-hour deadline belongs to public service. A source module arriving at hour thirty does not pass if transformation, rejection, distribution, building connection, testing or civil release pushes occupied service to hour sixty.

The model gives every lineage enough nominal electrical and thermal capacity to close its unstressed base. This is deliberate. The competition asks what happens after the sources meet the easy nameplate question.

## 3. Energy is an inventory over time

Power and energy cannot substitute for one another. The cell's continuous energy is:

```text
1.42216 MW × 24 h/day = 34.13184 MWh/day

1.42216 MW × 168 h = 238.92288 MWh
```

The model adds a 15% inventory reserve, producing a source-delivered target of 274.761312 MWh. This is not a reliability margin on source nameplate. It is additional available energy.

The reserve is intentionally modest. Startup, extreme ambient conditions, unexpected building loads, distribution extensions and delayed handback can consume it. A mature service contract should allocate energy by priority and time, not place an undifferentiated percentage above the load.

### 3.1 The storage-only mass boundary

The storage competitor uses a declared transport-ready system density of 0.16 MWh per tonne, 80% usable depth and 92% AC efficiency. Its nominal charged inventory is:

```text
274.761312 MWh ÷ 0.80 ÷ 0.92 = 373.317 MWh
```

At the declared delivered density, the energy system alone weighs 2,333.23 tonnes. The complete cell reaches 2,784 tonnes after its common service spine, conversion hardware and heat rejection are added.

This does not establish that storage is inferior. It establishes its proper role. Storage is exceptionally valuable for no-break medical loads, grid formation, motor starting, source transition, quiet operation, peak control and short-duration protection. It is not automatically the best way to transport seven days of primary energy.

Even a sensitivity increase from 0.16 to 0.24 MWh per tonne reduces the complete cell only to 2,006 tonnes. That remains above the model's provisional 1,000-tonne mobile-cell boundary. An invention program can target higher system density, but it must count enclosure, protection, cooling, fire separation, handling, state-of-charge restrictions, spares and damaged-module custody rather than quote cell chemistry alone.

### 3.2 Liquid inventory is part of the formation

At 42% modeled conversion efficiency and a declared lower heating value of 11.94 MWhth per tonne, `L1` requires 54.79 tonnes of seven-day liquid inventory for one cell. That figure already includes the 15% energy reserve. `L4` requires 21.92 tonnes because only 40% of its electrical conversion is assigned to liquid energy. `L5` requires 10.96 tonnes for a 20% mobile bridge.

The numbers are comparatively light, but a fuel tank is not an independent energy lineage. The force must preserve supplier ancestry, testing, contamination hold, additive compatibility, custody, transfer equipment, spill control, exhaust acceptability, refueling time and second-mission replenishment.

Tank segregation protects against a local leak or transfer error. It does not create independence when both tanks contain a product released by the same terminal, laboratory, credential system or transport corridor.

### 3.3 Gas is imported infrastructure unless proved otherwise

`L2` models an efficient gas conversion system with a 36-hour liquid bridge. Its mobile liquid inventory is only 6.64 tonnes. Most energy arrives through a pipeline or locally conditioned source and therefore does not appear in transported wet mass.

That is not free endurance. It is a transfer of burden from convoy logistics into pressure, composition, upstream compression, metering, isolation, civil authority and infrastructure survival. The model gives `L2` seven-day base endurance when gas is available, only 1.5 days after external logistics denial, and 30% power after gas denial.

If the Department wants a genuinely organic gaseous cell, it must carry compressed, liquefied or chemically bound energy and count containment, conditioning, boil-off, separation, fire behavior and delivery mass. That would be a different configuration from the pipeline-fed competitor.

## 4. Heat rejection is the hidden force

The public mission moves heat out of occupied space. The energy source may then add more heat at the same site.

For a conversion source operating at efficiency `η`, the model estimates receiver-side source waste as:

```text
source waste MWth = net electrical MW × (1 / η − 1) × on-site conversion share
```

This is a first-order balance. It does not distinguish jacket water, charge air, exhaust, inverter loss, radiation, auxiliary motors or useful heat recovery. Representative calorimetry must do that.

The balance nevertheless prevents a major conceptual error. The cell cannot claim that its thermal system needs to reject only 2.801 MWth while placing a 42%-efficient engine beside it. That configuration must manage approximately 4.765 MWth before additional losses. The microreactor competitor reaches 5.689 MWth because its declared 33% electrical conversion produces 2.887 MWth of source waste at the modeled net load.

### 4.1 Full evaporative rejection competes with public water

If all site heat were rejected by idealized evaporation, then adjusted for a 1.25 blowdown and drift factor, the modeled configurations would consume approximately 131–251 m³/day per cell.

| Lineage | Full evaporative water |
|---|---:|
| `L1` | 210.2 m³/day |
| `L2` | 181.5 m³/day |
| `L3` | 130.6 m³/day |
| `L4` | 158.2 m³/day |
| `L5` | 140.9 m³/day |
| `L6` | 251.0 m³/day |

Twelve `L6`-scale cells could consume approximately 3,012 m³/day if they relied on full evaporation. That exceeds the 2,250 m³/day occupied-building water service modeled for the entire Pass 72 district. A cooling system meant to preserve life cannot silently consume more water than the population contract.

The competitors therefore use dry-dominant or hybrid rejection assumptions. Those assumptions add radiator, fan, intake, filtration, acoustic and site-area burdens. They must also survive high ambient temperature and smoke, conditions that directly attack air-cooled performance.

### 4.2 Heat must have a destination

"Heat rejected" is incomplete without a destination. The path may end in:

- ambient air through dry exchangers;
- evaporated water with controlled makeup and blowdown;
- a surface-water body through a permitted exchange boundary;
- ground loops or subsurface storage;
- a prepared district-energy system;
- industrial or public loads that can use low-grade heat; or
- temporary thermal storage that delays rather than eliminates rejection.

Every destination has a capacity, temperature, contamination, access, noise, plume, rights and handback contract. Heat recovery can improve total system value in a winter mission, but a heat wave rarely provides a large coincident useful sink beside the affected residents.

### 4.3 Rejection trains must fail locally

Every competitor carries enough declared rejection capacity to pass a largest-train-loss screen. The margin is narrow for some. `L5` retains a 1.014 ratio and `L3` a 1.027 ratio. A blocked intake, fouled exchanger, failed fan drive or contaminated water loop can therefore become the controlling failure even when electrical nameplate remains abundant.

The cell needs separately isolable rejection trains, independent controls, local temperature evidence, manual stop, protected maintenance access and a way to shed thermal and electrical loads together. Continuing to power compressors after the heat sink is lost is not degraded service. It is an unsafe command.

## 5. The common service spine

The competing energy packages sit behind one government-controlled service spine. The spine preserves the public contract while allowing sources to differ.

Its provisional 190-tonne account includes:

- grid-forming conversion and synchronization;
- transformation, grounding and protection;
- cell-local electrical and thermal storage interfaces;
- thermal production controls and load sequencing;
- electrical, fluid, air and sensing boundaries;
- disconnected configuration and authority records;
- energy and heat-sink metering;
- medical and life-safety priority controls;
- maintenance isolation and damaged-module custody; and
- safe stop, handback and later reconciliation.

The spine is not a universal trailer. It is a bounded technical architecture that can be distributed across several transport articles. Its importance is institutional: government owns the service definition, safety boundaries, evidence vocabulary and interchange tests. Prime contractors compete inside those boundaries.

Commonity must not recreate common-mode failure. Two source packages using the same connector may be desirable. Two source packages using the same unaudited controller, firmware library, protection file, credential server or specialist team may not be independent.

## 6. Six energy and rejection lineages

### 6.1 `L1`: organic multi-liquid bridge

`L1` uses two liquid-engine islands, cell-local storage and dry-dominant hybrid rejection. It is the rapid severe-environment comparator: 30-hour setup, seven-day organic inventory, 580 dry tonnes, 54.79 tonnes of liquid inventory and 634.79 transported tonnes.

It carries 2.4 MWe of net nameplate and 1.8 MWe after loss of its largest source module. Seven MWth of installed rejection provides a 1.047 ratio at the declared 50°C stress and a 1.175 ratio after loss of the largest rejection train.

`L1` passes sixteen of eighteen worlds. It fails complete loss or hold of its governing liquid lineage and a receiver that cannot accept combustion exhaust. This makes it a strong bridge and a poor universal doctrine.

The clean-sheet development target is not a better commercial genset. It is an occupied-site source train with extremely low sound and local pollutants, tolerant fuel conditioning, independently testable liquid lineages, integrated thermal recovery where useful, fast module exchange, physical fire separation and evidence-producing controls.

### 6.2 `L2`: conditioned gas with liquid bridge

`L2` uses a high-efficiency gaseous conversion train, independent storage start and a 36-hour liquid fallback. The modeled cell weighs 640 dry tonnes and 646.64 tonnes with its liquid inventory. It carries six MWth of installed rejection and produces 4.114 MWth of site heat at the design load.

The lineage passes fifteen worlds. It fails external logistics denial, gas-source denial and the unprepared compound world. Those are not minor cases. They define the boundary between exploiting surviving infrastructure and owning a response capability.

The Department should still invest heavily in `L2` because gaseous infrastructure can eliminate an enormous recurring liquid convoy where it survives. The package needs wide-composition conditioning, pressure reduction, compressor and freeze protection, rapid isolation, independently powered valves, safe venting, gas-quality evidence and an orderly transition to the liquid bridge.

It must never receive organic-endurance credit merely because a city normally has gas service.

### 6.3 `L3`: transported storage-only

`L3` carries charged electrical inventory, inverter islands and dry rejection. It has no combustion source and passes every source-denial, exhaust, water-loss, ambient and smoke world in the declared matrix. It fails the 1,000-tonne mobile-cell boundary, the three-day reset and the compound world because mass remains controlling.

The complete cell weighs 2,784 tonnes at the current density assumption and passes fifteen worlds. That is operationally useful. A rail, barge or prepared-site storage formation could exploit this configuration. A road-mobile urban cell probably cannot.

The force should not reject storage because it is heavy at seven days. It should disaggregate the duties storage performs best:

- milliseconds to seconds for power quality and fault behavior;
- seconds to minutes for black start and motor starting;
- minutes to hours for no-break medical and control loads;
- hours for source transition and quiet periods; and
- thermal storage for shifting cooling production away from peak ambient conditions.

The research error would be to require one chemistry and one duration to do all five jobs.

### 6.4 `L4`: local-resource hybrid

`L4` assigns 60% of energy to a locally available or conditioned resource and 40% to organic liquid conversion. The source could be surviving distributed generation, hydro, waste gas, industrial energy, a local microgrid or another validated supply. The cell includes additional survey, conditioning and collection equipment.

It weighs 860 dry tonnes and 881.92 tonnes with liquid inventory, requires 60 hours to establish, and occupies 6,500 m². It passes eleven worlds. Local-resource loss, slow setup, smoke, combustion prohibition, site area, reset and the compound case defeat it.

This is not evidence against local energy. It is evidence that discovery and integration are work. A resource has no force value until the formation can find it, establish ownership and authority, characterize quality, connect safely, maintain it during the hazard and prove that its upstream dependencies differ from those already counted.

A mature `L4` compiler could be one of the Department's most important software, sensing and field-engineering systems. It should treat local energy as a configured asset, not an optimistic percentage in a planning slide.

### 6.5 `L5`: prepared metropolitan anchor

`L5` stores much of the system in place. A prepared electrical or district-energy anchor supplies 80% of the service; mobile source and storage provide a 20% bridge. The mobile cell weighs 550 dry tonnes and 560.96 tonnes with its liquid inventory. It establishes in eighteen hours, the fastest competitor.

The price of speed is geographic dependence. The lineage fails anchor loss, no-water rejection, combustion restriction, commercial-network denial and the unprepared compound. It passes thirteen worlds.

Those failures identify what a real prepared anchor must contain. It cannot be a receptacle beside a building. It needs independently testable electrical and thermal boundaries, islandable controls, dry-rejection fallback, local records, owner and utility authority, protected routes, inspection, exercised crews and a known mode when the anchor itself is lost.

Prepared infrastructure is not a cheaper substitute for the mobile force. It is one part of the force. The Department's capital account should treat installed interfaces, thermal refuges, substations, water connections, heat sinks and district nodes as readiness assets with configuration state.

### 6.6 `L6`: strategic firm microreactor

`L6` tests a dual-module microreactor with storage, security and oversized dry-dominant rejection. It carries 3.0 MWe of net nameplate, retains 1.5 MWe after the largest source-module loss and has a declared 365-day energy horizon.

The cell weighs 1,310 dry tonnes, occupies 8,000 m², takes 120 hours to establish and needs thirty days for the modeled reset. Its 9.5 MWth rejection system is the largest in the competition because the source itself adds 2.887 MWth to the cooling-cycle rejection.

It passes thirteen worlds and fails the 48-hour arrival, mobile mass, occupied footprint, three-day reset and compound case. The result places strategic firm nuclear power in a serious but bounded role.

A pre-sited reactor at a prepared metropolitan anchor would be a different architecture and could pass the arrival deadline. A maritime or rail-borne reactor coupled through a surveyed energy corridor would also be different. Neither may inherit credit from this mobile-cell model without its own route, site, security, cooling and authority chain.

The [Nuclear Water–Energy System](../../systems/water/nuclear-water-energy-system.md) already establishes the broader principle: the reactor, public process, shore interface, corridor and support formation should be segmented. This cell-scale result reinforces it. Nuclear power is a strategic endurance source, not a magical first-arrival generator.

The model's narrow ratios also define experiments. The storage-only rejection system passes the 50°C world at 1.005. The microreactor no-water case passes at 1.002. The local-resource smoke case fails at 0.997. These are effectively boundary conditions, not robust designs.

## 8. Why the force needs four temporal echelons

The competition reveals that no single energy geometry performs equally well from minute zero through month twelve.

### `T0`: black start and no-break continuity

From zero to approximately six hours, every cell needs locally isolated storage, critical medical micro-power, protection, manual control and enough thermal buffering to start safely. This echelon must not depend on a pipeline, convoy, grid, cloud, anchor or strategic source.

Its duty is measured in successful starts, protected no-break loads and safe refusals. It is not seven-day energy.

### `T1`: rapid utility bridge

From approximately six to seventy-two hours, the force must establish complete occupied-building service. Mobile liquid sources, conditioned gas where verified, prepared anchors and bounded local resources can all contribute. The group compiles its package before dispatch and revises it after reconnaissance.

The bridge should carry enough organic energy that one uncertain external connection cannot prevent first service. That does not require every cell to carry seven days of liquid fuel.

### `T2`: seven-day endurance

From day two through day seven, the formation should transition away from continuous emergency consumption where possible. Verified gas, surviving grid, local generation, prepared district energy, replenished segregated liquid inventories and storage all become part of the operating portfolio.

The transition is governed. The cell retains islanding, evidence and safe-stop authority. An available feeder does not automatically become a trusted source.

### `T3`: strategic firm service

After approximately day five, separate firm-source attachments can take over long-duration loads, recharge storage, rebuild reserves and support a second event. These may include pre-sited nuclear, geothermal, hydro, maritime power, large gas or other enduring sources.

`T3` is a national-depth capability. It should not be embedded in every rapid cell. Doing so would force every first-arrival mission to carry the mass, site, security, workforce and reset burden of the longest campaign.

The echelons may overlap. They are not handoff dates. They are different readiness obligations with different physical optima.

## 10. Command and allocation

Energy allocation in a thermal emergency is a civil command function executed through technical limits. It cannot be reduced to automatic load shedding.

The geographic civil authority defines priority among protected in-place residents, refuges, medical devices, water, vertical access, communications and transfer. The `PTS-50` technical authority determines which service can be delivered safely with the available sources, heat sinks, buildings and crews. Neither may erase the other's responsibility.

The cell must maintain a local ledger connecting:

```text
energy lot or fixed source
        ↓
conversion train and configuration
        ↓
electrical and thermal island
        ↓
building or refuge interface
        ↓
released public service
        ↓
protected people-hours and exceptions
```

This lineage serves several purposes. It detects when a common fuel or controller defeats claimed independence. It permits selective holds rather than shutting down an entire fleet. It supports handback to utilities and owners. It also makes public reporting possible without exposing household-level data.

## 11. Clean-sheet technology programs

The Department should treat the failures as a major technology portfolio. The objective is not to pick a favored source early. It is to change the mission coefficients while keeping the full service path visible.

### `ER-01`: extreme-ambient rejection array

Develop modular dry and hybrid heat-rejection trains that retain net performance at 50°C, in smoke, dust, salt and partial blockage. Score them after fan power, water use, sound, plume, filter exchange and occupied-site exposure.

### `ER-02`: civil calorimetry and heat-destination ledger

Create instrumentation and controls that reconcile useful cooling, electrical output, source waste, stored thermal energy and final heat destination in real time. A cell should not be able to report full thermal service while heat accumulates in equipment, streets or occupied air.

### `ER-03`: low-emission multi-liquid source train

Compete engines, turbines, fuel cells and reformers behind one electrical boundary. Require wide fuel tolerance, high ambient output, low sound and pollutants, independently qualified fuel lots, rapid module exchange and safe operation near residents.

### `ER-04`: gas capture and conditioning system

Develop field systems that accept a bounded range of pipeline, waste, biogenic and industrial gases while proving composition, pressure, contaminants and upstream independence. The package must degrade safely to storage and liquid bridge power.

### `ER-05`: transport-safe long-duration storage

Target delivered MWh per transported tonne and cubic metre, including enclosure, power conversion, fire separation, cooling, handling, charge state, damaged-module custody and second-mission recharge. Chemical, thermal, mechanical and hybrid stores should compete.

### `ER-06`: local-energy compiler

Build disconnected tools and field equipment that discover, characterize, contract, isolate and integrate surviving local energy. The compiler should calculate what the resource can deliver during the actual hazard and record every shared dependency.

### `ER-07`: prepared-anchor exchange boundary

Create standardized but adaptable high- and medium-voltage, chilled-water, hot-water, heat-sink and control interfaces for metropolitan anchors. The interface must support islanding, manual control, utility handback and anchor loss.

### `ER-08`: strategic firm-source attachment

Develop nuclear and non-nuclear firm sources as separate attachments with complete site, route, security, cooling, workforce and residual contracts. Compare pre-sited, maritime, rail, road and fixed-campus variants rather than forcing one mobile shape.

### `ER-09`: robotic rejection and energy works

Develop machines that place exchangers, ducts, acoustic barriers, temporary heat sinks, fuel lines, cables and protective boundaries in occupied terrain. Measure complete released service per crew-hour, not installed metres.

### `ER-10`: multi-prime module exchange

Require no-notice physical and control exchange among at least two source, storage, rejection and protection lineages. The government owns connectors, evidence formats, fault behavior and minimum control modes.

### `ER-11`: heat-safe maintenance system

Develop cooled work positions, remote inspection, collaborative handling, low-exposure filter exchange, contamination control and work-rest design for crews maintaining hot equipment in the same thermal emergency.

### `ER-12`: energy ancestry graph

Serialize fuels, feedstocks, storage modules, power electronics, control components, labs, depots, specialists and fixed sources so national command can see correlated holds. Diversity claims should recalculate whenever a module is borrowed or substituted.

## 12. Industrial organization

The industrial object is a civil utility service cell, not a catalogue of separately procured boxes.

A `PTS-5` prime must integrate:

- the common service spine;
- one or more energy-source modules;
- electrical and thermal storage;
- heat-rejection trains and their destinations;
- starting, protection and control;
- energy custody and replenishment interfaces;
- site works and occupied-environment controls;
- complete transport and setup equipment;
- shift-complete operating and maintenance labor; and
- reset and second-mission condition.

The Department should keep multiple primes alive. It should own the public interfaces and evidence rules, fund government test articles, maintain depot access to serialized components and periodically require cross-prime substitution.

The supplier base would span rotating machinery, electrochemical conversion, reactors, batteries, thermal stores, power electronics, heat exchangers, fans, pumps, filters, conductor, switchgear, controls, sensors, robotics, acoustic systems, fuel conditioning, utilities, construction, nuclear services, skilled trades and technical education.

Defense-scale demand changes what industry is willing to invent. Multi-year orders for hundreds of complete source and rejection packages would justify new production lines, high-ambient test cells, domestic component capacity, reserve tooling and professional career paths. A small emergency-management market will not create that ecosystem.

## 13. Workforce and professional identity

The revised cell packages require 58–90 deployed people each under the declared hypotheses. The mixed group assigns 812 people to its twelve cell packages and 2,852 people to the complete `PTS-50` formation.

These are not generator operators. The profession combines:

- electrical switching and protection;
- thermal process and heat-sink operation;
- fuel, gas, storage or reactor custody;
- controls and configuration evidence;
- building and utility interfaces;
- environmental and occupied-site safety;
- module exchange and field repair;
- human thermal protection; and
- civil service handback.

Licensing and qualification will differ by lineage. A microreactor attachment cannot borrow its licensed operators from another mission. A gas-conditioning team cannot be assumed to operate a liquid source. The common service spine can support shared training, but the force registry must preserve lineage-specific watches and relief depth.

## 14. Export and allied force design

The transferable product is not a fuel dependency. It is a sovereign service architecture.

Partners could acquire the common spine, selected non-nuclear source packages, prepared-anchor standards, heat-rejection systems, storage, test infrastructure, training and local control. A partner with geothermal or hydro resources might emphasize `L4` and `L5`. An island partner might combine maritime firm power with mobile building interfaces. An arid partner would value dry rejection and thermal storage more than evaporative compactness.

The Department should not require continuous foreign access to building or household records. Interoperability should attach to service status, connectors, energy and heat ledgers, safety states and lawful handoff.

Nuclear export remains a separate sovereign decision. The common interface lets a partner receive public utility service from a locally controlled firm source without importing the entire U.S. custody model.

## 16. Sensitivity results

Four live perturbations were run and restored in the workbook.

First, increasing mission duration from 168 to 240 hours raises electrical energy from 238.923 to 341.318 MWh, storage-system mass from 2,333 to 3,333 tonnes and the complete storage cell from 2,784 to 3,784 tonnes. The seven-day lineages no longer close the ten-day base, while the strategic firm source retains energy endurance.

Second, increasing delivered storage density from 0.16 to 0.24 MWh per tonne reduces the storage system to 1,555 tonnes and the complete cell to 2,006 tonnes. A major technology improvement changes the coefficient materially but does not cross the present mobile boundary.

Third, reducing the `L1` 50°C rejection factor from 0.82 to 0.75 cuts the lineage from sixteen to fourteen passing worlds because both the hot-ambient case and the compound case fail. The result shows that modest rejection uncertainty can erase apparent source superiority.

Fourth, increasing the national force from seventy-two to eighty groups raises configured organic fleet capital from $1.017 trillion to $1.130 trillion and the total program from $3.543 trillion to $3.826 trillion.

All temporary inputs were restored. Thirty-nine integrity checks pass and the formula-error scan returns zero entries.

## 18. Research consequences

### 18.1 The cell is modular in function before it is modular in shape

The research now supports a common service boundary and swappable energy/rejection packages. It does not yet support ISO containers, trailers, railcars, barges or self-propelled vehicles. Those are transport geometries that follow from module dimensions, center of gravity, heat-exchanger area, hazardous separation, setup sequence and carrier constraints.

Calling the system modular before those interfaces close would be branding, not architecture.

### 18.2 Prepared geography is the lightest energy carrier

`L5` is the fastest and among the lightest mobile competitors because electrical capacity, heat sinks and interfaces already exist at the destination. A national retrofit program can reduce mobile mass more than incremental source efficiency.

Preparedness does not eliminate the need for the force. It changes what the force must carry and how quickly it can act.

### 18.3 Strategic firm power belongs in a separate echelon

The microreactor competitor proves that endurance alone does not define response value. Its strongest role is a pre-sited or separately deployed `T3` attachment that accepts the same service interface after the rapid cells establish the district.

This preserves an ambitious nuclear technology program without forcing nuclear mass, security and siting into every first-arrival unit.

### 18.4 The next geometry question is now bounded

The next pass should convert the common spine and `L1` rapid bridge into a configuration-level deployment geometry. It must determine module count, dimensions, axle or deck load, intake and exhaust separation, cable and hose handling, fuel segregation, 7 MWth rejection area, setup work, carrier count and occupied-site arrangement.

That comparison should include road trailer, rail-compatible, shallow-draft barge, prepared-anchor and mixed transport forms. It should not begin with a preferred vehicle silhouette.

## 19. Decision boundary

This pass supports six judgments.

First, one `PTS-5` cell consumes 238.9 MWh over seven days before reserve. Storage, fuel and fixed-source availability must be treated as force structure.

Second, source conversion can nearly double the heat that must be rejected beside the occupied population. Energy and heat rejection must be designed and tested as one system.

Third, no universal energy lineage exists. Liquid, gas, storage, local resources, prepared anchors and strategic firm power occupy different timing and geography envelopes.

Fourth, every cell needs a common service spine and cell-local `T0` bridge. Longer-duration sources should attach through government-owned interfaces without becoming common runtime dependencies.

Fifth, the `PTS-50` is larger than Pass 72's placeholder. The mixed comparator reaches 12,200 dry tonnes, 2,852 deployed people and $14.122 billion of configured unit capital before complete wet and transport closure.

Sixth, a $3.543 trillion national mission program is coherent with the proposed Department's ambition. It would create living source and rejection lineages, prepared metropolitan capacity, strategic firm-power depth, proving infrastructure and a new civil utility profession.

No lineage is selected. The mixed group account is a program comparator, not an order of battle. All representative gates remain open. No vehicle geometry or rendering is authorized.
