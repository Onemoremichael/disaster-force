# Heat-blackout power and thermal service

## The `PTS-50` Group and the occupied-building utility mission

**Research status:** first-principles configuration competition; no architecture admitted

**Decision date:** 7 September 2026

**Companion model:** [`heat-blackout-power-and-thermal-service-cell.xlsx`](../../../models/force/projection/heat-blackout-power-and-thermal-service-cell.xlsx)

## Executive judgment

A seven-day urban heat blackout is not principally a generator mission. It is an occupied-building service mission in which electricity, cooling, heat rejection, domestic water pressure, elevators, medical devices, refrigeration, communications, household access and lawful movement fail as one coupled civil system.

The wrong design object is a megawatt fleet. A source can energize a temporary bus while residents remain in hot apartments, water cannot reach upper floors, elevators remain unavailable, medical devices have no protected circuit, cooling equipment cannot reject heat, and no authority can safely connect the building. The public effect exists only when the force crosses the complete path from a person and building state to a continuously delivered service.

This pass derives that path for a declared archetypal district of 50,000 people in 100 occupied buildings over 168 hours. It allocates 30,000 people to protected in-place service, 15,000 to neighborhood thermal refuges and 5,000 to selective transfer. These are transparent design hypotheses, not demographic estimates, codes or outputs from the protected New York campaign.

Under the declared coefficients, the district requires:

| Service requirement | Derived value |
|---|---:|
| Thermally protected population | 45,000 people |
| Selective transfer | 5,000 people |
| Medically power-dependent continuity | 2,000 people |
| Vertical-continuity service | 5,000 people |
| Communications and charging | 20,408 households |
| Pumped occupied-building water | 2,250 m³/day |
| Useful cooling | 19.65 MWth |
| Continuous net electricity | 14.22 MWe |
| Peak electricity | 19.20 MWe |
| Seven-day electrical energy | 2,389 MWh |
| Heat-rejection duty | 28.01 MWth |
| Building-connection work | 1,280 crew-hours |

The study compares four architectures. A facility power park and a central refuge system fail every declared service world because neither reaches the occupied population. A distributed building-interface mesh passes the base case and three bounded equipment-loss cases, but it fails energy denial, high ambient, human-interface loss, water-pressure loss, smoke, medical surge, disconnected operation, common-depot loss and the compound case. Only the upper `H4` comparator passes all sixteen deterministic service worlds and preserves the separate lawful universal stop.

`H4` is a **Power and Thermal Service Group**, or `PTS-50`. It contains ten primary and two reserve `PTS-5` cells. The group carries 24 MWe of net electrical nameplate, 36 MWth of useful thermal output, 60 MWh of electrical storage and 180 MWhth of thermal storage. It also contains seventy-two building-interface teams, thermal distribution and clean-air teams, medical micro-power teams, water-pump and vertical-service teams, neighborhood refuge nodes, disconnected communications, technical authorities, maintenance and an organic seven-day field base.

The resulting upper comparator contains 2,688 deployed people, 7,740 dry tonnes, 8,901 chargeable tonnes before wet energy and process consumables, $6.808 billion of direct equipment and $8.170 billion of configured unit capital. A national comparator of seventy-two groups, twenty-four prepared metropolitan programs, twenty-five years of operations, and a $220 billion research and industrialization portfolio totals approximately **$2.320 trillion in constant 2026 dollars**. That is the scale of treating urban thermal survival as a permanent national force rather than an improvised generator contract.

The result is not an acquisition recommendation. The upper comparator has twenty-four open representative gates. Its eight-day declared endurance is not a closed energy system because the workbook deliberately excludes wet fuel, feedstock, makeup water, filters, refrigerants and other process consumables. Its cooling coefficients are hypotheses rather than building simulations or health thresholds. No architecture has earned rendering.

The most important finding is institutional. Power restoration for occupied populations cannot remain a supporting commodity dispatched after another formation defines the mission. The Department of Resilience needs a permanent profession and formation family that owns the service chain from building reconnaissance through utility release, thermal control, human access and transfer. Its central industrial object is not a generator. It is a repeatably configurable **civil utility service cell**.

## 1. The correction to the power-force idea

The [power restoration force-engineering paper](../../systems/power/force-engineering.md) already distinguishes a deployable utility from a generator inventory. The [cellular power-and-thermal study](wrg-fr10-cellular-power-and-thermal-independence.md) then proves that three nominally independent water sources are not independent when they share power, heat rejection, controls, fuel ancestry or reset capacity. This pass carries that correction into an occupied district.

The water case could state the internal load of a bounded process configuration. The occupied-building case is harder. The force does not initially know:

- which buildings remain structurally and electrically safe;
- how many residents remain and what services they need;
- where temperature and humidity have crossed a dangerous exposure trajectory;
- which elevators, pumps, fans, chillers, controls and service conductors survive;
- whether a building should be protected in place, partly served, used as a refuge or emptied;
- what can be connected without backfeed, fire, contamination or egress failure;
- how thermal service reaches occupied rooms rather than a machine room; or
- when the owner or utility can lawfully resume control.

Those unknowns are not reconnaissance details outside the power system. They determine the system. A formation that arrives with a fixed voltage, source size, chiller package and cable inventory before compiling the occupied load may be technically impressive and operationally irrelevant.

The design sequence must therefore run backward from public effect:

```text
person and household state
        ↓
building safety and occupancy decision
        ↓
thermal, medical, water, vertical and communications service bundle
        ↓
room, refuge and building distribution
        ↓
electrical and thermal connection
        ↓
source, storage, energy, heat rejection and control
        ↓
maintenance, safe stop, transfer and restoration handback
```

Every downward step creates an engineering requirement. Every upward step creates an evidence obligation. The group may not count energy delivered at the bottom as service delivered at the top.

## 2. The mission and its boundary

### 2.1 The declared district

The model uses one intentionally simple reference object:

| Attribute | Design hypothesis |
|---|---:|
| Population | 50,000 people |
| Occupied buildings | 100 |
| Mission duration | 168 hours |
| Prepared-building share | 40% |
| In-place protection | 60% of population |
| Neighborhood refuge | 30% |
| Selective transfer | 10% |
| Average household size | 2.45 people |
| Medical-power dependence | 4% |
| Vertical-continuity demand | 10% |

Fifty thousand is a formation design scale. It is not a jurisdiction, forecast incident population or claim about New York. One hundred occupied buildings create an average of 500 people per building only for arithmetic. A real district compiler must preserve building archetype, occupancy, floor distribution, envelope, solar exposure, internal gains, ventilation, equipment, service voltage, water pressure zone, elevator group, access, ownership and utility topology.

The allocation across in-place service, refuges and transfer is a portfolio, not a moral hierarchy. Remaining in a familiar apartment may preserve medication, caregivers, mobility equipment, food, privacy and household unity. It can also become unsafe when temperature, smoke, water, fire protection, egress or clinical support fails. Moving to a refuge concentrates high-quality service but creates access, crowding, sanitation, infection, security and continuity burdens. Regional transfer can be necessary, but it consumes time and creates a receiving obligation.

The force needs all three paths because none is universally correct.

### 2.2 What the mission includes

The `PTS-50` mission begins when a geographic command assigns an occupied district and civil authority defines the thermal-survival objective. It ends only when one of three conditions is true for every governed service record:

1. the person remains in a building with released thermal and continuity service;
2. the person occupies a released neighborhood refuge with the complete service bundle; or
3. the person has been lawfully transferred and admitted to another complete service system.

The formation must establish and maintain:

- building and household state;
- electrical isolation, grounding, protection and energization authority;
- thermal production, distribution and heat rejection;
- medical-device continuity;
- domestic water pumping where potable supply exists;
- vertical-service and elevator interfaces where safe;
- communications, charging and local compute;
- food and pharmaceutical refrigeration;
- temporary distribution and physical connection;
- public information, consent, refusal, accommodation and exceptions;
- shift-complete operation, maintenance and energy custody; and
- safe stop, transfer, handback and regeneration.

The mission excludes permanent grid reconstruction, permanent building rehabilitation, household compensation and the macroeconomic value of avoided harm. Those may become Department programs, but including them here would obscure the formation question.

### 2.3 The campaign firewall

The [New York vertical heat-blackout campaign](../../proving/campaigns/new-york/README.md) has registered architectures, catchments and statistical controls while deliberately preventing premature outcome production. Its posterior preflight amendment has not yet been rerun. This pass does not borrow unpublished population joints, building states, service outcomes or architecture results from that campaign.

The separation is essential. A generic formation study can declare a 4% medical-power share to expose a capability. It cannot claim that share describes a New York catchment. The campaign may later place a probability distribution over real demand and test whether the formation succeeds. Until then, the `PTS-50` is a controlled design hypothesis.

## 3. The public-effect ledger

### 3.1 Protected people-hours

The primary output is protected people-hours inside a declared thermal and air-quality envelope:

\[
H_{safe}=\sum_{b,t} P_{b,t}\,\Delta t\,I(T_{b,t},RH_{b,t},AQ_{b,t},S_{b,t})
\]

where (P_{b,t}) is the protected population in building or refuge (b), (T) is indoor temperature, (RH) is humidity, (AQ) is air quality, (S) is the wider safety state and (I) is one only when the released condition holds.

The present workbook does not implement the indicator as a physiological threshold. It uses a simpler capacity trajectory because no building archetype, health authority or observed load has been admitted. That is a limitation, not permission to substitute chiller nameplate for effect.

The model also reports unprotected person-hours:

\[
H_{unprotected}=\sum_t \left(P_{district}-P_{protected,t}\right)\Delta t
\]

This prevents final completion from concealing dangerous delay. Under the declared ramp, `H4` reaches 95% protected service at hour 48 and accumulates approximately 1.086 million unprotected person-hours. `H3` reaches the same threshold at hour 78 and accumulates approximately 1.578 million. `H1` and `H2` never reach 95% because their operating concepts exclude too much of the district.

These values do not predict mortality or morbidity. They compare service delay under the same artificial district.

### 3.2 Conjunctive service

Thermal occupancy is only one account. A person may be inside a cool room and still lose life-sustaining equipment, water, accessible egress, medication refrigeration or communication. The architecture therefore passes a world only if every required ratio is at least one:

\[
M_{a,w}=\min_{k\in K}\left(\frac{C_{a,w,k}}{D_{w,k}}\right)
\]

The service set (K) contains:

- continuous electricity;
- useful thermal output;
- thermally reachable population;
- medical-device continuity;
- occupied-building water pumping;
- vertical continuity;
- household communications and charging;
- building-connection work;
- selective transfer;
- energy endurance; and
- common-mode independence.

No arithmetic surplus can cross accounts. Extra megawatts cannot compensate for households outside the connection plan. Excess refuge space cannot compensate for a failed water pump. A spare chiller cannot compensate for a common control credential that prevents safe start.

### 3.3 Lawful stop is not a failed service world

One condition remains outside the sixteen service worlds. If a building is structurally unsafe, electrically unisolated, fire protection is untenable or the thermal intervention would make egress worse, the correct output may be refusal to energize and a controlled transfer.

The model therefore includes a separate universal stop. An architecture does not fail because it refuses an unsafe connection. It fails if it lacks medical micro-power, household custody, communications, accessible movement and a released receiver while refusing.

`H4` receives only a design claim for this behavior. Representative evidence remains open.

## 4. Deriving the load from service

### 4.1 Thermal demand

The model assigns 0.38 kWth per person to in-place survival zones and 0.55 kWth per person to neighborhood refuges. The higher refuge coefficient represents denser occupancy, outside-air and filtration burdens. Both are hypotheses.

The resulting useful cooling is:

```text
30,000 in-place people × 0.38 kWth/person = 11.40 MWth
15,000 refuge people   × 0.55 kWth/person =  8.25 MWth
                                                   ─────
total useful cooling                              19.65 MWth
```

At a high-ambient coefficient of performance of 2.35, compressor demand is 8.36 MWe. Thermal pumps, fans and controls add an 18% auxiliary allowance, or 1.51 MWe. The service must reject approximately 28.01 MWth when the useful cooling and compressor work are combined.

Heat rejection is a first-order design driver. A formation that can generate electricity but cannot move heat to safe air, water or ground fails. High ambient temperature reduces condensing performance precisely when the load rises. Smoke and dust foul intakes and filters. Dense urban sites constrain plume, sound, exhaust, water use and exclusion. Rejecting heat beside occupied residents can transfer risk from indoors to streets and worker positions.

### 4.2 Non-thermal electricity

The model adds the following continuous equivalents:

| Load | MWe |
|---|---:|
| Medical-device continuity | 0.50 |
| Vertical service | 1.00 |
| Communications and charging | 0.31 |
| Food and pharmaceutical cold chain | 0.60 |
| Lighting, controls and life safety | 0.75 |
| Water pumping | 0.06 |

The 0.06 MWe water figure looks small because it represents hydraulic energy for 2,250 m³/day under a declared intensity. It does not include water treatment, lost pressure, damaged risers, booster zoning, hot water, wastewater lifting or fire flow. A building-specific model could make pumping much larger.

After an 8% temporary-distribution loss, the total continuous requirement is 14.22 MWe. A 1.35 peak factor yields 19.20 MWe. Seven continuous days require 2,389 MWh before counting additional outage, startup or reserve energy.

These calculations matter because they prevent category confusion:

- MWe is an electrical rate;
- MWth is useful thermal rate;
- MWh and MWhth are stored or consumed energy;
- heat-rejection capacity is neither electrical nameplate nor useful cooling;
- peak starting and ride-through requirements are not continuous energy; and
- accessible human service is not any of those quantities.

### 4.3 Connection labor

Forty of the one hundred buildings are assumed to have maintained prepared interfaces requiring two crew-hours each. Sixty require field adaptation at twenty crew-hours each. Total connection work is therefore 1,280 crew-hours.

At sixteen productive hours per team-day, 70% availability and a two-day deadline, the formation needs fifty-eight simultaneously governed connection teams. `H4` carries seventy-two. Losing the equivalent of one company leaves a narrow 1.046 ratio after the scenario's reach and availability reductions.

This is one of the model's most consequential results. The group is not sized by generation. It is sized partly by how many legally and technically distinct occupied buildings it must inspect, adapt, connect, test and revisit within a public deadline.

Prepared interfaces change the force more than marginal generator efficiency. Raising the prepared share from 40% to 60% reduces modeled connection work from 1,280 to 920 crew-hours and the nominal team requirement from fifty-eight to forty-two. A national retrofit and inspection program is therefore operational capacity stored in place.

## 5. Four rival architectures

### 5.1 `H1`: facility power park

`H1` concentrates four large source positions around named facilities. It carries 20 MWe but only 12 MWth, reaches 22,500 thermally protected people, and maintains limited household, water, vertical and communications capacity.

This is the conventional megawatt solution. It may restore hospitals, treatment plants, telecom hubs or selected shelters. Those are essential effects, but the architecture does not own the district. It passes none of the sixteen worlds because its service floor is incomplete even before loss.

The failure is not insufficient electrical nameplate. `H1` carries more electricity than the declared continuous requirement. It fails because electricity has not been converted into population reach.

### 5.2 `H2`: central refuge campuses

`H2` creates a small number of high-service thermal campuses. Its 22 MWth exceeds the district's modeled useful cooling requirement, but it reaches only 18,000 people. It assumes residents can leave hot buildings, cross the neighborhood, enter the sites and remain there.

Central refuges can be technically excellent and strategically necessary. They are not a district architecture when used alone. Concentration also creates common feeder, intake, sanitation, crowding, infection, transport and management failures. `H2` passes none of the sixteen worlds under the full service contract.

### 5.3 `H3`: building-interface mesh

`H3` distributes ten service cells and sixty building-interface teams. It carries 20 MWe, 26 MWth and enough nominal capacity for 45,000 thermally protected people. It is the first architecture to close the base district.

It passes four worlds: base operation, largest source-module loss, largest thermal-train loss and hottest-night coincident load. Its failures reveal the difference between distributed equipment and independent service. It retains shared energy, communications, connection, water and reset domains. Losing those domains defeats several nominal cells together.

`H3` is the serious lower comparator. Its 2,150 deployed people, 6,500 dry tonnes and $5.6 billion direct equipment account demonstrate that a distributed urban utility is already a large formation before full independence.

### 5.4 `H4`: layered `PTS-50` group

`H4` combines twelve `PTS-5` cells with three service paths:

1. protect suitable occupied buildings in place;
2. create neighborhood thermal and clean-air refuges; and
3. move the bounded population that cannot safely remain.

The group adds medical micro-power, water and vertical teams, disconnected communications, independent control and energy domains, building-interface depth, technical authorities, maintenance, field basing and a second-mission reserve. It passes all sixteen deterministic worlds and the lawful universal stop.

That result makes `H4` an upper proving comparator. It does not select the architecture. Deterministic margins built from assumptions establish what evidence would matter; they do not provide that evidence.

## 6. The `PTS-5` cell

### 6.1 A service cell, not a machine

`PTS-5` denotes a nominal 5,000-person service responsibility inside the primary ten-cell district lattice. It is not a promise that one cell always serves exactly 5,000 residents. Building geometry may require two cells at one high-rise cluster and a partial cell elsewhere. The number creates an accountable planning grain and limits single-cell consequence.

Each cell is provisionally assigned:

| Capability | Per-cell planning value |
|---|---:|
| Net electricity | 2 MWe |
| Useful thermal output | 3 MWth |
| Electrical storage | 5 MWh |
| Thermal storage | 15 MWhth |
| Operating detachment | 54 people |
| Dry mass | 260 t |
| Direct equipment cost | $180M |

The cell includes more than source and chiller modules. It needs:

- grid-forming source trains and dark start;
- electrical and thermal storage with independent isolation;
- transformation, conversion, protection and grounding;
- pumps, heat exchangers, air handlers and filtration;
- ambient-rated heat rejection;
- cable, hose, duct and connection handling;
- local control, sensing, metering and evidence custody;
- energy and process-material interfaces;
- maintenance access, spares and module exchange;
- worker habitability and safety; and
- manual stop and bounded degraded operation.

The cell must remain locally safe without group connectivity. Group headquarters can allocate, compare and coordinate cells. It cannot be the only location that knows protection settings, authority state, medical load, safe-stop conditions or current energy.

### 6.2 Why there are twelve

Ten primary cells supply 20 MWe and 30 MWth, already above the declared continuous electrical and thermal requirements. Two reserve cells raise the installed group capacity to 24 MWe and 36 MWth.

The reserve is not an undifferentiated spare park. It absorbs:

- one complete cell loss;
- high-ambient derating;
- coincident nighttime load;
- building clusters that require nonuniform assignment;
- maintenance and filter exchange;
- medical, water or vertical-service surges; and
- the beginning of a second mission before full reset.

Reserve accounting must preserve location, crew, energy, configuration and transport. Twelve parked cell shells with ten qualified watches are not twelve service cells.

### 6.3 Optional exchange

Cells should be able to exchange electrical or thermal service through fault-blocking, permissioned ties. Exchange is useful during load migration or repair. It must not create a permanent common bus whose fault, controller or switching error defeats the lattice.

The normal state is bounded local authority. The optional state is controlled exchange. The emergency state is rapid separation with critical loads retained. The evidence program must test all three.

| Building-interface teams | 72 | 360 | 180 t |

| Command, civil authority and technical release | 1 | 180 | 180 t |
| Maintenance and regeneration group | 1 | 280 | 600 t |

The 15% handling and configuration factor raises chargeable mass to 8,901 tonnes. That still excludes wet energy and process consumables.

The cell fleet is only 40% of dry mass. Corridors, adapters, refuges, maintenance, base, spares and human-service systems occupy the rest. This is exactly what a complete service rule should reveal. Hardware that produces energy is not the majority of the deployable system once the force must reach people, operate for a week and regenerate.

### 7.2 The operating groups

The formation needs five interacting command relationships rather than one monolithic chain.

The **civil effect command** owns the district service objective, priority, public communication, exceptions and transfer decision. It cannot waive electrical, building, clinical or fire safety.

The **technical utility authority** owns isolation, grounding, protection, energization, thermal process release and safe handback. It cannot choose which households deserve priority.

The **building and household command** binds actual people to actual buildings, service states, medical needs, accessibility, consent and follow-up. It cannot certify electrical plant.

The **cell operating commands** own local source, storage, heat, distribution, maintenance and safe stop. They must operate disconnected.

The **independent evidence authority** owns trial configuration, challenge injection, instrumentation, custody and admission. The program office does not certify itself.

Operational unity comes from a common service record and declared authority boundaries, not from giving one commander unlimited technical discretion.

### 7.3 Relationship to the Household Collection Group

The [Household Collection Group](../generation/household-collection-group-formation-design.md) keeps household contact, vertical continuity and local handoff organic while treating power and water as external technical contracts. `PTS-50` is one such supporting formation, but the relationship is not a supplier ticket.

The two formations share a service ledger:

- `HCG` identifies household and building need, access state and lawful disposition;
- `PTS` measures and releases the service configuration;
- `HCG` verifies that service reaches the household or executes movement;
- `PTS` maintains the building and utility state; and
- both retain the unresolved record until transfer or handback.

If the utility formation reports 14 MWe delivered while the household formation reports 8,000 unreachable households, the mission is not partly successful in two separate databases. It is one failed service chain.

## 8. Prepared buildings as force structure

The Department should treat building interfaces as an installed reserve component. A prepared building would carry a maintained, inspected and digitally governed set of features such as:

- external or protected electrical connection points;
- isolation, grounding and load-shedding boundaries;
- essential-load panels separated from ordinary tenant circuits;
- elevator and domestic-water pump connection paths;
- refuge-zone air and fluid connection points;
- temporary duct, hose and cable routes that preserve egress and fire separation;
- indoor environmental and equipment-state sensing;
- accessible refuge and transfer areas;
- owner, utility and fire-authority technical records; and
- periodic live connection and safe-stop tests.

This is not simply a standardized plug. The useful interface includes documentation, maintenance, training, authority and a known occupied-service topology.

The industrial implication is large. Twenty-four metropolitan programs in the national comparator receive $216 billion across the horizon for interface construction and renewal. That would create a permanent civil-utility retrofit market involving electrical equipment, building controls, pumps, elevators, fire protection, mechanical systems, data standards, inspection and skilled trades.

The prepared lattice also changes mobility. A service cell that can connect to a known boundary can protect people before the rest of the mobile formation arrives. A cell facing an undocumented, damaged switchboard becomes a construction project. Pre-disaster capital therefore substitutes for response mass and time, but only when maintenance and release evidence are real.

## 9. Layered thermal protection

### 9.1 In-place survival zones

The objective is not whole-building comfort. The initial mission can create bounded survival zones: selected floors, community rooms, lobbies or apartments with controlled temperature, humidity, filtration, water, charging, refrigeration and accessible circulation.

Concentrating thermal service reduces load, conductor and duct burden. It also requires household support. Residents may need assistance reaching the zone, transporting medication or equipment, caring for children, maintaining pets, sleeping with privacy and returning periodically to apartments. The operating design must address those functions rather than counting room area.

### 9.2 Neighborhood refuges

Refuges provide higher service density and can accept residents from buildings that cannot be economically cooled. The `H4` comparator carries twenty node sets. Their functions include:

- high-quality thermal and air control;
- medical observation and device continuity;
- potable water and sanitation;
- food and medication refrigeration;
- accessible sleeping and circulation;
- communications and charging;
- registration, household unity and information; and
- transfer staging when the refuge cannot close a need.

A refuge is part of the same utility lattice, not a separate shelter system. It should be able to receive thermal or electrical service from more than one cell and isolate without cascading failure.

### 9.3 Medical micro-power

Some clinical loads should not wait for a building connection. Forty four-person teams carry a micro-power and continuity mission for declared dependent residents. Their equipment could include isolated battery modules, device-specific adapters, oxygen support interfaces, refrigeration, monitoring, charging and record custody.

The micro-power layer does not replace safe building service. It creates a bridge during reconnaissance, connection, fault isolation and transfer. It also prevents a utility decision from becoming an immediate clinical cliff.

### 9.4 Selective transfer

The group carries a modeled interface capacity of 1,500 people per day, but long-distance carriers and receiving complexes remain external. Transfer credit requires:

- household contact and consent or other lawful authority;
- medical and accessibility regulation;
- vertical and local collection;
- protected service while waiting;
- transport allocation;
- named receiver acceptance; and
- complete handoff.

The PTS group owns the utility-side remain-or-move evidence. It should not become a general transport formation.

## 10. Energy endurance remains open

The upper comparator declares eight days of energy endurance so that a seven-day mission retains some margin. The workbook tests that claim and shows how service fails when it is reduced. It does not close the physical energy burden.

At 14.22 MWe average, seven days require 2,389 MWh. Sixty MWh of electrical storage covers only about 4.2 hours of average district load if used alone. One hundred eighty MWhth of thermal storage covers about 9.2 hours of useful cooling at the declared load. Storage can provide dark start, ride-through, peak management and controlled shedding. It is not seven-day primary energy.

A mature `PTS-5` family needs rival energy configurations:

1. liquid-fuel dispatchable cells with protected regional custody;
2. gaseous or locally conditioned fuel cells with separate supply ancestry;
3. grid-forming storage paired with surviving local generation;
4. thermal-first cells using large cold stores and smaller electric sources;
5. long-endurance firm cells using microreactor, geothermal or other strategic sources where justified; and
6. prepared metropolitan anchors that exchange electricity, heat or cooling with mobile cells.

Each rival must disclose conversion efficiency, gross and net output, wet mass, volume, safety separation, exhaust, cooling, refueling time, supplier ancestry, maintenance, crew, residuals and reset.

The Department should resist an early universal source choice. Urban heat, winter cold, island operation, smoke, flood and overseas partner missions may select different source families behind the same public interface. Commonity should attach to service, protection, connectors, records and training where possible. Energy diversity should remain real where common sourcing would recreate a strategic cut.

## 11. Degraded-service results

The sixteen worlds are deterministic forcing cases. They are not probabilities and should not be averaged.

| World | Stress | `H4` minimum ratio |
|---|---|---:|
| `W0` | base seven-day district | 1.111 |
| `W1` | largest source module unavailable | 1.111 |
| `W2` | largest thermal train unavailable | 1.111 |
| `W3` | high ambient with generation derating | 1.111 |
| `W4` | hottest-night coincident load | 1.111 |
| `W5` | external energy corridor denied | 1.000 |
| `W6` | one building-interface company unavailable | 1.046 |
| `W7` | domestic water pressure lost | 1.067 |
| `W8` | smoke and filtration co-event | 1.111 |
| `W9` | medical-device demand surge | 1.038 |
| `W10` | one refuge cluster unavailable | 1.056 |
| `W11` | commercial network and cloud denied | 1.020 |
| `W12` | vertical-service demand surge | 1.037 |
| `W13` | one complete service cell unavailable | 1.100 |
| `W14` | common depot unavailable during second mission | 1.100 |
| `W15` | cell loss plus high-ambient compound | 1.046 |

The limiting worlds are not dominated by electricity. The narrowest continuous margins are disconnected communications, vertical service, medical continuity and building-interface capacity. The energy-denial world is exactly at one because the endurance claim is only one day above the mission.

This is a healthy design signal. The force should not respond by adding another generic generator. It should test whether the human and institutional coefficients are achievable, then redesign connection teams, medical bridges, vertical systems, offline records and energy depth.

The 55,000-person sensitivity is similarly informative. Without changing the formation, the model passes only nine worlds. A lower coefficient of performance of 2.0 raises continuous load from 14.22 to 16.10 MWe and peak load to 21.73 MWe, but `H4` still passes all declared worlds because its tighter constraints remain elsewhere. The result should not be interpreted as thermal physics being unimportant; it shows that the selected nameplate has more electrical margin than human-service margin.

## 12. Clean-sheet technology programs

The formation creates a civil technology portfolio comparable in method—not hardware—to a major defense mission area.

### 12.1 `PT-01`: extreme-climate thermal machine

Develop modular chillers, heat pumps, heat exchangers, fans, pumps and rejection systems that retain net output in high ambient temperature, smoke, dust, salt, icing and partial blockage. The competition should score useful thermal service after auxiliaries, water use, filtration, sound, plume, maintenance and worker exposure.

### 12.2 `PT-02`: occupied-building interface system

Develop standardized electrical, fluid, air, sensing and authority boundaries for prepared and unprepared buildings. Include safe isolation, adaptable transformation, protected temporary routes, backfeed prevention, fire and egress compatibility, rapid testing and tamper-evident configuration records.

The system is partly installed infrastructure and partly deployable construction equipment. It should be procured as one governed interface ecology.

### 12.3 `PT-03`: robotic utility corridor

Develop machines that survey, pull, elevate, bridge, trench, guard, connect and patrol temporary cable, hose and duct through dense civil terrain. Success is released service per crew-hour under occupation and access constraints. Metres laid without a safe receiver do not count.

### 12.4 `PT-04`: thermal storage and distribution

Compete cold-water, ice, phase-change, reversible heat and other storage pathways against electrical storage for actual protected people-hours. Include charging conditions, insulation, pump energy, leakage, freeze risk, transport, modular repair and end-of-life recovery.

### 12.5 `PT-05`: medical continuity bridge

Create a medically governed family of isolated micro-power, device adapters, refrigeration, monitoring and transfer modules. It must operate without cloud service, preserve patient-device identity without permanent surveillance, and hand off cleanly to building or receiving power.

### 12.6 `PT-06`: vertical continuity system

Develop temporary motor-drive, elevator-interface, powered-stair, hoist, pump and refuge systems that preserve accessibility without compromising egress or fire operations. The unit of performance is a safe completed service cycle under heat and darkness, not device lifting capacity.

### 12.7 `PT-07`: disconnected civil utility operating system

The group needs local building, household, load, configuration, authority, maintenance and energy records that function without commercial networks or cloud identity. It must reject unsafe commands, reconcile later and reveal unresolved conflicts.

This is not a smart-city surveillance platform. Data minimization, local custody, expiry, consent, purpose limitation and independent audit are architectural requirements.

### 12.8 `PT-08`: multi-vector endurance

Compete rival dispatchable and firm energy lineages at cell scale. Require transparent supplier ancestry and no-notice substitution. A nominal dual-fuel machine receives no independence credit when both fuels share the same depot, precursor, transport corridor, technician or control component.

### 12.9 `PT-09`: heat-safe human work

Connection, maintenance, household and refuge labor occurs in the same hostile environment as the public. Develop cooling wear, work-rest sensing, low-mass lifting, remote inspection, collaborative robotics, safe tooling, training simulation and shift design as a performance program.

Automation earns credit only when it reduces complete crew-hours without moving risk to residents or creating a brittle remote dependency.

### 12.10 `PT-10`: prepared metropolitan utility lattice

Fund repeatable design, construction, inspection and live testing of building interfaces, thermal refuges, neighborhood nodes, energy anchors and utility transfer boundaries. The program must preserve local variation while controlling the national configuration space.

Prepared geography is not an ancillary grant program. It is one half of the weapon-system analogue: the fixed receiver architecture that makes mobile force useful.

## 13. Industrial organization

### 13.1 Prime contractors integrate service cells

A `PTS-5` prime should deliver a configuration that closes source, storage, thermal production, heat rejection, protection, distribution, control, energy, maintenance, crew and reset. The government should not contract separately for high-performing boxes and assume integration will appear during catastrophe.

Prime performance should be measured against:

- protected people-hours under the locked building configuration;
- setup and connection time;
- net electric and thermal output after auxiliaries;
- high-ambient and smoke derating;
- largest-module and whole-cell loss;
- energy and process-material endurance;
- crew-hours, heat exposure and maintenance;
- deployable mass and transport geometry;
- independent safe stop and manual operation;
- configuration and evidence completeness; and
- second-mission readiness.

### 13.2 Government-owned interfaces

The Department should own the reference boundaries for voltage, grounding, protection evidence, connectors, chilled-fluid or air exchange, building state, authority tokens, load classes, device handoff and configuration records. Vendors should innovate behind those interfaces.

Open government interfaces do not imply one implementation. The force needs multiple commonity for training and substitution and diversity against correlated design failure. At least two living source, thermal, protection and control lineages should periodically exchange modules in no-notice trials.

### 13.3 The supplier base

The program would create sustained demand across:

- rotating machines, fuel cells and power electronics;
- batteries and thermal-storage materials;
- compressors, refrigerants, pumps, fans and heat exchangers;
- switchgear, transformers, conductor and connectors;
- filters, duct, hose and temporary structures;
- elevators, motor drives and water systems;
- robotics, sensors and offline compute;
- accessible mobility and medical devices;
- construction, inspection and skilled trades; and
- depots, proving grounds and technical education.

This is the sought-after parallel industrial economy in concrete form. It is not created by declaring resilience a priority. It is created by multi-decade orders, government interfaces, test infrastructure, maintained production lines, reserve tooling, trained labor and periodic surge exercises.

### 13.4 Export object

The export is a sovereign service capability rather than a remotely controlled generator fleet. Partners could acquire:

- complete `PTS-5` cells and selected mission modules;
- prepared-building interface standards and retrofit packages;
- metropolitan test and maintenance programs;
- training, qualification and technical orders;
- locally governed offline controls;
- independent source and thermal lineages; and
- multinational exchange and separation standards.

The receiving government retains civil priority, utility, building, health and employment authority. Interoperability should not require permanent foreign contractor access to household or infrastructure data.

It also demonstrates why national force design must later reconcile overlapping power, communications, medical, mobility and field-base requirements across formations. The Department should not buy a separate complete power lattice inside every mission group if a governed shared architecture can meet simultaneous claims without common-mode failure.

## 15. Workforce and professional identity

The formation creates a profession that does not exist as a coherent national force: **occupied civil utility operations**.

Its members combine utility engineering, building systems, thermal processes, electrical safety, clinical continuity, accessibility, household service, field construction, control systems, maintenance and civil authority. It should not be subordinated to a generator branch or reduced to a temporary incident-management function.

The 2,688-person deployed account is shift-complete. It is not the number simultaneously working. Home-station generation will require additional instructors, maintainers, depot staff, test personnel, planners, medical support, trainees, replacements and reserve depth.

Personnel design should distinguish:

- permanent technical authority and high-currency operators;
- regional reserve building and utility trades;
- locally affiliated building owners, superintendents and inspectors;
- industrial field-service and manufacturing reserves;
- health and accessibility professionals;
- civil-rights, privacy and public-service officers; and
- independent test and safety personnel.

An electrician, HVAC technician or building engineer in the economy is not a ready billet. The Department must govern identity, qualification, current training, employer release, equipment assignment, team membership, availability conflicts, replacement and authority.

## 16. Command and rights

### 16.1 Civil priority

When supply is insufficient, load shedding becomes public doctrine. The group needs a signed load registry that maps circuits and thermal zones to consequences:

- no-break medical and life-safety loads;
- survival thermal loads;
- water, vertical and communications continuity;
- food and pharmaceutical cold chain;
- stabilization loads; and
- deferrable recovery loads.

The order cannot be generated only from electrical convenience. Disconnecting an awkward low-voltage building may be easy electrically and catastrophic socially. Priority must combine technical state, population exposure, medical dependence, accessibility, alternatives and time.

### 16.2 Consent and entry

Occupied-building work implicates property, privacy, bodily autonomy, disability, family unity and unequal exposure. The formation needs a lawful basis for inspection and emergency work, but authority should not erase consent or procedural safeguards where time permits.

The household record should capture:

- service offered and actual condition;
- contact, language and accessibility;
- consent, refusal or other authority;
- medical and equipment dependencies;
- caregiver and household relationships;
- temporary movement and receiver;
- unresolved exceptions; and
- expiry and reconciliation state.

The same record must not become a permanent national vulnerability registry. Purpose limitation and deletion are operational controls.

### 16.3 Utility and owner handback

The Department does not create an unrecorded parallel grid. Every temporary service boundary needs an owner, protection state, configuration record and transfer condition. Withdrawal should be staged so that removal of a cell does not collapse the restored building.

Handback requires joint inspection, test, documentation, operator training where needed, removal of temporary hazards and a plan for unresolved occupants. A restored utility feeder does not automatically mean the building can absorb full load safely.

## 17. Representative evidence contract

Twenty-four configuration-locked gates govern movement beyond the model:

1. occupied-building thermal load measurement across declared archetypes;
2. high-ambient net cooling and heat rejection at full auxiliary load;
3. cold black start with actual pumps, compressors, elevators and controls;
4. largest source-module loss during hottest-night peak;
5. largest thermal-train loss with smoke filtration active;
6. wet-ground connection, backfeed, grounding and step-touch challenge;
7. prepared-building connection through owner and utility release;
8. field-adapted connection within the crew-hour ceiling;
9. temporary chilled-air or fluid distribution through occupied space;
10. medical micro-power continuity during cell isolation;
11. domestic water-pump restart and contamination-safe service;
12. elevator or vertical-service operation with fire and egress controls;
13. disconnected household, building and load registry reconciliation;
14. commercial network and cloud denial for seventy-two hours;
15. external energy-delivery denial for seven days;
16. energy-lot contamination or supplier-lineage hold;
17. smoke, plume, noise and heat exposure at occupied receivers;
18. storage fire isolation with adjacent cells operating;
19. one full `PTS-5` cell removed during compound heat;
20. one building-interface company unavailable at mission start;
21. lawful building hold and assisted transfer with household custody;
22. seven-day integrated district mission with blinded failures;
23. independent reset after common-depot denial; and
24. immediate second mission with unrecovered assets and crew.

The test article is the complete service configuration. A prime may not change its source, refrigerant, filter, cable, controller, technician, building interface or energy ancestry after learning the challenge.

Trials should publish:

- protected and unprotected people-hours;
- indoor temperature, humidity and air-quality trajectories;
- medical-device interruptions and transfer events;
- water, vertical, communications and refrigeration service;
- net electrical and thermal output after auxiliaries;
- heat-rejection performance and occupied exposure;
- every unsafe command, refusal, load shed and stop;
- connection, setup, repair and module-exchange time;
- energy and process-material ancestry and inventory;
- crew work, fatigue, heat exposure and injury;
- building, household and authority record completeness;
- configuration borrowing and common dependencies; and
- state at the second mission.

All gates remain open. Strict admission is zero.

## 18. Research consequences

### 18.1 Power becomes a joint public service

The Department should retain a deep Infrastructure Restoration Service capable of generating power professionals and equipment. But an occupied heat-blackout formation cannot be organized only inside a utility branch. It must bind Community Stabilization, Health Protection, Civil Mobility and Continuity, Land Engineering, communications and sustainment to the same district effect.

`PTS-50` is therefore a joint force package generated from several services and held to one service ledger.

### 18.2 The force needs scales below `PRG-100`

The existing `PRG-100` concept governs up to 100 MW of critical power. `PTS-50` needs only 24 MWe of installed nameplate, but it carries far more household, building and thermal work per megawatt. This suggests at least three power-force echelons:

- facility and immediate bridge cells below 5 MW;
- occupied-district power-and-thermal groups in the 10–30 MW class; and
- regional grid-restoration groups around and above 100 MW.

They should share interfaces, training and selected modules without being collapsed into one unit.

### 18.3 A thermal force has winter and smoke variants

The present mission is heat blackout. The formation family must later compete variants for cold, smoke, high humidity, infectious-air isolation and compound water failure. Reversible heat pumps, boilers, waste heat, insulation, ventilation and clean-air equipment will change the cell.

A universal thermal cell may be too heavy. A common service spine with climate-specific mission modules may perform better. That is a future competition, not a reason to keep the present concept generic.

### 18.4 The wet burden is the next physical stop

Pass 71 found that power-and-thermal independence can dominate a water formation. This pass finds the reverse dependency: occupied thermal service consumes 2,389 MWh in one week before its energy path is specified.

The next technology pass should compare complete energy-and-heat-rejection lineages for one `PTS-5` cell. It should force liquid, gaseous, storage-heavy, local-resource, prepared-anchor and strategic firm-source competitors to close wet mass, volume, safety, supplier ancestry, refueling, maintenance and reset under the same 5,000-person service contract.

That competition should not choose a vehicle shape. It should first determine whether the cell is fundamentally:

- a containerized road formation;
- a trailer and skid system;
- a rail-compatible utility package;
- a barge or littoral-fed urban system;
- a prepared-building anchor with mobile auxiliaries; or
- a family that requires several of those transport geometries.

Only after energy, heat rejection, distribution, connection, crew work and maintenance close should the research begin a rendering brief.

## 19. Decision boundary

This pass supports five judgments.

First, the Department needs an occupied-building power-and-thermal formation family. Existing generator contracting and facility restoration do not close the civil service chain.

Second, the correct unit is a small service cell combined into a district group. The cell limits failure consequence and aligns technical accountability with actual buildings and populations.

Third, preparedness is force structure. Maintained building interfaces and neighborhood refuges reduce response labor, mass and delay.

Fourth, the largest unresolved risk is not electrical nameplate. It is the combination of wet energy, building connection, medical continuity, vertical access, disconnected records and human work under heat.

Fifth, defense-scale ambition is technically meaningful here. A $2.320 trillion national program could create a permanent urban thermal-survival force, a prepared metropolitan lattice, several living industrial lineages and a large skilled labor system. The figure is not offered as a final budget. It demonstrates the size of the institution implied by the public guarantee.

No architecture is selected. `H4` advances only as the upper comparator for representative engineering, energy-lineage competition and force integration. Rendering remains blocked.
