# `DCP-2` physical configuration

## A force becomes real when every position has a carrier, every carrier has reserve, and every path can be released without borrowing from the other

**Research status:** first-principles requirements configuration; behavior and cost hypotheses only; no carrier architecture, vehicle program or rendering admitted

**Decision date:** 7 September 2026

**Companion model:** [`tafg1-dcp2-physical-configuration.xlsx`](../../../models/force/projection/tafg1-dcp2-physical-configuration.xlsx)

## Executive judgment

The `DCP-2` dual-carriage detachment remains the least declared formation able to preserve a complete source-independent public-service effect in an unprepared second region after losing one entire access mode. But its Pass 81 carrier labels do not survive physical configuration. Assigning all thirty-two dry service positions and four wet launch packs to specific vehicles leaves only five tonnes of spare payload per complete cell—0.8 percent—under the former `HBL-320` and `ATUT-160` ratings. That is not design reserve. It is arithmetic coincidence.

This pass replaces those labels with requirements envelopes. Each 635-tonne cell contains 580 dry tonnes and four separately governed 13.75-tonne wet packs. Its thirty-two dry positions are divided into four exact 145-tonne load blocks. Atmospheric cell `A` loads two blocks and two wet packs on each of two clean-sheet `HBL-340` carriers, producing 317.5 tonnes per vehicle. Terrain cell `B` loads one block and one wet pack on each of four clean-sheet `ATUT-170` carriers, producing 158.75 tonnes per vehicle. The resulting six-carrier system installs 1,360 tonnes of payload capacity to field 1,270 tonnes of service payload, a rounded ninety-tonne allowance.

The ratings are payload requirements, not gross vehicle weights and not vehicle designs. Five-percent mass reserve and three-percent stowed-volume and load-deck reserve are declared research controls intended to expose fragile packing. They do not derive from a certified rule. Under those controls all six carrier manifests close. Mass utilization is 93.4 percent. Maximum volume utilization is 96.7 percent, on `HBL-A1`; maximum deck utilization is 95.7 percent, on `ATUT-B4`. Calculated payload centers of gravity remain inside declared envelopes. These are spreadsheet balances awaiting measured articles, dynamic load cases and representative transfer trials.

The four-block grammar is nested logistics, not fourfold service redundancy. A block is an independently handled loading unit; it is not an independently useful public-service cell. Loss of either liftship makes atmospheric cell `A` incomplete. Loss of any terrain carrier makes terrain cell `B` incomplete. `DCP-2` preserves the public effect because the other mode retains one complete cell—not because either mode contains vehicle-level `N+1` capacity. This distinction prevents a load plan from being mistaken for an assurance result.

The two cells remain institutionally separate. Cell `A` uses interface implementation `I-A`, an air arsenal and buoyant-lift base, an air energy lineage, air crews and air technical release. Cell `B` uses `I-B`, a terrain arsenal and recovery base, a separate energy lineage, terrain crews and terrain technical release. Their paired origin nodes are declared at least 160 kilometres apart. At the destination they use separate air and terrain entry zones, separate release teams and separate interface implementations before joining a shared service yard. One standard defines the public functions; it does not authorize one custody chain, one loader, one base or one certificate.

The deterministic second-region clock still closes. Region `B` activates at absolute hour 6 and receives service by absolute hour 48 through the atmospheric path or hour 51 through the terrain path, against an absolute hour-54 deadline. The formation holds the Pass 81 workforce boundary of 486 deployed and 1,065 affiliated people. Fifteen representative assignments remain at or below a twelve-hour duty limit. Eight reset workstreams complete inside 168 hours; the longest is the 132-hour terrain-vehicle path, leaving thirty-six hours. These are designed sequences without measured delay distributions.

Physical detail materially changes the economy. A ready `DCP-2` package now carries a declared $14.85 billion lifecycle account: $8.10 billion for the `HBL-340` pair and $6.75 billion for the four-vehicle `ATUT-170` set. Across forty-three ready packages, lifecycle, separated origin nodes and operations, configuration research, receiver construction, and integration and test produce a $933.2764 billion reinforcement program. Added to the dynamic `F1-C1` base, the national boundary is $8.31641795 trillion. The configuration correction is $162.6431 billion above Pass 81 and consumes 84.3 percent of the former $192.8631 billion modeled advantage over full duplication.

The remaining $30.22 billion gap is quarantined. Pass 81 full duplication has not been configured position by position to the same depth, so it is not a like-depth cost control. The important economic result is not that `DCP-2` is still cheaper. It is that almost the entire apparent saving was vulnerable to omitted physical reserve, base separation, receiver construction and representative integration. Physical configuration is therefore part of strategy, not a downstream engineering detail.

All sixty evidence gates remain open. Current and strict readiness credit are zero. Rendering remains blocked because external form still depends on unmeasured structure, tare mass, buoyancy, propulsion, energy, mooring, weather, ground pressure, articulation, obstacle negotiation, recovery, restraint, unload and receiver behavior. The next research boundary is a carrier-architecture competition: first the `HBL-340`, whose structural and atmospheric uncertainty is greatest, then the `ATUT-170`. Only those passes can establish exterior geometry worth drawing.

## 1. What this pass decides

Pass 81 answered a formation question: what is the least declared second-region reinforcement that retains a complete service effect after one vehicle loss and the loss of an entire access physics? It established a 1,270-tonne lower bound inside the current service-cell grammar and selected `DCP-2` as the universal research reference.

That result did not answer whether its payload could be packed, handled, balanced, dispatched or received. The carrier ratings were division results: half a cell on each of two liftships and one quarter on each of four terrain vehicles. Division is not configuration. Real positions differ in mass, cube, footprint, height, hazard, unloading order, connection sequence and custody. A manifest can fit by total mass and still be physically impossible because one vehicle runs out of deck, volume, permissible center of gravity or accessible unloading paths.

This pass therefore asks:

> Can two independently governed 635-tonne service cells be serialized into physical positions, assigned to six carriers with declared reserve and balance, dispatched through separate bases and authorities, assembled at separate receiver zones, placed in public service by absolute hour 54, and reset inside 168 hours without borrowing a decisive resource from the other mode?

The answer is **yes under the declared behavior model, but only after superseding the earlier carrier ratings**. The answer is not an admission of either carrier family. Every physical value beyond the inherited service-cell register remains a design hypothesis.

## 2. The prior ratings fail the reserve test

One complete cell weighs 635 tonnes at dispatch. Under the Pass 81 arrangement:

```text
two HBL-320 carriers × 320 t = 640 t
four ATUT-160 carriers × 160 t = 640 t
```

Each mode therefore had five tonnes of nominal unused capacity:

```text
(640 - 635) / 635 = 0.787 percent
```

That margin would need to absorb manufacturing growth, tie-down and restraint hardware, protective packaging, connector changes, contamination barriers, configuration corrections and any misestimate in the thirty-six loaded records. A design whose public guarantee disappears after 0.8-percent growth is not a stable force requirement.

This pass imposes two deliberately simple controls:

- payload rating must exceed calculated payload by at least five percent when reserve is measured as capacity divided by load minus one;
- usable stowed volume and usable load-deck area must each exceed the assigned manifest by at least three percent.

These controls are not represented as engineering standards. They are research tripwires. Their purpose is to prevent exact-fit arithmetic from hardening into a program baseline before physical data exists.

The minimum payload ratings are 333.375 tonnes per atmospheric carrier and 166.6875 tonnes per terrain carrier. Rounded program requirements become `HBL-340` and `ATUT-170`. The rounding has institutional value: it gives the design authority a clear payload class while retaining a small increment above the declared reserve floor. The labels describe usable payload capacity. Tare mass, gross vehicle weight, fuel, crew, installed mission systems and structural reserve will be added only by the carrier-architecture competitions.

## 3. The service cell becomes a position system

The dry service cell is now a thirty-two-position standard organized into sixteen functional packages. Each position has a serial identity, function and subfunction, dry mass, stowed cube, deck demand, height, hazard class, unload wave, external standard and declared functional redundancy.

The positions retain the full source-to-service chain:

| distributed black start | 3 | three isolated storage partitions |
| useful cooling | 3 | three useful-cooling trains |
| final heat rejection | 3 | separated external sink capacity |
| water treatment and quality | 3 | two production trains plus release laboratory |
| wastewater and residual custody | 2 | isolated processing and custody trains |
| potable and fire-water storage | 2 | segmented storage banks |
| medium-voltage distribution | 2 | separate protection lineages |
| water and thermal distribution | 2 | separate product paths |
| building interfaces | 2 | ten-building connection sets |
| controls and communications | 1 | offline-capable control core |
| survey, robotics and civil works | 1 | air and ground survey tools |

The register totals 2,630 cubic metres of stowed dry volume and 614 square metres of deck demand. Four wet packs add fifty-five tonnes, sixty cubic metres and twenty square metres. Wet material is kept outside the dry position count because its custody, compatibility, replenishment and leakage controls differ from machinery and sealed dry stocks.

The standard is intentionally functional. It specifies what must arrive and connect without pretending that all future implementations share external dimensions. A mature Department of Resilience would use this kind of position standard as an industrial interface: multiple firms could compete module technologies while the formation retains load identity, custody, acceptance and substitution rules.

## 4. Four load blocks create a nested grammar

The thirty-two positions are divided into four exact dry-mass blocks:

| Block | Positions | Dry mass | Dry cube | Dry deck | Wet pack | Loaded mass |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `L1` | 8 | 145 t | 567 m³ | 136 m² | 13.75 t | 158.75 t |
| `L2` | 8 | 145 t | 757 m³ | 166 m² | 13.75 t | 158.75 t |
| `L3` | 8 | 145 t | 591 m³ | 141 m² | 13.75 t | 158.75 t |
| `L4` | 8 | 145 t | 715 m³ | 171 m² | 13.75 t | 158.75 t |

The exact mass equality is useful but not sufficient. Block volume varies by thirty-four percent and deck demand by twenty-six percent. `L2` is volume-dominant; `L4` is deck-dominant. A mass-only allocation would conceal those constraints.

The grammar nests cleanly across modes:

```text
one ATUT-170 = one 145 t dry block + one 13.75 t wet pack
one HBL-340 = two dry blocks + two wet packs
one complete cell = four dry blocks + four wet packs
```

Atmospheric carrier `HBL-A1` receives `L1` and `L2`; `HBL-A2` receives `L3` and `L4`. Terrain carriers `ATUT-B1` through `ATUT-B4` receive `L1` through `L4` respectively. The same functional standard is instantiated twice, once under cell-`A` serials and once under cell-`B` serials.

The blocks are handling and reconstruction objects. They allow origin staff to scan, load and verify eight-position lots; allow a carrier plan to be rebuilt from a signed offline record; and provide a stable unit for robotic transfer, depot work and industrial test. They are not fractional public-service effects. No block can satisfy the service guarantee by itself.

## 5. Atmospheric carrier requirement: `HBL-340`

The atmospheric cell uses two payload vehicles:

| Carrier | Loaded mass | Stowed volume | Deck demand | Mass use | Volume use | Deck use | Calculated payload CG `(x,y,z)` |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| `HBL-A1` | 317.5 t | 1,354 m³ | 312 m² | 93.4% | 96.7% | 88.6% | `(16.000, 0.000, 2.527) m` |
| `HBL-A2` | 317.5 t | 1,336 m³ | 322 m² | 93.4% | 95.4% | 91.5% | `(16.000, 0.000, 2.406) m` |

The declared usable payload envelope for each carrier is 340 tonnes, 1,400 cubic metres and 352 square metres. Longitudinal payload center of gravity must remain between fifteen and seventeen metres from the load datum; absolute lateral center of gravity may not exceed 0.35 metre; vertical center of gravity may not exceed three metres above the load-deck datum. Both static plans pass.

`HBL-A1` is the volume driver at 96.7-percent use. This is more important than its mass margin: later packaging growth, access aisles, fire separation and pressure-management volumes could force a larger hull or a different internal arrangement before mass does. The next pass must not treat 1,400 cubic metres as a decorative specification. It must derive the usable bay from structure, buoyancy, propulsion, access, restraint and damage-control systems.

The atmospheric carrier must do more than lift 340 tonnes. It must accept two independently serialized blocks at a base, maintain hazard separation, preserve load access in unload order, measure mass and balance without network dependence, transit in relevant weather, hold position over or beside a damaged receiver, transfer the load without intact heavy infrastructure, recover after an aborted placement and return for inspection and reload. Those are vehicle requirements, not equipment added after selection.

## 6. Terrain carrier requirement: `ATUT-170`

The terrain cell uses four payload vehicles:

| Carrier | Block | Loaded mass | Stowed volume | Deck demand | Volume use | Deck use | Calculated payload CG `(x,y,z)` |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| `ATUT-B1` | `L1` | 158.75 t | 582 m³ | 141 m² | 72.8% | 76.6% | `(10.928, -0.076, 2.445) m` |
| `ATUT-B2` | `L2` | 158.75 t | 772 m³ | 171 m² | 96.5% | 92.9% | `(11.000, -0.013, 2.609) m` |
| `ATUT-B3` | `L3` | 158.75 t | 606 m³ | 146 m² | 75.8% | 79.3% | `(10.694, 0.315, 2.368) m` |
| `ATUT-B4` | `L4` | 158.75 t | 730 m³ | 176 m² | 91.3% | 95.7% | `(10.987, -0.013, 2.444) m` |

Each carrier has a declared usable payload envelope of 170 tonnes, 800 cubic metres and 184 square metres. Longitudinal center of gravity must remain between 10.5 and 11.5 metres, with the same lateral and vertical limits used in the atmospheric screen. All four plans pass.

The allocation exposes two different design drivers. `ATUT-B2` is volume-limited; `ATUT-B4` is deck-limited. A uniform box architecture may therefore be inferior to a common mobility chassis with plural mission-deck implementations, provided the implementations do not create a maintenance or supplier common mode. The external width, length and height cannot be inferred from the usable deck alone. Ground pressure, axle or bogie loads, turning geometry, bridge bypass, articulation, slope stability and self-recovery will decide the actual form.

The terrain requirement should be understood as infrastructure mobility rather than an enlarged highway truck. Its mission begins where ordinary heavy haul loses assurance. It must cross saturated, debris-loaded and geometrically damaged approaches while protecting a precision service payload. Clean-sheet technology may include distributed electric traction, active load leveling, variable-footprint running gear, robotic route preparation, detachable powered modules and coordinated multi-body articulation. No choice receives credit until a representative vehicle carries the position load through saturated-terrain and recovery trials.

## 7. Independence is physical, organizational and informational

Two copies of the same manifest are not two assured cells if they wait behind one release, occupy one site, depend on one loader or reconstruct from one corrupted configuration database. The model therefore separates five origin properties:

| Property | Atmospheric cell `A` | Terrain cell `B` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| custody and control | `AIR-A` | `LAND-B` |
| external interface | `I-A` | `I-B` |
| technical release | air release authority | terrain release authority |
| loading system | lift-tower rail grid | side-load platform |
| firm-energy ancestry | hydrogen-electric lineage `A` | liquid-electric lineage `B` |

The air origin contains two vehicles, two load towers or pads, thirty-two dry positions, four wet cells, eighteen megawatts of firm power and forty-eight megawatt-hours of black start. The terrain origin contains four vehicles, four side-load pads, a separate thirty-two-position lot, four wet cells, ten megawatts of firm power and thirty megawatt-hours of black start. The nodes are declared at least 160 kilometres apart and retain separate signed offline configuration records.

The separation distance is a hypothesis, not a universal safety result. It exists to make geographic common mode visible in the cost and site plan. Hazard-specific studies may require more separation, hardened colocation, distributed micro-arsenals or a dynamic rule based on fire, flood, earthquake, storm and infrastructure topology.

The deeper principle is **common public standard, plural implementation**. `I-A` and `I-B` must expose equivalent service semantics to the receiver without sharing a decisive internal mechanism. This creates a genuine industrial-policy requirement: the Department must sustain two qualified interface lineages, two loading-equipment industries and two certification chains. Interoperability is valuable only if it does not erase fault independence.

## 8. Loading, dispatch and receiver assembly

Each origin sequence starts with technical release, crew muster, retrieval and serial scanning of thirty-two dry positions, loading of four segregated wet packs, and restraint and measured-balance release. The atmospheric path adds flight preparation, transit and placement; the terrain path adds route confirmation, traverse and unload. Carrier release remains distinct from public-service release.

At the receiver, the service cell is assembled in an eight-hour wave plan:

1. establish survey, exclusion and civil-control boundaries and activate offline command;
2. establish black start and medium-voltage protection;
3. deploy distribution and building interfaces;
4. place firm-power conversion, useful cooling and final heat rejection;
5. connect water treatment, wastewater and storage;
6. connect medical, vertical and final building service; and
7. complete integrated assay, test, civil acceptance and public release.

The order is a dependency graph, not merely an unloading preference. Black start must precede energized distribution. Distribution must precede broad connection. Heat rejection must be available before sustained thermal loads. Water-quality release cannot be replaced by visual completion. A carrier architecture that traps an early-wave position behind a late-wave module fails even if its mass and volume close.

The destination contains an air entry zone and a terrain entry zone separated by five kilometres, plus a joint service yard. The air zone declares a 420-by-420-metre clear transfer area and four-kilometre approach-clearance radius. The terrain zone declares a 200-by-100-metre transfer area, twelve-metre minimum access width and six-percent maximum operating slope. Both zones declare sixty-kilopascal minimum bearing capacity for the receiving surface, though the relevance and conservatism of that value must be tested separately by mode. Each zone has its own public-service release team and can support a seventy-two-hour offline period.

Separate zones avoid a subtle common mode: a receiver can be large enough for both systems on paper but configured so that one wreck, one exclusion perimeter or one failed transfer device blocks both. The shared service yard is permitted only after each mode has completed its independent entry and release path.

## 9. Time, people and authority

Region `B` activates at absolute hour 6. The two declared schedules are:

| Path | Origin and transit | Receiver build | Relative service | Absolute service | Margin to hour 54 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| atmospheric cell `A` | 34 h | 8 h | 42 h | 48 h | 6 h |
| terrain cell `B` | 37 h | 8 h | 45 h | 51 h | 3 h |

The terrain path remains the timing driver. Three hours is enough to distinguish a closing arithmetic schedule from an already failed one; it is not enough to establish reliable operational performance. Route reconnaissance, obstruction removal, saturated-soil delay, recovery, civil-control friction and receiver rework must become distributions in later trials.

The formation retains 486 deployed people and 1,065 affiliated positions. Deployed strength includes command, atmospheric and terrain operations, two payload-custody and loading chains, two receiver-service and release chains, mode-specific maintenance and recovery, medical, safety and communications. Minimum watch is 158 people.

Fifteen representative duty assignments cover technical release, loading, two atmospheric watches, three terrain movement watches, receiver teams and four command watches. No assignment exceeds twelve hours, and each tested assignment has at least twelve hours of pre-duty rest. This does not close the labor problem. It establishes a schedulable skeleton. Casualty substitution, travel to base, protective-equipment burden, heat exposure, multi-day receiver operations, reset staffing and local-force integration remain evidence gates.

Authority is treated as an operating resource. The team that proves a carrier safe to depart is not automatically the authority that accepts water, power, cooling or medical service for public use. The model preserves technical release, receiver release and civil acceptance as separate records so schedule pressure cannot silently turn vehicle arrival into service readiness.

## 10. Loss semantics

Twenty loss worlds test the physical configuration. Nineteen preserve a complete public-service path in the behavior model. The sole designed failure removes both atmospheric and terrain access modes. That world sits outside the present guarantee and is retained to make the boundary explicit.

The permitted cuts include loss of either liftship, loss of any one terrain vehicle, denial of either whole mode or base, quarantine of either complete payload lot, failure of either interface implementation, denial of either receiver zone, loss of either release team and denial of network access. In each single-cut case, one complete independently governed cell remains.

The vehicle-loss cases require careful interpretation:

```text
loss of HBL-A1 or HBL-A2
→ cell A incomplete
→ terrain cell B remains complete

loss of any ATUT-B1 through ATUT-B4
→ cell B incomplete
→ atmospheric cell A remains complete
```

The formation does not regenerate the missing block in the affected mode. It shifts the mission to the other complete mode. Any later claim of within-mode vehicle-loss continuity would require spare payload capacity, functional redistribution, another vehicle or an experimentally proven partial-service doctrine.

The representative-evidence world behaves like the nominal world arithmetically but receives no current credit because all gates are open. Thus the loss table distinguishes **behavior pass** from **readiness pass**. A formula can show that a remaining path exists. Only evidence can show that it works.

## 11. Reset is part of force projection

A force able to reach one disaster and unavailable for the next week is not a strategic parallel to military force projection. The model therefore carries vehicle, payload, crew, base and receiver work through an independent 168-hour reset target for each mode.

The declared workstreams run in parallel. Atmospheric vehicle reset requires 126 hours; terrain vehicle reset requires 132. Both payload streams require ninety-six hours. Crew reset requires forty hours, and base-and-receiver handback requires eighty. The terrain-vehicle path governs with thirty-six hours of margin.

Reset includes shutdown, recovery or handback, decontamination, inspection and repair, replenishment or qualification, certification and reload. The distinction between “replenish” and “qualify” matters. Wet products, treatment media, batteries, structural elements, software configurations and medical supplies may be present but unusable until their lineage and condition are accepted. The force needs depot laboratories, contamination control, robotic inspection, replaceable wear structure and signed configuration reconstruction as much as it needs fast vehicles.

Later modeling must add queueing across simultaneous returns, depot casualty, supplier delay, cannibalization rules and campaign tempo. The current 168-hour result is a single-package target under uncongested parallel work.

## 13. Technology and industrial mobilization

The force should not wait for existing commercial systems to become slightly larger. It should organize mission-driven technology campaigns comparable in ambition to major defense programs, but governed by public-service effects and representative civil conditions.

The immediate configuration portfolio is $56 billion of research and $85 billion of integration and test. It includes:

- full-scale position articles that measure mass, cube, access, hazard separation and teardown rather than relying on notional modules;
- `HBL-340` cargo transfer, mooring and unload systems able to place two-block loads in weather without intact destination cranes;
- `ATUT-170` modular decks, low-ground-pressure movement and self-recovery across saturated and debris-loaded terrain;
- rapid certified loading of segregated wet packs;
- two independently engineered external-interface implementations that deliver common service without a common decisive defect;
- dual-zone receiver construction and public-service acceptance under damaged-district constraints;
- offline configuration compilation whose signed load record can be reconstructed without network access;
- randomized physical-loss trials in which vehicle, crew, authority, interface and receiver failures are inserted without warning;
- separated two-base mobilization inside one mission clock;
- automated load compilation from measured article data at operational tempo;
- climate-diverse receiver proving ranges;
- compound regional campaigns; and
- an independent certification institution that separates builder, operator and certifier.

Each campaign should produce both capability and industry. The position standard can create qualified module families. Lift transfer can create a heavy robotic cargo sector. Terrain mobility can create a new class of infrastructure vehicles. Plural interfaces can sustain competitive suppliers. Receiver ranges can become national laboratories for climate service. Offline mission compilation can form a resilient-logistics software industry. The Department's industrial policy is not a subsidy attached to procurement; it is the deliberate creation of production systems for civil force projection.

Innovation remains welcome where it changes the requirement itself. Lighter power electronics, high-temperature heat rejection, compact water treatment, structural batteries, self-deploying distribution, autonomous assay and local fabrication could reduce cell mass or assembly time. A technology that removes a service-chain term should be rewarded more than one that merely fits the current envelope. But the burden is explicit: it must preserve the accepted public effect under the same loss, custody, receiver and reset grammar.

## 14. Evidence, rendering and the next research boundary

Sixty evidence gates cover all sixteen functional packages, two complete cells, four load blocks, six carrier balances, wet-load custody, mass and geometry reserve, centers of gravity, mooring and transfer, terrain unload and recovery, base separation, energy and supplier ancestry, receiver zones, interface implementations, timing, duty cycle, casualty substitution, reset, cost, production, compound mission and rendering authorization.

Every gate is open. Current evidence credit is zero. Strict credit is zero. Calculated fit does not establish structural fit; declared timing does not establish a distribution; a named interface does not establish independence; and a program account does not establish producibility.

Rendering stays blocked for a substantive reason. The present work can depict a load grammar, but not a truthful vehicle. The `HBL-340` exterior depends on lift mechanism, hull or envelope structure, tare mass, propulsion, energy storage, weather authority, mooring loads, cargo-bay geometry, transfer kinematics, damage tolerance and emergency recovery. The `ATUT-170` exterior depends on ground pressure, contact geometry, articulation, drive architecture, slope stability, debris clearance, self-recovery, deck implementation and transportability. Drawing either now would smuggle unmade engineering choices into the research program.

The next pass should compete at least three `HBL-340` physical architectures under one payload and mission envelope. Each rival must close a mass statement from tare through gross operating weight; buoyancy or lift; propulsion, range and loiter; energy and refueling; weather and icing; mooring and terminal operations; cargo restraint and access; emergency descent or recovery; maintenance; production rate; base footprint; crew; cost; and representative tests. It should explicitly test whether a single vehicle can combine transit, hover or station-keeping and off-airfield transfer, or whether the force needs a coupled carrier-and-transfer system.

A following pass should do the same for the `ATUT-170`, with ground pressure, bearing-capacity mismatch, obstacle geometry, water crossing, articulated control, recovery, deck variants, convoy behavior and route-preparation robotics as first-order variables. Only after both architecture competitions establish stable exterior envelopes should renderings begin.

## Pass stocktake

This pass makes the thirty-two-position physical register, four exact load blocks, two 635-tonne cell instances, six carrier assignments, `HBL-340` and `ATUT-170` payload requirements, separated origin and receiver chains, eight-hour assembly sequence, representative duty schedule, 168-hour reset target, configured program account and sixty-gate evidence burden authoritative at `R2`.

It supersedes `HBL-320` and `ATUT-160` as `DCP-2` carrier requirements. It falsifies exact-fit division as physical design reserve, load-block multiplicity as public-service redundancy, a common interface as proof of independent implementation, vehicle arrival as public-service release and the remaining Pass 81 cost gap as a decision-grade saving.

It does not admit a carrier architecture, a gross vehicle weight, an exterior dimension, propulsion, range, route performance, weather capability, production quantity or rendering. All performance, organization and cost values remain declared hypotheses. All sixty gates remain open.

The research has crossed an important boundary. `DCP-2` is no longer merely a formation diagram. It is a serialized physical requirement whose omissions can be named, priced and tested. The next task is not to make it look real. It is to decide what kinds of machines could actually carry it.
