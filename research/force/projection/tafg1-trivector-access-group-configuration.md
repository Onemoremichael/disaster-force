# `TAFG-1` tri-vector access-group configuration

## A fourth path becomes a formation only when carriage, service bridging and receiver entry compose

**Research status:** first-principles formation configuration and force-structure competition; behavior only; no article or formation admitted

**Decision date:** 9 September 2026

**Companion model:** [`tafg1-trivector-access-group-configuration.xlsx`](../../../models/force/projection/tafg1-trivector-access-group-configuration.xlsx)

## Executive judgment

The Department of Resilience should organize fourth access around a deployable **tri-vector access group**, not a catalog of exotic vehicles. The reference `TAFG-1` formation combines three different actions against a common public-service contract:

- `M1` carries one complete `PTS-5` service cell through the atmosphere with two heavy buoyant liftships;
- `M3` carries a second complete cell across damaged terrain with four autonomous terrain vehicles; and
- `M5` projects power, water, thermal exchange and communications from a surviving source with four robotic utility-corridor systems.

The formation is deliberately redundant in effect but dissimilar in physics. It can carry a complete 635-tonne launch article by either of two independent vehicle families or substitute a service bridge where bulk movement is the wrong answer. The resulting fielded payload is 1,670 tonnes: two 635-tonne complete physical cells plus a 400-tonne corridor payload. That mass is not an inventory flourish. It is what permits the group to lose an atmospheric path, a land path or a usable receiver zone without treating partial equipment as public service.

Under the fifteen behavior worlds inherited from Pass 78, the baseline formation closes fourteen. Adding `M5` removes the leading `M1+M3` pair's 80-kilometre utility-gap failure. The remaining behavior failure is simultaneous demand in a second region: one complete formation cannot be credited twice. Two rivals close that case. `F2` adds an eight-aircraft distributed heavy-VTOL concurrency squadron; `F3` duplicates the entire tri-vector group. The first is the lower-cost declared challenger, but neither is selected and both have zero current evidence.

The more consequential result is institutional. Three unlike vehicles do not create three independent access paths if they share one payload magazine, mobilization base, energy source, control stack, supplier lineage, weather authority, receiver gate, maintenance depot, crew pool or release official. The formation therefore registers ten ancestry domains and requires each vector to remain useful when a named source, path or authority is withheld. A compound test exposes common payload custody as the new critical failure: if the same module pool or loading node controls all dispatch choices, the nominally diverse force has no surviving effect.

The baseline calls for 488 deployed people, 286 trained local reservists, 158 regionally shared specialists, a minimum 124-person watch-and-launch posture and 1,056 total affiliated positions when training, relief, depot and institutional depth are counted. Its longest declared recovery path is 144 hours against a 168-hour reset target. These values make the formation a standing public-service institution rather than an emergency collection of pilots, robots and cable reels.

The national research force retains the Pass 78 requirement for twenty-two assigned hard-access groups. Applying the 1.5 concurrency factor and 1.3 readiness factor yields forty-three ready groups. At the declared coefficients, the `F1` successor costs $726.7 billion for formation lifecycle, $165 billion for article-family research and development, $160 billion for shared command, bases and proving, and $180 billion for cross-cutting formation technology: **$1.2317 trillion over twenty-five years**. Replacing the Pass 78 paired program once recompiles the current whole-force boundary from $6.848 trillion to approximately **$7.231 trillion**.

That is an intentional expression of coequal civil power. It buys an enduring ability to enter places after infrastructure, geography and weather have defeated ordinary logistics; establish usable power, water, thermal safety and communications; work under civil authority around occupied communities; recover; and do it again. It does not authorize procurement. All thirty-four evidence gates remain open, strict admission is zero and rendering remains blocked.

## 1. What Pass 78 left unresolved

Pass 78 showed that no universal fourth carrier exists. Eight mechanisms faced fifteen hard-access worlds. Heavy buoyant lift plus autonomous terrain trains produced the strongest pair and closed thirteen, but the pair failed when the receiver was separated from a surviving utility source by an 80-kilometre damaged zone and when a second hard-access region demanded service at the same time.

That comparison established three access actions—carriage, bridging and conversion—but stopped at portfolio level. It did not answer whether selected mechanisms could operate as one formation, whether each had a complete service payload, who would command and release them, how many people and bases were implied, or whether apparent diversity survived shared institutional ancestry.

This pass resolves the first of those questions. It configures `M1`, `M3` and `M5` as one deployable group, retains `M4` as a concurrency challenger, and forces common payload, workforce, receiver, recovery and cost boundaries into the same design object. It does not yet configure every hose, cable, mooring element, axle load, rotor, depot bay or source contract. Those remain acquisition-level work.

## 2. The unit of force is a public effect

### 2.1 Three vectors, one service contract

The group exists to establish a complete `PTS-5` public-service cell by hour 48 at a receiver where normal access has failed. Its effect includes firm electrical conversion and black start; useful cooling and a final heat destination; treatment, storage and quality control for water; wastewater and residual custody; medium-voltage, water, thermal and communications distribution; occupied-building interfaces; medical and vertical-service kits; offline command; survey and civil works; life support; spares; and certified initial consumables.

The acquisition article is therefore not a liftship sortie, terrain convoy or length of cable. It is verified service at the occupied edge. A vehicle can arrive and the mission can still fail. A corridor can be continuous and the mission can still fail if the source is unqualified, protection cannot isolate a damaged segment, potable custody is lost or local authority cannot lawfully energize the receiver.

### 2.2 The three actions

**Atmospheric carriage.** Two `HBL-320` liftships divide the 635-tonne launch article while preserving the governed position manifest. They create long-range access without a runway or navigable water surface. The pair must arrive as a complete effect; loss or diversion of a critical position cannot be hidden by reporting tonnes delivered.

**Terrain carriage.** Four `ATUT-160` autonomous terrain vehicles carry an independently constituted copy of the same article. They use distributed traction, active suspension, route robotics, obstacle work and carried short-span infrastructure to approach through landslide, debris, flood, smoke, wind and damaged roads. Human route authority and recovery remain essential even when driving is highly automated.

**Service bridging.** Four `RUC-100` corridor projectors deploy a 400-tonne bridge payload between a surviving source and the receiver. The bridge is not a smaller cell. It substitutes source-side conversion, standoff heat rejection, protected electrical conductors, potable and process-water paths, thermal loops, communications repeaters, sectional isolation and building terminators for selected carried plant. Its usefulness ends when no qualified source exists.

Local conversion remains a fourth action family in the larger force, but it is not included in `TAFG-1`. Pass 78 assigned it one distinct feedstock-rich mission. It should remain a separate experimental force until rapid assay, lawful material custody, production recipes and output certification are configured well enough to avoid importing an unproved dependency into the reference group.

## 3. Reference formation `F1`

### 3.1 Vehicles and payloads

One `TAFG-1` contains:

- two `HBL-320` heavy buoyant liftships;
- four `ATUT-160` autonomous terrain vehicles;
- four `RUC-100` robotic utility-corridor projectors;
- one complete atmospheric-carriage service cell;
- one complete terrain-carriage service cell; and
- one complete utility-bridge payload.

The two physical cells each use the same thirty-two governed positions and 580 tonnes of dry equipment. Fifty-five tonnes of initial consumables raise each launch burden to 635 tonnes. The manifest does not imply that every position has equal size or that every carrier receives the same number. It establishes configuration custody: all sixteen functional packages and all thirty-two positions must be accounted for before dispatch, at transfer, after arrival and at service release.

The bridge payload replaces mass only where a verified source and route permit substitution. It must preserve the same end services and quality boundaries. This is why the group fields 1,670 tonnes rather than treating the 400-tonne bridge as a fractional third cell.

### 3.2 Command and decision rights

The formation commander owns mission composition, dispatch sequencing, abort coordination, cross-vector support and reset. Three vector commanders retain technical authority over safe flight, route traversal and utility energization. A receiver integration element owns local survey, occupied-area exclusion, source acceptance, building-interface custody and public-service handoff. An independent release authority decides whether the complete service contract has been met.

These authorities should be federated, not collapsed. The incident commander may set priorities and accept civil risk within law, but cannot declare an unsafe aircraft fit, contaminated water potable or an untested electrical island energized. Technical authorities cannot choose public priorities or override community rights merely because their equipment is ready. The formation must work when national networks are unavailable, using signed offline configuration records and bounded synchronization rather than one cloud control plane.

### 3.3 Operating sequence

The reference mission has six overlapping phases.

**Phase 0—persistent preparation.** Regional commands maintain candidate source, mooring, landing, unloading, corridor and occupied-building interface records. Payload arsenals hold sealed positions in multiple lots. Crews rehearse dispatch, cross-loading, loss insertion and civil handoff. This work occurs before a forecast or request.

**Phase 1—incident compilation.** The group determines which of the three actions can close the receiver: physical atmospheric delivery, physical terrain delivery, service bridging or more than one in parallel. Survey teams confirm terrain, weather, obstructions, source state, contamination, legal authority and human exposure. Unknowns remain visible; they are not converted into favorable assumptions.

**Phase 2—independent mobilization.** Each vector draws from its own energy, crew, control, supplier and base lineage. The formation may exchange a common interface specification, but no vector should need another vector's live control service to launch. Payload records are reconciled at position level.

**Phase 3—access and entry.** Liftships approach a surveyed mooring or low-speed placement zone. Terrain vehicles create and traverse a distributed route. Corridor systems survey, span, bore, crawl or aerially place service tissue from a qualified source. The group maintains abort zones and recovery paths that do not externalize risk onto the receiving population.

**Phase 4—service establishment.** Receiver teams connect source, power, thermal, water, control and building interfaces; isolate unsafe branches; perform quality and protection tests; and release services in a declared sequence. The clock stops only when public service is available, not when equipment crosses a perimeter.

**Phase 5—sustainment and regeneration.** The group operates for the declared seven-day mission, transfers durable functions to regional or local operators where appropriate, recovers vehicles and modules, quarantines contaminated equipment, replenishes energy and consumables, relieves crews and completes independent recertification.

## 4. The service manifest

The controlled manifest contains sixteen functional packages across thirty-two positions. It reserves 82 dry tonnes for firm power conversion, 42 for distributed black-start storage, 48 for useful-cooling trains, 62 for final heat rejection, 43 for water treatment and quality, 27 for wastewater and residual custody, and 26 for potable and fire-water storage. Distribution and occupied-edge packages add medium-voltage equipment, water and thermal paths, building interfaces, medical support and vertical-service kits. Survey robotics, controls, life support, repair and consumables complete the cell.

This balance carries two design implications.

First, final heat rejection and residual custody remain first-class payload. A generator or treatment skid without a safe sink is not a service system. The force must be capable of carrying cooling towers, dry coolers, heat exchangers, hoses, return paths, waste containment and monitoring even when those objects are visually less dramatic than the carrier.

Second, interfaces deserve mass and organizational ownership. Medium-voltage protection, potable boundaries, building termination, assay, controls and release work are not negligible accessories. The last metres into an occupied building determine whether strategic projection becomes survival, especially during heat, medical-power and vertical-access failure.

The common interface should standardize geometry, lifting points, electrical and fluid boundaries, data and identity, environmental limits and acceptance records. It should not standardize every internal implementation. Competing suppliers must be able to produce interoperable positions without one design defect or software lineage becoming a fleet-wide stop.

## 5. Independence and common-mode discipline

### 5.1 Ten ancestry domains

`TAFG-1` registers separation across energy, launch and base, control, workforce, supplier, receiver, weather, reset, payload and authority. Each has three declared lineages:

- liftship, terrain and corridor systems draw from different operating bases and dispatch paths;
- aerostat, land-robotics and utility-field professions have different schools and relief pools;
- flight, ground-state and corridor-route control stacks exchange bounded information but remain independently operable;
- receiver entry uses a mooring or placement zone, a terrain unloading zone and a standoff corridor terminus;
- mode-specific recovery depots prevent one wash, inspection or repair queue from halting every path; and
- aviation release, land-route release and utility energization remain distinct technical acts inside one civil appeal structure.

The register is a design hypothesis, not proof. Declaring three supplier lots does not establish independent material ancestry. Separate depots located on the same power feeder are not independent. Two crews whose certification records depend on one unavailable network are not independent. Each claim requires a named source, path, failure insertion and measured consequence.

### 5.2 The payload paradox

The group needs common interfaces so either physical carrier can transport the service cell. It also needs independent payload availability so one arsenal or custody defect cannot erase both. This creates a deliberate tension: interchangeability increases operational flexibility while common production and storage can increase correlated risk.

The provisional answer is **common specification, plural custody**. Atmospheric cell `A`, terrain cell `B` and corridor payload `C` should be separately sourced, stored, inspected and released. Cross-loading remains possible through a controlled emergency procedure, but routine readiness cannot assume it. The formation-level configuration authority owns compatibility; it does not own every physical lot.

The compound-withholding screen confirms the distinction. Removing one vector's source and base leaves another complete effect. Removing shared payload custody removes all effects. Independence therefore has to be tested at the arsenal and loading node, not inferred from vehicle diversity.

## 6. Workforce and professional formation

The 488-person deployed formation is organized around nine role groups: command and access control; liftship operations; terrain-train operations; corridor operations; receiver survey and acceptance; power, water and thermal service; maintenance and recovery; medical, safety and civil liaison; and logistics and supply.

The group is not fully described by its deployed headcount. It also requires 286 trained local reservists and 158 regionally shared specialists. Its 1,056 affiliated positions include the people needed to sustain schools, evaluation, relief, depot work, specialist recertification and institutional continuity. The 124-person minimum watch-and-launch posture preserves immediate incident compilation and first dispatch while the rest of the formation mobilizes.

This produces new civil professions at strategic scale: buoyant-lift loadmaster and damage-control specialists; autonomous terrain route officers; rapid-corridor protection and water-quality engineers; receiver acceptance officers; configuration custodians; compound-weather analysts; field heat-rejection operators; robotic recovery technicians; and public-service release authorities. Their careers require academies, simulators, ranges, standards, promotion systems and recurrent representative exercises. Contractors can build and maintain equipment, but the state must retain the knowledge needed to judge safety, readiness and public effect.

## 7. Bases, energy and the receiver enterprise

The group should not live at one giant joint base. Concentration would simplify management while defeating the purpose of a dissimilar access force. A mature basing pattern needs geographically separated liftship bases, terrain depots and corridor arsenals connected by interoperable payload interfaces and a federated mission-command layer.

Liftship bases require weather observation, protected envelope and propulsion maintenance, variable-buoyancy services, mobile mooring, large-volume sheltered inspection and safe launch corridors. Terrain depots need test terrain, modular bridge and earthwork yards, high-capacity charging or fuel systems, wash and quarantine lines and heavy robotic recovery. Corridor arsenals need long linear deployment grounds, cable and hose manufacture and testing, high-voltage protection laboratories, hydraulic and water-quality loops, boring and route robots, sectional repair stocks and source simulators.

The formation cannot assume a single energy economy. The liftship hypothesis uses a hydrogen-electric lineage; the terrain train uses a liquid-electric or independently supplied hybrid lineage; and the corridor force draws operating energy from its own depot while treating the standoff utility as mission payload supply. These labels are placeholders for separated source contracts and conversion paths. A later compiler must trace primary material, conversion plant, transport, storage, connector, software, workforce and emergency reserve.

Receiver preparation is part of force projection. Regional commands need standing surveys of potential mooring and unloading zones, intact source locations, route rights, contamination risks, utility protection boundaries, building interfaces and community authorities. The Department should fund those interfaces as national resilience infrastructure while keeping activation and service release under civil law.

## 8. Recovery, abort and reset

An access force that cannot recover is a one-event demonstration. `TAFG-1` therefore treats mission termination, payload inventory, decontamination, vehicle repair, energy replenishment, crew relief, receiver handback and certified reload as part of readiness.

The longest declared path is 144 hours, driven by vehicle inspection and repair, against a seven-day reset target. Payload recovery and inventory reaches 96 hours; certification and reload 92; energy replenishment 84; decontamination 78; and crew relief 72. These are preliminary critical-path envelopes, not sums. Separate vector lines can work concurrently, but joint custody and release steps can still serialize the force.

Abort design must begin before vehicle design. Liftships need diversion, safe ballast or buoyancy management, payload retention and occupied-area exclusion. Terrain systems need stuck-vehicle bypass, distributed tow and bridge recovery without blocking the entire route. Corridor systems need rapid de-energization, product isolation, rupture containment, contamination recovery and lawful abandonment criteria. Every mode needs casualty evacuation, lost-communications action, public warning and a clear authority for choosing equipment loss over population risk.

Reset should be proven by repeated missions, not inspection at the end of one successful trial. A candidate must complete representative operation, return, quarantine, repair, reload and re-dispatch while another group remains on watch. Otherwise the readiness multiplier is accounting rather than capacity.

## 9. Concurrency and rival force structures

The national screen begins with twenty-two assigned hard-access groups. A 1.5 concurrency factor recognizes that disasters and invited international missions overlap; a 1.3 readiness factor covers training, overhaul and attrition. The ceiling is forty-three ready groups.

`F1` closes fourteen behavior worlds and one region at a time. It fields 1,670 tonnes and 488 deployed people per group. Its failure in the second-region world is intentional: capacity already committed cannot be counted again.

`F2` adds eight `VHL-80` heavy-VTOL aircraft. It closes all fifteen behavior worlds and supports two concurrent regions with 2,310 fielded tonnes and 674 deployed people. Its declared whole-force boundary is $7.812 trillion. The aircraft lattice is useful not because it is universally superior, but because it can split governed positions and create a second dispatch while the core group remains committed.

`F3` duplicates the entire tri-vector group. It also closes all fifteen worlds and serves two regions, with 3,340 fielded tonnes, 932 deployed people and an $8.043 trillion boundary. It buys deeper symmetry, more independent utility bridges and greater recovery depth than `F2`, but at higher cost and with more pressure on every industrial lineage.

`F4` combines the corridor force with eight heavy-VTOL aircraft. It closes ten worlds and serves two regions with a $7.245 trillion whole-force boundary. It is the lowest-cost declared route to concurrency, but it loses the long-range, maritime and source-denied breadth supplied by `M1` and `M3`. It is therefore a useful falsification rival, not a substitute for the reference mission set.

The choice between `F2` and `F3` should be made by loss insertion, not arithmetic alone. The decisive questions are whether an eight-aircraft lattice can deliver a second complete service effect after diversion and module loss; whether its energy, rotor, control and base ancestry are independent of the liftship force; and whether duplicating `TAFG-1` actually produces separate arsenals, crews, receivers and reset paths rather than two labels on one enterprise.

## 10. Technology programs implied by the formation

This force cannot be acquired from mature catalog equipment. The Department needs a sustained mission-technology system with permission to alter the frontier.

### 10.1 Heavy buoyant lift

The liftship program requires damage-tolerant ultra-large envelopes; active load distribution; variable buoyancy without routine atmospheric release; high-authority propulsion in gusts; low-speed precision station keeping; mobile mooring; rapid load exchange; weather-aware flight control; and safe operation near occupied terrain. Full-scale structural sections and destructive weather trials should precede a representative flight article.

### 10.2 Autonomous terrain mobility

The terrain program requires many-axle active suspension, low-ground-pressure traction, cooperative power sharing, robotic geotechnical survey, carried short bridges, autonomous obstacle work, distributed recovery and auditable control under communications loss. Its research target is not driverless trucking. It is a mobile civil-works and utility formation able to create a temporary route while carrying a complete service cell.

### 10.3 Robotic utility tissue

The corridor program requires high-capacity flexible conductors and fluid paths; self-surveying placement; aerial, surface and subsurface route machines; automatic sectional isolation; potable custody; leak and contamination detection; thermal-loop management; embedded communications; fast repair; and safe recovery. Standard segments should identify themselves, report physical state offline and permit local rerouting without trusting a single remote controller.

### 10.4 Common service modules

The thirty-two-position cell requires high-power-density conversion, distributed black start, compact heat rejection, variable-feed water treatment, sanitary residual handling, rugged protection, rapid building interfaces, offline configuration identity and automated acceptance. The governing innovation is modularity without common-mode fragility: several internal technologies and supplier lineages must satisfy the same external contract.

### 10.5 Mission intelligence and proving

The group needs compound weather and terrain prediction, rapid source and contamination assay, receiver digital twins that remain useful offline, machine-readable safety cases, signed configuration custody, human-supervised multi-vector scheduling and independent consequence reconstruction. These systems must be tested against deliberately wrong maps, corrupted records, degraded sensors, conflicting authority and incomplete communications.

The `F1` model separates $165 billion of article-family research and development from a $180 billion cross-cutting technology increment. The former creates representative liftship, terrain, corridor and payload articles. The latter creates the integration primitives, receivers, test infrastructure, data and assurance system that no single vehicle program would rationally own. The $160 billion shared enterprise capitalizes bases, command, configuration and proving. This separation makes the innovation state visible rather than burying it in unit procurement.

## 11. Economics and industrial structure

The forty-three-group `F1` formation lifecycle is $726.7 billion under the declared $16.9-billion per-group coefficient. Adding $165 billion of article-family research, $180 billion of cross-cutting technology and $160 billion of shared enterprise produces the $1.2317-trillion successor program.

The whole-force comparison removes the $848.3235-billion Pass 78 `M1+M3` paired program once and adds the successor once. The resulting $7.23141195-trillion boundary is not the cost of `TAFG-1` alone and is not a federal budget forecast. It is a consistency control showing how the new formation changes the latest national research architecture without stacking successor and predecessor.

At maturity, the program would create industrial sectors rather than occasional emergency contracts: large buoyant-structure yards; electric and hybrid propulsion lines; articulated terrain-vehicle factories; high-capacity cable, hose and connector production; water and thermal module plants; robotics and power-electronics supply chains; distributed depots; test ranges; professional schools; and configuration laboratories. Forty-three standing groups plus training, replacement and allied variants are sufficient to sustain multiple competing suppliers if acquisition policy protects interface openness and second-source qualification.

The export opportunity is a consequence of trustworthy capability. Allies and partner states may buy complete groups, narrower corridor or terrain formations, prepared receiver packages, licenses, spares, training, exercises and mutual-aid access. The United States can project power through invited restoration without creating dependence on a black-box American operator. Export designs should therefore include local maintenance, sovereign data custody, transparent safety cases and nonexclusive interfaces from the beginning.

## 12. Departmental structure

The Department of Resilience should establish a **Fourth Access Systems Command** to generate and allocate these formations. Its responsibilities would include formation doctrine, readiness, bases, arsenals, exercises, deployment and regeneration. Mode-specific centers would retain technical knowledge for buoyant flight, terrain systems and utility corridors.

An independent **Access Test Directorate** should own representative environmental trials, failure insertion, safety evidence and reconstruction. An **Access Configuration Authority** should own the public-service position standard, interface versions and payload records. Regional Federation Commands should maintain receiver contracts and employ the complete public effect. The Resilience Industrial Base Authority should sustain competing production lineages and surge options.

These bodies should not be combined simply for administrative neatness. The organization that wants a formation declared ready should not control the evidence that proves it. The body that owns the module interface should not become the sole module supplier. A national commander can allocate a force but cannot replace local civil authority or technical release. Separation is an operating requirement.

## 13. Evidence gates and rendering boundary

Thirty-four evidence gates remain open. The first three require complete representative `M1`, `M3` and `M5` payloads. The remainder cover cross-mode custody, sources and bases, receiver survey, hour-48 entry, seven-day operation, weather, smoke, steep terrain, offline control, civil authority, crews, supplier and energy ancestry, payload independence, decontamination, recovery, 168-hour reset, second-region concurrency, abort, public safety, rights, environmental limits, cyber safety, full-formation trials, repeat mission, cost, schedule and independent reconstruction.

Every behavior result has zero current and strict credit. The evidence-withheld world remains a universal stop even when the declared configuration would otherwise pass. A successful subsystem trial cannot admit the formation, and a successful first mission cannot establish reset or repeatability.

Rendering remains blocked. The study now specifies which objects a later image must contain, but not their evidenced dimensions, geometry or arrangement. Producing persuasive vehicle art now would harden arbitrary choices about liftship envelope, mooring, axle and bridge geometry, cable and hose deployment, heat-rejection layout, human access and receiver footprint. Physical configuration and representative envelopes must come first.

## 14. Pass 79 stocktake

### What became authoritative at `R2`

The reference research object is now one tri-vector formation rather than the loose `M1+M3+M5` portfolio. Its defining properties are two complete physical service cells, one complete service-bridge payload, thirty-two governed positions per physical cell, 1,670 tonnes of fielded payload, 488 deployed people, a 124-person immediate posture, plural technical authorities, registered ancestry and a complete return-to-ready cycle.

Service bridging becomes a coequal access action inside the formation and removes the leading pair's utility-gap failure. Concurrency becomes a separate force-structure variable rather than an unspoken promise. The formation comparison makes an eight-aircraft VHL squadron and a second complete `TAFG` explicit rivals.

### What the pass falsified

It falsifies the idea that adding a third mechanism automatically creates an independent third path. Shared payload custody can erase every vector. It falsifies the idea that a group deployed to one region can also satisfy a simultaneous second-region demand. It also falsifies a vehicle-only unit of account: receiver teams, service operators, civil release, depots, arsenals, recovery and reset materially define the formation.

### What remains provisional

Every vehicle rate, payload allocation, workforce value, base lineage, energy chain, reset duration, cost coefficient and behavior closure remains a design hypothesis. `F1` is the reference because it exposes the full system with the least additional concurrency machinery, not because it is selected. `F2`, `F3` and `F4` remain rivals. No operating credit, acquisition decision or rendering is authorized.

### Where the burden moved

The decisive uncertainty is now the formation's hidden middle: independent payload arsenals, cross-loading, source and base ancestry, time-phased dispatch, receiver entry, abort, recovery and recertification. Those functions determine whether the three vectors compose under stress or only coexist on an organization chart.

### Next highest-leverage pass

Pass 80 should build a **tri-vector payload, arsenal and dispatch compiler**. It should assign every governed position to separately sourced physical lots; map those lots to bases, loading equipment, crews, energy and release authority; schedule `M1`, `M3` and `M5` from incident declaration through hour-48 service; insert loss of an arsenal, loading node, source, control partition and receiver zone; and require a complete surviving effect without cross-credit. The same compiler should run `F2` and `F3` against a true second-region timeline so concurrency is earned by discrete assets and people rather than a declared count.

Only after that pass establishes representative spatial and loading envelopes should vehicle renderings begin.
