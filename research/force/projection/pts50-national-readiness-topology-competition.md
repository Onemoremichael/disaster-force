# `PTS-50` national readiness topology competition

## Where the Department stores readiness determines the force

**Research status:** first-principles national force-composition screen; seven topologies competed; no topology admitted

**Decision date:** 7 September 2026

**Companion model:** [`pts50-national-readiness-topology-competition.xlsx`](../../../models/force/projection/pts50-national-readiness-topology-competition.xlsx)

## Executive judgment

The Department of Resilience should not organize its power-and-thermal force around fleets of nominal assets. It should organize around **readiness topologies**: governed arrangements of complete service cells, prepared receiving geography, independent access paths, crews, control domains, suppliers, heat destinations, civil authority and reset capacity that preserve a stated public-service promise after stated losses.

Pass 75 showed that an unprepared `PTS-50` receiver requiring ten complete `PTS-5` cells after loss of any two of three mobile access modes needs thirty complete mobile cell-equivalents: ten by road, ten by rail and ten by navigable water. It also showed that nine fixed cells plus one complete cell on each mobile mode can reduce that inventory to twelve, but only while the fixed anchor survives.

This pass tests the choices between those poles. It adds fixed-island failure, source and supplier ancestry, launch bases, crew schools, control partitions, heat-sink lineages, receiver boundaries, reset and concurrent national allocation. It then assigns seventy-two provisional `PTS-50` groups to the thirty-two Service Catchment Areas created in Pass 32.

The central result is an architectural correction.

The most interesting clean-sheet option is not initially a spectacular fourth carrier. It is a more radical form of prepared geography: **ten independently releasable one-cell service islands per `PTS-50` group, plus one complete cell on each of three legacy access modes**. This `T7` topology carries thirteen complete packages per group. If one fixed island and any two mobile modes fail, nine fixed cells and one surviving mobile cell remain. The ten-cell public contract closes exactly.

That result depends on a constraint that preserves the Department's projection mission. If mobile inventory were allowed to fall to zero, eleven independent fixed cells could survive one fixed-cell loss with only eleven packages. That would be a receiving lattice, not a force-projection organization. This pass therefore requires at least one complete cell on each credited mobile access mode.

The thirteen-package result does not validate the topology. It moves the hardest requirement from fleet multiplication into neighborhood-scale fixed independence. Ten sites located on one floodplain, substation, district heat sink, control root, workforce pool or civil-release authority are one island with ten labels. `T7` receives no admission credit until those dependencies are serialized and failed in representative campaigns.

The seven universal alternatives produce the following twenty-five-year boundaries in constant 2026 dollars:

| Topology | Fixed cells | Access modes | Cells per mode | Packages per group | Retained after one fixed-island loss plus two mode losses | National lifecycle |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `T1` expeditionary triple replication | 0 | 3 | 10 | 30 | 10 | $8.402T |
| `T2` concentrated forward anchor | 9 | 3 | 1 | 12 | 1 | $4.477T |
| `T3` federated forward lattice | 9 in 3 islands | 3 | 2 | 15 | 8 | $5.090T |
| `T4` balanced six-cell hybrid | 6 in 3 islands | 3 | 4 | 18 | 8 | $5.785T |
| `T5` four-path expeditionary lattice | 0 | 4 | 5 | 20 | 10 | $6.956T |
| `T6` layered eight-cell lattice | 8 in 4 islands | 4 | 2 | 16 | 10 | $5.561T |
| `T7` ten-island neighborhood lattice | 10 in 10 islands | 3 | 1 | 13 | 10 | $4.654T |

The least expensive row is not the strongest row. `T2` is cheap because it treats a nine-cell anchor as one surviving fact. It passes only three of eighteen declared worlds. `T6` passes seventeen and fails only total fixed-lattice loss plus one access-mode loss. `T7` passes sixteen. It also fails total fixed-lattice loss plus one mode and a source-lineage-plus-mode compound. World counts are indexes into failure identities, not weighted scores.

The geography-assigned research portfolio uses `T7` where the screen identifies dense or compound receiving geography, `T6` for island, territorial and inland hard-access classes, `T3` for other high-exposure multimodal catchments and `T4` for the remaining regional multimodal class. Across seventy-two groups, it contains:

- 607 fixed complete cells;
- 153 complete road cells;
- 153 complete rail cells;
- 153 complete water cells;
- 22 provisional fourth-path cells; and
- 1,088 total complete packages.

Its base twenty-five-year program is approximately **$5.165 trillion**, with a structured low-to-high planning range of **$4.153–6.936 trillion**. This is not a probability interval or budget estimate. It is a way to keep fixed-site, legacy-mobile, fourth-path and common-program uncertainty visible without allowing one point estimate to decide doctrine.

The pass therefore recommends no procurement winner. It recommends a research reference and a disciplined fork:

1. challenge the ten-island topology with actual fixed-island physics, siting, authority, workforce and covariance; and
2. continue the clean-sheet fourth-access competition because island, territorial and hard-access geography may not be able to create ten genuinely independent fixed service islands.

Rendering remains blocked. The program now knows more clearly what must be drawn later, but it does not yet know enough to let an image become an accidental requirement.

## 1. Pass stocktake before adding another design layer

### 1.1 What Pass 75 made authoritative

Pass 75 established four durable rules for this research program.

First, the acquisition object is a complete source–host–receiver configuration rather than an attractive source, carrier or subsystem.

Second, a `PTS-50` group owes ten simultaneous complete `PTS-5` service cells. Partial energy, cooling, controls or distribution modules do not add into service unless the missing functions arrive and release on time.

Third, access diversity is counted in complete cell-equivalents. Twelve cells divided into three mutually dependent fragments do not become thirty independent cells.

Fourth, an unprepared-receiver promise and an extreme access-loss promise create an enormous replication burden. The burden can be moved into prepared geography, but it cannot be wished away.

### 1.2 What Pass 75 left unresolved

The prior theorem treated fixed cells as one scalar `p`. It did not specify whether nine fixed cells occupied one site, nine sites, three utility islands or one common workforce and authority domain. It also did not connect the seventy-two provisional groups to the national receiving geography.

That omission permitted two opposite errors.

One error was to reject prepared capacity as inherently fragile because one anchor could fail. The other was to credit every fixed cell as independent because it occupied a different equipment pad. Neither follows from first principles.

This pass therefore makes fixed-island count, access-mode count, mobile replication and failure ancestry explicit design variables.

### 1.3 Quality rule for the pass

Every claimed improvement must identify where the burden moved.

- Fewer mobile packages move burden into land, civil works, local stocks, heat fields, staff, inspections and authority.
- More fixed islands move burden into distributed ownership, configuration control, exercises and common-mode surveillance.
- A fourth access path moves burden into a new carrier, node, energy, crew, base, weather and regulatory enterprise.
- More source or supplier domains move burden into smaller production lots, interchange, qualification and dormant capacity.
- Faster reset moves burden into depots, quarantine, spares, wash, repair, certification and crew depth.

An architecture that reduces a visible fleet by hiding these burdens is not more efficient. It is less completely described.

## 2. The readiness topology is the force-design object

A mature Department would define a topology as:

```text
TOPOLOGY = COMPLETE SERVICE CELLS
           × FIXED-ISLAND PARTITION
           × ACCESS-DOMAIN PARTITION
           × SOURCE AND HEAT LINEAGES
           × BASE AND DEPOT PARTITION
           × CREW AND CONTROL PARTITION
           × RECEIVER AND AUTHORITY PARTITION
           × RESET AND NATIONAL ALLOCATION
```

The word “partition” is demanding. It means that the Department can identify which physical and institutional objects fail together. A map marker, vendor name or separate budget line is not a partition.

The topology owns the guarantee. Individual programs supply pieces of it.

This changes organizational behavior. A road systems prime cannot declare victory when a convoy reaches a transfer node. A receiving command cannot declare capacity when it has a building without an independent heat destination. An energy office cannot count a generator whose fuel lot, protection firmware or operator school is common to every nominal alternative.

## 3. Generalized access and fixed-island bounds

Let:

- `R` be the required complete service cells;
- `p` be independently releasable fixed cells;
- `d` be the number of fixed service islands;
- `m` be credited mobile access modes;
- `k` be mobile modes lost; and
- `q` be complete cells carried on each symmetric mobile mode.

The simple access condition is:

```text
p + (m − k)q ≥ R
```

The minimum symmetric mobile loading is:

```text
q ≥ ceiling((R − p) / (m − k))
```

Total complete packages are:

```text
N = p + mq
```

If one fixed island containing the largest fixed-cell share also fails, the condition becomes:

```text
p − ceiling(p / d) + (m − k)q ≥ R
```

These expressions are lower bounds. They assume that every surviving package is complete, its receiver can release service, modes are independent, and mobile cells can be allocated to the affected geography inside the deadline.

### 3.1 Why the twelve-package anchor fails

For `T2`, `p=9`, `d=1`, `m=3`, `k=2` and `q=1`.

With the anchor intact:

```text
9 + (3 − 2) × 1 = 10
```

With the anchor lost:

```text
9 − ceiling(9 / 1) + (3 − 2) × 1 = 1
```

The architecture closes transport denial and fails geography denial. Calling the anchor “hardened” does not change the arithmetic. Hardening has value only after its hazards, utilities, heat sink, workforce, control and authority are measured.

### 3.2 Why the four-path architecture is useful but not automatically best

For `T5`, `p=0`, `m=4`, `k=2` and `q=5`:

```text
0 + (4 − 2) × 5 = 10
N = 0 + 4 × 5 = 20
```

The fourth path reduces the unprepared lower bound from thirty to twenty complete packages. Across seventy-two groups, it reduces the all-in base program by about $1.446 trillion relative to `T1`, even with a provisional $4.5-billion lifecycle cost for each fourth-path cell.

That is a legitimate technology pull. It is not yet operational credit. If the fourth path shares the receiver, source lot, launch base, crew school, control root or weather regime of another path, the value can disappear in the compound world.

### 3.3 The thirteen-package topology

For `T7`, `p=10`, `d=10`, `m=3`, `k=2` and `q=1`:

```text
10 − ceiling(10 / 10) + (3 − 2) × 1 = 10
N = 10 + 3 × 1 = 13
```

The result is not a loophole. It states the burden plainly: ten fixed cells must behave as ten separate service islands. Each island requires its own safe source and conversion path, final heat destination, protected controls, receiver boundary, local crew or remotely supportable operating state, civil release and stock custody.

The topology retains one complete cell on each legacy mode because the Department is a projection force. If `q` were allowed to equal zero, eleven independent fixed cells could survive loss of one and meet a ten-cell local promise with eleven packages. That architecture belongs in the receiving lattice, not in the projected force.

### 3.4 The theorem does not select policy

The equations expose feasible package counts. They do not decide:

- how much fixed exposure is acceptable;
- whether ten islands can be independent inside one urban hazard field;
- how many groups must remain available for a second simultaneous region;
- whether local governments and communities accept the land, workforce and authority obligations;
- whether a mobile cell can cross jurisdictions and enter service inside forty-eight hours; or
- whether national industrial capacity can build and sustain the resulting diversity.

Those are the subjects of the force program, not caveats outside it.

## 4. Seven competing force architectures

### `T1`: expeditionary triple replication

`T1` is the pure projection boundary. It can serve an unprepared receiver after any two of three access modes fail because every mode carries ten complete cells. It needs 2,160 complete packages across seventy-two groups and produces the largest lifecycle burden.

Its apparent independence remains vulnerable to common sources, control, crew and receivers. It passes sixteen of eighteen worlds under the declared partitions and fails all three legacy modes lost and the receiver-interface-domain loss.

### `T2`: concentrated forward anchor

`T2` is the minimum twelve-package transport-denial solution. It demonstrates why counting fixed cells without island structure is dangerous. It passes baseline, one-mode loss and two-mode loss, then collapses under fixed loss, source ancestry, crew, control, heat, receiver, reset and national-pool screens.

Its low cost is the economic value of an untested common mode.

### `T3`: federated forward lattice

`T3` places nine fixed cells in three islands and two cells on each legacy mobile mode. It closes one fixed-island plus one-mode loss but retains only eight cells after one island and two modes fail. It is a plausible high-exposure intermediate when a fourth path is unavailable and ten fixed islands cannot yet be created.

### `T4`: balanced six-cell hybrid

`T4` moves more capacity back into the mobile force: six fixed cells in three islands and four cells per legacy mode. It has useful mobile depth and retains twelve cells after an island plus one mode fail. It still falls to eight after an island plus two modes fail.

### `T5`: four-path expeditionary lattice

`T5` is the clean-sheet mobility bet. It keeps the receiver unprepared and uses five complete cells on each of four access domains. It is more efficient than three-mode replication, but its twenty packages remain expensive and the fourth domain is unproved.

### `T6`: layered eight-cell lattice

`T6` combines eight fixed cells in four islands with two cells on each of four mobile paths. It is the strongest architecture in the declared world set: seventeen of eighteen worlds pass. It closes an island plus two modes, source plus one mode, base plus one mode, second mission and national-pool loss. It fails only the intentionally severe case in which the entire fixed lattice and one access mode disappear.

The architecture is valuable for island, territorial and hard-access research because those geographies may need a non-legacy access path. Its national base cost is $5.561 trillion.

### `T7`: ten-island neighborhood lattice

`T7` is the emergent architecture. It uses thirteen packages per group and costs $4.654 trillion as a universal seventy-two-group comparator. Compared with `T2`, a $177-billion national premium buys ten fixed islands instead of one anchor and one extra fixed cell. Compared with `T6`, it saves about $907 billion by using three legacy modes and much more prepared capacity.

`T7` passes sixteen worlds. It fails total fixed-lattice loss plus one route, as expected. It also fails source-lineage plus one access-mode loss, retaining nine cells. That failure matters. Fine-grained sites do not guarantee fine-grained source ancestry. The next design must cross fixed-island identity with source, heat, supplier, control and workforce identity rather than assigning each coordinate independently.

## 5. Geography changes force composition

The national allocation inherits all thirty-two Pass 32 Service Catchment Areas. It does not create sites or commands. Every SCA receives one group; the remaining forty groups follow population by a largest-remainder rule.

The class rules are deliberately simple and reproducible:

| Receiver class | Rule | SCAs | Groups | Research topology |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| `C1` island / territorial | non-CONUS | 3 | 3 | `T6` |
| `C2` inland hard-access | no Pass 32 water-access flag | 4 | 8 | `T6` |
| `C3` dense / compound | population at least 15 million or NRI composite at least 95 | 12 | 33 | `T7` |
| `C4` multimodal high-exposure | remaining NRI composite at least 90 | 3 | 7 | `T3` |
| `C5` regional multimodal | remaining SCA | 10 | 21 | `T4` |

These rules do not rank human worth, predict events or claim that an NRI score is a failure probability. They assign a research topology based on access and the provisional opportunity to distribute fixed service. Every class retains the same ten-cell service contract.

The assignment is intentionally contestable. Dense geography may offer many candidate buildings and utility boundaries, but those sites may share flood, heat, grid, labor and authority. A large SCA may cross several states and contain more topology diversity than one class can express. Island and territorial access modes cannot simply be labeled road, rail, water and fourth path; they require locally meaningful domains such as sea, air, prepared stock and local conversion.

The selected portfolio is therefore an experimental allocation, not a siting recommendation.

## 6. Failure ancestry is a first-class military-equivalent discipline

The Department requires a **National Resilience Configuration and Ancestry Service** with authority comparable to a military airworthiness, nuclear surety or configuration-control institution.

It should serialize at least:

- prepared service islands and their hazard fields;
- substations, circuits, switchgear, protection firmware and grounding;
- heat destinations, rejection trains, water sources and smoke regimes;
- energy sources, feedstocks, fuel lots and conversion equipment;
- road corridors, rail terminals, waterways, launches and transfer nodes;
- launch bases, reset depots, cranes, loaders and quarantine facilities;
- crew schools, qualifications, simulators and certification authorities;
- control roots, offline identities, time sources and communications;
- receiver interfaces, building owners, inspectors and civil-release authorities;
- primes, factories, upstream materials, software and critical components; and
- second-mission stocks, spares, wash, repair and recertification capacity.

The service should maintain a live dependency graph, not a static registry. Campaign planners should be able to ask: “If this flood field, signing root, supplier lot, crew school and rail terminal fail together, which public-service claims remain?”

The government must own the schema, interface rights and test evidence. Contractors can build implementations, but they cannot own the only map of how national capacity fails.

## 7. The fourth access domain as a technology program

The fourth path remains strategically important even though `T7` improves the fixed solution. Some receiving geographies cannot create ten independent islands. Others may lose their fixed lattice as the same disaster creates demand. A fourth path can reduce replication and diversify strategic reach.

The program should compete mechanisms, not choose a silhouette.

### 7.1 High-mass atmospheric access

One branch should investigate hybrid buoyant lift, large distributed-electric aircraft, amphibious ground-effect carriers and other clean-sheet systems able to move complete cell sets without conventional runways or ports.

The metric is not payload brochure mass. It is complete-cell arrival and release after accounting for carrier tare, weather, energy, crew, loading, landing or hover field, noise, downwash, ground handling, receiver access, maintenance and return.

The system may use several packets and sorties. It earns one cell only when every required function is present and public service is released.

### 7.2 Autonomously emplaced utility corridors

Rapidly installed power, thermal, water, wastewater, communications or fluid corridors could substitute for transported subsystems. Robotic trenching, autonomous pipe and cable trains, temporary bridge manifolds, high-voltage direct-current links, modular pumping stations and self-surveying protection could change the cell mass balance.

These systems should be credited as **functional substitution paths**, not automatically as fourth access modes. A pipeline from a floating desalination source may be a powerful independent water path. It is not a complete power-and-thermal cell unless source, heat, control, distribution, receiver and public release also close.

### 7.3 Local conversion and manufacturing seeds

A compact seed force could turn local fuels, water, aggregates, scrap, structural members or standardized stocks into service equipment. Mobile microfactories, automated inspection, additive repair, chemical synthesis and robotic construction could reduce the mass that travels.

The approach is a fourth path only if qualified feedstock, energy, process control, product acceptance and production time survive the same event. Otherwise, it is a prepared-site dependency.

### 7.4 Prepositioned protected stock

Subsurface caches, protected vertical stores, hardened utility vaults and distributed building kits can be extremely valuable. They are fixed readiness, not mobility. Correct classification matters because their hazards and replenishment belong in the fixed-island ancestry.

### 7.5 Research scale

The workbook assigns $35 billion to the independent fourth-access path, $30 billion to heavy air and ground-effect access, $12 billion to distributed austere nodes and additional funds to cross-mode exchange, receiver acceptance, offline control and full-scale campaigns. The broader topology technology portfolio totals $320 billion over twenty-five years.

This is the appropriate scale for a Department expected to drive technology rather than shop for whatever the existing transport market happens to offer.

## 8. The fixed service island as a clean-sheet national system

`T7` implies 720 fixed `PTS-5` cells across a universal seventy-two-group program. These are not generators parked behind ordinary buildings. Each island is a purpose-built public-service system responsible for approximately 5,000 people under the Pass 72 contract.

A mature island could include:

- multiple source interfaces and a bounded autonomous source;
- electrical protection, storage, black start and district distribution;
- useful thermal production, clean-air support and final heat rejection;
- water, wastewater and fire-water interfaces needed for occupancy;
- protected local controls and offline operating authority;
- hardened connection corridors to a portfolio of buildings and refuge nodes;
- local stocks, spares, adapters and accessible service equipment;
- robotic survey, connection, inspection and repair systems;
- a trained local cadre backed by regional technical forces; and
- an exercised alternate receiver and mobile-cell docking boundary.

Its innovation program is at least as ambitious as a new carrier. It requires fault-contained neighborhood microgrids, high-temperature and smoke-tolerant rejection, autonomous utility construction, machine-verifiable building passports, distributed safety controls, rapid civil release and public interfaces designed for emergency occupation.

The program must also prevent its own standardization from becoming a national common mode. Universal interfaces should permit substitution while protection implementations, source lineages, control roots and suppliers remain diverse.

## 9. A Department organized around topologies

The force implied by this pass is not a larger emergency-management agency. It is a permanent national service institution with operational commands, arsenals, schools, test authorities and industrial programs.

### National Service Lattice Command

Owns fixed service islands, receiver interfaces, local compacts, public-service contracts and readiness. It treats land, utility boundaries, heat destinations, building portfolios and civil release as operational capacity.

### Access Forces Command

Owns road, rail, shallow-water and future access formations as complete origin-to-effect chains. Its readiness measure is service released at the receiver, not vehicles available or cargo delivered.

### Receiver Engineering Command

Surveys, prepares, activates and repairs occupied-service geography. It operates robotic acceptance, utility connection, structural and environmental assessment, clean-air, accessibility and alternate-boundary systems.

### National Resilience Configuration and Ancestry Service

Owns the mission compiler, interface definitions, serialized dependency graph, configuration passports and compound-failure analysis.

### Industrial Mobilization Command

Maintains competing prime ecosystems, government arsenals, dormant production, strategic inputs, workforce pipelines, second sources and cold-start exercises. It measures industrial independence through upstream lineage, not logo count.

### Resilience Personnel and Schools Command

Creates multiple qualification lineages for operators, maintainers, engineers, logisticians, medical and public-health staff, inspectors, civil-release officers and campaign commanders. It exercises school loss and rapid reciprocal certification.

### Independent Public Effect Test Authority

Admits no topology on component performance alone. It runs multi-week, compound-denial campaigns and verifies that safe, accessible and accountable public service actually begins and repeats.

### Service Catchment Commands

Maintain regional topology options without turning an SCA into a jurisdiction. They integrate federal capacity with state, local, territorial, Tribal, utility, health, labor and community authority.

## 10. Program economics

The model replaces the $2.564-trillion Pass 75 cell layer with topology-specific complete packages, retains $2.129 trillion in common national systems and adds $140 billion above the Pass 74 technology account to create the $320-billion topology research portfolio.

Representative complete-cell lifecycle boundaries are:

| Position | Configured capital | Mature annual O&S | 25-year lifecycle |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| fixed prepared cell | $1.100B | $0.086B | $2.460B |
| road complete cell | $1.200B | $0.081B | $2.479B |
| rail complete cell | $1.350B | $0.092B | $2.799B |
| water complete cell | $1.550B | $0.107B | $3.239B |
| fourth-path complete cell | $2.200B | $0.145B | $4.500B |

The fourth-path figure is a declared pre-prototype comparator. The fixed figure is also incomplete until land, hardening, workforce, heat rejection, local agreements, recertification and replacement are engineered. Their apparent precision exists to reconcile the model, not to imply estimating maturity.

### 10.1 What the cost differences buy

`T2` to `T7` adds about $177 billion nationally. The modeled purchase is not one more reserve cell alone. It is conversion of one nine-cell anchor into ten one-cell islands while retaining one mobile cell per legacy mode.

`T7` to `T6` adds about $907 billion. The modeled purchase is lower fixed dependence, a fourth mobile path, greater source-plus-route compound margin and four-island rather than ten-island operation.

`T7` to `T1` adds about $3.748 trillion. The modeled purchase is the ability to project ten complete cells through any one surviving legacy mode without assuming fixed service survives.

These are different public guarantees. A cost comparison without the guarantee is meaningless.

### 10.2 Geography-assigned portfolio

The selected research portfolio assigns:

| Topology | SCAs | Groups |
|---|---:|---:|
| `T6` | 7 | 11 |
| `T7` | 12 | 33 |
| `T3` | 3 | 7 |
| `T4` | 10 | 21 |

The program's $5.165-trillion base exceeds a universal `T7` force because island, territorial, inland and regional geographies receive more mobile depth. Its 1,088 packages are still almost half the 2,160-package `T1` fleet.

The low case is not a procurement promise and the high case is not a worst case. They apply different structured multipliers to common systems, fixed cells, legacy mobile cells, fourth-path cells and the topology technology increment. Schedule, inflation, financing, local contributions, displacement, environmental remediation and macroeconomic crowding remain outside the model.

## 11. Industrial economy

A force of this scale creates a new industrial base rather than a specialist disaster market.

The fixed-service economy includes switchgear, power electronics, storage, thermal machinery, heat exchangers, pumps, protected controls, water and wastewater systems, building interfaces, structural and civil works, sensors, robotics, fire safety, accessible refuge equipment and long-term maintenance.

The mobile economy includes heavy road formations, rail consists, shallow-water vessels, transfer nodes, loading systems, autonomous route recovery, energy logistics, maintenance depots and potentially a new atmospheric or ground-effect transport sector.

The institutional economy includes schools, simulators, certification, public-health laboratories, independent testing, configuration data, industrial mobilization, community agreements and a permanent technical workforce.

Exports should be organized around complete service architectures and partner sovereignty. A partner should be able to acquire an island, access formation, training pipeline, evidence standard and lifecycle-support system without becoming dependent on a remote proprietary control root. Export success therefore requires modular authority, local production, transparent interfaces and negotiated custody.

The Department should use production reservations, recurring cold starts, long-term framework contracts, government-owned tooling where necessary and competitive complete-system demonstrations. Industrial plurality must survive teardown to upstream materials, software, labor and facilities.

## 12. Acquisition and research sequence

### Phase I: topology articles

Build full-scale fixed and mobile `PTS-5` articles. Require common service boundaries but diverse sources, heat destinations, controls and suppliers. Do not yet optimize appearance.

### Phase II: service-island federation

Create representative two-, four- and ten-island `PTS-50` geographies. Test whether separate sites actually fail independently across hazard, utility, heat, workforce, control, authority and stock custody.

### Phase III: access-domain competition

Run complete road, rail, shallow-water and clean-sheet fourth-path chains against the same cell contract. Include node damage, receiver ambiguity, communications loss, crew loss and second mission.

### Phase IV: cross-coordinate campaigns

Fail island plus route, source plus route, base plus route, control plus receiver, and school plus second mission. Require safe public effect, not equipment survival.

### Phase V: national allocation

Replace the class rules with site-route portfolios, hazard covariance, travel time, industrial readiness, workforce and authority. Optimize against multiple simultaneous campaigns without allowing lower service rights for remote or territorial populations.

### Phase VI: design authorization

Only after the system requirements survive these campaigns should the Department authorize detailed vehicle and facility renderings. At that point, images will communicate an earned architecture rather than determine it.

## 13. Evidence and falsification

All thirty evidence gates remain open. The most consequential are:

1. prove ten complete cells after any two legacy access losses at an unprepared receiver;
2. prove ten complete cells after one fixed-island loss and any two access losses;
3. prove that ten fixed islands do not share one hazard, utility, heat, workforce, control, supplier or authority ancestor;
4. demonstrate a complete fourth access path rather than a novel carrier alone;
5. deliver and release representative road, rail and shallow-water cells through damaged nodes;
6. substitute source and heat lineages across primes under live load;
7. sustain safe offline control and civil release for seventy-two hours;
8. restore at least ninety percent of complete cells for a second mission;
9. allocate at least ninety percent of groups during concurrent regional demand; and
10. bound fixed, mobile and fourth-path lifecycle costs with independent estimates.

The architecture should be rejected or revised if:

- fixed-island covariance turns ten nominal islands into fewer than the required independent domains;
- the source-plus-route compound cannot be closed without material extra inventory;
- the fourth access path cannot carry or assemble complete cells inside the service deadline;
- local staffing and authority make fixed readiness unavailable during the event;
- national simultaneous demand consumes the mobile reserve assumed by local topologies;
- reset capacity produces a one-mission force; or
- environmental, safety, rights or community requirements prevent lawful service release.

## 14. Pass 76 stocktake

### Now authoritative within the project

- Readiness topology, not fleet count, is the correct group-level acquisition abstraction.
- The generalized access bound is `p + (m-k)q ≥ R`.
- The mixed fixed-and-mobile bound is `p - ceiling(p/d) + (m-k)q ≥ R` for loss of the largest symmetric fixed island.
- A projection force requires a nonzero mobile floor; a purely fixed numerical solution is a different institution.
- `T2` proves that twelve packages close only a narrow transport-denial promise.
- `T7` proves that thirteen packages can close one fixed-cell-island plus two legacy-mode losses if ten fixed cells are genuinely independent.
- Access, source, base, crew, control, heat, receiver and reset ancestry must be crossed in compound worlds.
- The thirty-two existing SCAs can carry a reproducible provisional allocation of seventy-two groups without being treated as sites or jurisdictions.

### Falsified or materially weakened

- A single prepared anchor is not an adequate national reference architecture.
- A fourth mobile path is not automatically the most efficient way to improve assurance.
- More fixed cells do not imply less ambition. Cell-scale fixed federation demands a new national infrastructure, robotics, controls, industrial and civil-authority program.
- Mode labels and vendor names do not demonstrate independence.
- A universal topology is unlikely to fit island, inland, dense and regional receiving geography equally well.

### Still provisional

- Every unit cost and cost multiplier.
- The five receiver-class rules and their topology assignments.
- The physical form, footprint and achievable independence of a fixed `PTS-5` island.
- The existence, mass flow and operational independence of a fourth access domain.
- The number and geographic distribution of bases, depots, schools, suppliers and control partitions.
- The eighteen world severities and ancestry-loss arithmetic.
- Any site, route, platform, production quantity or acquisition schedule.
