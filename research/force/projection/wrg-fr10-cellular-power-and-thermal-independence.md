# Cellular power and thermal independence

## A common power plant recreates the common source

> What must a Water Service Cell carry, control and survive for “independent power” to be a real service-chain property rather than a generator label?

Pass 69 found the first hard power contradiction in the projected-water chain. The `C3+E2` regeneration-and-dewatering architecture requires 1.575 MW of critical average power. Its inherited 2 MW allocation appears comfortable until available output falls to 75 percent: 1.5 MW remains, and the water system fails. Pass 70 then represented receiver power as one of twelve end-to-end independence domains, but assigned each complete cell only a 160-tonne placeholder. That graph could distinguish common and separate power domains; it could not show whether any power domain worked.

This pass replaces the placeholder with a first-principles power-and-thermal competition. It adds receiver-island load, separates continuous and starting power, distinguishes power from energy, makes heat rejection co-governing, registers fuel and stored-energy endurance, tests generation and thermal module loss, and requires every cell to black start, protect itself, operate locally without connected control and exchange power only through optional fault-blocking ties.

The companion [cellular power and thermal model](../../../models/force/projection/wrg-fr10-cellular-power-and-thermal-independence.xlsx) contains eleven linked sheets: declared assumptions, four rival architectures, twelve architecture-cell balances, seventeen formation worlds, a one-cut dependency register, formation reconstruction, twenty-five-year program economics, sensitivity sweeps, ten clean-sheet technology programs, twenty representative gates and sixty terminal integrity checks.

The result is severe but useful. A large common plant passes seven of sixteen service worlds while qualifying no independent cell. Separate engine islands reach ten worlds and still qualify none. Hybrid black-start cells reach twelve worlds and still qualify none because module loss, thermal margin, energy lineage and reset remain open. Only the clean-sheet multi-vector cellular architecture qualifies all three cells and passes all sixteen service worlds plus the lawful universal stop under the declared arithmetic.

That is not admission. The winning comparator is 11,250 dry tonnes, 12,960 land-chargeable tonnes, 598 deployed crew and $9.72 billion of equipment per three-cell formation. Its replacement power program is $232.80 billion and raises the whole twenty-four-formation `K4` comparator to $796.88 billion over twenty-five years. All twenty representative gates remain open. Fuel/feedstock wet mass is not closed. No prime mover is selected. Rendering remains blocked.

## Executive judgment

Twelve findings should govern the next stage of Department of Resilience utility design.

First, **more generation is not power resilience**. `P1` has 6 MW of common dispatchable capacity against 2.475 MW of primary-pair critical average load. It survives broad scalar derating and a large generation-module loss. It still loses all service when its common black-start chain, bus, fuel lineage, control plane, energized tie or reset path fails. Capacity margin cannot compensate for topological dependence.

Second, **a separate generator is not a separate utility**. `P2` gives each cell its own dispatchable set and bus. The two regenerative cells still miss their 2.396 MW peak/start requirement, fail at 75-percent output, and lose nearly all long-duration power when their single source module is removed. Common start, fuel, thermal supervision, control and reset dependencies also remain. Physical separation at one box does not close the service chain.

Third, **storage buys specific temporal effects**. `P3` uses batteries to form each island, start loads, cover transients and bridge the 25-percent derating for twenty-four hours. It does not turn four or six megawatt-hours into fourteen days of energy, replace a lost dispatchable module indefinitely, reject process heat or create another fuel lineage. “Battery-backed” is not a complete endurance statement.

Fourth, **heat rejection is coequal with electrical production**. A process can have adequate electric power and still trip, derate or become unsafe when heat cannot leave. The regenerated inland cells require a declared 1.8 MWth of rejection. `P3` retains only 1.75 MWth when its thermal system falls to 70-percent output. The miss is small and decisive.

Fifth, **cell readiness and formation survival are different measures**. `P2` and `P3` preserve the 5,000 m³/day public floor in several worlds because Cell A or the reserve can cover for a failed regenerative cell. That does not qualify the failed cell for deployment, reserve credit or a second theater. A force can pass an aggregate service screen while owning zero ready cells.

Sixth, **black start is an operating sequence, not a component attribute**. The cell must energize auxiliaries, establish voltage and frequency, ground the temporary system, coordinate protection, pick up loads in order, absorb inrush, start additional modules, and enter a stable local operating state. A common starting cabinet or remote identity service can invalidate several otherwise separate generators.

Seventh, **mutual aid must not become a propagation path**. Cells may share standards and may exchange power after deliberate release. No cell may require the tie to meet minimum service. A tie must be normally open or equivalently bounded, locally isolable, fault blocking and able to reject an unsafe synchronization command. Interoperability is desirable; runtime dependence is not.

Eighth, **energy independence is an ancestry problem**. Two fuel tanks filled from one contaminated lot are one lineage. Two nominal fuels derived from one precursor plant may be one lineage. A multi-vector architecture earns credit only when conversion equipment, feedstock, custody, delivery, testing and reset can survive a named hold without silently converging upstream.

Ninth, **the endurance burden is larger than the battery line**. The primary pair requires 1,247 MWh net over twenty-one days at critical average load. All three cells concurrently require 2,142 MWh. The P4 formation carries fourteen MWh of electrochemical storage for start and bounded ride-through—not campaign energy. Conversion efficiency, fuel or feedstock volume, wet mass, tankage, fire separation and carrier positions remain unresolved.

Tenth, **power independence is an industrial structure**. It requires several active source implementations, distinct critical suppliers, replaceable modules, independent reset pathways, exercised substitution, qualified cold and hot variants, a trained utility workforce and government-owned interfaces. It is not achieved by buying three copies from one production line and painting different serial numbers on them.

Eleventh, **P4 is a requirements envelope rather than a technology verdict**. Reciprocating machines, turbines, fuel cells, batteries, thermal stores, renewable inputs, closed-cycle sources, microreactors and other systems may occupy different cells and geographies. They compete against the same net-service, module-loss, heat, mass, safety, maintenance and independence contract.

Twelfth, **this closes one water-derived contradiction and opens a force-wide design rule**. Every medical, communications, shelter, food, mobility, flood and fire formation needs the same separation between source, network, load, thermal effect, fuel, control and regeneration. The method should now move beyond water rather than continue indefinitely down one subsystem.

## 1. The correction to the inherited architecture

### 1.1 What Pass 69 actually proved

The [closed-loop endurance study](wrg-fr10-closed-loop-water-endurance.md) added regeneration, endogenous chemistry, media restoration, residual dewatering and thermal equipment to the inland treatment chain. Its `E2` architecture reached 1.575 MW of critical average load while retaining the inherited 2 MW installed allocation.

The relevant balance was:

```text
available power = 2.000 MW × 0.75 = 1.500 MW
critical average = 1.575 MW
margin = -0.075 MW
```

That screen correctly denied the architecture. It did not establish what combination of generation, storage, starting support, distribution or cooling would close the failure.

### 1.2 What Pass 70 could and could not say

The [end-to-end independence study](wrg-fr10-end-to-end-independence.md) then made power one of twelve failure domains. The upper `K4` comparator contained three complete Water Service Cells:

- Cell A: one protected-aquifer `C2` chain;
- Cell B: one variable-feed `C3+E2` chain; and
- Cell R: one separately generated `C3+E2` reserve chain.

Each cell received its own power-domain identity. That was sufficient for a structural cut analysis. It was not a physical configuration. Each power cell was represented by 160 dry tonnes, 190 chargeable tonnes, eight crew and $0.14 billion of equipment. Those values intentionally prevented independence from remaining massless, but they were placeholders.

Pass 71 removes all three placeholders before adding a rival power architecture. The non-power `K4` base is therefore:

| Quantity | Pass 70 `K4` | Removed power allowance | Non-power base |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| dry mass | 10,090 t | 480 t | 9,610 t |
| chargeable mass | 11,650 t | 570 t | 11,080 t |
| deployed crew | 568 | 24 | 544 |
| equipment | $8.29B | $0.42B | $7.87B |

This subtraction is essential. Without it, a detailed power subsystem would be added on top of an invisible prior allowance and the formation would double count capacity it never defined.

### 1.3 The receiver is now inside the power balance

Source-process power is not the whole cell. Reception, product transfer, storage, pressure, quality work, controls, lighting, communications and protected operating spaces require power even when the source machine already carries an internal allocation.

Pass 71 declares 0.20 MW of receiver-island load per cell. It is a controlled hypothesis, not a measured receiver design. It changes the critical averages to:

| Cell | Source/process MW | Receiver-island MW | Whole-cell critical MW | Peak factor | Peak/start MW | Heat rejection MWth |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A — `C2` | 0.500 | 0.200 | 0.700 | 1.35 | 0.945 | 0.450 |
| B — `C3+E2` | 1.575 | 0.200 | 1.775 | 1.35 | 2.396 | 1.800 |
| R — `C3+E2` | 1.575 | 0.200 | 1.775 | 1.35 | 2.396 | 1.800 |

The 1.35 peak factor is deliberately simple. It stands in for motor starting, transformer inrush, pump sequencing, regeneration cycles and transient support. Representative work must replace it with a time-resolved load schedule and protected one-line diagram.

## 2. The complete power-and-thermal contract

A Water Service Cell is power-ready only when it can meet the public-service claim through the following linked functions.

### 2.1 Continuous electric balance

For every operating interval:

```text
net dispatchable output
+ imported power released through a valid interface
+ storage discharge
= protected load
+ conversion and network loss
+ auxiliary load
+ storage charging
```

The workbook uses net installed output and includes source and receiver critical loads. It does not yet model reactive power, harmonic behavior, fault duty or time-step dispatch. Those belong to representative configuration evidence.

### 2.2 Peak and starting balance

Continuous output does not prove the island can start. The declared screen requires:

```text
dispatchable output + storage discharge power ≥ peak/start load
```

Storage power and storage energy are separate. A high-power pack may start a pump and still contain too little energy for a long source outage.

### 2.3 Bounded ride-through

For a challenge lasting `T` hours, the simplified balance is:

```text
derated dispatchable MW + usable storage MWh / T ≥ critical MW
```

This is a conservative average-energy screen, not a dispatch model. It locates whether storage can plausibly bridge a declared deficit. It does not credit recharge during the same deficit or assume a repair arrives before the registered horizon.

### 2.4 Largest-module loss

Each cell must preserve critical average service for twenty-four hours after its largest dispatchable module is removed:

```text
installed MW - largest module MW + storage MWh / 24 ≥ critical MW
```

The P4 dispatchable train goes further: installed output after module loss already exceeds critical average load. Storage therefore remains available for transients, start and additional failure management rather than being consumed merely to claim `N+1`.

### 2.5 Thermal balance

Electrical sufficiency is conditional on safe heat removal:

```text
available heat rejection MWth ≥ process + conversion + storage + enclosure heat MWth
```

The workbook applies two distinct thermal tests:

1. 70-percent available output under high ambient, dust or smoke; and
2. loss of the largest thermal module.

Thermal water consumption, plume, noise, exhaust interaction, intake fouling, icing and worker exposure remain outside the current scalar and inside the evidence gates.

### 2.6 Organic energy endurance

The cell must preserve service for fourteen days without external fuel delivery. P4 declares twenty-one days, leaving seven days of planning reserve. That declaration is not allowed into dry mass as though it were weightless.

At critical average load:

```text
primary pair: 2.475 MW × 24 h/day × 21 days = 1,247.4 MWh net
all three cells: 4.250 MW × 24 h/day × 21 days = 2,142.0 MWh net
```

The installed fourteen MWh of electrochemical storage is less than one percent of the three-cell campaign-energy quantity. Its mission is start, transient support and bounded ride-through. The eventual energy configuration must publish conversion efficiency, usable feedstock energy, parasitic loads, tank and transfer systems, degradation, maintenance, hazard separation and replenishment.

### 2.7 Grid formation, protection and local control

Every cell requires:

- a dark-start source for essential auxiliaries;
- a local voltage and frequency reference;
- grounding and protection matched to temporary topology;
- controlled load pickup and restart after trip;
- fault recording and configuration evidence;
- a manual physical stop;
- local operation without a national network or cloud identity service; and
- a safe transfer sequence when connecting to a recovering utility or another cell.

The common technical architecture may standardize messages, connectors, voltage classes, evidence and authority states. It may not require one remote service for safe local operation.

### 2.8 Optional exchange

A healthy cell may voluntarily support another cell only after its own service reserve is protected. The exchange interface must be:

- normally open or equivalently bounded;
- physically and logically isolable at both ends;
- protected against reverse energization and fault propagation;
- unable to override local stop or quality authority;
- unnecessary for the donor's minimum service; and
- recorded as a temporary dependency that changes both cells' readiness state.

This is the power equivalent of a sanitary cross-connection. A tie that silently makes two islands one system defeats the reason for having cells.

## 3. Four rival architectures

### 3.1 `P1`: shared central utility island

`P1` fields one 6 MW common dispatchable plant with two nominal 3 MW source modules, one common bus, one black-start chain, one cooling system, one fuel estate, one connected control plane and one reset path. Its size is intentionally generous relative to the 2.475 MW primary-pair critical average.

The architecture demonstrates why aggregate margin is an incomplete metric. It passes the baseline, grid-loss, 25-percent derating, generation-module-loss, high-ambient, thermal-module-loss and cold-start worlds. It fails every world in which the shared operating chain is the challenge. It also cannot send an independent reserve power cell to a second theater while the plant remains committed to the first.

`P1` passes seven of sixteen service worlds and eight of seventeen correct behaviors, including the universal safe stop. It qualifies zero cells because no cell owns an independent power path.

Its 650 dry tonnes, 720 chargeable tonnes, eighteen deployed crew and $0.55 billion of equipment make it the lightest comparator. That apparent efficiency is purchased by collapsing several cells back into one utility.

### 3.2 `P2`: isolated engine islands

`P2` gives Cell A a 1.2 MW set and gives Cells B and R 2 MW sets. It separates three buses and can fail an inter-cell tie open. It retains common black start, fuel ancestry, thermal supervision, connected control and depot support.

The architecture fails even before those common modes are challenged. Cell B and the reserve each require 2.396 MW of peak/start support against 2.25 MW of declared generator-plus-storage discharge power. At 75-percent dispatchable availability, each carries only about 1.51 MW for the twenty-four-hour screen against a 1.775 MW critical requirement. Loss of its single source module leaves only a negligible storage bridge.

The formation nonetheless passes ten service worlds because Cell A or the reserve often preserves the 5,000 m³/day floor. That aggregate result must not be reported as three-cell readiness. `P2` qualifies zero cells.

Its declared increment is 840 dry tonnes, 950 chargeable tonnes, thirty deployed crew and $0.78 billion of equipment.

### 3.3 `P3`: hybrid black-start cells

`P3` gives each cell local grid-forming storage, offline control, an isolated bus and a separate thermal zone. Cell A receives 1 MW / 2 MWh of storage; Cells B and R each receive 1.5 MW / 4 MWh. The dispatchable trains use two modules per cell.

The hybrid closes important failures. Every cell can meet the declared peak/start load. Storage bridges the 25-percent output derating for twenty-four hours. Cells can start and operate without the wide-area control plane. Bus and tie faults remain bounded.

The remaining failures show the limit of hybridization:

- loss of one generation module leaves Cell A at a negative 0.017 MW twenty-four-hour margin and each regenerative cell at negative 0.508 MW;
- the regenerative cells retain negative 0.050 MWth under the high-ambient thermal case;
- loss of a thermal module removes 0.125 MWth of Cell A margin and 0.550 MWth from each regenerative cell;
- seven organic fuel days do not cover the fourteen-day delivery denial; and
- one energy lineage and one reset pathway remain common.

`P3` passes twelve service worlds and thirteen correct behaviors, yet qualifies zero complete cells. The distinction is central: storage can improve formation survival without creating a deployable independent service object.

Its declared increment is 1,160 dry tonnes, 1,320 chargeable tonnes, forty-two deployed crew and $1.20 billion of equipment.

### 3.4 `P4`: multi-vector cellular power force

`P4` makes the power-and-thermal system part of each Water Service Cell rather than a shared theater utility. Each cell owns dispatchable modules, stored energy, heat rejection, protection, conversion, organic energy custody, local control, maintenance and reset interfaces.

| Cell | Dispatchable train | Storage | Heat rejection | Thermal train | Whole-cell critical | Peak/start |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 1.65 MW, `3 × 0.55` | 1 MW / 2 MWh | 0.75 MWth | `3 × 0.25` | 0.700 MW | 0.945 MW |
| B | 3.00 MW, `3 × 1.00` | 1.5 MW / 6 MWh | 2.70 MWth | `3 × 0.90` | 1.775 MW | 2.396 MW |
| R | 3.00 MW, `3 × 1.00` | 1.5 MW / 6 MWh | 2.70 MWth | `3 × 0.90` | 1.775 MW | 2.396 MW |

The formation has three bus zones, three thermal zones, three energy lineages, two energy vectors per cell, three reset pathways, local black start, offline control and optional fail-open exchange.

The declared configuration closes every cell-level test. In the tight regenerative cell:

```text
steady electrical margin = 3.000 - 1.775 = 1.225 MW
peak/start margin = 3.000 + 1.500 - 2.396 = 2.104 MW
75% / 24 h margin = 3.000×0.75 + 6/24 - 1.775 = 0.725 MW
generation-module-loss / 24 h margin = 2.000 + 6/24 - 1.775 = 0.475 MW
70% thermal margin = 2.700×0.70 - 1.800 = 0.090 MWth
thermal-module-loss margin = 2.700 - 0.900 - 1.800 = 0.000 MWth
```

Two observations follow.

The electrical train has deliberate overmatch. Its post-module-loss dispatchable output is already 0.225 MW above critical average before storage. That preserves the battery for start, transient response or another bounded fault.

The thermal train is much tighter. It only equals required load after one module loss and retains 0.090 MWth in the declared high-ambient case. That is an acquisition warning, not reassurance.

`P4` qualifies all three cells and passes all sixteen service worlds plus the lawful stop. It is the only rival allowed to advance to representative competition. It is not admitted.

## 5. The one-cut power graph

Ten power-specific domains determine whether the three cells are genuinely separate.

| Domain | `P1` | `P2` | `P3` | `P4` requirement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| dispatchable source | common | distinct | distinct | distinct `N+1` train per cell |
| black start | common | common | distinct | distinct local dark start |
| bus and protection | common | distinct | distinct | distinct with fault-blocking ties |
| stored energy | common | minimal | distinct | distinct fire-separated zones |
| energy lineage | common | common | common | three formation lineages, two vectors per cell |
| heat rejection | common | common | distinct | distinct `N+1` thermal train per cell |
| controls and identity | common | common | distinct | local offline operation and evidence |
| cross-tie | common | fail-open | fail-open | optional, fail isolated, not required |
| crew | common | partly common | distinct watches | cell-local electrical and thermal watch |
| reset and depot | common | common | common | three qualified pathways |

P4 does not prohibit common standards or coordinated dispatch. It prohibits a common runtime dependency from being necessary for minimum service. That distinction permits an integrated force without rebuilding a centralized single point of failure.

Dependency inspection must extend upstream. The government should be able to ask:

- Do two “independent” inverter lines use the same control component or firmware library?
- Do three fuel vectors rely on the same precursor, refinery, terminal, additive or test laboratory?
- Do separate thermal modules use one pump, fan controller, coolant or maintenance tool?
- Do different depots depend on one electric substation, water utility, credential service or specialist team?
- Has a reserve cell borrowed a black-start technician or battery module from the committed pair?

The answer changes readiness state. Independence is not a certification that remains true after configuration borrowing.

These are planning coefficients, not object-level designs. They do not yet establish container count, module dimensions, axle loading, center of gravity, lifting points, hazardous separation, cable reel positions, thermal intake and exhaust geometry, setup sequence or carrier fit.

The organic energy-custody line includes tanks, conditioning and transfer equipment. It does not include the mission fuel or feedstock itself. That wet burden must be derived after the conversion competition establishes heat rate or efficiency, then carried into the serial manifest.

### 6.1 A cell-local operating detachment

The fifty-four-person formation increment is not a pool of generator mechanics. Each cell needs a complete power-and-thermal watch capable of local action.

Core roles include:

- electrical authority and switching officer;
- grid-forming and source operators;
- protection, grounding and power-quality specialists;
- storage and high-voltage safety technicians;
- thermal-system and environmental-control operators;
- fuel or feedstock custody technicians;
- distribution and connection crews;
- controls, evidence and configuration technicians; and
- rotating-machine, power-electronic and thermal maintenance.

The exact watch bill remains open. P4 receives credit only because the model assigns separate watches and does not require one specialist to start or safely stop every cell. Representative trials must establish actual task time, fatigue, licensing and relief depth.

### 6.2 The group headquarters must remain nonessential

The Water Service Group may compare load, schedule maintenance, authorize optional exchange and coordinate with the local utility. A cell must not need group headquarters for protection, black start, safe stop or minimum dispatch.

Headquarters is therefore an allocation and evidence layer, not a common supervisory controller. If communications fail, every cell retains a locally bounded authority state and later reconciles its records.

## 7. Sensitivity and breakpoints

The P4 regenerative cell has two important deterministic breakpoints.

### 7.1 Twenty-four-hour electrical derating

With 6 MWh of storage and a 24-hour horizon:

```text
minimum dispatchable fraction
= (1.775 MW - 6 MWh / 24 h) / 3.000 MW
= 50.8 percent
```

At 50 percent output, the B-cell margin becomes negative 0.025 MW. The primary pair delivers approximately 9,930 m³/day and fails the 10,000 m³/day world. At 60 percent, the B-cell regains 0.275 MW of margin and the pair closes.

This threshold depends on the twenty-four-hour horizon. Extending the failure without repair reduces storage's average contribution. The workbook does not allow that temporal dependency to masquerade as permanent power.

### 7.2 High-ambient thermal output

The B-cell thermal threshold is:

```text
minimum thermal availability = 1.800 / 2.700 = 66.7 percent
```

At 60 percent, the margin is negative 0.180 MWth and the primary pair falls to 9,500 m³/day. At 65 percent it still fails. The declared 70-percent case retains only 0.090 MWth, 3.3 percentage points above the break.

This is the narrowest continuous P4 margin. Early prototypes should therefore prioritize real high-ambient, ash, dust, smoke, salt and partial-blockage trials rather than treating cooling as an auxiliary detail.

### 7.3 Mass and cost scale

Varying only the P4 power subsystem from 75 to 125 percent changes whole-formation dry mass from 10,840 to 11,660 tonnes around the 11,250-tonne base.

Varying only the replacement power-program comparator from 80 to 120 percent changes the revised whole `K4` program from $750.32 billion to $843.44 billion around the $796.88 billion base.

These are scale tests, not uncertainty intervals. They show that the conclusion does not depend on a narrow mass or cost point: full cellular power independence is a major acquisition program under every declared case.

## 9. The clean-sheet technology portfolio

P4 should be acquired as ten coupled technology competitions funded inside the declared $90 billion research and industrialization envelope.

### 9.1 Modular dispatchable source trains — $16B

Compete high-specific-power reciprocating, turbine, fuel-cell, closed-cycle and other source modules against net output after auxiliaries, ambient derating, maintenance and largest-module loss. The source that produces the best test-stand power but needs common cooling, unique tools or long depot repair may lose at whole-cell level.

### 9.2 Pulse and ride-through storage — $9B

Compete storage chemistries and architectures for dark start, motor inrush, grid formation, source trip and bounded ride-through. Fire separation, degraded cold energy, hot operation, replaceable modules and safe isolation matter as much as nominal MWh.

### 9.3 Extreme-climate heat rejection — $10B

Develop air, liquid, evaporative and hybrid systems that preserve net MWth in heat, dust, smoke, salt, icing and partial blockage. The program should treat plume, noise, cooling-water demand, intake fouling, worker exposure and transport geometry as system outputs.

### 9.4 Multi-vector energy logistics — $11B

Develop field custody, conditioning and conversion for genuinely distinct fuels or feedstocks. The program includes supplier ancestry, local-resource qualification, contamination detection, precursor separation and regional partner production. “Dual fuel” earns no resilience credit if both options share one upstream cut.

### 9.5 Solid-state protection and fault-blocking ties — $8B

Develop rapid isolation, DC/AC protection, keyed interfaces, adaptive-but-bounded settings, dead-bus verification and synchronization systems that allow optional exchange without propagation. The government should own the interface and evidence standard.

### 9.6 Robotic rapid power corridors — $8B

Develop machines that survey, pull, place, trench, elevate, connect, ground, test and patrol temporary conductors and switchgear. Their metric is protected MW safely commissioned per crew-hour under damaged conditions—not metres of cable handled in isolation.

### 9.7 Offline autonomous utility control — $7B

Develop local dispatch, anomaly detection, maintenance support, signed authority, configuration records and later reconciliation without continuous external connectivity. Autonomy must reduce workload while retaining human physical stop and safe manual operation.

### 9.8 Thermally integrated water processes — $6B

Compete reversible heat exchange, waste-heat use and thermal storage across water and power cells. Coupling receives credit only when it reduces whole-chain mass or energy without allowing one process fault, contamination event or maintenance state to remove both services.

### 9.9 Strategic long-endurance sources — $9B

Maintain microreactor, geothermal, closed-cycle and other firm-source paths for selected coastal, inland and fixed-anchor missions. These are not baseline assumptions. They enter a cell only after safety, transport, exclusion, waste, authority, maintenance and end-of-mission boundaries close.

### 9.10 Thermal-service projection — $6B

Extend the power program beyond electricity. Develop mobile cooling, heat, air-quality and thermal-storage cells plus prepared-building interfaces. The decisive metric is people-hours maintained inside a declared thermal envelope. Megawatts at a temporary bus are an intermediate output.

## 10. Industrial organization

The Department needs a power-and-thermal industrial base with the productive depth associated with defense acquisition but a different public effect.

### 10.1 Prime integration around service cells

Prime contractors should deliver a configured cell that closes source, storage, heat, protection, distribution, control, fuel, maintenance, crew and reset. The government should reject contracts that optimize one box while treating the rest as host support.

The prime earns performance against:

- net safe service under the locked mission configuration;
- setup and black-start time;
- largest-module-loss output;
- high-ambient and cold performance;
- energy and thermal efficiency;
- deployable and chargeable mass;
- crew-hours and maintenance burden;
- safe fault containment;
- supplier and software independence; and
- second-mission readiness.

### 10.2 Government-owned interfaces

The Department should own voltage classes, grounding states, protection evidence, physical connectors, control messages, configuration records, authority tokens and safe separation rules. Vendor systems may innovate behind those interfaces.

Open interfaces do not mean every implementation should be identical. Critical stop, protection and control paths may require deliberately diverse implementations. Commonality reduces training and inventory; diversity limits correlated failure. Acquisition must optimize that tension openly.

### 10.3 Several living lineages

The government should fund more than paper alternatives. A second lineage is real only if its factories, supplier tiers, software, tooling, people, test facilities and materials are periodically exercised. Dormant drawings do not create surge or independence.

No-notice substitution trials should require cells to accept another qualified source, storage, protection or thermal module without losing service or evidence custody. Failures become interface and industrial-program requirements.

### 10.4 Export structure

The export object is not a generator set. Partners could acquire:

- complete power-and-thermal cells;
- prepared receiver interfaces;
- regional energy custody and reset systems;
- training and certification;
- test ranges and evidence standards;
- sovereign offline control implementations; and
- local production rights for selected modules and spares.

The partner retains switching, safety and employment authority. Interoperability should allow multinational cells to exchange service without requiring permanent remote contractor control.

## 11. Command, readiness and employment

### 11.1 Four readiness statements

Power reporting should distinguish:

1. **module ready** — one component meets its bench or subsystem specification;
2. **cell power-ready** — one complete cell meets steady, peak, derating, module-loss, thermal, endurance, control and safety requirements;
3. **pair power-ready** — two service cells retain the normal effect and minimum floor under the registered single failures; and
4. **depth power-ready** — the pair can remain committed while an independently generated cell supports another theater or a compound loss.

A warehouse can contain many ready modules and no power-ready cell. A formation can preserve one mission and possess no depth.

### 11.2 Authority boundaries

The **generating service** owns the people, equipment, configuration, maintenance and readiness record.

The **local electrical authority** controls switching, grounding, protection release, energization and safe separation inside its delegated boundary.

The **campaign commander** allocates effects, priorities, locations and reserve. The commander cannot order an electrically unsafe energization or count the same reserve against two simultaneous claims.

The **host utility or owner** retains or resumes system authority through a declared interface. The Department does not create an unrecorded parallel grid.

The **independent test authority** owns blinded challenges, evidence custody and admission. The acquiring program may not certify itself by completing a workbook.

### 11.3 Load shedding as civil doctrine

When capacity falls, load shedding must follow public-service priorities rather than equipment convenience. Every cell requires a signed load registry that distinguishes no-break, survival, stabilization and recovery loads, their tolerated outage and the civilian effect of disconnection.

The water cell's internal hierarchy should protect quality control, safe stop, minimum pressure, critical treatment, communications and crew safety before discretionary throughput. A power shortfall may reduce product rate. It may not disable the ability to detect unsafe operation or isolate contaminated product.

## 12. Representative evidence contract

Twenty gates govern the move beyond the deterministic model.

1. cold black start from a dead bus with actual auxiliaries and load sequence;
2. twenty-four-hour operation at 75-percent dispatchable output;
3. largest generation module removed for twenty-four hours;
4. 30-percent heat-rejection derating at boundary ambient, dust and smoke;
5. largest thermal module removed under full process load;
6. fourteen-day external fuel-delivery denial;
7. one energy lot, precursor or supplier lineage held;
8. seventy-two-hour connected-control and identity denial;
9. arc, ground and protection fault inside one island;
10. energized cross-tie fault and false synchronization command;
11. actual motor start, transformer inrush and protected-load ride-through;
12. wet-ground, step/touch, backfeed and temporary-grounding challenge;
13. storage fire or thermal-runaway isolation with an adjacent cell operating;
14. exhaust, plume, noise, heat and habitability at an occupied receiver;
15. independent reset and recertification after depot denial;
16. freeze, thaw, recharge and restart with degraded storage;
17. optional island synchronization, exchange and separation without outage;
18. integrated source-process-receiver trial with complete energy and heat ledgers;
19. blinded thirty-day mission with maintenance and one surprise failure; and
20. immediate second mission after field reset and configuration audit.

Every trial locks the whole configuration. A vendor may not replace the fuel, cooling loop, source module, cable, control service, crew specialty, test instrument or prepared receiver after seeing the challenge.

Results must publish:

- safe water delivered at the public receiver;
- every interruption and load shed;
- net generation, storage state and auxiliary demand;
- heat generated and rejected;
- fuel or feedstock ancestry, consumption and remaining endurance;
- power quality, grounding, protection and disturbance records;
- setup, restart, repair and module-exchange time;
- maintenance tasks and crew exposure;
- unsafe commands rejected or accepted;
- configuration borrowing among cells; and
- state at the start of the next mission.

All twenty gates remain open. Strict admission is denied and rendering remains blocked.

## 13. What this changes outside water

The water lineage was useful because it forced a supposedly secondary power system into the foreground. The same structure applies across the force.

### Medical care

A hospital or surgical cell needs no-break clinical loads, oxygen and vacuum, sterilization, imaging, blood and pharmaceutical cold chain, isolation pressure, cooling, waste, patient movement and staff habitability. A common generator or common chiller can remove several nominally independent clinical units.

### Heat and cooling protection

The public effect is people-hours inside a safe thermal and air-quality envelope. The formation must close building entry, load measurement, electrical service, chillers or heaters, pumps, air distribution, filtration, condensate or water demand, accessible occupancy and local operation.

### Communications

Power autonomy must cover radios, fiber bypass, satellite terminals, local compute, identity, cooling, towers, repeaters and household access. A resilient communications network that depends on one common fuel route or cloud credential service is not independent.

### Food and essential supply

Cold storage, safe preparation, pumping, ventilation, packaging, handling, distribution and public access convert power into food continuity. Electricity at a warehouse does not preserve households if last-mile refrigeration or cooking fails.

### Flood control

Pumping requires starting overmatch, head-dependent load, fuel or grid access, debris-tolerant intake, heat rejection, discharge permission and alternate routes. A flood fleet organized around pump nameplate will reproduce the same error as the common water plant.

### Fire control

Airbases, retardant plants, water points, dispatch, sensors, communications, maintenance and crew protection are power and thermal systems. Distributed aviation cells need independent energy and control if one smoke-affected base is not to remove the fleet.

### Earthmoving and access

Electric, hybrid or fuel-powered machines shift the logistics problem but do not remove it. Charging or fueling, thermal conditioning, spares, autonomous-control custody and maintenance must close at the work front and through regeneration.

The general rule is:

> A service formation does not possess independent power until the entire source-to-effect-and-reset energy chain survives as an independent graph.

## 14. Research boundary and next move

The model is an executable argument, not a validated design. It does not establish:

- a selected prime mover or storage chemistry;
- a time-resolved electrical one-line or protection study;
- heat-exchanger, radiator, cooling-tower or plume geometry;
- actual fuel/feedstock volume, wet mass or carrier positions;
- environmental, emissions, noise or exclusion boundaries;
- object dimensions or deployment sequence;
- reliability, repair or maintenance distributions;
- representative crew workload;
- a safety case for any nuclear or other strategic source; or
- a procurement recommendation.

The pass does establish a stronger acquisition object. The Department should not buy a larger shared generator fleet for its service cells. It should compete deployable utilities whose electrical, thermal, energy, control, workforce and reset boundaries close together.

The next force-design pass should use this contract to widen the research program. It should define a **multi-effect Power and Thermal Service Cell** for a seven-day heat-blackout mission: not only electric megawatts, but occupied-building connection, cooling and air-quality output, medical-device continuity, water pumping, communications, fuel or energy endurance, household access and safe transfer. That move connects the mature water method to the New York neighborhood seam and begins balancing the corpus across mission domains.

Renderings should remain deferred. A useful future rendering must show source modules, storage fire zones, protection and conversion, thermal intakes and rejection, fuel or feedstock custody, cable and switchgear handling, local control, crew workspaces, cross-tie boundaries, maintenance access and receiver connection. A heroic generator silhouette would hide the service system the research has now made visible.
