# `PTS-5` fixed service-island requirements and independence compiler

## The prepared geography is itself a first-line force system

**Research status:** first-principles requirements and architecture competition; six island designs and seven federations tested; no architecture admitted

**Decision date:** 8 September 2026

**Companion model:** [`pts5-fixed-service-island-independence-compiler.xlsx`](../../../models/force/projection/pts5-fixed-service-island-independence-compiler.xlsx)

## Executive judgment

The Department of Resilience should reject the idea that a fixed service island is a hardened building containing backup equipment. A mature island is a complete, independently releasable **civic metabolism system**: safe sources, conversion, black start, final heat destinations, water production, wastewater and fire water, protected distribution, occupied-building interfaces, local control, communications, crews, legal authority, consumables, repair, docking and reset. Its boundary is defined by what can fail together, not by a fence or an organizational label.

This correction matters because Pass 76 placed most of the proposed national power-and-thermal force in prepared geography. Its differentiated portfolio contained 607 fixed `PTS-5` cells. Its most package-efficient universal rival—ten fixed cells plus one road, one rail and one navigable-water cell per `PTS-50` group—appeared to preserve the ten-cell service floor with only thirteen complete packages. That conclusion was arithmetically true only if the ten fixed cells behaved as ten independent islands.

This pass compiles what that independence would require. Six fixed-island architectures face twenty-four declared behavior worlds. A conventional hardened utility hub passes one. An `N+1` campus passes four. A dual-campus system passes seventeen. A five-node neighborhood mesh passes eighteen. A ten-node sovereign mesh passes twenty-two. Only the clean-sheet `A6` **twelve-node civic metabolism fabric** closes all twenty-four declared behaviors. It does so with two reserve service nodes, three firm source yards, five energy and black-start paths, five final heat destinations, three water and wastewater trains, segmented potable and fire-water storage, five utility corridors, five control partitions, four communications paths, five workforce lineages, three authority paths, ten supplier lineages, two mobile exchange docks and a local repair-and-conversion layer.

That is a behavior result, not evidence. All thirty-four representative gates remain open. No island is admitted, no federation is admitted and no rendering is authorized.

The national topology result also changes. Ten fixed cells with five common ancestry domains are not the thirteen-package solution implied by Pass 76. A correlated domain loss removes two fixed cells; after two mobile modes are also lost, only nine complete cells remain. The genuine thirteen-package solution, `G2`, requires ten fixed cells separated across **ten decisive ancestry partitions** plus one cell on each of three mobile modes. It is not enough to have ten sites. Energy source, final heat destination, buried route, control root, workforce, authority, supplier, protection system and reset dependencies must each be partitioned strongly enough that the governing correlated loss removes no more than one fixed cell.

There is a more forgiving alternative. `G3` adds an eleventh fixed island. Eleven islands across six balanced ancestry domains plus the same three mobile cells retain ten complete cells after a two-cell correlated fixed loss and loss of any two mobile modes. It uses fourteen packages rather than thirteen, but it relaxes four additional domain partitions. At the configured `A6` lifecycle cost of $3.776 billion per island, the break-even price of those four additional partitions is about **$944 million per partition per group**. Below that amount, strict ten-way separation is cheaper; above it, one spare fixed island is cheaper. This is not merely an accounting result. It shows that independence is a design commodity with a measurable alternative cost.

The clean-sheet island is estimated at $1.8 billion configured capital and $125 million in mature annual operations, producing a $3.776 billion twenty-five-year lifecycle comparator under the inherited ramp convention. Universal `G2` produces a $5.791 trillion national boundary. Recompiling the 607 fixed cells in the Pass 76 differentiated portfolio around `A6`, while retaining its mobile force, common program and fourth-path hypotheses, produces a $6.099 trillion base boundary. These are architecture accounts in constant 2026 dollars, not budget estimates.

The result is ambitious by design. The island assumes technology that does not yet exist as an integrated civil system: source-agnostic grid-forming power blocks; high-temperature, low-water heat rejection; underground reconfigurable utility corridors; distributed water metabolism; autonomous inspection and isolation; fail-operational local controls; machine-readable civil authority; robotic repair; and microfactories able to restore standardized components without importing an intact industrial ecosystem. The Department should fund those gaps as a coupled national technology mission, not ask existing utilities to assemble them from commercial catalogs.

The program adds a $135 billion fixed-island technology increment to the Pass 76 portfolio, raising the relevant technology account to $455 billion. The aim is not novelty. It is to make a prepared service cell behave as a sovereign public-service unit after the failures most likely to reveal hidden dependence.

## 1. Stocktake before opening the fixed island

### 1.1 What Pass 76 made authoritative

Pass 76 made readiness topology—the governed relationship among fixed capacity, mobile access, receiving geography and failure ancestry—the force-design object. It established the lower-bound equation for a required service floor `R`:

```text
p − ceiling(p / d) + (m − k)q ≥ R
```

where `p` is fixed cells, `d` is fixed islands, `m` is mobile modes, `k` is modes lost and `q` is complete cells per mobile mode. It also retained at least one mobile cell on every credited mode so a prepared system remained a projection force rather than becoming local infrastructure alone.

The pass made seven topology rivals, thirty-two receiving geographies, seventy-two provisional `PTS-50` groups and the geography-assignment method current force-design references. It rejected the twelve-package concentrated anchor as the preferred structure and showed that a compact fixed/mobile federation could outperform pure mobile replication in package count.

### 1.2 What it did not prove

Pass 76 used island count as a proxy for independence. The proxy was deliberately provisional. It did not specify whether nominally separate islands shared:

- one upstream substation, fuel terminal or firm source;
- one river, aquifer, cooling field or atmospheric operating regime;
- one buried utility throat or access corridor;
- one supervisory controller, credential root or time source;
- one operating workforce, school or relief pool;
- one mayoral, state or federal release authority;
- one vendor, firmware branch, process-material lot or spare-part lineage;
- one maintenance depot, wash line or certification authority; or
- one floodplain, fire exposure, security perimeter or contamination plume.

If they did, the fixed lattice could disappear in blocks larger than one cell. The package-count advantage would have been purchased by hiding correlated loss inside the word “island.”

### 1.3 The quality rule

This pass makes a stronger claim falsifiable: a fixed island receives independence credit only to the extent that its complete public service survives the registered loss without borrowing an uncounted source, sink, route, crew, authority or reset chain from another island.

More equipment is not automatically more independence. `N+1` machines behind one switchboard remain one electrical object. Two chillers exhausting into one failed heat sink remain zero cooling. Five water trains sharing one contaminated intake remain one source. Separate controllers signed by one unavailable credential root remain one authority path. Ten neighborhood sites staffed by one commuting workforce remain one labor domain.

The burden must be visible wherever it moves.

## 2. The service island as a complete civil boundary

### 2.1 Reference public contract

The island inherits one `PTS-5` service cell: safe heat-blackout continuity for 5,000 people, equal to one tenth of the `PTS-50` group. The planning floor is 90 percent of the required public-service bundle for 168 hours, with governed service entry by hour 48. The island is not permitted to preserve an electrical nameplate while losing occupied-building cooling, water, medical devices, vertical access, communications or lawful service release.

The inherited requirement is:

| Quantity | Reference requirement |
| --- | ---: |
| Continuous electricity | 1.42216 MWe |
| Peak electricity | 1.91992 MWe |
| Useful cooling | 1.965 MWth |
| Design heat rejection | 4.8 MWth |
| Seven-day energy | 238.92288 MWh |
| Potable/process water | 225 m³/day |
| Wastewater acceptance | 180 m³/day |
| Occupied-building interfaces | 10 |
| Initial connection labor | 128 crew-hours |
| Clean-water bridge | 450 m³ |
| Separate fire-water reserve | 1,000 m³ |
| Black-start continuity | 6 hours |

These numbers are not a complete design. They are a common mission surface on which competing island architectures can be compared.

### 2.2 Eleven inseparable service functions

An island exists only when eleven functions close together.

1. **Firm energy:** a source or portfolio that supplies the continuous and peak electrical contract through the declared campaign.
2. **Black start and stabilization:** enough separated stored energy, inverter capacity, protection and local control to restart without the regional grid.
3. **Useful thermal service:** conversion equipment that actually produces cooling or heat at occupied interfaces.
4. **Final heat destination:** a lawful physical place for rejected heat under ambient, water and contamination constraints.
5. **Water metabolism:** intake or inventory, treatment, pressure, storage, testing, distribution and residual handling.
6. **Wastewater and fire water:** separate safe acceptance and a protected firefighting reserve that cannot be consumed as ordinary potable inventory.
7. **Distribution and building entry:** routes, transformers, pumps, thermal loops, isolation, meters and standardized interfaces across occupied civil terrain.
8. **Control and communications:** local operation without cloud, wide-area identity or one supervisory root, while preserving auditable exchange with the larger force.
9. **Workforce and authority:** qualified watches, relief, safety, public-health and civil-release decisions available even when the normal jurisdiction is degraded.
10. **Repair and supply:** stocks, tools, materials, process consumables, quarantine, fabrication and certified return-to-service.
11. **Mobile exchange and reset:** interfaces through which projected cells, casualties, stocks, waste and damaged modules can enter, leave and regenerate.

The island is therefore closer to a small sovereign utility district, emergency public-health system and depot fused into one governed formation than to a generator park.

### 2.3 What “fixed” means

Fixed does not mean passive. It means the high-mass, site-specific and routinely maintained parts of the service chain already occupy the receiving geography. A mature island may contain mobile machines, removable storage, robotic repair systems and rapidly reconfigurable corridors. Its fixed character comes from prepared rights, buried networks, heat destinations, protected inventories, trained local personnel, rehearsed authority and known interfaces.

That distinction preserves strategic mobility. Mobile cells bring reinforcement, substitute sources, rare specialists and cross-theater depth. Fixed islands prevent the national force from hauling every pipe, transformer, tank, heat exchanger, access agreement and operator into every catastrophe.

## 3. The internal independence theorem

For any island service dimension, let:

- `C` be installed capacity;
- `r` be the required service floor;
- `n` be the number of nominal nodes or paths;
- `L` be the largest capacity share removed by the registered loss;
- `a` be the fraction still autonomous after external support loss; and
- `t` be the duration for which the surviving system can hold the service.

Behavioral closure requires:

```text
C − L ≥ r
a(C − L) ≥ r for the autonomous interval
t ≥ the declared bridge or campaign duration
```

This capacity condition is necessary and insufficient. The surviving capacity must also have a source, final sink, route, controller, crew, legal release and reset path. The complete-island condition is therefore the intersection, not the sum, of surviving functions:

```text
SERVICE = min(energy, thermal, heat destination, water, waste,
              distribution, control, workforce, authority, supply, reset)
```

An island passes a world only when every required dimension remains at or above the public floor. Excess electricity cannot compensate for zero wastewater acceptance. Spare cooling cannot substitute for an unavailable release authority. This “minimum operator” is the civil equivalent of combat-system kill-chain closure: the weakest indispensable link governs the effect.

## 4. Six rival island architectures

### 4.1 `A1` — conventional hardened utility hub

`A1` concentrates one energy lineage, one heat destination, one water and waste path, one control root and one workforce domain in a hardened site. Its capital is comparatively low at $900 million. It passes only the baseline world. Machine hardening does not protect against source, sink, route, software, labor, authority or reset loss.

This is the architecture that conventional infrastructure programs are most likely to call resilient. In this study it is retained as a negative control.

### 4.2 `A2` — `N+1` hardened campus

`A2` adds spare machines, a second black-start path, two local nodes and improved distribution. It passes four of twenty-four behavior worlds. It survives selected component losses but remains one campus, one energy ancestry, one final heat destination, one workforce lineage and one supplier family.

Its failure demonstrates the distinction between **redundancy**, which duplicates components, and **independence**, which separates the conditions that make components usable.

### 4.3 `A3` — dual-campus service island

`A3` creates two sites, energy lineages, heat destinations, water paths, waste paths, control partitions, communications paths, workforce lineages, authority paths and supplier lineages. It passes seventeen worlds. It begins to behave as an island rather than a facility, but five service nodes are insufficient for a two-node loss at the occupied edge. Ninety-six hours of autonomy also fails the seven-day denial, and shared reset remains a decisive weakness.

### 4.4 `A4` — five-node neighborhood mesh

`A4` distributes conversion, control and access across five hazard zones and three lineages. It passes eighteen worlds. Its limitation is not mainly generation. It lacks enough reserve service nodes, water and waste margin, supplier depth and long-duration autonomy to preserve a 90-percent complete bundle through compound loss.

### 4.5 `A5` — ten-node sovereign service mesh

`A5` assigns one active service node to each occupied-building interface, uses five energy lineages, segmented controls and two mobile exchange docks. It passes twenty-two worlds. It fails the declared two-node occupied-edge loss because ten active nodes leave no full-service reserve. It also fails the reset world because its local repair-and-certification factor is 0.88 against a 0.90 floor.

`A5` is important because it shows where ordinary distributed-energy thinking stops. A fine-grained mesh can preserve operation while still being unable to recover, certify and repeat the mission.

### 4.6 `A6` — twelve-node civic metabolism fabric

`A6` is the clean-sheet upper research comparator. Twelve nodes provide ten active-equivalent service positions and two reserves. Installed electrical conversion reaches 2.2 times the continuous reference, useful cooling 2.4 MWth and final heat rejection 8 MWth. The heat system is spread across five destinations so no single destination controls the island.

Three 120 m³/day water trains provide 360 m³/day; three 90 m³/day wastewater trains provide 270 m³/day. Six separated 90 m³ potable tanks retain the full 450 m³ bridge after one tank loss. A distinct 1,000 m³ fire-water reserve prevents routine service from consuming the firefighting function.

The twelve nodes share five protected corridor families but are placed in twelve hazard zones. Forty-eight MWh of partitioned storage supports black start, ride-through and controlled service shedding. Five control partitions and four communications paths allow local operation without turning every node into an uncoordinated machine. Five workforce lineages, three authority paths and ten supplier lineages seek to keep operation, public-health release, safety action and repair available after one domain is withheld.

The island includes two complete mobile docks. These are not parking areas. Each is a governed exchange boundary for power, thermal service, potable water, wastewater, data, personnel, stores, damaged modules and custody. The docks allow a projected cell to reinforce or substitute for part of the fixed island without improvising interfaces during the event.

Finally, `A6` includes a **civic metabolism microfactory**. It is not expected to manufacture turbines or reactors. It holds digital product definitions, metrology, controlled materials, machine tools, additive and subtractive production, cable and hose fabrication, power electronics repair, water-quality consumables, seal and fitting production, decontamination, quarantine and test stands. Its mission is to keep standardized service nodes operable when national supply is delayed and to restore modules to an auditable intermediate state before regional depot work.

`A6` passes all twenty-four declared behavior worlds. It still receives zero strict admissions because no representative article has proved the coupled behavior.

## 5. The registered island worlds

The twenty-four worlds are a compact failure grammar, not a probability distribution. They include baseline release; loss of a firm energy yard, peak inverter, storage partition, useful-cooling nodes, final heat destination, water train, wastewater train, potable tank, fire-water separation, corridor, control partition, communications path, workforce lineage, civil authority, supplier lineage, mobile dock and reset layer; seven-day external denial; heat-wave derating; cold-start conditions; smoke or contamination; two-node loss; and an internal evidence-withheld stop.

World counts cannot be read as reliability percentages. A design that passes twenty-two easy or duplicative worlds may be inferior to one that passes a smaller number containing the governing public catastrophe. The register exists to preserve failure identity and force rivals to reveal where their burden moved.

The internal evidence-withheld world is deliberately impossible to admit. It separates calculated behavior from empirical authority. Every architecture can look complete in a spreadsheet; none may become a procurement baseline until measurements establish source behavior, heat rejection, connection time, protection coordination, potable integrity, local controls, workforce performance, civil release and reset under representative conditions.

## 6. Federation mathematics after opening the island

### 6.1 The corrected national bound

Let:

- `p` be fixed cells in a `PTS-50` group;
- `d` be the minimum number of ancestry partitions across decisive domains;
- `m` be mobile access modes;
- `k` be modes lost;
- `q` be complete cells on each surviving mobile mode; and
- `R=10` be the required complete-cell floor.

If fixed cells are balanced across `d` domains, the largest correlated fixed loss is at least:

```text
L = ceiling(p / d)
```

The federation closes only if:

```text
p − ceiling(p / d) + (m − k)q ≥ R
```

For ten fixed cells, three mobile modes, loss of two modes and one cell per mode:

```text
10 − ceiling(10 / d) + 1 ≥ 10
```

Therefore `ceiling(10/d) ≤ 1`, which requires `d=10`. The thirteen-package result is inseparable from ten-way decisive-domain partition.

### 6.2 Seven federation rivals

| Code | Structure | Packages/group | Largest fixed-domain loss | Retained after ancestry + two modes | Behavior worlds passed |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `G1` | 10 fixed / 5 domains / 3 mobile | 13 | 2 | 9 | 6 of 18 |
| `G2` | 10 fixed / 10 domains / 3 mobile | 13 | 1 | 10 | 14 of 18 |
| `G3` | 11 fixed / 6 domains / 3 mobile | 14 | 2 | 10 | 16 of 18 |
| `G4` | 10 fixed / 5 domains / 4 mobile | 14 | 2 | 10 | 16 of 18 |

| `G6` | 12 fixed / 6 domains / 3 mobile | 15 | 2 | 11 | 16 of 18 |
| `G7` | 10 fixed / 4 domains / 3 cells on each of 3 modes | 19 | 3 | 10 | 15 of 18 |

`G1` is the nominal Pass 76 interpretation. It retains only nine cells after loss of one two-cell ancestry domain and two mobile modes. The result materially weakens the prior claim that ten physical islands are sufficient.

`G2` preserves the thirteen-package count by making every decisive fixed dependency ten-way. It is the lowest-package compound federation in the declared contest, but perhaps the most institutionally demanding. Physical separation has to be matched by separate ancestry in power, heat, corridors, controls, labor, authority, supply and reset.

`G3` buys one spare fixed cell and accepts six balanced ancestry domains. It passes two more behavior worlds than `G2` and has more tolerance for real-world coupling. It is the principal research challenger.

`G4` adds a fourth mobile path. In declared design behavior it closes the same ancestry-plus-two-mode world as `G3`, but one of its sixteen passes disappears when current evidence credit is applied. A novel carrier cannot receive survival credit before its independent source, base, crew, route, receiver and weather envelopes exist.

`G5` and `G7` buy mobile depth by replicating complete cells on legacy modes. They are valuable upper comparisons for highly uncertain fixed geography, but they are inventory-heavy. `G6` buys deeper fixed reserve and retains an eleventh cell in the governing compound world.

No federation receives strict admission because the island beneath it remains unproved.

### 6.3 Independence has a shadow price

At ten fixed cells, `G2` requires four more ancestry partitions than `G3`; `G3` requires one additional fixed island. Let `I` be the lifecycle cost of one `A6` island and `x` be the incremental lifecycle cost of one additional partition per group. The break-even is:

```text
4x = I
x = $3.77625B / 4 = $0.9440625B
```

The workbook carries a provisional $150 million per extra partition, so `G2` is cheaper in the base case. When the premium is stressed to $1.1 billion, the model correctly switches to `G3`. Restoration returns the baseline exactly.

The coefficient should not be mistaken for the cost of buying equipment from a second vendor. True partition cost includes separate sites, source contracts, heat rights, controls, credentials, operator pipelines, inventories, qualification, depots, safety cases and recurring exercises. If these cannot be separated, the correct input is not a high premium. It is zero additional independence credit.

## 7. Physical composition of the `A6` reference island

### 7.1 Three firm source yards, five usable lineages

The island should not be tied to one prespecified prime mover. It should expose standardized DC, medium-voltage AC, thermal and fuel interfaces to at least five source lineages. Three geographically and physically separated source yards host different combinations: long-duration firm generation, high-rate dispatchable generation, storage and local conversion. At least one lineage should be able to black-start another without regional communications.

The technology program should deliberately compete advanced fission microgrids, closed-cycle geothermal, high-temperature electrochemical conversion, synthetic and locally derived fuels, long-duration electrochemical or thermal storage, and future high-specific-power machines. The requirement is source-agnostic but not performance-agnostic: low-water operation, protected shutdown, islanded frequency and voltage control, maintainability, emissions containment, cyber independence and campaign logistics are mandatory.

### 7.2 Five final heat destinations

Thermal service fails when designers specify chillers and omit where heat goes. `A6` treats final heat destination as infrastructure equal to generation. Candidate destinations include dry or hybrid towers, bore fields, aquifer thermal exchange where lawful, subsurface seasonal stores, water bodies with bounded ecological discharge, phase-change reservoirs and industrial heat users capable of islanded acceptance.

The five-destination requirement is intentionally severe. Heat destinations are exposed to common ambient conditions. The program must therefore test combined heat, drought, smoke, flood, fouling and power derating rather than assign statistical independence to five identical devices in one yard.

### 7.3 Twelve service nodes and protected corridors

Ten nodes align with ten occupied-building interfaces; two are reserves able to assume lost loads. A node contains protection, conversion, pumping, thermal exchange, water-quality monitoring, local storage, communications and manual control. It is small enough to isolate, replace and defend, but large enough to deliver a meaningful share of the service contract.

Five corridor families carry power, thermal fluid, potable water, wastewater, communications and service access through separate alignments. Corridors should use sacrificial, self-isolating branches and machine-readable connection points. Autonomous inspection robots, distributed acoustic and thermal sensing, and robotic excavation can reduce discovery and repair time, but local manual operation remains necessary.

The long-term technology objective is a **reconfigurable utility tissue**: buried or protected conduits whose switchgear, valves and routing elements can create new service paths after physical damage without excavating an entire street grid. That requires new standards in connectors, insulation, contamination control, pressure management, protection coordination and civil works.

### 7.4 Water, waste and fire as one metabolism

Water production cannot be independent of wastewater and residuals. `A6` therefore uses modular trains with feed-flexible pretreatment, advanced membranes or thermal processes, brine and solids custody, continuous quality sensing and isolated product classes. Potable, hygiene, process and fire water remain physically and digitally separated.

Longer-term research should target low-fouling membranes, electrochemical contaminant destruction, supercritical or hydrothermal residual treatment, closed-loop nutrient recovery, low-energy atmospheric and wastewater recovery, and autonomous sample preparation. The island should be able to accept a degraded local feed, create multiple product qualities, retain hazardous residuals and exchange sealed streams with regional treatment formations.

### 7.5 Control without a brittle digital center

Five control partitions can operate locally and reconcile later. Each service node holds an offline safety case, approved operating envelopes and a minimal manual control surface. Cross-island optimization is desirable, but no public-service floor may depend on cloud access, one identity provider, one national time source or continuous remote command.

Machine-readable authority should express who may energize a building, release potable water, connect an external cell, shed a service, override an optimization system or transfer custody. Cryptographic credentials can improve audit and delegation only if emergency continuity includes offline verification, expiry behavior, revocation alternatives and lawful human override.

### 7.6 A standing human formation

The reference island carries 174 permanent billets, 132 trained local reservists and 74 billets of regional shared depth. At least 53 people are on duty in the minimum operating and connection posture. Roles span power, thermal, water, wastewater, controls, communications, building interface, public health, safety, logistics, fabrication, security, civil liaison, planning and command.

The loaded labor account is $56 million per year. This is not overhead attached to machinery. The workforce is one of the machines. Its qualification, housing, family continuity, relief, transport and authority must be partitioned like power sources. A workforce that cannot reach the island during heat, flood, smoke or grid failure provides no independence.

The Department should create a national service-island profession with common licenses, exchange standards and a reserve obligation. Local operators would own routine service; regional cadres would provide rare skills and relief; national mobile formations would reinforce or replace whole functions.

## 8. Fiscal and industrial consequence

### 8.1 Island account

The $1.8 billion configured-capital screen includes land and rights, three source yards, distribution and black start, thermal production and five heat destinations, building nodes and corridors, water and waste systems, offline controls, robotics and repair, two mobile docks, initial stocks, training, integration, safety, cyber assurance, full-scale test articles and program reserve.

Mature annual operations total $125 million: permanent and regional labor, local reserve compensation, maintenance, energy and process consumables, exercises, land and utility agreements, software and safety authority, major reset, environmental monitoring, logistics and insurance. Applying the inherited 15.81 ramp-weighted operations factor gives $1.976 billion of twenty-five-year operations and a $3.776 billion lifecycle.

These coefficients are deliberately explicit and weakly evidenced. They allow architecture comparisons without pretending that a novel civic metabolism fabric has an established market price.

### 8.2 National reconciliation

Universal `G2` contains 720 fixed islands and 216 mobile cells across seventy-two groups. Its fixed-island lifecycle is $2.719 trillion; the inherited mobile account is $613 billion. After retained common programs and the technology increment, the all-in national comparator is $5.791 trillion.

The Pass 76 geography-assigned portfolio contains 607 fixed cells, 459 legacy-mobile cells and twenty-two provisional fourth-path cells, 1,088 packages in total. Replacing its provisional fixed-cell coefficient with `A6` raises the base program from $5.165 trillion to $6.099 trillion. A simple low planning screen is $5.001 trillion. Neither figure is a probability distribution.

The increase is not cost growth on a program. It is the price of opening a previously opaque acquisition object and discovering that the fixed cell needs its own complete institution.

### 8.3 A new industrial sector

The industrial objective is not to award one “resilience prime” a vertically integrated monopoly. The Department needs several durable industrial lineages across power conversion, thermal systems, water metabolism, protected corridors, controls, robotics, civil works, mobile exchange, consumables and repair. Common interfaces should permit substitution; safety cases and test data should remain government-accessible; critical digital and material ancestry should be serialized.

The $455 billion research, development, test and industrial portfolio contains nineteen coupled programs. The $135 billion Pass 77 increment funds service-node demonstrators, multi-destination thermal fields, source-agnostic power yards, distributed water metabolism, reconfigurable utility corridors, offline control and authority, robotic inspection and repair, civic microfactories, workforce simulation, representative island articles and federation campaigns.

At maturity, this sector could export systems rather than surplus equipment: utility nodes, modular water and waste metabolism, protected district cooling, autonomous corridor repair, service-island control, training, certification and long-term sustainment. Export architecture should preserve recipient sovereignty and local production. Strategic value comes from helping partners own durable service, not from creating permanent dependence on U.S. emergency shipments.

## 9. Institutional structure

Pass 76 proposed a Fixed Service Island Command. This pass makes its functions more concrete.

**Fixed Service Island Command** should own readiness, doctrine, crew generation, configuration and local agreements for the fixed lattice. It should not own every utility asset; it owns the certified service promise and the conditions under which capacity is credited.

**Civic Metabolism Systems Directorate** should acquire the coupled power, thermal, water, waste and corridor architecture. Its budget must cross present utility categories because the public effect fails at their seams.

**Service Island Test Directorate** should be independent of the acquiring command and industrial primes. It owns representative worlds, evidence custody, fault insertion, cyber and authority denial, public-health stops and admission.

**Resilience Industrial Base Authority** should serialize decisive ancestry, fund dormant and surge capacity, maintain government-accessible interfaces and prevent nominal competition from collapsing into shared suppliers or software.

**Regional Federation Commands** should combine islands into `PTS-50` groups, control mobile exchange, allocate regional staff and conduct compound exercises. They own the federation guarantee rather than treating local sites as a collection of unrelated grants.

**Civil Service Compacts** should define routine ownership, emergency authority, rights, data limits, compensation, labor obligations, environmental standards and restoration handback. Prepared geography cannot be built through temporary incident waivers.

## 10. Acquisition and proving sequence

The Department should not begin with 607 production islands. It should begin with competing evidence articles.

**Phase 0 — requirement serialization.** Freeze the public-service contract, node interfaces, failure identities, ancestry ledger, evidence gates and lawful-stop behavior. Create digital configuration custody before prototypes diverge.

**Phase 1 — subsystem competitions.** Compete source yards, final heat destinations, water metabolism, protected corridors, offline controls, microfactories and exchange docks separately, but score them inside the full island service graph.

**Phase 2 — three rival integrated islands.** Construct full-scale `A4`, `A5` and `A6` articles in different climates and industrial lineages. Operate them as public utilities during ordinary periods so maintenance and workforce evidence is real rather than staged.

**Phase 3 — destructive and denied-service campaigns.** Withhold regional grid, water, communications, cloud identity, workforce cohorts, authorities, suppliers and transport modes. Physically remove nodes and corridors. Impose heat, cold, smoke, flood, contaminated feed and repeat-mission reset.

**Phase 4 — federation campaigns.** Build `G2` and `G3` representative groups. Test whether declared partitions remain independent when logistics, training, software, spares, release and repair are traced to origin. Run simultaneous events in separate regions so national common pools cannot be double-counted.

**Phase 5 — limited operational lattice.** Field the winning evidence architectures across several receiving classes. Preserve at least two industrial lineages and one intentionally different control stack. Admit only the functions whose representative evidence closes.

Production decisions should follow measured service availability, not component maturity or construction completion.

## 11. Evidence gates and falsification

Thirty-four gates remain open. They cover source and storage performance; heat-rejection behavior; water, wastewater and fire separation; corridor isolation; controls and cyber; communications; workforce availability; authority continuity; supplier ancestry; mobile exchange; reset; safety; public health; environmental effects; community rights; cost; and compound federation campaigns.

The architecture should be rejected or materially revised if any of the following occur:

- two nominal service nodes share one failure boundary under representative damage;
- loss of one final heat destination removes the public cooling floor;
- seven-day source, water or consumable autonomy cannot be sustained without uncredited logistics;
- offline control cannot preserve safe local operation and later audit reconciliation;
- the minimum workforce cannot live, reach, operate and rotate through the declared hazard;
- civil authority cannot delegate and release service during communications or jurisdictional loss;
- supplier, firmware, material or depot ancestry makes the ten-way partition fictional;
- mobile exchange cannot connect inside the forty-eight-hour service-entry deadline;
- reset cannot return the island to certified readiness before the next required mission; or
- the cost of ten-way partition exceeds the value of a spare island or a different national topology.

These are research objectives, not footnotes to be waived after the system is visually or politically attractive.

## 12. Pass 77 stocktake

### 12.1 What is now authoritative

At `R2` research maturity, this pass makes the following current:

- a fixed service island is a complete civic metabolism and authority boundary, not a facility;
- the inherited `PTS-5` service contract is the common island comparison surface;
- the eleven inseparable service functions and the minimum-operator closure rule;
- six island rivals and twenty-four registered behavior worlds;
- `A6` as the upper research comparator, with twelve nodes, five heat destinations and local reset;
- the corrected federation equation using minimum decisive-domain partitions;
- seven federation rivals and the explicit difference between declared behavior, current evidence credit and strict admission;
- the $944 million partition-versus-spare-island break-even under declared coefficients; and
- the $6.099 trillion recompiled base boundary for the Pass 76 selected national portfolio.

### 12.2 What this pass falsifies or materially weakens

Ten physical islands are not sufficient evidence for the Pass 76 thirteen-package topology. With five balanced ancestry domains, the nominal topology loses two fixed cells together and retains only nine complete cells after two mobile-mode losses. The thirteen-package claim survives only as the stricter `G2` ten-partition hypothesis.

The pass also rejects hardened central infrastructure, `N+1` campuses and node count alone as valid independence proxies. It weakens any economic comparison that prices a fixed cell as merely a prepared power-and-thermal machine.

### 12.3 What remains provisional

Every numerical coefficient, physical architecture, workforce complement, technology program, site requirement and cost remains a design hypothesis. `A6` closes declared arithmetic but has no representative coupled evidence. The minimum number and true cost of ancestry partitions are unknown. Heat destinations, source technologies, environmental limits, local rights, community acceptance, construction tempo, software safety, industrial diversity and repeat-mission reset remain open.

No island or federation is selected. Strict admission is zero.

### 12.4 Where the burden moved

The burden moved from an abstract fixed-cell count into land, corridors, final heat destinations, local water and waste metabolism, continuous workforce, civil authority, supplier diversity, repair, safety and evidence. It also moved from package multiplication into the expensive discipline of keeping nominal alternatives truly separate.

The result makes the Department larger in institutional depth even where it makes the mobile fleet smaller. That is not a defect. It is the central meaning of coequal civil power: readiness resides in an industrial and civic system, not only in deployable vehicles.

### 12.5 Highest-leverage next pass

Pass 78 should compete clean-sheet **fourth-access mechanisms** only against the geographies and failures the fixed-island compiler cannot economically close. Candidate families should include heavy-lift buoyant aircraft, wing-in-ground or amphibious utility carriers, autonomous overland utility trains, rapidly laid aerial or surface utility corridors, local feedstock conversion and distributed microfactory substitution.

The competition must hold one complete `PTS-5` cell and receiver contract constant. It should derive carrier, launch base, weather, terrain, source, crew, control, loading, unloading, interface, reset and industrial requirements from first principles. Its task is not to pick the most futuristic vehicle. It is to determine whether a genuinely independent fourth path buys more service assurance than another fixed island, another ancestry partition or greater replication on existing modes.

Rendering should remain blocked until that competition and the representative fixed-island evidence define actual form.

## Method and source boundary

This pass uses only the repository’s prior models and declared design assumptions. It introduces no external-site research and makes no claim that the coefficients are observed market prices or validated engineering performance. The workbook preserves formulas, behavior worlds, evidence gates, sensitivities and integrity checks so later evidence can overturn the architecture without erasing the reasoning that produced it.
