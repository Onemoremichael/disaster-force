## Adversarial findings

Three attacks matter.

First, `RVS` could be accused of making pictures before physics. The branch survives because the images remain inseparable from claim-bounded metadata, dimension authority comes later through root-native registers, and no image creates bidding, evidence or selection authority. Its five withdrawals demonstrate adverse disposition rather than aesthetic protection.

Second, `FSA` could be accused of funding all rivals to avoid choosing. The branch survives because the USD538M ceiling purchases a bounded evidence pathway, not production optionality. USD179M activates one balanced cohort while USD359M remains protected; early results cannot alter sibling access. The cost of neutrality is explicit and finite.

Third, the terminal compact could be accused of excessive institutional separation. The attack fails at the present resolution. Collapsing artifact acceptance, payment, observation and selection would allow administrative completion to manufacture technical truth. Whether the separation works in practice is now the next falsifiable object, not a reason to restore a unified program office by assumption.

## Method decision

The ten-pass review remains useful, but an external authorization halt does not. From this review forward it is an **autonomous adversarial checkpoint**: the research lead records the audit, applies correction or issues a conforming numbered `KILL` when warranted, resets the counter and continues. The checkpoint is not a user-approval gate. Five-pass branch limits, prior registration, resolution tests and honest branch death remain mandatory.

This review supersedes the stopping instruction recorded in Pass 230. It does not rewrite that historical record.
