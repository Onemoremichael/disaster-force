## Kill-candidate attacks

`EXR` could be killed as elaborate process around nonexistent hardware. The attack fails because the branch’s claim is not that hardware exists; it fixes the transaction, facility, event, custody and disclosure laws necessary to learn from one-shot destructive articles without sponsor-controlled evidence. Its zero states and source-empty records remain explicit.

The 144-article cohort could be killed as unaffordable duplication. That question was properly resolved at the parent decision layer: integration, fatal break and virgin reconstruction are mutually contaminating roles, while two builder roots reveal tacit ancestry. No evidence yet supports a smaller design that preserves both separations. The $25.0B figure remains exposure, not authorization.

`SYN` could be killed as editorial polish that adds no first-order result. The attack fails because it overturned chronology as the thesis architecture, reconciled figures whose conflation would materially misstate the proposal, supplied the missing invited-power doctrine and imposed a representation ladder that prevents imagery from claiming a selected fleet.

The atlas could be killed as concept art. The attack fails conditionally: its publication unit binds image and metadata, only `PUB-0` and `REP-0`–`REP-3` issue, rival forms retain equal salience, and the manuscript states that all technical objects are unbuilt and unmeasured. If those captions are stripped, the detached image loses authority.

No consumed branch should stop. Rejected central control, common machinery, evidence-by-spend, chronology-led publication, hero renderings and the discarded emblem-bearing B05 variant were assumptions or alternatives, not branches. Calling them deaths would violate the method.
